# Philanthropy as R&D

Philanthropy funds R&D the market won't price yet. Not guilt management.

Most people give to charity out of guilt or obligation. Frank's model is different: give to things the market won't fund because they don't have a direct ROI, but the world needs them. This is R&D framing, not guilt framing.

Examples of "R&D philanthropy":
- Fund AI safety research (market won't price existential risk)
- Fund open-source tools for underserved communities (market won't build for non-wealthy users)
- Fund education in undersampled countries (market ROI is negative, human ROI is infinite)
- Fund biotech breakthrough moonshots (VCs want 10-30x in 7 years; cures take 15)

Frank's emerging model (post-BV formation, target 2027):
- 1-2% of profits → cause-driven giving (initially AI safety + biotech)
- 5-10% of time → advisory roles on boards or in funded orgs
- Direct participation in problems (not just checks)

The leverage: philanthropy done right is brand-building + network-building + impact. You're not just writing a check; you're saying "this problem matters enough to me that I'm committing time and capital." This attracts collaborators, press, and users who share your values.

The veto: don't give to something just because it's fashionable or because it makes you feel good. Philanthropy is R&D, which means you should be able to articulate the hypothesis: "If we solve X, then Y becomes possible." Without the hypothesis, it's just guilt.
