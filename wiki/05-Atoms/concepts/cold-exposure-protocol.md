# Cold Exposure Protocol

Cold exposure is a state-reset tool, not a longevity silver bullet. Use it for acute nervous system recalibration, not as a replacement for the basics.

The mechanism: 30-60 seconds at 5°C triggers the dive reflex and floods the bloodstream with catecholamines (norepinephrine, dopamine). This is a mild stress. The recovery (warm-up) triggers parasympathetic rebound. Repeated cycles build tolerance to stress and faster recovery. This is useful, not magical.

What cold exposure *does*:
- Acute recovery post-workout (reduces soreness, minor DOMS benefit)
- Sympathetic tone practice (you become less reactive to stress over time)
- Metabolic flexibility (small thermogenesis effect, ~20-50 extra kcal/session)
- Mood modulation (norepinephrine release → dopamine release in recovery)

What cold exposure *doesn't do*:
- Build muscle or strength
- Replace cardio
- Replace sleep
- Replace proper diet
- Cure inflammation
- Extend lifespan (no robust human data; rodent studies are not humans)

Frank's protocol: 3-4x per week, 45-60 seconds at 5-10°C (ice bath or cold plunge), immediately post-sauna or post-workout. Paired with 2-3min warm-up to parasympathetic recovery. Cost-benefit is positive: 3 min/session, noticeable mood + recovery boost, no meaningful risk if you're otherwise healthy.

The veto: if VO2max is dropping or you're sleep-deprived, cold exposure is a stressor you don't need. Close the fitness gap first. Use cold as polish, not foundation.
