# One-Medium Mastery

Master one medium before sprawling. Sprawl after mastery is multiplication; sprawl before is noise.

The law is sequential: you build taste in medium A by drilling depth until the medium can't surprise you anymore. Then, and only then, you spread that taste to medium B. The taste transfers — but only if A was mastered first. If you start in multiple mediums simultaneously, you build jack-of-all-trades taste, which is an oxymoron. Taste requires depth.

The pattern:

1. **Depth phase (18-36 months).** One medium. 500+ outputs. You learn the constraints, the tools, the audience, the history, the edge cases. You build recognition faster than the medium changes. You reach what violinists call "unconscious competence."

2. **Transfer phase (next 12 months).** That taste now applies to a second medium. You skip the noise phase because taste is portable. Your 100 tweets are better than a generalist's 1000 tweets because taste is compressing signal.

3. **Multiplication phase (ongoing).** Each new medium is faster to master because taste is now a universal filter. Your third medium takes 12 months, not 36. Your fourth takes 6.

The leverage: Frank was 18 months into music production before he started design. 12 months into both before writing. Now any new medium—filmmaking, fashion, writing apps—will be fast because taste is written.

The brake: if you start in three mediums at once, you never reach unconscious competence in any. You become the person who has "several interests" instead of the person who mastered one and acquired the rest. Acquirers, institutions, and communities trust the latter.
