# Bandwidth of Being Witnessed

Relationship quality is measured by the bandwidth of being fully witnessed without performance.

Most people are never witnessed. They're observed, evaluated, used, entertained, but never fully received. To be witnessed is to be seen—your contradictions, your doubts, your realness—without the observer trying to fix you, improve you, or extract value from you. Witnessing is pure attention without agenda.

The scarcity: bandwidth of witnessing is your scarcest resource. You can only witness a handful of people deeply. Try to witness 100 and you're performing the role of "good listener" rather than actually witnessing. The person being "listened to" feels it. They don't feel witnessed; they feel performed at.

So the real metric isn't "how many close friends do I have" but "with how many people can I sit in full presence without my phone, without thinking about what's next, without trying to solve their problem or prove my cleverness?" For most people that number is 1-3. Frank's is probably 4-6.

The compounding: if you invest in witnessing a few people deeply, they feel the difference. They tell others what that presence feels like. People want to be around people who actually witness them. So paradoxically, by narrowing the bandwidth of witnessing, you become more attractive to people who matter.

The trap: the modern default is to spread attention wide (hundreds of acquaintances, social media). This trains you to *perform* for attention rather than *receive* it. If you're always broadcasting, you're never being witnessed.

The rule: protect the bandwidth. Keep the circle small. When someone new enters, someone old may need to exit. This is not cruelty; it's honesty about your capacity.
