# Unfair Distribution

Distribution is built before the product. The reverse almost never works.

Most founders invert this. They build in stealth, ship on launch day, and expect distribution to materialize because the product is good. It doesn't. Distribution is not a stage that follows launch. It's a parallel infrastructure that you're building while you're building the product. If you wait until the product ships to start distribution, you've already lost.

The unfair advantage is being distributed before anyone knows what you're distributing.

The pattern Frank uses:

1. **Launch the distribution first.** For Arcanea: 12,000 AI songs (music credibility), open-sourced Arcanea packages (developer credibility), published Prompt OS v1.4.0 (framework credibility). No sales page. Just proof.

2. **Distribute that distribution.** OSS gets forked. Music gets remixed. Frameworks get cited. You're not pushing product; you're giving people things to push.

3. **Extend the distribution into the product.** When the product ships, it lands on a surface that's already warm. GenCreator users came from the OSS. Starlight users came from Prompt OS. The product shipped to a warm audience, not a cold market.

The unlock: most competitors build a better product and then try to get an audience. You get the audience first, then ship something they already want. Audience == distribution == moat. The product is almost secondary.

The leverage: a mediocre product on a hot distribution network will out-earn an excellent product on no distribution. So build the network first. Build it public. Build it with shared values. Then ship the product.
