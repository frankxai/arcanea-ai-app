# Skill Stacking Over Specialization

Skill stacks beat specialization when the stack is unique and each skill is top-20%.

A specialist in JavaScript is competing with 500,000 other JavaScript specialists. A specialist in music is competing with 100,000. But someone who is top-20% in JavaScript + top-20% in music production + top-20% in AI frameworks—this person has approximately zero competition. The market has almost no other supply.

The stack doesn't have to be deep. Top-20% means "expert enough that people recognize your competence." For music production, that's 500+ released songs. For JavaScript, that's 5+ shipped products or 2K+ GitHub stars. For AI frameworks, that's 3+ published libraries or 100+ hours building AI systems. It's not world-class; it's demonstrably good.

The compounding: the first two skills take the time-to-mastery curve—18-36 months for each. The third and fourth are faster because you understand how to learn and how to combine domains. By the fifth skill, you're 60% of the way through each one when you'd normally be 30% done. The curves bend.

Frank's stack: AI (architecting at enterprise scale), music production (12K+ songs), product design (FrankX, Arcanea, GenCreator), prompt engineering (Prompt OS), founder mode (shipping multiple brands). Each is top-20% independently. The intersection is unique. That's why he doesn't compete on any single axis; he dominates on the combination.

The trap: trying to get to top-1% in one thing then stacking is slower than getting to top-20% in two things then stacking. By the time you're top-1% in JavaScript, the market has moved to something else.
