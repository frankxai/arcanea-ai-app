# VO2max as Leading Indicator

VO2max—the amount of oxygen your body can utilize per minute per kilogram of body weight—is the leading indicator. Everything else is lagging.

VO2max predicts longevity better than BMI, cholesterol, or blood pressure. It predicts cognitive performance (oxygen to the brain). It predicts injury recovery (aerobic capacity fuels healing). It predicts motivation (a fit nervous system is a motivated nervous system). VO2max is the single number that summarizes whether your health operating system is upgrading or degrading.

The causality: you can't fake VO2max. You can eat clean and have poor VO2max (all diet, no cardio). You can have great cholesterol and poor VO2max (statins don't build aerobic capacity). You can meditate daily and have poor VO2max (mind ≠ mitochondria). To build VO2max, you have to stress your cardiovascular system consistently.

The measurement: VO2max is tested via incremental treadmill or bike test to exhaustion (gold standard, $300-500 at a sports lab) or estimated via resting heart rate + max heart rate + age + a formula (free, ~80% accurate with a smartwatch).

Frank's baseline (Apr 2026): ~48 mL/kg/min (good for age 35, top 20% male). Target: 55+ (excellent, top 5%). This requires 4-6 cardio sessions per week, zone 2 + zone 4-5 intervals.

The rule: if VO2max is dropping quarter-over-quarter, every other health metric is secondary. That's the signal to re-prioritize. If VO2max is stable or rising, everything else (sleep, strength, diet) can vary and you'll still be compounding.

The compounding: VO2max builds slowly (6-12 weeks per meaningful increase). But it persists. A month of missed training drops it ~3%. A month of focused training raises it 3-5%. It's not a sprint; it's a momentum game.
