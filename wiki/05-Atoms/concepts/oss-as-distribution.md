# OSS as Distribution

OSS is distribution infrastructure, not charity. Give the atoms away; keep the orchestra.

Most creators view open-source as sacrifice: "I'm giving away my code for free." This is a loss-framing. The better frame: you're building distribution infrastructure that money can't buy.

When you ship Arcanea packages on npm (MIT license, free), you're not losing revenue. You're:

1. **Building credibility.** Every developer who uses your code is a proof of competence.
2. **Creating surface area.** Every npm package is an ad. "Built by Frank Riemer, used by 10,000 devs."
3. **Seeding network effects.** Every package forked or cited is a relationship that didn't cost you anything.
4. **Binding users to your universe.** A developer who uses your package will look at your other packages, your site, your playbook.

The business model isn't "sell packages." It's "use packages to own distribution, then monetize the orchestra." The orchestra is the platform, the services, the courses, the consulting around the packages. The packages are free; the orchestration is paid.

Frank's model:
- Arcanea packages (OSS, MIT): seeding distribution
- Prompt OS (free, open learnings): proving taste
- GenCreator (paid product): monetization
- Consulting (paid services): revenue

The packages aren't sacrifices; they're the wedge. The wedge drives distribution. Distribution drives revenue.

The moat: you can't outspend someone on packages. But you can out-distribute them. A founder with fewer resources can ship smaller packages more frequently and reach more people than a VC-backed company shipping one big package annually.
