# Biggest Problem Rule

Work on the biggest problem you can credibly move. The size selects the quality of people you attract.

This is the most under-appreciated rule in career construction. Most people choose problems based on interest or income potential. The right selection criterion is: how big is the problem, and can I actually move it?

The mechanism: large problems attract smart people. Small problems attract whoever has free time. A team working on "cure Alzheimer's" will have different median competence than a team working on "build a better email client," even if both are well-funded.

The corollary: if you pick a problem that's too small, you'll be the smartest person in the room, which is boring. If you pick a problem that's too big (you have 0% credibility to touch it), you'll waste cycles trying to get started. The sweet spot is: biggest problem where you have enough credibility to get the first 5 people in the door.

Frank's evolution:
- **2016-2020:** Music production (interesting, but not big enough to attract world-class collaborators)
- **2020-2024:** AI + music (bigger; attracted some AI researchers, but still niche)
- **2024-2026:** AI orchestration for human flourishing (much bigger; attracted Trinity co-founder Ahmad, who's working on global AI governance)

With each step larger, the people who said yes changed. The caliber went up.

The rule: every 18-24 months, ask: "Is this the biggest problem I can credibly claim to be moving?" If no, upgrade.

The constraint: you have to *credibly* move it. If you have zero track record, you can't tackle climate change. But if you have a track record in AI, you can credibly tackle AI governance or AI safety in biotech.
