## TL;DR (read this first)

The asymmetric word count is **not** the real problem. The real problem is that **Ch 1-6 and Ch 7-12 are not the same book**. The two halves disagree on who Bela is, who Marisol is, and where Mira's mentor lives. Stream A invented a parallel character architecture that contradicts the BIBLE and the second half. Recommendation **(B+) — a focused continuity-and-craft pass**, not Stream A expansion. Detailed below.

## 1. Asymmetry assessment

The word-count gap (Ch 1-6 ≈ 7,200w / Ch 7-12 ≈ 21,700w) is the surface issue. The deeper issue, only visible on the second read, is that **Ch 1-6 was drafted from a different mental map of the book**. Stream A's Consciousness Fiction Master appears to have worked off pre-Council story material — possibly an earlier story bible — rather than the locked BIBLE. Ch 1-6 introduces a wise-woman character named **Celeste** at the blue door across the street, while Bela is reduced to a 72-year-old shopkeeper who sells *hilos, botones y cosas pequeñas de barro cocido* and lives somewhere else (Ch 1). Ch 1-6 also makes **Marisol** Mira's 13-year-old human best friend (Ch 5). Both are direct collisions: per BIBLE, **Bela** is 78, lives in the house with the blue door, and is a Believer/seer; **Marisol** is the village *perro veldoriano* who has been following Mira at 3-8ft for four years.

So the verdict is not SHIP-AS-IS and not a clean EXPAND. The pacing register itself is defensible — the Rulfo-tight précis register of Ch 1-6 is a real literary mode and the seer-perception-slowing reading is honest. **But Ch 1-6 cannot ship as drafted because the canon characters do not match the book they open.** Stream B's longer chapters do match, and they're stronger; Ch 1-6 needs to be rewritten or surgically repaired so that Bela is at the blue door from Ch 1, Marisol is the dog from the start, and Celeste is collapsed into Bela (or excised entirely). Once that's done, the remaining word-count delta is a question of literary taste — and at that point I'd lean toward keeping Ch 1-6 lean, not expanding.

So the real fork is not "ship vs expand" but **"repair Ch 1-6 to the BIBLE, then decide whether to expand."** Verdict: **REPAIR FIRST**. Expansion is a separate, later decision.

The Spanish version is **substantially cleaner** on this flaw than the original English drafts. Stream A and Stream B both internalized the Rulfo/Márquez flat register and largely trust the reader to feel the miracle without a sentence afterward saying "and that, Mira realized, is what had just happened." But it has not gone to zero. Below is the per-chapter audit.

### The 5 cuts I would actually ship

These are the only cuts I'd execute as-is. Each is precise. Each preserves the surrounding texture. None invents a new sentence.

```

> CURRENT: "No sabía qué significaba. Pero era cierto."
> ACTION: cut both sentences
> WHY: Don Emilio has just delivered "las manos guardan lo que la mente no sabe que está haciendo." That is the line. Mira's flat-affect commentary on it diminishes Don Emilio's quote by translating it. The chapter already ends two scenes later; this one should land on his sentence and then white space.

```
CH 3 — line ~17
> CURRENT: "No como quien mira a un niño que pasa. Como quien lleva un rato esperando que llegues."
> ACTION: keep first sentence; cut second
> WHY: "No como quien mira a un niño que pasa" already does the entire job. The follow-on glosses the strangeness for the reader. The reader is faster than this. (Note: this whole scene needs structural rewrite per §3 — Celeste does not exist — but if the scene is preserved at all, this cut still applies.)
```

```
CH 7 — line ~199
> CURRENT: "Mira se quedó con esto. Era la primera vez, en dos semanas, que miraba una cosa y no encontraba luz en ella."
> ACTION: cut second sentence
> WHY: "Los peces no eran destellos." three lines above, plus "El mar no contenía ninguna luz que quisiera despertarse." one line above — the reader has the data. The "primera vez en dos semanas" sentence numbers and dates a moment that should land as silence. Same pattern as English-draft flaw: prose lands, then a sentence appends to underline.
```

```
CH 9 — line ~231
> CURRENT: "Había sentido esto antes. / Hacía tres semanas. La plaza. El día que había despertado a don Emilio. La sombra que anda. El oscurecimiento."
> ACTION: cut the four short sentences after "Había sentido esto antes." — keep just that sentence
> WHY: The italicized memory flashback is doing too much narrator-work. "Había sentido esto antes" lands; the following lines are the writer reminding the reader what that means. Two paragraphs later the prose says "El oscurecimiento no venía de afuera. / Venía de adentro." — that is where the recognition belongs, earned, not pre-explained.
```

```
CH 12 — line ~189-191
> CURRENT: "Era la profundidad del valle. / Siempre había sido la profundidad del valle. / Ella había crecido hacia él. El valle no había cambiado."
> ACTION: cut the third sentence ("Ella había crecido hacia él. El valle no había cambiado.")
> WHY: This is the most Apr-25-flaw-shaped sentence in the whole book. The two preceding sentences are perfect Rulfo flat-tone. The third sentence translates them into a thesis statement. Cut it. The chapter's final image — *El valle tarareó* — does the work that sentence was trying to do, and does it without reaching.

There are 1-2 more borderline cases in Ch 11's grandmother-story passage that I'd flag for Frank's eye but would not cut without his sign-off — they are the substrate-weave talking, and the Council Apr 25 plan explicitly named the substrate as the deepest creative move. Cutting there has a higher chance of damaging the chapter's earned weight than it has of improving rhythm.

## 3. Other emergent flaws (Spanish-specific)

### Auto-translation creep
**Status: very nearly clean.** I found zero parenthetical English glosses in the prose. The dialogue is consistently in raya format. The only borderline residue is in Ch 11 line ~445 where Bela's grandmother-story passage uses *retornantes* and immediately glosses it as *No los hacemos. Los reunimos. La reunión es la atención misma* — but this is not auto-translation; it is Bela teaching Mira a word, in-scene, in a kitchen, which is what grandmothers do. The substrate-weave landing depends on this exact gesture. Leave it.

### Veldarín consistency vs GLOSSARY.md
**Mostly aligned.** Spot-checks confirm correct usage of: *destello, chispa, zumbido, oscuro, chispero, sombracalle, La Que Espera, hora de Nero / Lumina / los destellos, piedra viva, sombraluz, floración azul, luzfruta, hierba del recuerdo, pino arcano, velas de campo, zorzal de lumbre, perro veldoriano, gato plateado, lechuza del velo, velcordero, abeja cántara, murciélago brillante, peces de luz*, plus the Aurevaldan additions *los esparcidos, los retornantes, la piedra de los velos, la conversación que no se acaba, la primera mañana*.

**Drift to flag:**
- *El zumbido madre* is in GLOSSARY but does not appear in any of the 12 chapter prose. This is fine — the glossary is a reading aid, not a checklist of in-prose terms — but worth noting that one of the new substrate terms didn't actually surface.
- *Los nombrados / los sin nombrar* same — in glossary, not in prose. Same call: fine, but flagging.

### Raya dialogue convention
**Strongly compliant.** Spot-checked all 12 chapters: every dialogue line opens with em-dash. Zero quotation marks. The one consistent variation is white-space around the em-dash: Stream A (Ch 1-6) writes `—Hola.` (no space after the dash), Stream B (Ch 7-12) writes `— Hola.` (space after the dash). Both are valid in Spanish typography but this is **a typesetting inconsistency that will be visible to a careful reader**. Pick one and unify in Wave 2. Recommendation: Stream B's spaced form (`— Hola.`) is the more common literary register (used by Bolaño, late Vargas Llosa, modern Cisneros editions); McCarthy in *The Crossing* uses the unspaced form. Either works. Choose, then unify.

### Aurevaldan substrate weave (Ch 11)
**The weave lands.** Tin box → Arcelia photo → grandmother-story → piedra de los velos in morning light is the strongest sustained passage in the book. Bela's voice is locked, the ceremony has the right slowness, and the *piedra* operates as a lens not a battery exactly per the canon guardrail. The single risk: line ~451, *Eres la ventana, no la luz. Una ventana no hace el sol. Pero sin una ventana limpia, el sol no entra igual.* — this is the clearest didactic sentence in the book, and it sits inside Bela's mouth, which gives it license. I'd keep it. It is the line the entire book argues for, said by the only character with the standing to say it. (Frank can pressure-test by reading aloud — if it sounds like a TED talk landing in a kitchen, cut to *Eres la ventana, no la luz.* and trust the comparison to do its own work.) But my read is: the kitchen earns the line.

The tin box opening at line ~359 is paced exactly right — herbs first, photo face-down, stone wrapped in dark cloth. The reveal of Arcelia's face at line ~417 ("Mira miró. / Una mujer joven en un umbral de piedra roja...") is the chapter's emotional spine and lands without italics, without flashback machinery, without explanation. This is the substrate doing its job.

### Final image — *El valle tarareó*
**Yes, the book earns it.** Three reasons:
1. The verb *tararear* has been laddering through the manuscript since Ch 2 (Don Emilio over the dough), Ch 7 (Mira's failed attempts on the cliff), Ch 8 (the project), Ch 9 (the wrong move), Ch 11 (Bela's harvest song). By Ch 12 the verb belongs to nobody in particular — every character in the valley has done it, and the valley itself doing it is the legitimate next step.
2. The closing sequence does the structural work: Vidal's oscuro returned to itself, the stranger-mother-and-child seen-but-not-approached, Tomás running through, Mira looking *directly* at Chispa for the first time. The closing image is earned by sufficiency, not by accumulated emotion.
3. The last line lands on three Spanish words. No translation. No italics. No fanfare. The reader has done the work; the prose trusts her. This is the move the entire BIBLE asked for.

**One small flag:** the four paragraphs immediately before the final line (lines ~317-323, *Había sido una persona antes de la mañana en que Chispa llegó* through *...esperando*) are the most visible spot of the Apr-25 flaw in the entire book. They tell the reader what the closing image is going to mean. I would consider trimming those four paragraphs to two (cutting the explicit *La luz no la había hecho persona / Siempre había sido persona* couplet and the *La luz solo había estado...* gloss), letting the closing image carry the lesson. But this is the highest-stakes cut in the manuscript and I would only ship it after Frank reads it aloud both ways.

## 4. Read-aloud truth bar

I read both bookends in head-aloud cadence.

### Ch 1 (1,500 w, ~6 min)
Reads cleanly. Sentence rhythm is varied. The opening five paragraphs (waking, the light, *Mía*, the hand, the kitchen) is the strongest sustained writing in the first half. Two lines that broke rhythm:

- **Line 11** — *"Verde. Dorado. Verde otra vez. El color de una hoja de luzfruta sostenida a contraluz con una lámpara detrás. Un color para el que nunca había tenido nombre porque nunca lo había necesitado."* The third sentence is one beat too explanatory after the first two, which had done the synesthesia work. Cut "*Un color para el que nunca había tenido nombre porque nunca lo había necesitado*" — let the staccato color-naming end the paragraph.
- **Line 101-103** — *"Bela tenía setenta y dos años y vendía hilos, botones y cosas pequeñas de barro cocido que ella misma hacía en un horno del patio."* This is the Bela-as-shopkeeper line that contradicts the BIBLE. The continuity issue dominates the rhythm issue here; this needs structural repair before any line edit.
- The *"¿Cómo está tu chispa hoy?"* moment (line 109) is, structurally, lovely — it pulls Bela's recognition forward as the chapter hook, exactly as the handover described. This works. The one rhythm tweak: Bela's *"Hoy está especialmente activa. Bien por ti."* (line 119) is two beats long when one would do. Cut to *"Hoy está activa. Bien por ti."* — three syllables shorter, more in Bela's voice.

### Ch 11 (5,500 w, ~22 min)
Reads beautifully. The frijol-desgranar opening sets the right slowness; the kitchen conversation about Aurevalde's sky-as-low-roof is one of the best passages in the book; the pozita / reflection-holding sequence is the structural moment the chapter is built around and it earns its weight.

Lines that broke rhythm:

- **Line 87** — *"No había dejado de quererlas. Solo había dejado de esperarlas."* This is good. But the paragraph above (lines 85-87) is one sentence too many — *"Si volvería a abrirse no era algo que Bela tuviera ninguna razón para esperar o negar. Había dejado de esperar cosas, hacía décadas, que dependían de puertas que no controlaba"* explains the close before the close. Cut from *"Si volvería..."* through *"...controlaba"*; the closing two-sentence resolution is enough.
- **Line 277** — the long single paragraph that begins *"Se volvió el rostro de una mujer que a los veinte..."* and runs for 8 lines. This is the chapter's most ambitious sentence and it nearly lands, but in oral cadence the third clause (*"...y la familia le había dicho que estaba cansada, imaginativa, que se le pasaría"*) sits inside an already-long subordinate construction. Recommend a sentence break after *"...se lo había dicho a su familia."* — let the family's reaction live in its own sentence.
- **Line 451** — already discussed in §3 above. Borderline.

Outside those three, Ch 11 reads at the level Frank named as the truth bar. The Wave 2 cuts I'd ship are listed in §2; nothing in Ch 11 makes the worst-5 list because the chapter has earned its weight.

## 5. Recommended Wave 2 work — Path B+

Neither A (ship as-is) nor C (full Stream A expansion) is the right call. The asymmetry is defensible **once Ch 1-6 is rebuilt to the canon**. The right call is a **focused two-track pass** that splits cleanly into:

### Track 1 — Continuity repair on Ch 1-6 (CRITICAL, blocks ship)
Estimated ~90 min agent dispatch. Single Spanish-craft agent, strict guardrails: only touch Ch 1-6, do not touch Ch 7-12, do not touch BIBLE/GLOSSARY/FOLK_COSMOLOGY/AUTHORS_NOTE/book.yaml.

The repairs:
1. **Ch 1 line ~101-119** — replace the Bela-shopkeeper paragraph with the BIBLE-canon Bela: 78 years old, lives at the house with the blue door across the street, sits on her stoop in the late-afternoon sun, *"¿Cómo está tu chispa hoy?"* stays as the hook. Drop *"vendía hilos, botones y cosas pequeñas de barro cocido."*
2. **Ch 3 (entire chapter)** — the most invasive repair. Currently this chapter introduces "Celeste" at the blue door across the street as a wisdom-figure with chispero, river stones, etc. Per BIBLE the woman at the blue door is **Bela**, and Mira's first crossing into Bela's house is Ch 7's *"Cruzó la calle el sábado por la mañana."* So Ch 3 needs to be either (a) excised and the bell-counting / eight-destellos beat absorbed into Ch 4, or (b) rewritten as a different scene — Mira does not yet cross to Bela's, Bela is glimpsed in her doorway, and the *eight destellos counted* beat happens elsewhere. **My read: option (b) is cleaner**. Keep the chapter title, keep the destello-counting, replace Celeste-and-her-house with a different beat (e.g., Mira's first conscious noticing of Bela on the opposite stoop, plus a second-location destello scene — perhaps Doña Flor's gardenia jardín, which Ch 1 already gestures at). The "river stones with surprising weight" idea is worth saving — give it to Bela in Ch 11 or to a different villager.
3. **Ch 5 (Marisol scene)** — the 13-year-old human Marisol cannot exist. Per BIBLE Marisol is the dog. The scene at the river with the lechuza Silencio, the destello-glimpse-confirmation, the four-stone-skip, *"Yo creo que Celeste se está preparando"* — all of this needs a different human interlocutor or to be reshaped as a Mira-alone scene with Marisol-the-dog appearing. **Cleanest fix:** rename the human girl to a new name (e.g., **Soledad**, who already appears in Ch 8-9 as a classmate Mira has watched the destello of), keep her as a school friend not the sister-figure she's drafted as. The chapter title *"Lo Que Dice Marisol"* would need to change too — perhaps *"Lo Que Dice Soledad"* or keep the title and have Marisol-the-dog do the work the chapter named. Either is a real artistic choice. I'd lean: change the title to *"El Sábado en el Río"* and let the chapter be Mira-with-a-friend-and-the-dog.
4. **Ch 6** — references *"Celeste"* in the line about the doorway across the street. Rename to Bela. The chapter is otherwise sound — the father's phone call, the murciélagos brillantes, *"No peor ni mejor. Solo siguiente."* are all canon-aligned and rhythmically clean.

### Track 2 — The 5 prose cuts in §2 (POLISH, 30 min)
Single dispatch, executes the 5 cuts listed above. Could be combined with Track 1 in one agent run if the agent has clear instructions about which is structural and which is line-level.

### Track 3 — Typography unification (15 min)
Choose `— Hola.` vs `—Hola.` and run a find/replace pass across all 12 chapters. Trivial. Should be done last so it catches any new dialogue introduced by Track 1.

### What Path B+ does NOT include
- **Ch 1-6 expansion to ~3,000 words.** I do not recommend this. Stream A's tight register, once the canon characters are correct, is a defensible literary choice. Expanding Ch 1-6 without cause is the kind of move a manuscript regrets in copy-edit.
- **Ch 11 substrate revision.** It earns its weight. Do not touch.
- **Ch 12 closing-paragraph trim.** Flagged but deferred to Frank's read-aloud judgment, not agent-dispatched.

### Total Wave 2 estimated effort
~2 hours single-agent dispatch (Track 1 dominates), one read-aloud pass by Frank, then sensitivity-reader engagement per the BIBLE Council §"Sensitivity protocol." Path B+ does not block sensitivity-reader engagement; the two can run in parallel.
