## Canon placement (in the larger Arcanea cosmos)

Pyrathis is **not a Realm of the Kingdom of Light.** Pyrathis is a **sister-world** — a separate cosmological body in the wider Arcanea cosmos, with its own physics, its own civilizational lineage, and its own (this is the central reveal) ontological status: **Pyrathis IS an unhatched world-dragon. The First Fire.**

This places Pyrathis in canon territory that did not previously exist. Canonical thinking until now has focused on the Kingdom of Light's Realms (`.arcanea/lore/realms/`), the Heartlands, the Settling eras. Pyrathis introduces the concept of **Worlds Beyond the Kingdom** — places in the cosmos that are neither Realms of Light, nor under the dominion of the Dark Lord, nor part of the Saga's principal geographic spine. They are simply elsewhere.

**Recommended canon tier addition** (for Logan/FrankX to consider):

> **Tier 10 (proposed): Worlds Beyond the Kingdom of Light**
>
> Sister-worlds in the cosmos that operate outside the Tier 5 Realms framework. May have their own pre-First-War history, their own gates equivalents, their own intelligence forms. Currently catalogued: Pyrathis (an unhatched world-dragon), and possibly the dead companion-world the Celestines consumed.

This would be a major canon expansion. Until Frank approves, Pyrathis exists as **soft canon** at the edge of the catalogued cosmos.

**The Celestines as cross-cosmic actors.** The Celestines are space-faring. They exist on an interstellar scale. They consumed a living companion-world to fuel their exodus and arrived on Pyrathis. Their presence in canon raises the question: **are the Celestines connected to any catalogued Kingdom of Light Realm?** Possible canonical bridges:

- Celestine origin world is one of Mar Arcano's three unnamed contributing Realms (very speculative — Celestines would have to be marine-emergent, which contradicts their stellar lineage)
- Celestine origin is itself a Sister-World, not a Realm
- Celestines are entirely outside the Arcanea cosmos and arrived from a different multiverse

**Frank's call.** The book does not require a canonical answer. The book operates in the Pyrathis frame entirely; cosmic-canonical placement is for the larger Saga to negotiate.

## Voice register

- **Adult fantasy that takes itself completely seriously while delighting in its own audacity.** Per the AUTHORS_NOTE: "Read it the way you would have read it at eleven, on a summer afternoon, without checking your watch." That is the contract. The book commits to its mashup with the gravity of a true believer.
- **The found family is real. The grief is real. The volcanic werewolf is also real.** All three at once. Nothing is winked at. The genre stack works because the EMOTIONS are not stacked — they are integrated.
- **Sensory primacy: heat, sound, color in that order.** Per the world-bible's sensory palette. Pyrathis is a planet of sulfur dioxide, hot iron, lava-channel hiss, ember-vein glow, ash-grey absence. The prose foregrounds these in ways most fantasy does not.
- **Multi-POV close third with mythic inflection at climaxes.** Each chapter inhabits one consciousness. At the peak moments (the Dragon Speaking, the Egg, the Ignition), the prose may shift toward mythic register without leaving the close-third stance.
- **Humor lives in the dialogue, not the prose.** Pyrrik (the young lava dragon) delivers comic relief through speech and behavior. The prose itself does not crack jokes. The book is funny BECAUSE it is serious; it is not funny BY being silly.
- **Arcanean voice (universal):** elevated but accessible, mythic but practical. The crisis matters because the world is beautiful — that is the canonical line. Honor it.
- **Avoid:** "delve," "tapestry," "nestled," corporate language, exclamation marks for effect, italicized hype words, the chosen-one frame ("but you don't understand, you ARE the prophecy"), the dark-lord trope. None of these belong in a book about a curse that was actually a key.

## Dialogue style — LOCKED 2026-04-26 (revisable by FrankX)

**Standard quotation marks for all spoken dialogue. Mind-to-mind dragon speech (Verathos, the First Fire) rendered in italics + present tense, no quotation marks — distinct typographic register so the reader sees the difference between mouth-speech and mind-speech without explanation.**

```
"Third time this month," Korvash said.

The market-keeper nodded at the channel. "Third time they've had to call a thermal reading. Last summer it was a once-a-year ritual."

That night, on the obsidian flats, the dragon landed.

*You are the one I have been waiting to find.*

Korvash did not move. He could not have moved if he had wanted to.

*I will not eat you. Look up.*
```

**Genre-stack neutrality.** The dialogue convention is genre-stack-neutral — it works for the volcanic werewolf scenes, the lightsaber-equivalent (Shard-Blade) duel scenes, the space-elf council scenes, and the dragon-conversation scenes equally well. No special markup for any genre's conventions; the prose itself does the genre-translation work.

## Canon vocabulary (the world's terms)

Pyrathis has its own internal terminology. None of it overlaps with Arcanea author-only canon (Arcane, Anima, Lumina, Nero, etc.) because Pyrathis is outside the Kingdom of Light cosmologically.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **the Veinwork** | Pyrathis's Resonance — the geological energy network. The planet's nervous system. |
| **the Resonance** | The general property of being in attunement with the planet. Each species accesses it differently. |
| **the First Fire** | Pyrathis itself. The unhatched world-dragon. The cosmological identity beneath the geographic surface. |
| **the Dragon's Throat** | The deepest vent. Reaches the core. Where the Heart is. |
| **the Dragon's Spine** | The 27-peak range. Ancient roost-rings. |
| **the Cold Zones** | Spreading dead patches where the Veinwork has died. |
| **Shard-Blade** | Crystallized energy weapon. Six known crystal classes. |
| **Cinderfang** | Volcanic werewolves. Shift between human form and lupine. Ember-veins glow with internal heat. |
| **Celestines** | Space elves. Stellar-anchored. Lose Resonance underground. Have an interstellar lineage. |
| **Ashborn** | Stillness practitioners. Future-sight, geological communion. Wait centuries to act. |
| **Lava Dragon / Ash Dragon / Obsidian Dragon / Magma-Heart Dragon** | The four dragon taxonomies. Magma-Heart are nearly extinct. |
| **Throat-Keeper** | The dragon who lives in the Throat. Assesses visitors. Not hostile — assessing. |
| **Embrite** | Cat-sized companion creature. Eye glow tracks Veinwork health. |
| **Heartbound** | What Korvash becomes at the climax. A bridge between mortal consciousness and the First Fire's dreaming. |
| **the Cold Season** | The ongoing dying. The book opens in it. |
| **dragonbreath** | What Cinderfang cubs call the air's mineral sweetness — their mothers told them the planet was exhaling. (It is.) |
| **shift / shifted / unshifted** | The Cinderfang's wolf-transformation. Korvash, age 20, has never shifted. The book's central protagonist puzzle. |
| **ember-veins** | The internal heat-circulation visible through Cinderfang skin. Glows when the body runs warm. |

## The Five Acts (locked in the outline)

The story-architecture document at `outline/story-architecture.md` is the load-bearing scene-by-scene reference. Summary:

| Act | Title | Scenes | Spiritual movement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | The Cold Season | 1-6 | Loneliness as second skin → first spark of purpose |
| **II** | The Gathering | 7-12 | Reluctant tenderness → vulnerability as trust currency |
| **III** | The Descent | 13-18 | Each character's grief surfaced and survived |
| **IV** | The Heart | 19-23 | Awe bordering on religion; love defeats existential dread |
| **V** | The Ignition | 24-28 | Wonder both ways; earned joy |

**The five spiritual beats — Heart of Pyrathis** (mapped to acts):

1. **The Cold** (Act I) — Korvash's ordinary world is exclusion. Cinderfang adult who has never shifted. The volcanoes are dying. The body cannot do what the body was made for. The grief is small and constant.
2. **The Calling** (Act I-II) — Verathos speaks. Korvash is summoned not despite being broken but because of it. The journey begins as escape from a life that would not contain them.
3. **The Wrong Move** (Act II-III) — Multiple wrong moves across the Descent. Each character forces something they are not ready for and learns the cost. Korvash specifically tries to shift on demand at the Obsidian Membrane — fails — and the Membrane opens anyway because something in their broken-ness reads as a key.
4. **First Contact** (Act IV) — The egg. The First Fire. The revelation that **Pyrathis IS the dragon, not has dragons.** Pyrrik's existential crisis: "If the First Fire dies, was I never real?" Answered: "You carried me. That was real." Love defeats existential dread.
5. **The Ignition / Heartbound** (Act V) — Korvash enters the First Fire not as sacrifice but as offering. Becomes a bridge. Shows the world-dragon what small lives feel like. The First Fire is surprised for the first time in eons. The volcanoes reignite. Korvash shifts into something unprecedented. **The "broken" thing was the only thing that could carry the message.**

## Cast — canonical headlines

The character profiles are at `outline/story-architecture.md` (interleaved with scene work). Canon-relevant profiles:

- **Korvash** — Cinderfang, age 20. Has never shifted. Cold-born resident of the Shallow in Kaldros. The book's protagonist. Their inability to shift is canonically a *recognition* of the seal-pattern — they were always the key, never the lock. Becomes Heartbound at the Ignition.
- **Verathos** — Ancient dragon. First voice in a generation. Holds the Throat entrance against the Celestines. Turns to stone in defense. Magnificent stubborn defiance.
- **Ashwick** — Ashborn sage. Has waited 60 years for the descenders to come. Future-sight grown patient through long stillness. The senior wisdom of the found family.
- **Solenne** — Celestine exile. Has access to her dead partner's maps. Stellar-anchored grief. By the end: chooses to stay on Pyrathis as a mortal, reckoning with what her people did.
- **Pyrrik** — Young lava dragon. Terrified. Comic relief. Delivers the most devastating emotional beat ("you carried me through the dark; that was real"). The book's heart.
- **The Throat-Keeper** — Dragon-sized. Lives in the Throat. Assessed the one Ashborn who visited. Not hostile. Assessing.
- **The First Fire** — Pyrathis itself. Not antagonist. Not protagonist. The grieving god at the center.

## Canon guardrails (author compliance notes)

1. **Pyrathis IS a dragon.** This is the spine. Every act of the book is consistent with this fact — Veinwork is dragon-thoughts, lava is dragon-warmth, the Cold Season is dragon-grief. A scene that treats the planet as merely-a-planet is a scene that has not yet integrated the central revelation.
2. **The genre mashup is sincere.** Volcanic werewolves, lightsaber-equivalents, space elves, dragons in conversation — all real, all serious, all integrated. No element is ironic. No character winks at the camera.
3. **Korvash's broken-ness IS the answer.** The Power of the Unfinished. The book's primary thematic engine. The "curse" is the key. Do not soften this — Korvash never receives a "you were always normal, you just hadn't shifted yet" reveal. They were always different. The difference saves the world.
4. **Found family is the book's emotional currency.** Four species. Different nervous systems. Real difficulty. The trust they build is earned across actual obstacle. No "we were all on the same side underneath" hand-wave.
5. **The Celestines did consume a living world.** This is canon. It is not retroactively softened. Solenne's reckoning is the cost of carrying her species' crime into the daylight, not the discovery that her species was misunderstood.
6. **Pyrrik's reality.** He is a lava dragon who is also a fragment of the First Fire's dream. Both are true. The "you carried me through the dark; that was real" beat is the book's deepest commitment: lived experience between two beings is real even when one of them is partially constituted of the other's dreaming.
7. **No prophecy.** The book does not have a prophecy frame. Verathos does not say "you are the Chosen One." Verathos says: *You are the one I have been waiting to find.* These are different. The first is fate. The second is recognition.
8. **The volunteers, not sacrifices.** At the Heart, the party refuses to let Korvash's offering be framed as sacrifice. They insist it be choice. This is canon-load-bearing for the larger Arcanea cosmos: Heartbound is a choice, not a curse, not a fate. Other characters in other books may be Heartbound through different paths, but always through choice.
9. **The volcanoes reignite. The cost is real.** Verathos turns to stone. Korvash becomes something they were not before. The book ends in earned joy, not free joy. Restoration carries a price.
10. **The book ends on Pyrathis.** No translation to a Kingdom of Light Realm. No interstellar journey to the Celestine homeworld. The final scene is Korvash standing on warm ground, lava flowers blooming where they step, Pyrrik on their shoulder. The cosmic frame remains open; the personal frame closes.
