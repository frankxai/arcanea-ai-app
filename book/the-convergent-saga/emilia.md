name: Emilia

## POWER PROFILE

```yaml
gate_primary: Fire (Gate Three, 528 Hz — Draconia's domain; where she is most
  native and least controlled)
gates_open: Six (five pre-bond; sixth catalyzed by bond; ranked as five with
  an asterisk by the Academy for administrative reasons she finds irritating)
element: Fire (primary); Light/Frequency (secondary, accessed through Prism-Luxin
  drafting, which she treats as a practical tool rather than a theoretical discipline)
power_source: Frequency (primary — Prism-Luxin drafting accesses light as a physical
  substance); Arcane (secondary); Song (tertiary, accessed through bond — Vael
  is a Storm-Singer; song enters her drafting when she is flying)
signature_ability: |
  Combat-drafting integration. Emilia can draft Prism-Luxin constructs while in
  flight — a technical achievement that most combat theorists considered impractical
  because Prism-Luxin requires the drafter to maintain simultaneous awareness of
  multiple color spectra while in physical motion, and combat flight produces
  exactly the conditions most hostile to sustained spectral awareness.
  
  She does it anyway. The constructs she produces in flight are not the clean,
  architectural Prism-Luxin of Academy demonstrations; they are ragged, luminous,
  asymmetric, and effective in ways the clean constructs are not. The visual
  signature is unmistakable: in flight she leaves a brief spectral wake — not
  drafting residue but actual color: fragments of every color in the Prism-Luxin
  spectrum, briefly visible, briefly structural, deployed and dissolved in the
  same sequence of motion.
  
  When bonded with Vael and airborne: the Storm-Singer's wind-fire breath interacts
  with her Prism-Luxin in ways that produce a third output neither of them generates
  alone — a luminous cutting force that several observers have documented as capable
  of separating two things that were not previously in contact with each other
  in directions that should not be geometrically possible.
limitation: |
  Color-blindness under adrenaline. In the ten seconds after a high-adrenaline
  event — a near-miss, a bad landing, an unexpected attack — she loses spectral
  discrimination entirely. Everything she drafts in those ten seconds is a single
  dominant color regardless of what she intended. Which color varies and cannot
  be predicted. She has spent three years of combat training working around this.
  She has not told her ranking instructors that it is getting worse, not better,
  since the bond.
  
  The bond's cost: Vael's emotional state enters her drafting. When Vael is calm,
  Emilia's constructs are more stable than her baseline. When Vael is distressed —
  which happens rarely and matters enormously when it does — her constructs become
  structurally unpredictable. She cannot filter this. She has worked out a signal
  system with Vael to communicate emotional state mid-combat. Vael considers the
  signal system beneath both of them and uses it anyway.
```

## THE CHARACTER DIAMOND

```yaml
desire: |
  To perfect the combat-drafting integration — to get to the place where the
  Prism-Luxin constructs she produces in flight are as intentional as the ones
  she produces on the ground, and where Vael's resonance is a part of the drafting
  system rather than a variable she has to compensate for. She frames this as a
  technical goal and she is not entirely wrong about the framing; she is wrong about
  what reaching the goal requires.

wound: |
  She earned the bond with Vael by being the only candidate in the Crucible cohort
  to say something true out loud — something she had never admitted to anyone, that
  she was afraid she wasn't good enough and had been performing not-being-afraid
  for so long she had forgotten there was a performance happening. She said this
  in front of witnesses. Vael landed on the platform and chose her thirty seconds
  after she said it. She has never spoken about that moment again. The moment
  terrifies her in proportion to how clearly it worked: she obtained the thing
  she wanted most by being undefended in a way she had never permitted herself
  to be. This means the thing she wants most is protected, in her psychology,
  by keeping the access code — that specific vulnerability — locked away. If
  vulnerability is what created the bond, vulnerability might be what ends it.
  She has not examined this logic carefully because she does not want to.

mask: |
  The toughest rider in the Pavilion. Confidence performed as a fixed state rather
  than a earned response to each situation. She is the first in the air and the
  last to admit difficulty; she volunteers for the worst pairing assignments;
  she maintains a commentary track in every difficult situation that is composed
  of humor and competence in equal measure. This is not dishonest — she is genuinely
  capable and genuinely funny — but it is performed at a volume that fills the room
  and ensures no one can hear the quieter channel running underneath.

truth: |
  Strength is not grip. The bond she has with Vael is not a system she controls;
  it is a relationship she is in. The combat-drafting integration she wants to perfect
  cannot be perfected through mastery alone because Vael is not a technique — Vael
  is a three-hundred-year-old dragon with opinions, and the opinions are part of
  the drafting now. What she learned in the Crucible was not that vulnerability
  produces results; it was that real contact — between her actual self and Vael's
  actual self — is the only foundation the bond can be built on. She has been
  trying to build the rest of it on performance. The bond is patient about this.
  Vael is less patient.
```

## PHYSICAL PRESENCE

```yaml
build: Compact, muscular, built for repeated physical stress and fast recovery —
  the body of someone who has been doing something demanding since she was fifteen
  and has not stopped. She moves like someone accustomed to having less than a
  second to respond to the environment.
skin: Warm gold-brown, with a faint heat shimmer at the forearms and the V of
  her collarbone — a Prism-Luxin drafter's ambient thermal signature, more
  pronounced since the bond. Dragon Rider training has left her with a collection
  of small, well-healed scars that she does not catalog and people she trusts
  sometimes trace with curiosity.
hair: Dark amber-brown, kept short and practical — cut to stay out of her eyes
  in flight regardless of wind conditions. She cuts it herself at intervals that
  align with whether she has been annoyed by it recently. The result is approximately
  right and occasionally uneven in the back.
eyes: Amber-gold before the bond; shifted post-bond to amber-gold shot through
  with threads of silver-blue, Vael's color. This is a permanent bond-mark.
  Under high drafting load or in flight, they go fully luminous — gold-white —
  for the duration and fade back within minutes of landing.
distinguishing: |
  Bond-marks at both palms: fine silver-gold lines that appeared in the first
  week after Vael's choice, following the pressure-lines of her hands as though
  tracing where contact is strongest. They are warm to the touch — not hot,
  but noticeably warm; ambient Frequency from the bond, always present. She
  wears no gloves by preference because the bond-marks give her better tactile
  feedback during drafting and she finds this useful.
  
  A burn scar on her left shoulder from a combat exercise gone wrong in her
  second year — she was demonstrating something she had not yet perfected,
  which is a category of injury she intends to stop having and keeps having.
clothing_default: Rider leathers without the formal pavilion insignia when not
  on duty — comfortable, worn, functional, in warm browns and amber. Boots
  that have seen significant use. She dresses for the next flight.
clothing_formal: Full Dragon Rider Pavilion uniform — structured, dark bronze
  with silver-blue accent at collar and cuffs (Pavilion Three Storm-Singer
  coloring). Worn correctly but without the additional personal sigils that
  senior riders accumulate; she has not accumulated them.
carries: |
  A short, dense length of Storm-Singer feather braided into a cord at her
  left wrist — shed naturally by Vael in his first year after bonding, which
  does not happen often and which she does not have a ready explanation for
  beyond "Vael put it near my hand." She does not discuss the cord. Anyone
  who attempts to touch it without permission learns something useful about
  her reaction time.
```

## SIGNATURE SILHOUETTE

```yaml
silhouette_description: |
  Seen from below, in flight: a compact rider shape against the silver-gold
  underscale of a Storm-Singer banking into wind. The spectral wake trails
  her left arm where the drafting exits — a color-fragment dispersal that
  lasts four seconds and contains every hue. It is the most visually complex
  thing in the sky.
  
  Seen on the ground: a slightly-below-medium figure with the alertness of
  someone who does not fully relax indoors. Her eyes are at moderate height
  but seem higher because they are always at the position of someone about
  to respond to something. The bond-marks at her palms catch light the way
  metal catches light — briefly, specifically. Vael, if present, is two
  lengths behind her and watching the room's exits.
```

## VOICE & SPEECH

```yaml
speech_pattern: |
  Direct, fast, and slightly ahead of the formality level of the room — not
  because she doesn't know the formality level but because she finds it takes
  too long to get to the part that matters. She uses humor as punctuation,
  deployed so consistently that people sometimes take the humor as the point
  when the point was the sentence before the joke.
  
  Under pressure: the jokes stop and the speed stays. Shorter sentences. Active
  verbs only. Nothing ambient.
  
  One-on-one, in genuine conversation: slower, and more specific. The humor
  drops away. She is not sentimental; she is, when she chooses to be, precise
  in a completely different register from Mera's precision.
vocabulary_level: Educated-practical; heavy Rider-Pavilion technical register;
  Prism-Luxin drafting terminology used correctly and occasionally used in
  everyday metaphors in ways that confuse non-drafters.
verbal_tics: |
  - "Right, so—" (as a transition from observing a situation to stating her plan)
  - Rhetorical questions used to structure her own thinking out loud: "What's
    actually moving? Vael says the south approach, which means—"
  - "Fine" as a word that means something between "acceptable" and "I have decided
    to accept this even though I reserve the right to revisit later"
  - Does not finish sentences that end with an obvious implication; assumes the other
    person is keeping up.
what_they_never_say: |
  "I was scared." She has been scared precisely three times in her adult life in
  ways she recognized as fear rather than adrenaline. The first time she said it
  out loud, Vael chose her. She has not said it since. The sentence she cannot say
  is not "I was scared" as a category — it is "I was scared of this specific thing
  in front of this specific person," which requires both vulnerability and specificity
  at once. She can do one or the other. Both together is the Crucible, and the Crucible
  only happens once.
sample_lines:
  - casual: |
      "Vael's already mapped the northern approach — he did it while I was in your
      briefing, which is his way of telling me the briefing was too long.
      He's not wrong."
  - under_pressure: |
      "Vael, south. I'm on the construct. Move."
  - intimate: |
      "I said something true in a room full of people and a three-century-old dragon
      landed in front of me. I know what that means. I know what it cost." [pause]
      "I'm not ready to say it again. That's not — I'm not telling you I won't.
      I'm telling you where I am."
  - battle: |
      "Spectral wake left, Vael takes the gap, I'll handle whatever's on the other
      side of the gap. Standard irregulars." [beat] "And try to be impressed after,
      not during."
```

## COMPANION / BEAST / MECH

```yaml
companion_name: Vael
companion_type: Beast — Storm-Singer dragon, bonded
companion_bond: |
  Irregular — Vael chose Emilia before she had entered the Crucible's formal bonding
  phase, in response to her unsolicited honesty during a Pavilion assessment. The
  Elder Council review concluded that the bond is valid (Vael chose freely; the
  Dragon Law's Sovereignty Principle was honored) but irregular (the choosing
  circumstances did not follow the established Crucible protocol). The review is
  still technically open, which the Elder Council finds appropriate and Vael finds
  irrelevant.
companion_visual: |
  Silver-gold scales in the Storm-Singer pattern — sleek, sinuous, built for
  wind and altitude rather than ground combat, with proportionally large wings
  that produce a distinctive harmonic resonance in strong crosswinds. Approximately
  three centuries old, which for a Storm-Singer puts him at the edge of early
  maturity — larger than a juvenile, smaller than an adult by roughly twenty percent.
  
  Eyes: storm-grey with a silver-gold limbal ring. His gaze is evaluative in a
  way that makes most humans feel they are being assessed and found interesting
  but not entirely comprehensible. This is accurate.
  
  When flying in bond-resonance with Emilia: the silver-gold scales pick up
  fragments of spectral color from her Prism-Luxin wake — a visual effect that
  makes the pair look, from the ground, like something between a dragon and a
  light construct. The Elder Council has noted this in the review file without
  reaching a conclusion.
companion_role: |
  Strategically: Vael reads airspace, approaches, and threat vectors better than
  any instrument the Academy operates, and communicates this to Emilia through
  a bond-sense that she experiences as spatial certainty — knowing where things
  are without being told. This is an active contribution to their combat capability.
  
  Dramatically: Vael is the relationship that reveals Emilia's wound and carries
  her truth. He communicates primarily in images, temperatures, and the spatial
  certainty described above; he does not speak. He does not need to. The temperature
  of a communication tells Emilia everything she needs to know about how he is
  assessing a situation. His opinions are clear. He has many of them. He thinks
  the theory sessions are a waste of time that could be spent flying, that Mera
  is interesting and should be observed more closely, that Arion's resonance
  is significant in a way he cannot fully communicate in temperatures, and that
  Emilia is ready for the next thing even though she doesn't know what the next
  thing is. He is patient with her. This is the only context in which Emilia
  permits herself to be the person who is patiently waited for.
```

## RELATIONSHIPS

```yaml
mentor: Commander Sarath Welkin, Pavilion Three — a senior rider of twenty-two
  years' experience who assessed Emilia's bond as irregular and has spent two
  years deciding whether she is impressed or concerned. (She is both. She has
  told Emilia the impressed part. Emilia suspects the concerned part.) Welkin
  is the relationship that models what integration looks like when it's been
  developed over decades; she is what Emilia is working toward.
rival: Corvain Stale, Pavilion Two — Fire-blooded rider, technically excellent,
  institutionally conventional, and profoundly irritated that an Irregular gets
  more observer attention than riders who followed the proper protocol. Emilia
  and Corvain are not enemies; they respect each other in the specific way of
  people who have tested each other and come out roughly equal and find this
  more annoying than they expected.
beloved: Vael — not in a way that requires qualification or explanation; the
  bond is the deepest relationship she has, which is something she rarely examines
  because examining it requires acknowledging that the relationship is built on
  the one moment of genuine vulnerability she has ever permitted herself.
enemy: The structural threat of Act Two: the Void Ascendants who target Vael
  rather than her, understanding that she is the wall and the bond is the lever.
  This is the threat that she finds genuinely frightening in a way she doesn't
  have defenses against, because it is not aimed at her and she cannot put herself
  between it and Vael the way she could put herself between a threat and a person.
crew_role: |
  In the forming trio: the forward motion. She is the one who does things while
  Mera is observing and Arion is calculating. This is occasionally reckless.
  It is also why certain things get done.
tension_with: |
  Arion — not hostile; unexpectedly generative. She treats him as a peer from
  the beginning, which is the treatment he wants and is least equipped to receive.
  Her refusal to treat him as a phenomenon has an effect that she doesn't fully
  understand until Act Three.
  
  The Elder Council — persistent and bilateral. She finds their review process
  slow and their categorization concerns beside the point. They find her indifference
  to their categorization concerns concerning.
```

  at her palms

  post-bond; visible in the thread pattern through her eyes' amber and in the
  spectral wake's secondary register
light_quality: Emissive. Light near her does not bend (Arion's quality) or
  clarify (Mera's quality) — it increases. Not brightly; not blindingly; but
  consistently more than the ambient source explains. This is the Prism-Luxin
  drafter's signature at low levels.
aura_effect: |
  The bond-marks at her palms are warm-visible in low light — a faint gold-silver
  glow that she can suppress during covert situations and has learned to suppress
  on instinct in contexts where light discipline matters. The effort of suppression
  is perceptible to Vael and produces a mild temperature-communication that
  translates approximately as "I see it, I agree, I don't like it."
faction_markers: Pavilion Three bond-cord at left wrist (Vael's feather); Dragon
  Rider Pavilion rank insignia on uniform collar; Storm-Singer scale-pattern

## MORAL ALIGNMENT

```yaml
moral_core: |
  The Dragon Law's Sovereignty Principle: no Dragon shall serve a God, a state,
  or an ideology against their will. She has extended this to herself and to
  the people she cares about: no one is an instrument. Not Vael. Not Mera or
  Arion. Not herself. She will not accept being weaponized and she will not
  permit those she works alongside to be weaponized.
  
  This is her deepest principle and also the principle that makes her response
  to the Void Ascendants' strategy in Act Two — targeting Vael — into something
  that approaches a breaking point.
moral_weakness: |
  She will accept enormous personal risk to protect the people in her immediate
  circle, in ways that bypass consultation and occasionally bypass sense. She has
  twice made decisions during Act One that were correct and also unilateral in
  ways that put Mera and Arion in positions they had not agreed to be in. She
  apologizes afterward with complete sincerity and then does it again in a
  slightly different configuration.
ideology: |
  Sovereignty is the principle; relationship is the practice. Nothing worth
  doing gets done alone, but nothing gets done at all if the people doing it
  are instruments of something else's agenda. Free and together or not at all.
temptation: |
  The offer that would almost work: "We can give Vael everything the bond
  restricts him from — full Elder status, recognized Awakened classification,
  access to the hidden Song registers that Storm-Singers were excluded from
  in the Sealing accord. All we need is the use of the bond, briefly, in
  one operation." The temptation is not the offer for herself. It is the
  fact that it is, on its surface, an offer for Vael. She would spend a
  very long time looking at this. Vael would communicate, in temperatures,
  something that translates approximately as "no" and also "I'm disappointed
  that you need me to say that." She would find this simultaneously irritating
  and clarifying.
redemption_path: |
  No fall arc. Her arc is integration — learning that the bond is a relationship
  of full contact, not a system she controls. The moment of resolution is Act Three:
  she is told something about what her bond to Vael actually looks like from the
  outside (specifically from Arion's spectral perception, which reads it as the
  closest thing to a Convergent's whole-spectrum relationship that has been documented
  in the Eighth Age outside of the Convergent himself), and instead of deflecting
  the observation with humor, she hears it.
```

## ARC TRAJECTORY

```yaml
arc_type: Transformation (integration arc — from performer-of-strength to person-in-relationship)
act_1_state: |
  The most disruptive bonded pair in the current Pavilion enrollment — not because
  they break rules, but because their combined resonance makes the older dragons
  briefly pay attention. Emilia is executing on a project of perfecting combat-
  drafting integration that nobody asked for and everybody watches. She is successful,
  visibly confident, and doing all of it from the position that the bond is
  something she manages rather than something she is in.
act_2_crisis: |
  The Void Ascendants target Vael during a patrol mission. Not as a combat threat —
  as an intelligence asset. They want to confirm that Vael's resonance with a Prism
  drafter of Emilia's caliber can be read as a Convergent's approaching Soul-link
  to the Whole Light. This assessment is correct; they know it is correct; what
  they are doing is determining whether Emilia can be separated from Vael through
  the bond itself — whether her care for Vael can be turned into a lever.
  
  The answer is no. But the reason it is no is not the one she expected: it is not
  her protective strength that makes her impossible to lever. It is that Vael refuses
  to be a lever — communicates, in a temperature she has never received before,
  that he chose her and the choice is not eligible for use by external parties
  and that he would like her to understand this rather than just act on it.
  
  She does not immediately understand what this means for her arc. She defends Vael,
  successfully, and then spends three weeks trying to figure out why the exchange
  felt different from every other combat situation she has been in.
act_3_resolution: |
  Arion, in a moment of Convergent-spectrum perception that he cannot fully control,
  looks at Emilia and Vael together and says something about what the bond looks
  like from outside — specifically, that it looks like what he is trying to learn.
  That it looks like non-grasping. That Emilia holds the whole thing without closing
  her hand around it.
  
  She does not believe this at first. Then she does something she has not done since
  the Crucible: she says something true out loud, to Arion, in front of Mera, about
  the specific vulnerability that got her the bond. Not the story of the Crucible —
  the content of the moment. What she was actually afraid of.
  
  Vael's temperature-communication translates, that night, as warmth. Just warmth.
  No additional content. This is the first time since the bond formed that she has
  not felt the need to compensate for it.
franchise_role: |
  The Saga's embodiment of the bond-as-relationship principle — the character who
  demonstrates, through action rather than philosophy, what non-grasping looks like
  when applied to something you love. In the wider Arcanea universe she and Vael
  become a reference case for the Dragon Law's Sovereignty Principle as a living
  practice rather than an institutional rule.
```
