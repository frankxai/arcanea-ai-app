## Canon placement (in the larger Arcanea cosmos)

The Hall of White is set in the **Solar Sector of the Kingdom of Light**, in the city of **Cinderwall**, in the building called the **Hall of White** — an octagonal chamber of polished Yumiko-Prism crystal that occupies one wing of the Resonance Guild's Cinderwall complex (per `LEAGUES_AND_ORDERS.md` Part Five). The Hall is the institutional home of the **Order of Refracted Light**, the small intensive study order that recognizes (does not recruit) practitioners with theoretical access to multi-frequency integration.

**Time:** The novella is set in **Year 799 of the Eighth Age** — twelve months into the Seventh League's continuing session, fourteen months before its five-year deadline expires. This is canonically a moment of accumulating institutional crisis: the Gate-system stress, the ward fractures, the Void Ascendant ideological pressure, the Architect Question all unresolved. The Order of Refracted Light operates at the edge of these institutional dramas without being subsumed by them.

**Why this setting now:** the existing canon (`LEAGUES_AND_ORDERS.md` Part Five) establishes that **eleven Prisms have been documented in recorded history; three are currently living.** What the canon does not establish is what happens when one of the three living Prisms approaches Halo's Edge with sustained-state capacity — the threshold of the **White Aspirant** tier. The novella opens at exactly that moment.

**Connection to the wider Author Team set:** *The Hall of White* is the Tier 6 **House Synthesis-centered** book that sits between the five Realm-and-World books shipped 2026-04-26 and Arcanea's institutional spine. It is also the **demonstration novella for the Prism-Luxin system** — every drafter mechanic, every halo-progression detail, every Wight-corruption fact appears on the page through narrative rather than exposition.

## Voice register

- **Adult literary fantasy in the institutional-thriller register.** The book operates inside an institution at a moment of institutional crisis, with the protagonist's personal threshold-decision as the spine. Lineage references: Brent Weeks' *Lightbringer* (the magic system is the explicit homage); Lois McMaster Bujold's late-Vorkosigan novels (institutional attention without thriller pyrotechnics); Susanna Clarke's *Piranesi* (a single chamber of polished surfaces becomes the world); Le Guin's *The Dispossessed* (the political weight of choosing within institutional constraints).
- **First-person close, single POV.** Lysara Sablecourt is the only consciousness on the page. The novella opens inside her body — in a drafter's body, drafting feels distinctive, and the prose foregrounds the bodily experience: the iris's pull, the back-of-skull ache after a draft, the specific quality of attention that drafting requires.
- **Spectrum on the page.** The colors of the Eleven Frequencies are rendered with sensory specificity. Sub-red is "the heat that has decided not to glow yet." Yellow is "the substance that holds itself together because it has always done so." Each color is a character in the prose. The reader leaves the novella knowing the colors the way a musician knows their instrument's voicings.
- **Time slows in drafting scenes.** A two-second draft can occupy a full page. The novella's pacing inverts conventional fantasy: combat-equivalents (drafting under pressure) are slow and dense; institutional politics scenes are fast and clipped. The reader learns from the pacing what is actually significant.
- **The Hall of White is sensory home.** The eight walls of polished Yumiko-Prism. The way the Prism reveals truth without distorting. The acoustic dampening (Yumiko-Prism absorbs distortion at multiple frequencies). The specific quality of light entering the eight clerestory windows at the hours of dawn, noon, dusk, and the small hours. The novella returns repeatedly to the Hall — it is the protagonist's gravity well.
- **Arcanean voice (universal):** elevated but accessible, mythic but practical. Light not dark.
- **Avoid:** the "magic school" frame (this is institutional, not pedagogical), exclamation marks for effect, italicized hype words, the chosen-one frame, the prophecy frame. The protagonist is not chosen. She is recognized. The recognition is institutional, not divine. There is no prophecy.

## Dialogue style — LOCKED 2026-04-27 (revisable by FrankX)

**Standard quotation marks for all spoken dialogue. Tide-style internal italic-present for the experience of being inside Prism State** — the moments when Lysara is in the state, the prose breaks register entirely, drops quotation marks, drops past tense, and renders the experience as a flow of unbroken light-knowing.

```
"How much can you hold now?" the Wellkeeper asked.

"Eight minutes." She paused. "Once. A week ago. The other times I have measured
have been four to five."

"And before the Hall of White exercise this morning?"

"I touched it for nine seconds and let it go before it touched me back."

   — and inside the state I am not Lysara. I am the unbroken light looking
   at the woman the unbroken light has been pretending to be, and she is
   — I am — looking back, both of us startled to find each other, both of
   us aware that this is the longest we have stood still in front of a
   mirror that is also the back of our own skull. The Wellkeeper's question
   is a long way away. The answer is here, but here is not where the
   question is.

She came back. Her hands were shaking. She had not realized she had been
holding them perfectly still until they began.

"Eight minutes is the threshold," the Wellkeeper said. Her voice was kind
the way a measurement is kind. "We need to talk about Freeing."

"Yes," Lysara said. "I know."
```

The italic-present in-state passages are the novella's most distinctive prose feature. They appear five times across the seven chapters — at the precise moments when Lysara is approaching, inside, or returning from Source-state. The reader experiences the state with her.

## Geography — the Hall of White and immediate surrounds

The novella does not leave Cinderwall. Most of it does not leave the Hall of White itself.

**The Hall of White** — Octagonal chamber, polished Yumiko-Prism walls floor and ceiling. Eight clerestory windows admit natural light at four canonical hours (the Order keeps these hours: Dawn, Noon, Dusk, Hour-of-the-Quiet at 3 AM). A central practice circle inscribed in the floor. Twelve seats around the perimeter for Aspirants, three at the cardinal points for the senior Prisms, one slightly raised for The White. The Hall is acoustically dampened by the Prism stone — voices land softly, no echoes, no distortion. The chamber is the closest thing in Arcanea to a place where the boundary between physical and spiritual experience is intentionally thin.

**The Hall is part of the Resonance Guild's Cinderwall complex** — donated to the Order at its founding in Year 312. The Order pays no rent; the Guild considers the Hall's presence a benefit to all Guild members (Hall-time accelerates Gate progression, per the Resonance Guild's contested-but-real research).

**The Wellspring at the Eastern Ironwood** — referenced. The Order of the Veil's Wellkeeper Aranya travels from the Wellspring to Cinderwall every three months to assess Lysara's halo. One scene in the novella takes place in the Wellspring (Chapter 4) when Lysara travels east for an extended assessment.

**The Athenaeum at Thal'Ara** — referenced. Lysara has been corresponding with Archivist General Sorvaine the Third about previous Prism-State case histories. The correspondence appears in Chapter 3 as letters.

**Starhold — the Corps' Ember Sector seat** — referenced. The Sixth Stellarch (in current canon: Stellarch Aelindra Starfall) and the senior Order leadership maintain quiet contact. One scene in the novella (Chapter 5) takes place at Starhold's outer gate, where Lysara meets briefly with Aelindra during the Stellarch's visit to Cinderwall.

**The Mirrorlands** — distant, never visited. Referenced only in Lysara's Chapter 6 conversation with the Order's Study members about whether White Aspirant emergence triggers Architect-tier ascension or precedes it. No one knows.

## The Cast — canonical profiles

### Lysara Sablecourt — the protagonist, current White of the Order

**Age.** 52.
**Origin class.** Arcan. House Synthesis graduate, Year 769, top of her class.
**Drafter status.** Spirit-aligned hexachrome. Drafts Yellow, Green, Blue, Super-violet, Chi, Paryl. Has touched the seventh (Orange) twice in extremity, both times with cost. Has never drafted Sub-red, Red, or Source as discrete colors.
**Halo state.** Wide ring, approximately 0.7 of the iris filled. The colors layer in concentric rings within the iris, visible to anyone in close proximity in good light. Eye color underneath: hazel, but no one has seen her eyes uncolored since she was 24.
**Prism State capacity.** Has achieved Source-flash twenty-three times across thirteen years. The shortest was four seconds. The longest, one week before the novella opens, was eight minutes.
**Wound.** Her older brother Vael was a Bichrome Yellow-Green drafter who did not register with the Compact, did not get Freeing access in time, and turned Wight at age 41. She was 23. She was present. The Wight that her brother became called her by her childhood name and asked her to come closer. She came closer. She held him until the Order of the Veil's response team arrived. The Wight was put down by Wellkeeper Aranya's predecessor. Lysara registered with the Compact the next week.
**Lie.** *I can hold the eight minutes long enough to teach the next Prism. After that, Freeing is acceptable.*
**Fear.** That the eight-minute state is not the threshold of White Aspirant capacity. That it is the threshold of Wight transformation along a slower curve than her brother's. The two thresholds are canonically not yet distinguished by the Order. She does not know which she is approaching.
**Voice.** Spare. Precise. Has spent twenty-eight years choosing each sentence in the Hall of White's hearing-distance, where every imprecision was reflected back to her in the Yumiko-Prism walls. Her speech has taken the discipline of the room. She is dry, kind, occasionally funny in a way her Aspirants only catch on the third meeting.
**Truth.** She has been protecting herself from the possibility that the Order's framework cannot hold her current state. That White Aspirant is not the next tier of the Order. That what she has been entering is something the Order has no words for, and that the words are the Order's, not hers, and she does not need their permission to keep entering. The novella's spine is the slow surfacing of this truth and what she does with it.

### Aspirant Reni Voss — POV Adjacent / the apprentice / Lysara's named student

**Age.** 22.
**Origin class.** Gate-Touched (Foundation manifestation at age 16; spontaneous Sub-red drafting; refused Academy admission, found her way to the Order through informal channels at 19).
**Halo state.** Faint inner sub-red ring; new, untrained.
**Function in the novella.** The Aspirant Lysara has been mentoring for three years. The closest thing the Order has to Lysara's named successor (though "successor" is not how the Order operates). Reni's presence on the page reflects Lysara's interior — Reni is what Lysara was, before everything; Lysara is what Reni might become, if. The novella's emotional engine is whether Lysara can teach Reni to inhabit the unbroken light without inheriting the weight that has consumed the woman teaching her.
**Wound.** Reni's manifestation at 16 was Sub-red — uncontrolled, ignited her family's home, killed her younger sister. The fire was put out before broader damage. Her family has not contacted her in six years. The Order took her in. The Order does not know what to do with a Sub-red drafter at the highest Spirit-aligned tier of the Order — Sub-red is not a Spirit color — but Reni's presence is canonically possible because every drafter eventually integrates beyond their first color, and Reni's Spirit-attunement testing showed Source-curiosity rare in Sub-reds. The Order is studying her. She is studying them.

### Wellkeeper Aranya — the Wellspring's senior, Lysara's halo-monitor

**Age.** 73.
**Origin class.** Bonded (linked to a Laeylinn-touched deer named Sifre; Sifre is 41 years old, much older than a normal deer; the bond extends both their lifespans). Per `LEAGUES_AND_ORDERS.md`.
**Function.** The institutional voice of Freeing. Has performed seventy-two Freeing rituals in her career. The novella's Chapter 4 takes place at the Wellspring during Lysara's three-month assessment. Aranya is not adversarial to Lysara — she is honest. The Order of the Veil's discipline is honesty; the Order of Refracted Light's discipline is sustained attention; neither order asks the other to be diplomatic. They are friends. They have been friends for thirty years. Aranya has read every halo-progression record Lysara has produced since registration. She knows.
**Voice.** Slow. Plain. The Wellspring's stone-hum has gentled her cadence. She does not speak with metaphor.

### Veyl Stoneshade — the Hall's lens-shaper

**Age.** 38.
**Origin class.** Bonded (Cinderfang-descended on her mother's side, three generations into Solar Sector residence; her wolf-form is small and white, rare). Per the existing `book/heart-of-pyrathis/BIBLE.md` her background traces to Pyrathis-immigrant Cinderfang who arrived through a (now-closed) Sister-World corridor four generations ago. She is canonically the only Cinderfang-Arcan hybrid documented in current Order records.
**Function.** The Hall's resident Yumiko-Prism lens-fitter. Has fitted Lysara's six-band lens twice (replaced once when Lysara progressed from polychrome to hexachrome). Knows Lysara's drafting habits more intimately than any non-drafter does, because she has measured the wear-pattern on every lens Lysara has used.
**Function in the novella.** Veyl is the character who knows what is happening to Lysara's body before Lysara articulates it. She does not interfere. She fits the next lens. In Chapter 5 she presents Lysara with a seven-band lens — capable of admitting Source-band light alongside the six chromatics — and says nothing about why. Lysara takes the lens.

### Master Tezerin Halevant — the Order's Aspirant-Master, Lysara's institutional counterpart

**Age.** 61.
**Origin class.** Arcan. House Synthesis graduate. Polychrome (five colors: Red, Yellow, Blue, Super-violet, Chi). Has approached Prism State three times in his career and never achieved it.
**Function.** Manages the Order's Aspirant cohort. The institutional voice of caution. Believes Lysara's eight-minute state is the Order's most significant achievement in 200 years. Also believes the responsible thing is for Lysara to accept Freeing within six months, while she can still pass on her techniques to Reni and the senior Aspirants. Loves Lysara. Cannot watch her risk the Wight path her brother walked.
**Function in the novella.** The institutional pull. Tezerin's love is the novella's most painful constraint — he is not wrong about the risk, and Lysara cannot make him wrong without breaking something in their thirty-year friendship. The novella's Chapter 6 conversation between them is the book's emotional climax.
**Wound.** Tezerin's failure to reach Prism State is his quiet life. He has held it well — has not become bitter. But he has spent his career watching colleagues reach what he could not, and Lysara's emergence to White was the moment he decided he was responsible for the Order's institutional integrity rather than his own ascent. He has been a steward, not a candidate. He needs Lysara's continued presence. He cannot ask her to extend her life past Halo's Edge for him. He can, and does, ask her to extend it for the Order.

### Stellarch Aelindra Starfall — the Sixth Stellarch (cameo)

Per `LEAGUES_AND_ORDERS.md`. Visits Cinderwall in Chapter 5. Briefly meets Lysara. The conversation is short and consequential — Aelindra has read the Athenaeum's classified Architect Compact annex (per `LEAGUES_AND_ORDERS.md` Sixth League). She knows what the threshold beyond White Aspirant looks like institutionally. She does not say it directly. What she says: *"If you go where I think you may be going, the Compact will not bind you. Neither will any obligation of friendship. I have seen what happens to those who cross. You will not return as my Stellarch's responsibility. Whatever you decide, it will be yours."* She says it as kindness. Lysara takes it as kindness. She also takes it as warning.

### Wellkeeper Aranya's predecessor — the Wight-killer (referenced, never seen)

The Wellkeeper who Freed Lysara's brother twenty-nine years ago. Aranya inherited the position three years later. Lysara has never met him and never will — he died in the Frequency Crisis (Year 447 ward-repair operations). His name is in the Hall of Names. Lysara has visited the Hall of Names twice. Both times she stood in front of his entry for a very long time without saying anything.

## The Five Spiritual Beats — The Hall of White

This novella runs the same five-beat structure as the rest of the Author Team set, in the literary-thriller institutional register:

1. **The Eight Minutes.** (Chapter 1) The novella opens with Lysara in Source-state for the longest interval she has ever held: eight minutes. The reader experiences the state alongside her. She returns. The Hall's other senior Order members are present. They have been measuring. The Wellkeeper has been called. The eight minutes is the threshold the Order has waited 800 years to see — and also the threshold at which the Order's standard recommendation is Freeing. Lysara understands both at once. She does not speak for an hour after the state ends. The hour is the chapter's center. It is the silence the novella will spend its remaining six chapters trying to articulate.

2. **The Compact's Calculus.** (Chapter 2) Wellkeeper Aranya arrives. The Order's senior council convenes. The institutional question is: does the eight-minute state indicate White Aspirant emergence (a category the Order has theorized but never verified) or does it indicate a slow-curve Wight precursor (a category the Order has documented twice in 500 years, both fatal)? The two cannot be distinguished by current Order methods. The recommended-by-protocol decision is Freeing. Lysara is asked, formally, to consider it. She asks for two weeks. The Order grants her two weeks. The chapter ends with Lysara walking back into the Hall of White alone, at the Hour-of-the-Quiet, and looking at the polished walls and seeing herself reflected from eight angles, all colored in halo-light from the iris outward, and thinking about what her brother's eyes had looked like in the seventeen seconds between the halo breaking and the recognition that the person she was looking at was no longer her brother.

3. **The Wrong Move.** (Chapters 3-4) Lysara attempts to force a decision through controlled Source-state extension — pushing past eight minutes deliberately, in private, with no Prism Guard, with the seven-band lens Veyl Stoneshade has just given her. The attempt is the novella's longest single sequence: a Prism State of eleven minutes, the longest documented in Order history. She comes back. She is shaking for two days. Her halo has visibly advanced; the hazel of her iris is now a thin line at the edge. The chapter's emotional inversion: she did not get clarity. She got more state, and the more state did not tell her what she needed. The Wrong Move is the canonical Author-Team-set Beat 3: she tried to make the threshold answer her on her timeline. The threshold does not answer questions like that. Aranya finds her at the Wellspring (Chapter 4). They do not argue. Aranya tells her she has six weeks before the halo will reach the sclera. Five if she drafts again. Aranya says she will perform the Freeing personally, when Lysara asks. *When* — not *if*. The chapter ends with Lysara and Sifre the deer in the Wellspring's central hall, the deer breathing slowly, Lysara watching the deer breathe, the Wellspring's Laeylinn Jade ambient hum present in her bones for the first time without her trying to interpret it. She sleeps that night for nine hours, the longest she has slept in three years.

4. **First Contact.** (Chapter 5) Stellarch Aelindra's Cinderwall visit. The conversation at Starhold's outer gate. Aelindra's quiet warning-as-kindness about the Architect Compact's classified annex. What Aelindra does not say (but Lysara hears): the Order's framework cannot hold what Lysara is becoming. The institutional path forward is one Lysara has been refusing to consider because considering it requires letting the Order continue without her. After Aelindra leaves, Lysara visits Veyl Stoneshade's workshop. Veyl is there alone. Lysara asks Veyl how the seven-band lens differs from the six-band. Veyl shows her. The seven-band has a faint silver thread woven into the band-junctions — Yumiko-Prism that has been twice-refined, accepting Source-light without burning the iris. Veyl says: *"The seven-band is not for sustained drafting. It is for one moment. The moment when you decide whether to keep the lens or set it down. Either is a choice. The lens does not require you to use it."* Lysara takes the lens home. She places it on her bedside table beside the drinking glass. She does not pick it up that night. She sleeps without dreams. The chapter ends here.

5. **The Carrying.** (Chapters 6-7) The conversation with Master Tezerin (Chapter 6). The institutional pull. The thirty-year friendship. Tezerin asks her, plainly, to accept Freeing. She refuses, plainly. He asks her to defer the refusal until they have spoken once more. She agrees, plainly. They sit together in the Hall of White at Dusk. Reni comes in, sees them both, leaves quietly. The light through the eight clerestory windows is gold-orange. Lysara reaches for her seven-band lens — she has been carrying it since Veyl gave it to her — and places it on her practice circle's central inscription. She does not draft. She tells Tezerin: *"I do not yet know what is on the other side. I do know that I have been treating the Order as if it could hold the answer for me. It cannot. The Order is what it is. I am what I am. The threshold is mine. If I cross, I cross alone. The Order has done its work. So have I, for it. We are not in debt to each other anymore."* Tezerin weeps. She holds him. They are both quiet for a long time. **Chapter 7:** Three weeks later. The novella's final scene. Lysara at Hour-of-the-Quiet in the Hall of White, alone, with the seven-band lens. Reni is asleep in her own quarters. The senior Order members have agreed not to monitor this attempt. Aranya is at the Wellspring but has been told. Tezerin is in his own quarters; he asked not to be told until after. Lysara breathes. She picks up the lens. The novella's final paragraph is Source-state — the italic-present register — but rendered for the first time without a return-to-prose. The state opens. The state does not close. The novella ends inside the light. The reader does not see her come back. **Whether she becomes White Aspirant, becomes Architect, becomes Wight, or simply stays inside the state is the canonical-open question the novella refuses to resolve.** A two-line author's-note coda confirms: she did not return that night. The Order has reserved her seat at the senior Prism table. It remains empty. The Hall of White, when entered at Hour-of-the-Quiet, sometimes glows faintly along the eastern wall.

## Canon guardrails

1. **The novella does not resolve the threshold question.** Lysara's fate after the state opens is canonically open. Future works may reveal — or may continue to leave open. The Author Team set's pattern of "the book ends inside the cosmology, not outside it" applies absolutely.
2. **Drafting mechanics align 1:1 with `PRISM_LUXIN_SYSTEM.md`.** Halo progression, Wight symptomology, Yumiko-Prism lens behavior, the eleven frequencies, polychrome ladder, the Element-bridge framework — all canon. No exceptions.
3. **No prophecy.** No one knows Lysara is the candidate-of-her-generation. The Order recognizes her competence and her current state; the Order does not foresee.
4. **No Lightbringer trope inversion.** The novella does not reveal that Lysara was actually the secret Black Prism (Shadow) all along, or that she was actually the Architect-emergent who only seemed to be a White, or any other genre-twist mechanism. She is what she is. The novella's craft is in the experience of being her, not in the surprise of who she turns out to be.
5. **The Order is not monolithic.** Tezerin, Aranya, Veyl, Stellarch Aelindra each have their own institutional position. The novella honors all of them — none is the antagonist; all are the friction of an institution facing a category-expansion event.
6. **The Wight thread is real.** Lysara could become a Wight. The novella does not soften this. The reader's tension across all seven chapters is genuine. The ambiguity at the end is not a fake-out; it is the canonical state of the threshold.
7. **First-person POV is locked.** No alternate POV chapters. The novella is Lysara's. The reader's intimacy with her experience is the engine.
8. **The Hall of White is its own character.** The Yumiko-Prism walls, the eight clerestory windows, the four canonical hours, the practice circle — all named, all consistent across chapters. The Hall is the only spatial continuity in the novella.
9. **Reni is the future.** The novella's hopeful thread is whether Lysara has taught Reni enough. Reni's halo is faint at the open. By the close, Reni has performed her first deliberate Sub-red draft in the Hall — with a Yumiko-Prism lens Veyl fitted for her, with Lysara watching, with a registered halo-progression entry beginning. The future of the Order is on the page. Lysara taught what she could. The teaching landed.
10. **The book ends inside the light.** No epilogue with a "five years later" institutional update. No revelation of whether Lysara emerged. The threshold was always going to be hers alone. The novella honors that.
