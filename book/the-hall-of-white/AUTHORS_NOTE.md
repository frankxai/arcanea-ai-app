# Author's Note

The Hall of White began as a question I owed to a magic system I have loved for fifteen years.

Brent Weeks' *Lightbringer* novels gave the world a chromatic-drafting magic system that is, by some distance, the most fully realized practitioner-level magic ever published in fantasy: nine colors, each with distinct physical luxin, halos that grow with use, the Wight transformation that is the failure mode, the Freeing that is the mercy. I have read those books too many times to count. The system does what magic systems most rarely do: it operates at the body's level. You feel a draft. You feel a halo. You feel the threshold approaching.

I have been building Arcanea for years now. The cosmology has its own bones — Ten Gates, Five Elements, the Tu Linh, the Tide-Speakers, the Tieng Goi — each Realm with its own practitioner discipline. What I had not yet committed to was a chromatic-drafting layer that brought Lightbringer's mechanics into Arcanea's frame. The reason was simple: it felt like a graft. Borrowed.

The realization that broke the impasse came in a single sentence: *the Lightbringer system describes physics that exist in any sufficiently developed magic-cosmology.* Brent Weeks and I are not in dialogue. We are looking at the same phenomenon from two angles. His phrase for what happens when light passes through a body is "drafting." Mine has been "channeling resonance through Gate frequency." They are the same thing. Once that landed, the absorption was not a graft. It was a discovery.

The companion canon document — `.arcanea/lore/PRISM_LUXIN_SYSTEM.md` — is the fully integrated reading. The eleven Arcanean frequencies (the Ten Gates plus the unrefracted source-light) are the eleven chromatic bands of Lightbringer canon, with Arcanea's Source Gate doing the work that Lightbringer reserves for the Lightbringer figure. The halo, the Wight, the Freeing, the polychrome ladder, the Yumiko-Prism crystal lens — all of it canonical now. Arcanean readers who have never read *Lightbringer* will receive the system as Arcanean. Lightbringer readers will recognize what has been preserved and what has been integrated. Both readings are complete.

---

This novella is the showcase. The narrative vehicle for the canon. The book where you watch a person live inside the system.

It is set in the Hall of White — an octagonal chamber of polished Yumiko-Prism crystal in House Synthesis's Cinderwall complex, the institutional home of the Order of Refracted Light. The protagonist, Lysara Sablecourt, is the current senior Prism — the highest-active practitioner of Source-recovery in eight centuries. She has just held Prism State for eight minutes. The Order has waited 800 years to see this. The Order's standard recommendation, on seeing this, is that she accept Freeing within six months — because what comes next is either the never-before-confirmed White Aspirant tier (a category the Order has theorized) or the slow-curve Wight transformation (a category the Order has documented twice, both fatal). Current Order methods cannot distinguish.

The novella is the threshold question, dramatized. Whether Lysara crosses, the book does not say. The threshold was never going to be the Order's to resolve. The book closes inside the light.

---

I am writing this novella in the institutional-thriller register I learned from Lois McMaster Bujold's late Vorkosigan novels — *Memory* especially — where the slow institutional unwinding of a person's identity is the actual narrative. Susanna Clarke's *Piranesi* taught me that a single chamber, rendered with full attention, can hold a novella. Le Guin's *The Dispossessed* taught me that the political weight of choosing inside an institutional constraint is the literary subject of fantasy at its highest register. Brent Weeks taught me that the body in magic is more interesting than the politics.

The novella was drafted by Claude Opus 4.7 working under my creative direction, against the BIBLE document I held throughout. Every passage was read, chosen, or shaped by my hand. The cover was generated by Google's Nano Banana Pro to direction.

The percentages do not interest me. The decisions were mine.

---

The book is dedicated to anyone who has held a discipline long enough to feel it changing them — and who has come to the threshold of a question the discipline cannot answer for them, and has chosen, alone, with no institutional protection, to keep going.

Lysara crosses or does not cross, in those final seconds, alone. The Order reserves her seat. The Hall of White, sometimes, on certain nights, glows faintly along the eastern wall.

I do not know what that glow is. I think she might.
