# LINH CHI — The Companion

**Name:** Linh Chi — "sacred mushroom, spirit fungus." The mushroom of immortality in Vietnamese folk medicine, found at the intersections of old wood and new growth. Named by An, reluctantly, at 3 AM, when the animal refused to be called anything else.

**Arcanean Origin:** A Vei'ranh — a Godbeast echo-creature. Fragment-echo of Kyuro (Unity Gate Godbeast), shaped over centuries by the Vietnamese wetland ecology. Not a full Godbeast. A living crystal-entity that formed in a corrupted Gate site and has been waiting.

---

## Form

Approximately the size of a large cat. The body reads as mammalian until you look closely and realize the "fur" is thousands of microscopic crystalline filaments, refracting light in patterns that shift with emotion — emerald green at rest, deep gold when curious, blue-silver when frightened. The structure underneath is closer to a tree than an animal: branching, connected, rooted. Linh Chi needs contact with earth or water to fully regenerate.

**The face:** Not cute. Ancient. Wide, dark eyes with no visible pupil — just depth, like looking into still water at night. A mouth that opens rarely, revealing something that might be teeth if teeth were made of jade. Ears like folded leaves that rotate toward sound with terrible precision.

**Scent:** Rain on limestone and green things in the dark. Intensifies when something significant is about to happen.

**Second form** (revealed late Book 1): The crystal filaments extend outward in a network — not physical, more like visible frequency — creating a sphere of Unity Gate resonance. Inside this sphere, An can hear every living thing within range clearly: intent, memory, presence. She uses it exactly twice in Book 1. Both times matter enormously.

---

## Personality

Linh Chi is not a sidekick.

Conscious since before the French colonial period. Has opinions about historical events that emerge contextlessly — a deep shudder at certain footage, ancient recognition at specific landscapes. An pieces this together slowly.

The attachment to An is not utility or hierarchy. It is recognition: *"Two beings who see each other clearly enough to say: I choose to walk alongside you."* Linh Chi chose An. An is still processing this.

**Communication:**
- Emotional transmission (what An feels from it)
- Forehead against An's wrist = *pay attention, something important*
- Lying flat, covering eyes with leaf-fold ears = *I disagree with this plan and want this on record*
- Sound like a slowly struck crystal bowl = *you are hurting yourself and I am watching you do it*

**With strangers:** Categorically refuses to be impressed by humans trying to impress it. Enthusiasm, dominance, cooing = ancient contempt. Quiet and waiting = mild interest. An's mother sat on the floor and talked about the weather — approved immediately.

**With Quan:** Four months of assessment before doing the forehead-press gesture in his direction. An explains this is the equivalent of a very good review from a very harsh critic. Quan, who has dealt with mythological entities his entire life, is somehow more flustered by this than by any of them.

**Fear:** Not death — Linh Chi is old enough for that peace. The fear is that the connection will fail: that the world will finish severing itself from its own deep nature before anyone can hear what the land has been trying to say. An becomes the answer to this fear. This is why the attachment is not cute. It is desperate, in the patient, ancient way of things that have been waiting too long.

---

## The Bond That Makes Readers Cry

Middle of Book 1. An has had a bad day — a rescue gone wrong, an animal she could not save. She is sitting on the floor of her apartment at midnight, not crying exactly, just very still in the dark.

Linh Chi climbs into her lap. Presses its forehead against her sternum, directly over her heart, and holds perfectly still.

An feels, for the first time clearly and completely, what Linh Chi feels about her.

It is not love in the human sense. It is recognition. It is: *I have been looking for the sound of you for a very long time, and you are exactly what I thought you would be, and I am not afraid anymore.*

An sits in the dark for a long time. Then she says, out loud, to no one: "Okay. Okay, I hear you."

This is the moment the wall comes down for real.
