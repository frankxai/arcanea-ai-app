# NGUYEN THUY AN (Nguyen Thuy An)

**Name etymology:** Thuy — "graceful, drooping like willow branches." An — "peace, tranquility." Together: the peace that bends without breaking. Her grandmother calls her "con lieu" — little willow.

**Age:** 24
**Gate:** Unity Gate (963 Hz, Goddess Ino, Godbeast Kyuro)
**Origin:** Gate-Touched (spontaneous awakening, mutant class)

---

## Physical

Five foot two, carries herself like she is deciding whether the room deserves her. Dark hair in a perpetually escaping low bun — a flyaway strand she tucks behind her ear when thinking, a gesture so habitual she does not know she does it. Hands calloused from fieldwork. A faint chemical burn scar on the left index finger from a wildlife rehabilitation incident she tells as a funny story but that actually frightened her badly.

Not beautiful in a way anyone warns you about. She becomes beautiful. People realize it mid-conversation, then cannot stop noticing. Wide-cheeked, expressive to a fault — she cannot hide what she feels and has largely stopped trying. When she laughs it is sudden and complete: head back, eyes shut.

**Fear tell:** She goes very still. Every other expression passes across her face like weather; fear turns her to stone.

**Scent:** Eucalyptus soap and something faintly animal, earthy.

---

## Character Diamond

**DESIRE:** To be ordinary. A career she can explain at Tet dinner. Her mother to stop worrying. To save enough animals to justify the decade of education she borrowed money for. Very quietly: to be good at something in a way other people can see.

**WOUND:** When An was nine, she had a dog named Mit. She could feel what Mit felt — some formless childhood sense of his emotions running underneath her own like a second heartbeat. She thought this was normal. One afternoon, Mit was hit by a motorbike. An felt it happen before she heard the sound. She ran out. Held him. Felt every last thing he felt as he died, all of it, without separation, without mercy. The entire ending of a consciousness passing through her like current.

She built a wall between herself and every animal she touched for the next fifteen years and told herself she was being professional.

**MASK:** Pragmatism. She is brisk, competent, scientifically rigorous, slightly exasperated by sentimentality. She does not name the animals she rehabilitates. She uses numbers. She has a reputation for being "level-headed." She has earned it by clamping down on something that has always wanted to scream.

**TRUTH:** The wall she built to protect herself has also kept out everything worth having. To feel what another being feels — to receive it fully, without armor — is not weakness. It is the Unity Gate itself: the frequency that runs between all living things, the realest thing in the world, which she has been calling a malfunction for fifteen years.

---

## Career & Ground

Wildlife biologist and veterinary technician at a conservation NGO in Hanoi — specializing in primate and pangolin rehabilitation. Earns 12 million VND a month (not enough). Lives with two roommates in a fourth-floor walk-up in Tay Ho. Cooks banh cuon on Saturday mornings — her grandmother's recipe, the one ritual she has kept. Watches wildlife documentaries the way other people watch comfort television. Has not had a relationship last longer than four months in five years.

---

## Gift Awakening

Night intake — a traumatized clouded leopard cub, orphaned, refusing to eat. Six hours of clinical detachment. At 2 AM, alone, exhausted, she puts her hand on the cub's side — against protocol — and the wall fails.

She feels the cub's terror. The specific memory of burning forest. Three days of silence after the mother's heartbeat stopped. The human hands that were different. An holds the cub and cries for the first time in years, and the cub, for the first time in three days, eats.

---

## Series Arc

- **Book 1:** Isolated and armored -> tentatively, terrifyingly open
- **Book 2:** Overwhelmed receiver -> learning to filter, set boundaries, choose
- **Book 3:** Passive receiver -> active speaker. She can send, not just receive. She discovers the Unity Gate.
- **Book 4:** She is not "the chosen one." She is the person who chose.

## What Makes Her Laugh

The specific comedy of animals doing things with complete confidence in the wrong direction. Bureaucratic absurdity. Her friend Linh's impression of their department director. The moment when two people realize simultaneously that neither of them wants to be first to say the obvious thing.

## What Keeps Her Up At Night

The math. She knows the numbers — how many animals taken from Vietnamese forests each year, how many survive, how many she can help. The wall she built to stay functional is also what lets her keep working, and she knows this, and she hates it, and sometimes at 3 AM she lies awake doing the calculation of how much she has traded away in order to be useful.
