# Spread Ch 10 — El Árbol de los Nombres

**Aspect ratio:** 4:3 landscape
**Output:** `lumara-valle-de-las-chispas-spread-ch10.png`
**Book beat:** Lila presses her hand to the warm bark of the great tree
and gives back her name. A new florchispa blooms where her hand touches.

## What this image must do

Close the book. This is the **single most important image** alongside
cover v2. The image must feel like a benediction — like the moment of
giving something away and receiving the whole world in return.

## Composition

A close low angle looking up the trunk of the great tree. The frame is
dominated by the **warm honey-coloured bark** of the tree itself,
which fills more than half the image as a vast vertical surface with
visible pencil-line bark texture, knots, and the slow rhythm of
growth.

At the lower-left third, in three-quarter view: **Lila**, standing
small in front of the trunk, her right hand pressed flat against the
warm bark. Her face is in three-quarter profile, calm, with **two
tiny silver tear-tracks just visible on her cheek** — not sadness,
the kind of tears that come when something opens.

Where her hand touches the bark: **a fresh new florchispa has just
bloomed** on the bark itself, glowing gold with the faintest violet
in its centre — brighter than any other florchispa visible in the
image. It has just opened. The bark around her hand is slightly warmer.

In the right third of the frame, partly behind Lila and slightly
back: **Aurelia** standing a few quiet steps behind, smiling softly
with her eyes (not with her mouth). She is the witness to this moment.

Conejito sits on the soft plum-violet ground at Lila's feet.

In the upper third of the frame, where the trunk recedes upward and
the great branches begin: **thousands of florchispas in the canopy**
visible as a scattered constellation of gold-silver metallic-ink
points against the dusty plum-violet sky.

## Negative space

The deep plum-violet sky between the upper branches is mostly empty —
the canopy is constellation, not mass. The lower-right corner of the
ground is soft empty meadow.

## Colour choreography

- **Warm honey-cream** on the tree trunk's bark (this is the warmest
  large surface in the entire book).
- **Cream linen** on Lila and Aurelia's clothing.
- **Brightest gold-violet metallic ink** at the new florchispa under
  Lila's hand — the single hottest point in the image.
- **Scattered gold-silver metallic ink** for the thousands of canopy
  florchispas, dimmer than the new one.
- **Silver-cream** for Aurelia's shift dress.
- Two tiny **silver tear-tracks** on Lila's cheek.
- Grey-violet paper for the sky and the meadow ground.

## Light

The canopy of florchispas casts a soft gold-silver ambient downward
across the whole foreground — catching on Lila's hair, on Aurelia's
braid, on the bark texture. The new florchispa under Lila's hand
casts an additional small warm gold halo onto her palm and onto the
bark around it. There is no other light source — the tree IS the
light.

## Lila's & Aurelia's faces

Lila: three-quarter profile, calm, eyes on her own hand against the
bark. Two silver tear-tracks visible. Mouth softly closed. The face
of a child who has just understood that she is giving and receiving
at the same time.

Aurelia: softer back-third position, eyes on Lila, the slight smile
of a friend watching a friend do something important. Not smiling
with her mouth — smiling with her eyes only.

## Make-or-break detail

The new florchispa blooming under Lila's hand is the **whole image.**
It must be drawn with extreme care — every petal edge, the gold-violet
heart, the slight halo where it has just opened. The bark texture
around it should feel slightly warmer, as if a tiny patch of bark has
also responded.

The two silver tear-tracks must be **subtle** — the viewer should see
them only on the second look, and recognise that they are not sad
tears. If the tears look dramatic, the image fails.

This is the image Ana's niece will remember the longest. Treat every
mark.
