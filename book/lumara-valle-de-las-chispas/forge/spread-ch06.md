# Spread Ch 6 — The Butterfly Paints the Air

**Aspect ratio:** 4:3 landscape
**Output:** `lumara-valle-de-las-chispas-spread-ch06.png`
**Book beat:** The butterfly leaves trails of colour in the air with each
wingbeat — and Lila, speaking softly, leaves her first colour-stroke too.

## What this image must do

Show the moment a child **realises she is also a maker of beauty.** The
butterfly has been painting the air; Lila opens her mouth and, surprised
into wonder, paints a small cream stroke of her own. The picture must
feel like the moment a new ability is discovered.

## Composition

The setting is the **floating island-garden** — a small platform of soft
plum-violet earth suspended in deep night sky, with no horizon line, no
above, no below. Florchispas the size of Lila's hand grow along the
edges of the platform.

Lila stands at centre on the small island, slightly turned in
three-quarter view, Conejito tucked under one arm. Her mouth is softly
parted — she has just whispered something.

Just past her face, hovering at the rule-of-thirds upper-right: **a
small black-winged butterfly**, wings tinted matte ink-black, body
small. The butterfly has been beating its wings; the air around it
holds **three faint brushstrokes of pure colour** left by recent
wingbeats: a gold curve in the upper right, a violet arc in the middle,
a green-water swoosh trailing toward the lower right. Each stroke is
clearly paint — a single brush-mark with visible texture, fading
softly at its tail.

And — the heart of the image — emerging from just past Lila's lips, a
single small **cream-coloured brushstroke**, her first. It is smaller
and shyer than the butterfly's strokes, but visibly the same kind of
mark. She has just discovered she can do this.

## Negative space

There is no horizon. The whole frame above, below, and to the sides of
the small platform is **deep plum-violet paper night**, with a scatter
of tiny pencil-point stars. The platform floats. The island is small
enough that it occupies less than the lower half of the frame.

The colour-strokes hang in the negative space — that's their gallery.

## Colour choreography

- **Cream linen** on Lila and on her own emergent cream-coloured stroke.
- **Gold, violet, green-water** for the butterfly's three trailing
  strokes. These are the ONLY moments where colours beyond the
  four-tone palette appear in the entire book. Use them sparingly —
  each is a single brushstroke, no bigger than a thumb.
- **Florchispa-gold** glow softly along the platform edge.
- **Ink-black** for the butterfly itself.
- Grey-violet paper everywhere else.

## Light

The platform's florchispas are the only light source — they cast a soft
gold-cream up onto Lila and onto the butterfly's underside. The
colour-strokes themselves carry their own slight inner glow but stay
matte and painterly.

## Lila's face

Three-quarter view, lips just parted in quiet surprise — not gasping,
not amazed, but **surprised into stillness.** Her eyes are on the
cream stroke emerging from her own mouth, not on the butterfly. She
has just discovered her own first brushstroke.

## Make-or-break detail

The colour-strokes must read as **paint suspended in air**, not as
glow or light. Every stroke should have a visible brush-direction, a
slightly drier tail where it fades, and an obvious "this is gouache"
quality. The cream stroke from Lila's mouth must be **smaller, shyer,
slightly more tentative** than the butterfly's — the first stroke of
a beginner alongside the work of a master.

The composition should feel like a small chapel: the platform a quiet
floor, the strokes hanging in the air like a chandelier of paint.
