# Chapter Three: Father's Secret

The cottage smelled of cold iron and burning cedar.

Orion lay on the narrow cot, his breathing a wet, shallow rattle that seemed to vibrate in the timber walls. The skin of his chest was grey, the dark, thready veins of the shadow corruption branching outward from the old scar on his ribs—a wound he had carried since before Arion was born, dormant for twenty years, now woken by the encounter in the dusk woods.

Mera was bent over him, her forehead slick with sweat, her hands glowing with a soft, pulsing blue water-light. She was trying to draw the heat from his brow, but every time the blue light touched his chest, it hissed and went dark, the grey veins pulsing back in angry, jagged lines.

"It's too deep, Arion," Mera said, her voice shaking. "It's not a fresh scrape. It's... it's like a root that's been waiting in the stone, and now it's found water."

Arion stood at the foot of the cot, his hand resting on the wool coat where Mamoru sat. The pup was quiet, his gold eyes wide, his head tilted as if listening to a sound that was too high for human ears.

*Dissonance,* the thought came into Arion’s mind—cold, like iron plates grinding. *The old hunter’s chord is out of tune. Let me touch the string.*

Mamoru leaped down from the coat, his paws hitting the pine floor without sound. He walked to the side of the cot, his blue-white fur beginning to glow with a sharp, gold-tinged light that smelled of clean ozone and high mountain snow.

"Mamoru, no," Mera warned, reaching out.

But the pup did not hesitate. He placed his front paws directly against the dark veins on Orion's ribs.

The reaction was immediate. A high, ringing tone vibrated through the room—a sound like a crystal glass struck in a silent hall. The black veins on Orion's chest flared, a dark, smoke-like mist rising from his skin as the starlight from Mamoru's paws poured into the wound. The pup’s head was thrown back, his teeth bared in a silent, agonizing effort, his blue-white fur pulsing with a light so bright it cast sharp shadows against the daylight coming through the window.

Arion felt the pull in his own chest—a hot, heavy wire of connection that ran from Mamoru's heart into his own, drawing on his own vitality to fuel the light. He did not break the connection. He leaned into it, letting his own energy flow down the bond.

With a final, clear ring, the dark mist cleared.

The grey veins on Orion's chest receded, turning back into the old, silver-white scar tissue. The rattling in his chest stopped. He let out a long, clean breath, his eyes opening slowly, the dull glaze gone, replaced by a quiet, exhausted clarity.

Mamoru collapsed onto the blanket, his ears drooping, but his gold eyes were warm as he looked up at Arion.

*Attuned,* the pup thought, a faint, sleepy warmth. *The old stone holds.*

Orion sat up slowly, his hand touching the silver scar. He looked at the wolf pup, then at Mera, and finally at Arion.

"You should not have done that, Arion," Orion said, his voice quiet but steady. "Some debts are meant to be paid in full."

"Not this one," Arion said.

Orion looked at Mera. "Leave us for a moment, child. I need to speak with my son."

Mera looked at Arion, her green eyes filled with a dozen questions she didn't ask, then nodded and stepped out into the morning fog, closing the heavy door behind her.

Orion reached under the cot, his fingers finding a hidden latch in the timber frame. A small drawer slid open, and from it he lifted a long, linen-wrapped bundle. He laid it across his knees and unwrapped it with slow, reverent hands.

It was a bow, but it was not made of ash or yew. The limbs were a pale, crystalline material that seemed to hold a faint, silver light even in the shadows, its grip wrapped in worn leather that had been darkened by years of sweat.

"This is Lumios," Orion said, his fingers tracing the curve of the upper limb. "The Starlight Bow. I carried it when I was a Sentinel of the Draconian Forge. Before I realized that the weapons they forge there are designed to make you forget who you are fighting for."

Arion stared at the crystal. "You were an Academy Arcan?"

"A Master," Orion said, a faint, dry smile touching his lips. "I opened six Gates before the corruption in the south broke our lines. I thought I could leave it behind. I thought if I came to Greenvale, if I became a hunter, the stone would forget my name. But magic is not a skill you put in a chest, Arion. It is a configuration of your very blood."

He lifted the bow, offering it to Arion.

As Arion’s fingers touched the crystal grip, a shock of clean, high-frequency warmth ran up his arm—not the heavy, gravity-dense hum of the earth, but a soaring, visual light that smelled of stars and cold sky.

"Your mother, Lyria, did not come from the Heartlands," Orion said, his eyes fixed on Arion's. "She was Seraphim. The last of the high bloodline of the Sight Gate. When she chose to stay with me, she gave up her seat in the Convocation, but she could not give up her nature. You carry her blood, Arion. Half-mortal, half-star. That is why you saw the lines. That is why the wolf answered your call."

Orion leaned back against the timber wall, his face pale but resolved.

"The shadow that attacked the stag is only the vanguard. Malachar’s seal in the Fen is breaking. The academies will try to claim you—the Forge for your strength, the Atlanteans for your structure, the Luminary for your blood. But you must not let them turn you into a tool."

He placed his hand over Arion’s.

"You were always meant for greater things than a hunter's path, my boy. Learn what you are. But choose your own frequency."
