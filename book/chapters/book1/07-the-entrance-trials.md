# Chapter Seven: The Entrance Trials

The morning bell of the Academy of Creation & Light did not ring so much as it cleared the air.

When the third bell struck, the bronze vibration rolled down the white granite terraces of the city, sweeping away the river-mist that clung to the canals. Arion stood in the center of the Plaza of Refraction, his shoulder-blades tight beneath his wool coat. The crystalline limbs of *Lumios* were wrapped in oiled canvas and strapped to his back, but the bow felt heavier now, as if the pulsing leylines beneath the flagstones were pulling at its core.

Beside him, Ismael was methodically checking the tension of the drive-chain on his copper spark-wheel. His thumbs were black with grease, his face pale in the clean morning light. 

"The copper is slipping," Ismael said, his voice dropping to a whisper as a group of candidates in embroidered green silk swept past them. "The humidity from the east canal is expanding the leather gaskets. If the friction plates don't bite..."

"It will bite," Mera said softly. She stood on Arion’s left, her fingers laced tightly around the strap of her herbal satchel. Her eyes were fixed on the raised dais in the center of the plaza, where three proctors in high-collared gray robes were preparing the geomorphic resonance plates. "Just keep the crank steady, Ismael. Don't force the gears."

At Arion's heel, Mamoru let out a soft, low breath. The wolf was the size of a mountain hound now, his starlight-blue coat drawing nervous glances from the candidates crowding the courtyard. He did not growl, but his golden eyes remained locked on the gray-robed warden standing on the stone dais.

*Many threads here,* Mamoru's thought brushed Arion’s mind, cool and heavy like fresh snow. *The stone is full of old paths. They are waiting for you to walk them.*

"Candidates!" the central proctor called. His voice was flat, dry, and amplified by a large copper horn wreathed in rotating silver bands. "Form the circles. The first trial is Alignment. Stand upon the plates when your province is called."

The crowd shifted, the sound of leather boots on granite like the rustle of dry leaves.

"Heartlands," the warden called, checking a slate plate. "Greenvale district."

Ismael stiffened. He looked at Arion, then at the spark-wheel in his hands. "That's us."

They walked toward the central ring. The plaza floor was laid out in concentric bands of white granite, each inlaid with thick bands of red copper that hummed with a low, bone-vibrating frequency. In the center of the ring stood five circular bronze plates, each placed directly beneath a floating geometric solid carved from hollow *Yumiko Prism*. The shapes—a tetrahedron, an octahedron, a hexahedron, an icosahedron, and a dodecahedron—slowly rotated in the air, casting sharp, rainbow-colored light across the granite.

"Candidate Ismael of Greenvale," the proctor said, his eyes scanning Ismael's simple homespun vest and the copper machine in his hands. "Step onto the first plate."

Ismael stepped onto the bronze circle beneath the tetrahedron. The plate remained dark.

"Begin the resonance," the proctor said.

Ismael did not close his eyes. He set his feet, gripped the wooden handle of his spark-wheel, and began to crank. The gears clicked—a dry, rhythmic sound that was the only noise in the quiet forest. The steel teeth ground against the copper friction plate, throwing off a quick shower of orange sparks. 

A Pyros proctor at the edge of the dais leaned forward, his ash-grey brow furrowed. "What is that device? This is a trial of Anima, not engineering."

"My Anima," Ismael said, his voice cracking slightly as he cranked faster, "is in the wheel."

The friction plates took hold. The copper wands wreathed around the device began to glow with a sharp, electrical heat. Beneath his feet, the bronze plate hummed. The floating tetrahedron above him shivered, its prism facets catching the sparks, until it locked into place with a heavy, metallic click, glowing a dull, orange-red.

"Alignment confirmed," the proctor said, his tone dry. "Fire-resonance. Minimal capacity. Next candidate."

Ismael stepped off the plate, his hands trembling as he tucked the hot wheel under his arm. The skin of his knuckles was red and blistered from the heat of the casing, but his jaw was set.

"Mera of Greenvale," the proctor called.

Mera took a slow breath and stepped onto the second plate beneath the icosahedron. She closed her eyes.

Arion watched her face. He knew she was trying to hold back, trying to keep her water-light small and flat to avoid the Atlantean proctors' eyes. But as she placed her hands on her satchel, the copper leylines beneath her boots flared. The icosahedron above her did not merely rotate; it dropped three inches, its blue-veined facets spinning so fast they became a blur. A column of pale, brilliant blue light erupted from the plate, rising ten feet into the air. The water in the city canals a hundred yards away rippled in sympathy, a low, rushing sound filling the plaza.

The Atlantean proctor, a tall woman with silver hair and webbed fingers, stood up from her seat, her eyes fixed on Mera's face.

"Alignment confirmed," the central proctor read, his voice losing its flatness for a fraction of a second. "Water-resonance. Royal-grade signature. Mark the plate."

Mera stepped down quickly, her face pale as she returned to Arion's side. "I couldn't stop it," she whispered. "The stone... it pulled it out of me."

"It's fine," Arion said, though he saw the Atlantean proctor marking a silver slate with a sharp, running script.

"Arion of Greenvale," the proctor called.

Arion walked to the center plate beneath the dodecahedron. The moment his boots touched the bronze, his stomach tightened. The low hum of the copper leylines entered his legs, but it did not feel like the warm, steady bedrock of the cave. It felt sharp, invasive, and cold.

His breath caught in his throat.

*The wall,* his mind whispered. He was back in the ruins of Greenvale, the sky red with fire, his hands pressed into the stone as he pulled the basalt block to cover his father's body. He could smell the scorched iron, the wet ash, the grey steam of the Fen. His chest felt as if it were wrapped in iron bands.

"Begin the resonance," the proctor said.

Arion stood still. He reached for his Anima, but the moment he tried to channel, his heart hammered against his ribs. The dodecahedron above him remained dark, spinning listlessly. The copper leylines beneath the plate began to hiss, the gold light turning to a jagged, flickering grey.

"Failure to align," the proctor said, his stylus hovering. "Greenvale candidate displays dissonance."

The candidates in the yard began to murmur.

Mamoru stepped forward. The wolf did not look at the proctors; he simply walked onto the bronze plate and pressed his shoulder against Arion’s knee. His starlight fur flared, a wave of cool, clean energy rising through Arion's boots, washing away the grey panic.

The dodecahedron stopped its spin. It locked down with a crack that sounded like ice breaking over a lake, its facets glowing with a deep, silver-white light that made the proctors shield their eyes.

"Alignment confirmed," the proctor said, his voice tight. "Void-resonance. Beast-bound. Proceed to the Capacity Trial."

---

The second dais held the Wellspring Crucible—a sphere of Sol Quartz the size of a man’s head, wrapped in thick copper bands and mounted on a granite pedestal. A clear glass tube rose from the top of the sphere, filled with a sluggish, blue liquid.

"This is the measure of your reservoir," the warden said. "Place your hands upon the quartz. The refraction will show your volume."

Ismael stepped up first. His spark-wheel was hot, the copper smelling of burnt oil. He looked at the quartz sphere, then at his hands.

"I have no reservoir," he said quietly.

"Then you cannot pass," the warden said.

Ismael gritted his teeth. He stepped forward, but instead of placing his bare hands on the quartz, he pressed the copper discharge wand of his spark-wheel against the base of the pedestal. He began to crank the handle with a frantic, desperate speed.

The gears shrieked. The steel teeth ground together, and a brilliant, blue-white spark jumped from the wand into the Sol Quartz. The blue liquid in the tube rose—five grads, ten grads, twenty grads.

"Wait," the warden said, stepping forward. "That is not Anima flow, that is—"

The Sol Quartz sphere began to vibrate. The copper bands wreathed around it turned orange, then white-hot.

"Ismael, stop!" Arion called.

A sharp, metallic pop echoed through the plaza. The drive-chain on Ismael's wheel snapped, the steel links whipping across his knuckles and drawing blood. The copper capacitors on the device erupted in a cloud of black, greasy smoke, and the gears inside ground to a halt with a sickening crunch of shattered teeth.

The liquid in the tube fell back to zero.

Ismael stood frozen, looking at his ruined machine. The wooden handle was cracked, the copper coils blackened and dead. He did not speak. He did not look at Arion.

"Device destroyed," the proctor said, writing on the slate. "Zero capacity. Candidate is—"

"Mark him as pending," the Atlantean proctor said, her eyes still on Mera. "The water candidate will test next."

Mera stepped to the pedestal. She placed her hands on the quartz. The Sol Quartz did not heat; it turned cold, its surface frosting over with a thin layer of white rime. The blue liquid rose slowly, steadily, stopping exactly at forty grads—the standard limit for a first-tier academic. She pulled her hands back quickly, her breath misting.

"Capacity confirmed," the proctor said. "Forty grads. Atlantean Academy candidate."

"Arion of Greenvale," the warden called.

Arion walked to the pedestal. The smell of Ismael's burnt copper still hung in the air. He looked at the quartz sphere, then looked at his hands. He knew if he touched it bare-handed, the block would take him. The panic would rise, the grey Fen-static would leak from his fingers, and they would see what he had done in the woods.

*The bow,* Mamoru's thought came, steady and sharp. *Use the string.*

Arion reached behind his back. He did not unwrap *Lumios*, but he placed his hand over the oiled canvas, his fingers finding the crystalline grip of the bow.

He closed his eyes.

He did not touch the quartz. He reached out with his Pattern Sight, finding the silver threads of the bow’s core and connecting them to the copper leylines running beneath the granite pedestal.

The Sol Quartz sphere did not merely hum; it began to sing. The blue liquid in the tube shot upward, passing forty, sixty, eighty grads. The quartz turned a brilliant, blinding white, the light leaking through the seams in the copper bands.

"Enough!" the proctor shouted, stepping back. "Break the connection!"

Arion couldn't. The leylines were pulling at the bow, the current running through his arms like fire. The quartz sphere was cracking, its surface webbed with fine, dark lines that began to leak a cold, black steam. The Fen-static was coming, drawn by the force of the flow.

"Arion!" Mera called.

Arion opened his eyes. He saw the black static spreading across the quartz.

He reached back, tore the canvas cover from *Lumios*, and drew the bowstring. A shaft of silver starlight materialized on the grip, humming with a clean, high frequency that set the teeth of every candidate in the yard on edge.

He did not fire at the target. He fired at the pedestal.

The starlight arrow struck the Sol Quartz sphere. The impact was not a bang, but a single, resonant note that sounded like a silver bell striking stone. The quartz sphere shattered into ten thousand clean, white crystals that rained down upon the granite dais.

The black static vanished, neutralized by the starlight.

The proctors stood frozen, their gray robes wreathed in the white mist of the shattered prism. The warden-wardens drew their copper rods, their faces dark with anger.

"Sabotage!" the central proctor shouted. "Guards, detain him!"

Two sentinels in basalt plate stepped forward, their heavy halberds raised.

"Stand down," a voice said.

The voice was not loud, but it carried a weight that made the sentinels freeze. A man in robes of white and gold walked down the steps of the Academy of Creation & Light. His hair was black, his eyes two calm circles of silver that looked at Arion’s bow, then at the starlight wolf, and finally at Arion’s face.

"Master Amiri," the central proctor said, bowing low. "The candidate has destroyed the testing crucible."

"The candidate," Amiri said, his voice quiet, "has a capacity that exceeds the tool. The tool is what broke, proctor, not the boy."

He walked to the edge of the dais, his silver eyes fixed on *Lumios*. "The bow of the Sight Gate. I have not seen its match since the fall of the Southern Marches." He looked at Arion. "You are admitted, Arion of Greenvale. Your House will be determined at the evening convocation."

He turned back toward the steps, his robes rustling against the stone.

The proctors did not speak. The warden marked the slate with a deep, silver stroke.

Arion lowered the bow, the crystalline limbs fading back to cold wood. His hands were shaking, his chest aching with the old static. He looked at Ismael, but Ismael was already walking back toward the edge of the courtyard, his ruined spark-wheel clutched to his chest, his head lowered.

Suddenly, a sound came from the center of the dais.

It was not a bell, and it was not a voice. It was a dry, clicking hum—like a telegraph key moving at impossible speed, or the sound of code compiling in a silent room.

Arion looked up.

Above the shattered pedestal, the air was tearing. A small, circular rift of black and gold was forming in the air, its edges wreathed in tiny, digital squares of light that flickered like dying stars.

The rift hummed, and through the opening, a faint, metallic voice spoke in a tongue no one in the plaza had ever heard:

`System anomaly detected. Attempting to compile...`

Arion stood frozen, his Pattern Sight flaring as he saw the gold threads of the city's leylines bend toward the rift, locking phase with a foreign, electric frequency that felt like a heartbeat from another world.

The rift widened, and the autumn sky above Convergence City began to turn the dark, silent color of the Void.
