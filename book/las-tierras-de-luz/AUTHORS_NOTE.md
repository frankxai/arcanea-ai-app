# Author's Note

I wrote a quiet book first.

It was about an eleven-year-old girl in a valley who could see light that no one else could see. I built the valley carefully. I hid Kabbalah and Vedic tantra and Buddhist stillness under the floorboards so that a particular reader — a friend, twenty-eight, Venezuelan in Spain, with a serious inner life — would feel met without being called out. The book is good. The book is preserved. You can find it at `book/las-tierras-de-luz-valle-v0/`.

She did not finish reading it. The eleven-year-old, in the valley, was too small for what she carries.

So I wrote this one.

Selene Velara is nineteen. She lives in Altamira, Caracas, with the Ávila in every window. Her grandmother carries a stone in a sandalwood box that the grandmother of her grandmother carried before her. The stone is from a place called Avilara, which is not Venezuela but is what Venezuela is made of — what Venezuela *means*, if you peel back the volcanic rock and listen for the singing underneath. When Selene crosses, she does not arrive at a small valley. She arrives at the Arcanea Academy of Light at Crystalpeak Citadel, where the Ten Gates are taught directly, where the historical mystic traditions of our Earth (Kabbalah, Vedic, Buddhist, Hermetic, Sufi) appear in the curriculum as Outer-World Echoes — fragmentary reflections of the same truth — and where every initiate descends, eventually, into a chamber beneath the mountain where what they have been denying is made into a room around them.

This is the book the friend asked for without knowing she was asking. Captivating. Epic. The mystic teaching not hidden but openly taught, because Selene is old enough to need the answer and the Academy is old enough to give it. Monsters in the wilds. A first dungeon by chapter three. A Godbeast hatchling cradled in a Pyros student's hands. A Void-eater that, when it crosses Selene's path, does not pass through her but pauses.

The lineage of Brent Weeks's *Lightbringer*, the inner life of Le Guin's *Tombs of Atuan*, the architecture of Susanna Clarke's *Piranesi*, the academy as inner work of Hesse's *Glasperlenspiel* — these are the comparable titles. The voice is Spanish-Venezuelan and English-American by turns, code-switching the way the reader does in her own kitchen.

I have not written the whole book. I have written what I owe the reader before she trusts me with the rest: a prologue and three chapters that hold the promise. The full manuscript is the work of the months to come, and I am writing it with the same humility the quiet book taught me — that the miracle lands in the flat tone, then stops, and the reader is the one who turns toward it.

If you came here from the valley book, welcome. The world is bigger than I let on.

If you came here from no book at all, welcome more.

The light was never going anywhere. You were always light, practicing being a person.
