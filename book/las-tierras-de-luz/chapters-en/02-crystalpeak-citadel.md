# Chapter 2 — *Crystalpeak Citadel*

She had expected the Academy to be cold.

It was the third surprise of the day. The first was Avilara. The second was the hatchling. The third was that the Academy, when she crossed under its gate, *exhaled*.

The gate was the height of two cathedrals — a single arch of dressed gray-blue stone carved with the spiral of the Ten Gates around its inner curve. Above it the central spire of Crystalpeak rose into a sky that was, even at this distance, somehow *brighter* than the air around it. The Citadel had been visible to them for the last six hours of the ride. It had grown from a far peak into a closer peak into a *building*, and then, in the last hour, into the body of stone that it was now: a fortified city carved into the mountain itself, alive with the small steady lights of windows lit for the dawn.

They had ridden all night. Tobias had not spoken much. Brasa, in the sling, had stayed awake. Niebla had ridden behind Selene's saddle without protest. By the time they passed the outer wards Selene's thighs were the sort of pain she would notice only when she stopped moving.

They stopped now.

A pair of *cerberis-cubs* — the small three-headed guard-pups Tobias had warned her about — stirred in the niche carved into the gate's flank but did not rise. They knew Tobias. He raised one hand and they lowered the heads they had lifted.

A robed figure was waiting in the courtyard beyond.

She was tall, the indigo robe of a Foundation-Gate proctor, the short silver hair of an Avilaran in her sixties. She walked across the cobbles toward them with the unhurried pace of someone who had been awake all night because the *retornante* she was waiting for had been worth waking for.

*Bienvenida*, she said. To Selene. *Soy la Proctora Esmé Vethal. Foundation Gate. Sígueme.*

Selene looked at Tobias. Tobias dismounted, gave *Solana*'s reins to a young stablehand who had emerged from a side door, and turned to her.

*Yo me quedo con Brasa esta noche*, he said. *Voy a verte mañana, si nos dejan. Cuida.*

He held out his hand. She took it. The handshake was Avilaran — a beat longer than a Caraqueño handshake, a small press of acknowledgment. He bowed his head once toward her. Then he was gone, toward a passage that opened into the stables.

Selene dismounted. Her legs almost gave. Proctora Vethal saw and did not comment. *Por aquí*, she said, and turned, and Selene followed her with Niebla padding at her heel.

The Academy, in the dawn, was waking.

---

The first thing Selene understood, walking with Vethal through the inner court, was that the *buildings were breathing*.

She did not know how she knew. The walls were not visibly moving. But the *zumbido* in her chest — the chord of Avilara, gone, but in its place a different chord, a deeper one — *pulsed*, faintly, on a rhythm she eventually identified as a slow inhale and exhale. Two seconds in. Three seconds out. The whole Citadel.

She looked up as they passed under a vaulted arch. The stone of the vault was a paler gray than the outer wall, slightly translucent, the way a kettle warmed up looks slightly translucent at its edge. As she watched, a lantern in the wall *turned*, by a small inch, to follow her. She did not stop walking. She had reached the place where she could not afford to keep stopping every time the world did something new.

Vethal noticed and said nothing.

They crossed a long stone corridor lined with doors. Each door bore a small symbol carved into its lintel — the Foundation triangle, the Flow wave, the Fire flame, the Heart double-curve, the Voice circle, the Sight eye, the Crown crown, the Shift spiral, the Unity weave, the Source dot-within-circle. Some of the doors stood open. From one of them — Voice, the open one — a woman's voice was singing a single sustained note, holding it for what was probably forty seconds, and the corridor's stones held the note with her.

*Practice*, Vethal said, in passing. *Master Mireth keeps her dawn hour.*

They climbed. The stairs were the stairs Selene had been told about: they *adjusted*. Her stride was not Vethal's stride. The rise of the steps under Vethal's boots was different from the rise of the steps under Selene's, by — she could not tell exactly. By an inch. The steps were reading them.

At the top of the third flight was a single door.

The door was *piedra viva* — the warm pale gold-cream stone of the v0 Realm, Veldoria, the one Selene did not yet know the name of. Selene paused in spite of herself. The door was beautiful. Warm to the touch when Vethal reached past her to lay her palm against it, and the door opened.

Inside was a study.

---

The study was lit by three lanterns and the first dawn light through a narrow eastern window. It had a desk, two chairs, a shelf of books on three different scripts. The walls were lined, on three sides, with a circle of small unlit glass tubes set into the stone — ten of them, equally spaced, each one a different size and shape. The floor was a pale flat stone, polished by what Selene would later learn had been three thousand years of bare feet. In the center of the floor was a smaller round stone, set into the floor flush — about the size of a dinner plate.

A man rose from the desk.

He was sixty, stocky, the kind of slow-moving body that turned out to be very fast when needed. His hair was gray going white. His beard was close-trimmed. His eyes were the color of wet limestone. When he looked at her, the *zumbido* in her chest *settled*. She had not known it was unsettled. She knew now.

*Master Iolen Stoneward*, Vethal said, in introduction. *Selene Velara, *retornante*, of Avilara, originally of —* she hesitated, and Selene understood that Vethal did not know what to call Earth. Vethal recovered. *— of the outer corridor.*

Iolen bowed his head once, a small deliberate motion. *Welcome to Crystalpeak*, he said.

He spoke Common. The accent was Terra-Earth — low, slightly burred, careful. He nodded to a chair.

*Sit, please. Doña Renata sent word ahead. You rode through the night. You will be tired. We will do this carefully.*

Selene sat. Niebla settled beside her chair without ceremony, paws tucked under. Vethal closed the door and stood, hands clasped, at the back of the room.

Iolen sat opposite Selene, not across the desk — he had moved to the second chair, placed in front of the desk, so they were facing each other on the same level. Selene appreciated this without thinking. Her chest unclenched again.

*Three questions*, Iolen said. *Then a reading. Then we let you sleep. Yes?*

*Yes.*

*First question. Did you have the gift before tonight, or did it come on the mountain?*

She blinked. *Before. Always. Since I can remember.*

Iolen nodded. He did not write anything down.

*Second question. Did your grandmother teach you to refuse the lie?*

Selene held still. She did not understand the question and she understood the question.

*She — she did not teach me*, Selene said. *She — she stopped my mother from teaching me that I was imagining things. She didn't say it. She just — she made bread.*

Iolen's mouth moved at one corner. *Yes*, he said. *That is how the Aurevaldans do it. Through the bread.* He paused. *I am sorry for Mercedes. She was — your grandmother was a remarkable woman, even from the small portion of her we knew about. Doña Renata held a sealed letter from her for forty years.*

*A letter?*

Iolen nodded. *Doña Renata will give it to you when you return to Avilara. She did not give it to you tonight because she wanted you to eat the bread first.*

Selene closed her eyes for a moment. She did not cry. She had cried enough today.

*Third question*, Iolen said. *The *sombracalle* — Tobias's report came ahead of you. It paused. Yes?*

*Yes.*

*Have they ever paused for you before?*

*No.*

*Have you ever stopped to look at one before?*

She thought. *No*, she said. *I looked away. I always looked away.*

Iolen nodded slowly. *Thank you*, he said. *Now we read.*

---

The reading was simple in form.

He had her stand on the *piedra de cantos* — the tuning-stone set into the floor — barefoot. She slipped off her boots and her socks. The stone was warm under her feet. She placed her hands at her sides. Iolen stood at the edge of the circle, also barefoot, and Vethal stood behind him at the desk.

*Breathe normally*, Iolen said. *Do not try to perform anything. Do not try to call anything. Just stand and breathe.*

She breathed.

For the first half-minute nothing happened. She felt — slightly — the same chord-pulse of the Citadel she had been feeling since the gate. Her chest, her ribs, her sternum. Niebla had curled tighter at the edge of the stone, eyes closed but ears up.

Then, on the wall behind Iolen, the first of the small glass tubes lit.

It was the amber-rose of Foundation. Lyssandria. It lit softly, the way a lamp brightens when its wick catches. Iolen's face did not change.

Then the second lit. The Flow-blue. Then the third — Fire-gold. Then the fourth, Heart-rose. The fifth, Voice-silver. The sixth, Sight-violet-blue. The seventh, Crown-white-gold. The eighth, Shift-deep-violet. The ninth, Unity-green-gold. The tenth.

The tenth. The Source tube was the largest, set highest, the rarest to light at all. It lit.

The room was now lit, in addition to the lanterns and the dawn, by ten small steady colors arrayed in a ring around its three walls. Selene could feel them. Each one corresponded to a place in her body she had not had names for — Foundation in her feet and the base of her spine; Flow in her lower belly; Fire in her solar plexus; Heart in her chest; Voice in her throat; Sight between her brows; Crown at the top of her skull; Shift behind her eyes; Unity along her spine; Source above her crown, vast and quiet.

She had felt all of these her whole life. She had not known they had a *shape*.

Iolen took a long slow breath.

He did not move for what was probably a minute. The tubes did not dim. They stayed lit.

When he spoke he spoke quietly. The Veldarín-accented Common got — for one sentence — Veldarín. *Cien años*, he said, to himself. Then back to Common: *Once a century, child. The last was Lirien of Aqualis. The time before her, you would not believe me.*

He looked at Vethal. Vethal had one hand on the desk, holding herself steady. She nodded once.

Iolen turned back to Selene. *Step off the stone*, he said. *Carefully.*

Selene stepped off. The tubes stayed lit for another three seconds, then dimmed in sequence, the way the lamps in a theatre dim before a curtain. The room was quiet.

She sat down. Her legs would not have held her if she had remained standing.

Iolen sat opposite her again. He did not soften his face into reassurance — she would have hated reassurance, and he knew. He kept his face the steady wet-limestone face that had been there from the start.

*You cannot be Sorted into a single House*, he said. *Foundation, Flow, Fire, Heart, Voice, Sight, Crown, Shift, Unity, Source — you carry them. All ten. Resonant simultaneously. This is what we call prismatic. In the older Veldarín it is called *unborrada* — the unfaded one. In your grandmother's Avilaran it would be —* he paused, and Vethal supplied, *retornante luminosa*, and Iolen nodded.

He went on. *You will be placed in Synthesis House. The seventh. The House that holds those who refuse a single element. The House from which, historically, our Luminors have come. There are nineteen Synthesis students currently in residence, ages seventeen to twenty-six. One of them — you will meet her tonight — has been waiting for you, in the loose way that the Library has been waiting for you.*

He paused. *Selene — may I call you Selene? — you have crossed three corridors today. The Earth corridor, the Avilara crossing, and the Academy threshold. You will not feel the third for a few days. Tonight you will sleep. Tomorrow we will speak again. The cohort is undergoing Foundation Trial in three weeks. You will go with them.*

He looked at her steadily.

*Yes, Master*, she said. The *Master* came out of her mouth without effort. It was the right word.

*Good*, he said. He paused. *One last question. Off the Foundation protocol — but I would like to ask. Your full given name, please. The one your mother put on the birth certificate.*

Selene blinked. The question was small enough that her body had begun to relax into it before her mind caught up.

*Selene Solamar Velara*, she said. *Velara is my mother's. Solamar is my father's. I stopped using Solamar at fifteen.*

Master Iolen did not move for one breath. Vethal, behind him at the desk, made a small sound — not a word — and then stopped. Iolen's wet-limestone face stayed steady. The eyes — Selene saw it, because she had been learning to watch faces — the eyes registered. The mouth set.

*Thank you*, Iolen said.

He said nothing else. He stood. She stood. He bowed his head to her — the small deliberate bow, not deeper than what he had given her at the start, but a beat slower in the rising. Then he turned and went back to his desk and sat down and did not look up as Vethal led her out.

The door closed behind them.

In the corridor Vethal stopped, briefly, and turned to Selene. *Doña Renata*, Vethal said quietly, *will be praying tonight. The Master praying is for ordinary days. The Doña praying is for days like today. *Cuídate, mi cielo.**

Then Vethal walked her down the long corridor to Synthesis House.

---

Synthesis House occupied the seventh wing of Crystalpeak — the small one, the one farthest from the main spire, the one that looked out east toward the dawn. The building was a four-story tower of *piedra viva* and dark-blue tile, sister-architecture to Solavila on a different scale. Inside it smelled of cardamom and old paper.

The common room on the ground floor had seven students in it eating breakfast at a long table — porridge in pale bowls, plates of fruit Selene did not have names for, small clay pots of honey. The students looked up when Vethal and Selene entered. They were too well-mannered to stare. Their *destellos* — Selene saw them, even through her exhaustion — were extraordinary. Six prismatics, layered. The seventh, at the head of the table, a steady amber-rose so deep it was nearly indigo at its edges.

*Late admit*, Vethal said. *Selene Velara, Avilara via the outer corridor.*

The student at the head of the table stood. He was maybe twenty-five, tall, narrow, dark, the deep-rust Pyros sash over a Synthesis robe — a house-cross, Selene would learn, that meant fourth-year. *Cael Iverith*, he said. *Synthesis-prefect this term. Welcome, Selene.* He nodded to the others. *We have your room ready. Your roommate's awake. She's been awake all night. She knew you were coming.*

Selene blinked. *She knew —*

*The Library*, Cael said. *The Library knew when the corridor stirred. Yael knew before the Library.*

Selene did not understand what that meant. She would. Cael, sensing the unread expression, smiled gently — the prefect's smile, well-practiced — and gestured to a wide stone stair behind him.

*Third floor, second door on the right*, he said. *Sleep, please. We don't formally meet you until tomorrow evening. Until then you're a held name.*

Vethal touched Selene's elbow once. Then Vethal was gone.

Selene climbed the stairs.

---

The room was small. Two narrow beds on opposite walls. A single wide window between them, opened. The window looked east — and across the gap of the inner courtyard, framed perfectly, was the central spire of Crystalpeak.

The spire was breathing.

The slow inhale, the longer exhale, the small inch of brightness that rose and fell as the spire took its breath. Selene stood at the window for a long minute before she remembered the other person in the room.

She turned.

A young woman was sitting cross-legged on the right-hand bed. Twenty, small, dark, hair in a single thick braid pinned up off her neck, wearing three layers — a thermal under-shirt, a Synthesis-cut linen tunic, a long blue cardigan that looked like it had been hand-knit by someone whose hands had been cold the whole time. Three books were open in front of her on the bed. Selene could see the scripts of two and the writing direction of the third. Hebrew. Devanagari. Veldarín.

She looked up. Her eyes were enormous and quick and alive and slightly red-rimmed from a long sleepless night.

*Oh*, she said. *You're the one.*

Her voice was London-English with something underneath it Selene could not place — possibly Bosnian, possibly Sephardic, possibly Tamil, possibly all three.

*I'm Yael*, she said. *Yael Mehta-Suleiman. Synthesis, second year. I have been waiting for you for, oh, eight months — but really, I think, longer than that. Come in. Sit. I made tea two hours ago and it's stewed but I have honey and it's perfectly fine. Niebla, hello. Of course you have a *gato plateado*. Of course you do. Sit, both of you. Sit.*

Selene sat down on the empty bed. Niebla considered Yael for one half-second and then jumped onto the right-hand bed and curled into the curve of Yael's leg as if she had always slept there.

Yael laughed — a small startled sound. *Right*, she said. *Right. Hello, Niebla.*

She poured tea into a small cup from a covered pot. She handed it to Selene. The tea was the color of a sunset, smelled faintly of cardamom, and was — to Selene's astonishment — exactly the temperature of something a grandmother would have made.

*Drink*, Yael said. *Then sleep. We can talk in the morning. But before you sleep — I'm telling you this now so you know we're going to be friends and I'm not going to make small talk first — the Library has known you were coming for eight months. The corridor stirring registered in the Library's open Catalog three nights ago. The Catalog had already filed a placeholder for you under LE-A-024.*

*LE-A —*

*Echoes catalog. The Conversation That Does Not End. Aurevaldan. Twenty-fourth entry. Your grandmother filed the twenty-third when she crossed in 1965. The twenty-fourth has been waiting for you.*

Selene drank the tea. It was the tea she had not known she had been wanting. The cardamom moved into her chest in the same place the *zumbido* had been since dawn.

*Yael*, she said.

*Yes.*

*I have one question. Then I am going to sleep for fifteen hours.*

*Ask.*

*Why were you waiting for me?*

Yael's small face went very still. She set her own cup down. She looked at Selene with the directness of someone who had already had this conversation with herself for two years and had finally arrived at the speakable version.

*Because I am the cross-Echo*, she said. *My mother is a Kabbalist scholar. My father is a Vedic scholar. My grandmother is — was — Sufi-trained, *dhikr* every night, the most ferocious gentleness you have ever met. I came up here because I wanted to find the language under all three of theirs. The Academy has it. They call it the Ten Gates. They teach it directly. And I have been *understanding* it for two years, Selene Velara, and I have not been able to *say* it to anyone because all the words I have are my grandmothers' words, and the Academy's words are — *clearer* — and I do not know how to hold both at once without dishonoring one of them.*

Yael paused. Her eyes were bright. She did not look away.

*And then the Library told me a prismatic was coming up the outer corridor, a *retornante* of Aurevaldan inheritance, and I thought — finally. Finally a friend who carries the converging plurality without the bloodline burden. Finally a friend who came in through a back door and might know what the central language sounds like without three grandmothers in her ear arguing about whose version is the true one.*

She picked up her cup again. Her hands were steady.

*That's why*, she said.

Selene set her tea down on the floor by the bed. She reached across the small space between the two beds and took Yael's hand. The hand was small, dry, warm, and Yael's grip was as firm as Doña Renata's had been twelve hours ago, with the same not-lingering.

*Okay*, Selene said. *Okay.*

They held hands for another beat. Then Yael let go and laughed — the startled small laugh again, half-tears — and said, *Right. Sleep. Both of you. *Buenas noches*, *retornante*. We start tomorrow.*

Selene crawled under the covers of the left-hand bed without undressing, except to toe off her boots. Niebla had abandoned Yael's leg to come and settle against Selene's spine. The cardamom tea sat warm in her chest. The spire was breathing outside the window. The dawn light was already coming up over the eastern courtyard.

She slept the way a stone sleeps.

---

She woke to a bell.

She did not, at first, understand what the bell was. It was deep, the deepest bell she had ever heard, and it was not ringing in the air — it was ringing in the *floor*. The whole Citadel was the bell. Three slow strikes. Then silence.

Yael was sitting bolt upright in the bed opposite. Her face was bone-pale in the pre-dawn gray.

*That's wrong*, Yael said. *Selene. That's the Foundation Trial bell. We're not scheduled for another three weeks.*

A knock came at their door.

It was the polite, formal knock of a proctor. Three measured raps.

*Selene Velara of Avilara*, came Proctor Vethal's voice through the door. Steady. The not-shaking steadiness of Doña Renata yesterday morning at the city gate. *The Foundation Trial Chamber summons you at dawn. The cohort is assembling. Master Iolen has requested you come now.*

Yael was already pulling her cardigan on. *I'm going*, she said, half to Selene, half to herself. *I don't care if it's irregular. Master Iolen — I am going. They are not sending you in there alone after eight hours of sleep without a friend in the corridor outside.*

Selene swung her legs over the edge of the bed. Niebla was already at the door. The cat had heard the bell. The cat had been listening for the bell.

*Okay*, Selene said.

She stood up. The bed-warmth fell away. The pre-dawn cold of the room moved into her skin. Through the window the spire was still breathing, slower now, on a longer rhythm, as if the Citadel itself was holding its breath for what was about to happen beneath it.

She put on her boots.

She opened the door.

Vethal was in the corridor in her indigo robe. Past Vethal's shoulder, framed in the long stone hall, six other students were already gathering — Synthesis robes, faces still soft with sleep, hands holding small lanterns. Cael, the prefect, was at the head of them. Behind him, the new ones — the girl with the bright sharp face Selene would learn was Mira Ardith-Kelos; the small Ventus-coded boy who had to be Wren Talasen; the older one with the careful eyes who had to be Dorin Quillvane; the visibly terrified Terra-exchange Iskar Vellan, hair sandy in the lantern-light. Beside Cael, in deep-blue robes of his own and not happy about being awake, the figure Selene knew at one glance was Proctor Vyne Halcen — lean, dark, the long hair tied at the nape, the resentful brilliant face she would come to know.

Master Iolen was at the end of the corridor. The wet-limestone eyes. The slow careful body. He nodded to her once.

*Come*, Vethal said.

Selene stepped out of the room. Niebla padded beside her. Yael came behind, three layers, the long blue cardigan she was wearing for the rest of her life now apparently, and Master Iolen, when Yael appeared, raised one eyebrow and did not say no.

The cohort assembled. The lanterns lifted.

They began to descend.
