# The memory pool's closed door

The borrowed taste of orange peel should have left Mera's mouth when the return bell sounded.

It remained at the back of her tongue: bitter white pith, citrus oil, the faint iron note from a thumbnail cut too close to the quick. Across the practice current, Iven opened both hands. Water streamed from his palms into the black channel.

Mera kept his memory for one breath longer.

The current moved around her waist, cold enough to make the healing cable burn in her palm feel newly drawn. Six other students stood at measured intervals between the inlet and return basin. Each carried one harmless sensory fragment donated by the person opposite. Nothing broad enough to become a scene. Nothing intimate enough to require interpretation.

Orange bitterness. Wool against a shaved wrist. The pressure of a wooden spoon held between two fingers. A bell tone heard through a closed door. Heat from a cup with one cracked side.

Every fragment had an owner, a stated boundary, and an agreed return cue.

The bell sounded again.

The bitterness stopped along a hard edge at the back of Mera's tongue. The thumb entered the rind and oil broke across skin, while pressure gathered on the far side of the boundary without crossing it. Someone had peeled the fruit in a room with another person. Mera could feel where that person's breathing should have met the memory.

Iven had donated only the peel.

Mera turned the fragment once inside the vessel of her mouth, searching for the clean boundary he had named.

“Return,” Instructor Neris said from the dry edge.

Mera released.

The bitterness left first. The oil followed through the current as a fine warmth. Iven caught the memory against both palms and drew it back into himself. His shoulders loosened.

The larger absence never entered the water. Mera had found no words, no face, no claim she could honestly make. Her delay still remained in the exercise record.

Neris touched a stylus to the slate beside her.

“Again,” she said.

Mera faced the next student. A young man with silver clips at both cuffs set a wooden spoon on the water between them.

“Fragment,” he said, “pressure of the handle between first and second finger. No taste. No hand attached to the spoon. Return on one bell.”

“Received as stated,” Mera said.

The current lifted the pressure from the wood and carried it into her left hand. She held the shape for one bell and returned it without examining the warmth around the absent fingers.

Neris made no mark.

That was better.

The practice channel occupied the highest ring of the Abyssal Athenaeum, where memory water could be exposed to moving air before it entered the archive below. Long shutters stood open to evening. Beyond them the city rooflines descended toward the harbor, slate dark after rain. The channel itself remained black because its stone swallowed reflection. Only donated fragments gave it color: a brief orange sheen, the grey ripple of wool, one round pressure from the cracked cup.

Mera's sealed rain vial stood in its external cradle on a dry plinth beside Neris. Red thread crossed the blue glass. Its amber and black seals faced outward, exactly as Mera had set them in the Lower Registry.

She had agreed to the practice current for two reasons. The first was that provisional entrants needed a completed return sequence before receiving catalogue access. The second was that Neris had refused to hear the first reason stated as a bargain.

“Practice and petition are separate,” Neris had said.

Mera intended to make them adjacent.

The students rotated. Iven came opposite her again.

He was narrow through the shoulders, with dark curls tied at the nape and a faded line of kitchen burns across one forearm. This time he placed no object in the water.

“Fragment?” Mera asked.

“The empty interval after return.”

“That is not sensory source.”

“It is pressure.”

“From what?”

“You keeping my memory after two bells.”

The students on either side became attentive without turning their heads.

Mera adjusted the edge of her wet sleeve. “I returned the donated content without addition.”

“Late.”

“The boundary was unclear.”

“It was orange peel.”

“There was an adjacent discontinuity.”

“There was a room I did not give you.”

The current pressed against Mera's burned palm. She had encountered only the shape around that room. She could have defended the distinction precisely.

Iven lifted both hands from the water. “Did you return the part I gave?”

“Yes.”

“Then the rest was not unfinished. It was absent from your permission.”

Neris still held the stylus but did not rescue either of them with a ruling.

Mera looked at the water between her and Iven. The orange sheen was gone. Nothing remained to prove she had delayed except the mark on Neris's slate and Iven's decision to speak in front of the group.

“I held it after the second bell,” Mera said. “The delay was mine.”

Iven lowered his hands into the current again.

“Fragment,” he said. “Pressure of an empty palm waiting for return. One bell.”

This time the memory entered Mera as a small cold weight against the center of her hand. No room surrounded it. No source waited beyond it. Only the exact shape of another person expecting her to finish.

The bell sounded.

Mera returned the weight.

Iven closed his hand around it. “Received.”

Neris marked the slate.

At the end of the sequence, students stepped from the channel one at a time and held their hands over a dry-stone rail. Water left skin at different speeds. A clean return dried first at the fingertips. Carried residue lingered in the creases. Mera's right hand dried at once. Her left stayed wet along the cable burn, though she had carried nothing there during the last round.

Neris gave each student a plain cloth and their score strip.

Mera's read: *source distinction—high; retention discipline—conditional; release—complete after correction.*

“Public catalogue?” Mera asked.

“Granted.”

“Shape comparison?”

Neris folded the wet practice slate into its drying rack. “A shape-only method cannot authenticate source.”

“It can establish recurrence.”

“It can establish similar geometry. A forged absence can imitate geometry. An accidental loss can resemble design when context is removed. A practitioner can choose coordinates that flatter a conclusion.”

“A full trace comparison exposes every voice captured by the storm.”

“Yes.”

“Then your accepted method destroys the boundary that preserved the cleanest source.”

“It opens that boundary under named access. You may refuse.”

“And receive no result.”

“Receive no authenticated result.”

Neris handed back the external cradle. She did not touch the vial or the red thread.

“Public catalogue access ends at the deep-pool door,” she said. “No source water, condensation transfer, or rubbing sealed bands. Public coordinates may enter an attributed catalogue note. The note remains in the tray; any duplicate or removal requires a citation seal.”

Late meal began before the public catalogue opened. The practice group crossed two bridges into a hall where long tables stood between shelves of duplicate stories. These were not memory vessels. They were written exercises, copied without names and left for marginal annotation.

Steam rose from pots of lentils, salt fish, and dark greens. The tea was strong enough to stain the inside of the white cups. Mera filled hers before taking food.

Iven sat three places away.

She chose the opposite bench instead of the opposite end of the room.

Each diner received a short anonymous story with the meal. Mera's concerned a woman who stole every spoon from a boarding house and returned one each winter without explanation. Three margins had already filled before Mera read the second page.

*Winter interval unearned,* someone wrote.

*Spoon count changes between paragraphs,* wrote another.

Mera added: *Narrator claims no explanation but identifies the returns as deliberate. Source of knowledge missing.*

The student beside her leaned over. “The narrator owns the boarding house.”

“Then the narrator knows the spoons returned, not the woman's intent.”

“You think no action may be called deliberate without deposition?”

“I think a locked drawer and a doorstep support different verbs.”

Across the table, someone read her note and drew a line beneath *different verbs*.

“This is why Tidecrest was marked conditional,” Iven said.

Mera sipped her tea. “You donated an absence and complained when I observed it.”

“I donated orange peel.”

“An ambitious peel.”

Iven used the tip of his spoon to underline *ambitious* in the condensation between them.

The argument spread. One student defended deliberate omission as craft. Another claimed every omission created false authority. A third held up the story and asked why none of them cared that the boarding house apparently owned more spoons than beds.

Mera won the narrator-source point when a later page revealed the woman had described the returns in a letter. She lost the spoon count after someone found a theft she had skipped on page one.

She crossed out her own note.

The student beside her wrote *withdrawn with unusual grace* in the margin.

Mera added *source: hostile witness* and passed the page on.

By the time the catalogue bell sounded, her tea had gone cold and Iven had given her half his bread in exchange for the right to annotate her next correction first.

The deep-pool catalogue occupied a descending corridor beyond the meal hall. Most students chose tables near the entrance. Mera carried the vial to the last permitted desk.

A round black door closed the corridor ahead. Its lower half stood below the waterline. Behind it, the archive pool held protected source vessels in separate currents, each pressure path kept from touching another. The door had no viewing glass. Public catalogue bands circled the stone around it like growth rings, thin strips of silver script listing file name, source class, custody transfers, access history, and restoration state.

Mera set the vial on the dry desk with its seals facing the door.

She did not need source water.

The rain-memory's engineered absence had a shape she could reproduce from pressure: a blunt interruption at the top, irregular along one side, narrow enough to remove a voice without disturbing the surrounding crowd. The sealed appendix to her invitation carried the same edge. Neither gave her content. Both gave her a boundary.

She drew it on an attributed note sheet and wrote her name at the top.

Then she read the catalogue bands.

Each active coordinate cooled its silver enough to gather a bead of condensation. When an entry was lawfully removed, dry neutral wax filled its position so surrounding water could not carry the change into neighboring script. Most redactions behaved like doors: a custody entry stopped, a reason code followed, and later access resumed from a named seal.

Three bands contained gaps.

The first belonged to a diplomatic source and removed an entire transfer period. Its edges were straight.

The second marked a damaged cistern record. Its script thinned gradually before breaking.

The third sat close to the waterline, old enough that silver had darkened around it.

*Hollow Root service record.*
*Source class: living structural field.*
*Restoration state:—*

The band continued after an empty interval with a later inspection date. No transfer seal entered the gap. No reason code bordered it. The missing coordinates formed a blunt upper edge and one irregular side.

Mera put her burned palm on the dry desk.

The wrist mark remained cool. The vial did not move inside its cradle. Nothing in the pool reached for her.

She copied the public coordinates into her attributed notes.

That note belonged in the catalogue tray. A duplicate required a citation seal and a second mark beside her name.

Instead, Mera opened the back cover of her meal story and wrote only the coordinate sequence inside the fold. No file title. No claimed match. Four numbers and a restoration interval missing its source.

She closed the story before the ink dried.

The copy proved nothing by itself. Any scholar could say she had selected the geometry after seeing the band. Neris could say shape did not authenticate source. Iven could ask whether a gap belonged to her merely because she recognized its edge.

Mera placed the official note in the catalogue tray and kept the meal story.

Water moved behind the closed door. Hundreds of private voices remained inside their named currents, unopened. Around the black stone, the silver bands gathered condensation while four missing coordinate positions stayed dry beneath neutral wax.
