# No further measurement

The council had copied Lio's boot into the evidence pane and cropped out the hand holding it.

The cut leather stood upright on a white field. Dust remained inside the heel. A measurement bar beside the sole proved scale. Below it, the incident times from Mera's slate ended with *secondary compression attributed to east approach load*.

Arion touched the corner of the terminal, not the image. “Did Lio approve this use?”

The council recorder answered through the wall speaker. “The course incident entered joint safety review.”

“I asked whether Lio agreed.”

“The image contains no body or identifying mark.”

“His name is three lines above it.”

Akamoto reached past Arion and closed the image. Six council names remained blue across the top of the terminal. Beneath them, the seventh line was still vacant.

The Roost lower assembly chamber had been cleared of benches. White chalk divided the old stone floor into three areas: local operation, evidence, and no entry. Emilia stood in local operation beside an open service panel. Mera had chosen the witness boundary, where she could see both the speaker and Arion's hands. Akamoto occupied no marked area. He moved between them as the room required.

The hammer lay inside a clear evidence shield at the far wall.

No one had closed the ash handle around its old seam. Brass pins crossed the break where Arion's father had repaired it. The open grain held grey dust from intake and nothing else. A numbered wire joined the shield to the stone plinth beneath it.

Below the chamber, three lower training fields remained closed. Emilia's exit witness still occupied the third field under its own clear shield. The surface isolation seals had not been cut. Nemi watched both devices from the stair landing through a pair of sight mirrors.

“State the proposal without showing me Lio again,” Arion said.

The recorder changed the terminal.

An empty comparison floor appeared in plan. No chairs. No Water source. No House observers in the room. The hammer would remain behind its boundary while a passive Foundation baseline opened beneath it. Emilia would place a local indicator across the floor's release. Akamoto would hold physical stop authority. Mera would witness state and time.

Three pale lines left the chamber diagram and entered a box marked *joint continuity*.

“Those lines,” Emilia said.

“Remote incident safety,” the recorder replied.

“Retention?”

“Required until the buried-field source is identified.”

“Control?”

“The joint service does not accept local control.”

Emilia put her injured arm into the open service panel. The yellow patch dragged against its edge. She found the first conductor and drew it out until its blue sheath lay visible across the floor.

“Local Foundation state,” she said.

The second was amber. “Local release.”

The third, green. “Local indicator.”

Behind them, three unmarked conductors disappeared into the party wall.

Emilia pulled each one into view without disconnecting it. Violet. White. Black.

“Remote mirror state, remote retention, remote source recovery,” she said. “No maker marks. No room-side stop. Same separation as intake.”

“The conductors are declared in the proposal,” the recorder said.

“After I opened the wall.”

“The joint service cannot rely on a single site's reporting during a structural anomaly.”

“Then who stops it?” Arion asked.

“A sitting council quorum.”

“Name the person.”

The six blue names remained blue. None moved to the answer line.

Mera set two blank paper sheets on the witness ledge. The folded meal story stayed inside her document sleeve; no copied coordinate entered the chamber record.

“Conditions,” she said. “Remote retention ends on verbal refusal or local release. Both local and remote state enter the same observer record. The participant receives an immediate copy. Refusal triggers no automatic adverse classification.”

“The final condition is outside witness authority,” the recorder said.

“It is inside mine if you want my name on the record.”

“A refusal may itself be relevant to risk classification.”

“Then you have made agreement the only answer that does not count as danger.”

Arion looked at the hammer.

The evidence shield distorted one brass pin near the center, making it appear crooked. His father had set it straight. Arion knew because he had held the handle under the drill and watched the bit enter on the old grain line.

“Why now?” he asked.

The terminal displayed the buried-field diagram from the exchange evening. The third field's two mounting anchors remained connected through a path no surface isolation had closed. Beside it appeared the rescue-course incident: hook failure, sight loss, false readback, second compression.

“Two uncontrolled structural conditions inside one Roost perimeter,” the recorder said. “One previously observed cross-domain subject. One repaired tool that carried the same pattern during intake. Continued non-testing distributes unknown risk to every student present.”

The buried path beneath the third field remained continuous under closed surface isolations. Lio's leg hung in the infirmary because a route state had not reached the right people. Beneath Arion's boots, the retaining wall thickened behind the hammer, one floor slab sat lower at its north corner, and a long filled joint crossed from the no-entry zone toward the stair. He needed no Foundation attention to see those facts.

“Lio did not authorize measurement,” Arion said.

“The council has not attributed consent to him.”

“Nor do we attribute the course failure to you,” the recorder continued. “The incident establishes the human cost of structural state failing to reach an active team.”

“You attributed need.”

“His injury is evidence of need.”

Akamoto stood beside the no-entry line. “The injury is evidence that my route procedure failed. It is also evidence that our current tools do not share enough state. Both remain true.”

Arion waited for him to say the test should not happen.

“The buried path is beneath my students,” he said. “I support your refusal. I cannot promise your refusal leaves them safe.”

Emilia arranged the six conductors across the floor so none crossed another. “I can build a local indicator that stops reporting when you say no. I cannot stop their mirror from preserving the last active state.”

Mera drew a box for the retention limit on both blank sheets. The line for enforcing authority remained empty. “The limit can be visible. It will not be binding until the council puts a name there.”

The recorder said, “The council is willing to consider automatic adverse-classification review rather than automatic classification.”

“Who can stop the mirror?” Arion asked.

“The sitting council.”

“Who receives the preserved state?”

“The joint service under council custody.”

“If I say stop?”

“Local practice ends immediately. Remote retention enters review.”

Emilia's fingers closed around the white conductor. “Then the remote instrument remains active after his stop.”

“Retention is not measurement.”

“It held the comparison channel open at intake.”

“Direction of cause remains unresolved.”

“Interaction does not.”

Mera took up her pencil. “I can draft a condition requiring physical severance on refusal.”

“Severance during an unknown field state may increase structural risk,” the recorder said.

“Then I can draft an independent stop quorum.”

“Not before today's session ends.”

“Then the protocol is not ready.”

“The field is already active.”

Mera slid the top sheet toward him. It held no terms yet. Only his name, the date, and the named participants in the room.

“You may wait,” she said. “That is my preference. It is not an instruction.”

Emilia released the conductor. “If you wait, I will map the mirror to its physical destination.”

“How long?”

“Longer than this council will call reasonable.”

Akamoto looked toward the old joint in the floor. “No one here can give you the consequence first.”

The council recorder opened a new line. *Participant position pending.*

Arion picked up Mera's pencil.

The wood had been sharpened with a knife, leaving one flat side. He pressed its point to the top sheet.

“No further measurement,” he said.

He wrote the same words.

The pencil passed through the first sheet and scored the second beneath it.

Mera signed as participant witness. Emilia signed local operator not engaged. Akamoto signed refusal received under local authority.

The recorder paused.

*Participant refusal entered,* appeared on the terminal.

In the open service panel, the green local-indicator conductor remained dark. Violet mirror state remained cold. The white retention line warmed once as the terminal sent the refusal entry into joint continuity, carrying status and time without a comparison value.

“Record-state transmission only,” Emilia said. “No local floor. No measurement channel.”

The black source-recovery conductor tightened against its ceramic clip.

A return pulse entered from the party wall.

Dust lifted from the filled joint in the old floor.

No stone moved at first. The dust rose in a line from the no-entry zone toward the stair, grain by grain, as if the chamber had turned sideways.

Arion put the pencil down and showed both palms.

“No practice,” he said.

Emilia dropped to one knee beside the exposed conductors. Blue, amber, and green remained cold. Violet, white, and black carried only the terminal's ordinary service warmth.

“Local floor inactive,” she said. “No comparison source. No indicator state.”

Mera wrote the time beside the refusal. The pencil point tore the damp paper where her palm had touched it.

“Response begins after refusal entry,” she said. “Sequence only.”

The floor joint opened.

Its filler did not crack upward. It withdrew along the old seam, exposing black stone beneath the pale surface slab. The north corner settled the width of a fingernail. Cool pressure left the gap and moved toward the no-entry line.

Arion kept Foundation closed.

The pressure passed under his boots without entering him. It followed the chalk boundary, found the doorway, and crossed into the stair landing.

Below, Nemi struck the field alarm once.

“Third witness changed green to amber under seal,” she called through the sight tube. “No contact. Surface isolations unchanged.”

“Lower stair clear,” Akamoto ordered. “No entry to any field.”

The gap widened to one finger. Beneath it, an angled plane of fitted stone descended away from the mountain. No lamp reached the bottom. The withdrawing filler and cool pressure matched the unfinished load Arion remembered from intake: held pressure, interrupted release, a structure left at a point after use and before return.

Every blue council name flashed white.

“Preserve all state,” the recorder said. “Initiate containment. Participant will establish—”

“Participant refused,” Emilia said.

“The event has changed the risk condition.”

“It has not changed his answer,” Mera said.

The cool pressure reached the hammer's plinth.

Arion saw it enter through the stone, not through him. The numbered shield wire remained whole. The ash handle lay untouched behind glass.

Inside its open seam, grey dust brightened.

The light formed no letter. It stopped bluntly near the top pin, narrowed along one irregular side, vanished across a short interval, and returned below the lowest repair. Mera opened the folded meal story but did not remove it from her sleeve.

“Geometry recurs,” she said. “Source not authenticated.”

The council recorder spoke over her. “Secure the tool under joint custody.”

Mera kept her pencil above the participant copy. “May I draw the mark’s boundary for an attributed public comparison?”

Arion looked at the light inside the seam. “The boundary only. Keep my name on its source. Nothing else from the tool or this room.”

Mera wrote his permission beside the drawing line.

Akamoto crossed to the wall speaker and pulled its physical shutter closed.

The six names remained on the terminal without voices.

He did not enter the evidence line. No one did.

Emilia marked every conductor *inactive for local practice*. Mera copied the refusal time, response time, witness change, and tool mark onto the second sheet Arion's pencil had scored. Akamoto sealed both pages in separate sleeves.

The hairline descent remained open in the floor.

“Withdrawal by the west stair,” Akamoto said. “Nemi verifies the route. Emilia leaves the conductors in visible state. Mera carries the participant copy.”

No one crossed the evidence line. Nemi called the west stair clear from the landing. Emilia clipped a transparent guard over the six exposed conductors without disconnecting them and entered their warm or cold state on the local slate. Mera took the refusal copy in its sealed sleeve and kept the folded meal story separate.

Akamoto lowered a clear shield over the seam and wired both anchors to the custody number. The Roost held the chamber; the council kept its remote record. Reopening required Akamoto, a structural operator, and an independent witness.

Arion withdrew first. Emilia followed, then Mera. Akamoto closed the iron chamber door from the stair and sealed its latch with the same custody number. Through the wired inspection glass, the tool remained visible behind its separate shield.

Behind the inspection glass, brass pins crossed the hammer's broken handle. The wordless shape lit one uneven strip of old ash between them.
