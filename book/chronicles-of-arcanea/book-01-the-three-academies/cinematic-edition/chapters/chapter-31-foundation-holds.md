# Foundation holds

Mera's lamp found the two fingers Arion still could not open.

He tucked the splint closer to his ribs and drew the folded coat under it with his left hand. A grain of stone rolled beneath his palm as weight settled somewhere above.

“Emilia.”

She was kneeling at the bowed carrier, both hands around the cord. Chalk had worked into the fibres and into the crease below her thumb. “What?”

“Bring my left heel under the knee.”

Her eyes went to his right shoulder.

“And pull the coat higher. The splint is touching stone.”

“You could have led with that.”

“I just did.”

She set the cord against her yellow cuff without giving it slack, shifted his boot, and pulled the coat until cloth lay between the binding and the wall. The small adjustment opened the ache through three ribs. It also gave him a left heel he could press into the floor without twisting.

Another grain travelled under his palm. Then three.

Emilia took the cord again. The kitchen-brass shim showed its narrow crescent inside the folded carrier. Neither had moved past her chalk line. The yoke remained jammed. One counterweight hung higher than the other two.

Beyond the low opening, Mera crouched on the far ledge. Her lamp sat behind her, fixed and plain. Her pencil stayed tucked beside two folded pages beneath one side of her coat. A third fold, kept apart beneath the other, made a straight edge against the fabric. The black retained-return bar remained one notch lower. Behind her, the dry housing held its blue-grey interval without a ripple.

The thinned water no longer erased her voice.

“How much is moving?” she asked.

Arion put his left palm flat again. He did not open the Gate. The floor gave him cold at the surface, a slight inward pull near the collar, and a dull knocking that did not keep one rhythm.

“Enough to reach the rooms above.”

“That was not a quantity.”

“It is the one I have.”

Dust loosened from a seam over Emilia's shoulder. She watched it fall across the mineral pane she had placed beyond the joint's travel.

“The return groove?” she asked.

“Taking water and something else.”

“Stone is something else.”

“Pressure. From the collar into the sidewall. The joints up there are already full.”

He looked toward the ceiling. He could not see the occupied rooms through it. He could see new grit under an ordinary mortar seam and a hairline grey mark where there had been none.

The shortest correction lay beneath his right shoulder.

Arion began to turn toward it.

The rib binding caught the breath before Emilia spoke.

“Take your hand off the floor.”

“Not until I know where it settles.”

“You said that about the wall.”

“I know.”

His palm came away.

He wanted an infirmary cot. He wanted bread he could tear one-handed without anybody watching the attempt. He wanted one hour in which no one asked him what he could carry. The wants arrived with the sourness in his empty stomach and seemed no more useful than the hidden third lamp.

Mera moved nearer the low gap, stopping before the wet edge. “I have the basin page, the state page, and the other fold. Dry and separate. I will call water, the return bar, the housing, and the far bearing. I will not open anything. If pressure approaches the housing, I stop.”

Emilia shifted her grip so the cord lay across the protected cuff rather than the old burn. “I hold this only to the chalk line. Changed cord travel, brass bow, collar position, wrist, or burn—I stop. The hinge carries no wall.”

Arion pressed his left heel into the floor. “Foundation is outside Anet's clearance. This is an emergency breach. She and the council can review it if we return.”

“When,” Emilia said.

“If is the accurate word.”

“Use it after we are above.”

Mera pulled her pencil free but did not unfold a page. “Your limit?”

“One exhale. Left palm. Dry visible bearing. Any change in the right hand, ribs, or carried line ends it.”

“And after it ends?”

“It is ended.”

He placed his palm on the operator-bay sill.

The cold in the stone acquired direction. Grit that had felt scattered became a set of edges under the skin: the worn sill beneath him, the floor-slot collar pulling sideways, the old mortar rising through the wall. He let one breath out and gave the sill the smallest downward pressure he could hold without shaping it.

The stone resisted.

Then the easiest path opened behind his right wrist.

The carried branch inside his forearm tightened. Its movement crossed the elbow before he could name the grade. His ring finger drew harder into the palm. A point under the third rib went hot.

“Stop.”

Arion lifted his left hand.

Grey vanished from the mortar line. The floor stayed where it was. The pressure in his wrist did not follow his palm out; it settled back to the lodged movement already there.

He counted the next breath by where the binding interrupted it. Same place. The next one ended a little shorter, then returned.

“Report,” Emilia said.

“Path closed through the right forearm. Reached the ribs. Ring finger tightened one grade.”

“Current?”

He waited. The finger eased to its previous curl. “Back to the recorded state.”

Mera had gone still beyond the water. “No transferred load?”

“None I can find.”

“That is also not a quantity.”

“None by breath, pressure, or movement.”

The seam above Emilia dropped a thread of plaster. It broke against the sill.

Warmth entered beside the pain in Arion's ribs.

It did not spread through the room. It drew one straight imagined route from the upper joints to the carried branch in his arm, clean where everything around it was crooked. The warmth came with a name he had not supplied.

*You know which floor will break first.*

*Let me close the other paths. No one above has to carry this.*

Arion kept his left hand in his lap.

“The warm voice is back,” he said. “It named itself Malachar. It says it can close the other paths through me.”

Emilia's hands stayed on the cord. “Change in the room?”

“No authenticated change.”

“Change in you?” Mera asked.

He checked the wrist, ribs, and two closed fingers. “Warmth beside the carried line. No new movement.”

“My housing limit stays,” Mera said.

“My hinge limit stays,” Emilia said.

Arion waited for the certainty to withdraw. It did not. The clean route remained available in the way a bad repair could remain available after a builder marked it wrong.

He set his left hand on the floor without opening Foundation.

“Where can this settle?”

No voice answered.

The next fall of grit landed closer to his thumb. Water hissed through the return groove. Somewhere behind the slab housing, a worn face gave a dull grain shift and stopped.

Arion began there.

He touched the sill for less than a breath. Cold narrowed under his palm. He pressed down and outward, no farther than the old bearing face already travelled. When he withdrew, two grains rolled toward the wall. The sill remained lower by their width.

“Cord unchanged,” Emilia said.

“Water unchanged,” Mera said. “Housing dry.”

He waited through his right hand.

“Body same.”

The next contact went toward the far ledge, following the central slab's side housing. The stone there was wider and resisted less. For half a breath, that made it the best path.

Mera's lamp sharpened across the gap.

“Pressure at the dry housing. Stop.”

Arion withdrew.

The sharpened line fell away from his palm. He left the far route out.

Mera crouched beside the housing without touching it. “Bar unchanged. Slips fixed. No water contact.”

“Can you see the slab bearing?”

“Lower edge only.”

“What is it doing?”

She held her lamp lower. “Settling toward you. Not toward the housing.”

Emilia looked from the cord to Arion. “That was better than your quantity.”

“She has better light.”

“I have the same amount of light,” Mera said.

“Then better habits.”

For one moment, nobody corrected him.

Arion tested the slab's side housing from the operator bay. This time he kept the cold below the channel and away from the far wall. Old stone ground against older stone. The sound stopped after his hand lifted, but the new clearance stayed.

One support had moved without him. The sill had done the same. Neither was enough.

The upward seam shed more plaster.

He leaned his head back against the coat rather than the wall. The repair house came to him as he had drawn it: wide door, level floor, a bench low enough for tired hands. In every sketch he had been working. He had never drawn where he sat on a day he fixed nothing.

“There is an upper joint running outward,” he said.

“Toward what?” Emilia asked.

“Foundation course. After that, I don't know.”

“Can it take this?”

“It can show us when it cannot.”

“That is a terrible recommendation.”

“It is the only honest one.”

He put his palm beside the new grey seam. Foundation attention carried the pressure upward and away, not into a hidden support but through mortar already visible between two courses. He gave it a fraction.

The joint cracked.

Emilia flinched but did not release the cord. Mera's lamp lifted. The line travelled across old mortar, crossed a patch of newer plaster, and stopped two handspans above the collar.

Arion pulled away.

The crack remained. A scatter of grit continued falling for three breaths, then slowed. The floor did not return to its previous angle.

“Rooms above?” Mera asked.

He watched the fresh grit settle and the new crack hold its width. “Still loaded. Less concentrated here.”

“How much?”

“Enough that I do not want to sleep under them.”

“That one was almost a quantity,” Emilia said.

His mouth hurt when he smiled. “Three places remain after release. Sill. Slab housing. Upper joint.”

“The hinge remains where I put it,” Emilia said. “It is not your fourth.”

“I know.”

“Say what it is.”

“Our exit condition.”

She looked at the chalk mark. “Presently.”

Mera lowered her lamp until light lay under the remaining water. “The return bar is down one notch. Housing dry. Far bearing settled toward the operator bay. Water still above the slab track.”

“If it drops?” Arion asked.

“I will call the drop. Not the cause.”

Emilia reset her hands on the cord. “Brass inside the mark. Collar unchanged. Wrist usable. Burn quiet.”

Arion put his left heel down and his palm between the three visible directions.

The first touch of Foundation found the straight warm route waiting.

He left it alone.

The sill took pressure with a low scrape. Grain moved against grain in the slab housing, weight shifting across a worn face. Above, the outward joint opened another pale width through the mortar.

“Water falling,” Mera called. “One finger.”

The return groove deepened its note.

Emilia's chalk trembled on the bowed brass. “Cord at mark. Carrier holding its shape.”

Arion widened the pressure no farther. He held the three directions long enough to feel their unequal resistance, then reduced his hand before any one of them went quiet. The stone under his palm cooled hard enough to ache.

“Two fingers,” Mera said. “Housing dry.”

The upper crack moved beyond the new plaster. A distant metal edge gave once inside the wall—not the source housing, not the hinge, farther outward and above.

The carried branch tightened toward his elbow.

“Right side changed,” Arion said.

He lifted his hand.

Water kept draining after his hand left the floor. Grit kept falling. The sill held its lower line. The slab housing gave another short grind after his palm was gone. Above them, the pale crack lengthened across ordinary mortar and then rested.

Arion kept both hands away from stone.

“Body,” Emilia said.

He followed the rib binding through one inhale. The caught place returned to its earlier height. The ring finger had drawn half a nail's width deeper. He waited until it eased. The carried movement remained lodged through the forearm and three ribs; no farther, no less.

“Back to the documented state,” he said.

“Wait another breath,” Mera said.

He did.

The clean warm route faded to a line he could still find if he chose it.

The water dropped below the lip of the central track, baring the slab in its side housing.

Arion studied it without touching. “Load settled. My body stop is clear for one local act. The slab only.”

“That does not make it cleared by Anet,” Emilia said.

“Or me,” Mera said.

“No.”

He set his left palm beside the housing.

The old track was rough with mineral grit. He found the slab's near edge through pressure and shaped only along the travel the stone had already made. It resisted at the tilted place. He eased off, changed the angle through his heel, and pressed again.

The slab moved out of its housing.

Water sheeted over its first edge and emptied into the return groove. The stone travelled a handspan, stopped, then travelled another. Nothing pulled it from the far side. Emilia held the hinge without feeding the cord. Mera stood clear of the housing and called the remaining distance.

“Half.”

Arion's ribs shortened the next breath.

“One handspan.”

He reduced Foundation as the slab entered its original bearing.

“Seated,” Mera said.

Arion released.

The slab stayed.

He took his palm away and tucked it against his left thigh. One breath reached the binding. A second reached the same place. He watched the two closed fingers until their nail crescents stopped whitening. The carried movement remained exactly where pain made it easiest to find.

“Foundation ended,” he said.

Emilia eased the cord by less than a finger. The carrier did not reset. The brass shim stayed bowed within the chalk. “Near bearing stable. Hinge unloaded.”

Mera tested the far edge with the toe of one boot. She added weight slowly, lamp in one hand, pages secured under her coat. “Far bearing stable.”

Mera crossed. Arion kept his left hand against his thigh; Emilia stayed with the hinge.

On the operator side, she set her lamp down and looked first at Arion's splint, then his face.

“You look terrible,” she said.

“Accurate.”

Emilia took one hand from the cord and pocketed the bent wick key. “I can improve the wording if we live long enough.”

Mera's hand went to the separate fold beneath her coat. It stopped there. She left the page where it was.

“It stayed dry,” she said.

Arion looked at the straight edge showing through the cloth. He did not ask for it. “Keep it that way.”

She took her hand from the fold. The violet mark across her palm showed once in the lamplight before her fingers closed around the handle again.

The floor shifted once under all three of them. Not downward. Sideways, a shallow correction through the gallery joint.

The turning wall relaxed far enough to reveal the level return passage. A dark side pocket remained closed behind it. No third lamp appeared. No bell-board cord came free.

Emilia let the surviving cord rest against the yoke. “That stays.”

“Everything here stays,” Mera said.

Arion looked at the bowed carrier, the pane beyond the joint, and the chalk line that no longer agreed with its mate. “Good.”

He tried to rise and made it as far as one knee. The empty tool loop at his belt struck the slab. He reached for a hammer that was not there, found cloth, and let the hand fall.

Mera waited. Emilia did not touch him.

“Left elbow,” he said.

Mera took it. Emilia braced the folded coat at his back while he came upright without loading the right arm. Nobody called the result graceful.

They moved through the gallery at the pace set by his ribs. Emilia carried the fixed-flame lamp. Mera kept her own light low and the three folds apart. Arion used the wall only with his left shoulder and only where they could see the joints.

The first sixteen steps cost him one rest. He asked for it before the rib binding made the choice. Mera set her lamp on level stone and kept her supporting hand at his left elbow. Emilia stood behind them, watching the passage they had just crossed rather than pretending the noises had stopped.

A loosened chip fell from the turning joint and lay pale-side-up in the lamplight.

“Breath?” Emilia asked.

“Shorter. Returning.”

They waited until it did.

At the next bend, the gallery had opened only to shoulder width. Mera passed first, turned, and lit the floor for Arion without pulling him through. The cuff scraped once. Emilia backed up, turned her wrist inward, and followed.

Behind them, water continued along the return groove.

The first vestibule had changed. A split ran beside the three-authority plate, opening old mortar behind a skin of newer plaster. The inner architectural closure had withdrawn partway along the travel they had seen on descent, leaving single-file clearance beneath the exterior shutter but no climbing height.

Emilia placed her lamp where it could be seen from the shutter above.

“Akamoto,” she called. Her voice broke on the second syllable. She tried again. “All three present at the buried threshold.”

For several breaths, only water sounded from the gallery.

Arion leaned on his left heel and counted the shutter bolts he could see. Four. One had been replaced with a brighter alloy than the others. The repair was competent and ugly. He wanted to meet whoever had made it, provided they agreed not to discuss stone.

Then a lever struck metal on the exterior side.

The shutter lifted by the same stubborn increments it had used to admit them. A wedge of air entered first. It touched the sweat at Arion's temple and was so cold he mistook it for more stone.

“Visible,” Akamoto called from above. He did not reach through. “The authorized return is now in use.”

“We have one injured, no climb,” Emilia called.

“Anet has the sledge.”

The shutter rose. Akamoto worked the manual lever through each stop until Mera could pass beneath it standing. She went first, carrying her lamp, pencil, and three pages, and nothing else from below. Emilia followed with the fixed-flame lamp, the light making her face look more tired rather than less.

Arion waited inside the threshold.

Anchored lines descended with the narrow stair-sledge. Anet's voice came after them, clipped and near.

“Can you transfer with left-side assistance?”

“Yes.”

“Do you want Akamoto at the elbow?”

Arion looked at the height of the first stair, then at his right hand. “Yes.”

Akamoto entered only far enough to give the requested support. He did not ask what Arion had done. Anet did not pronounce him safe. They settled him onto the sledge, secured the right side without moving the splint, and raised him through the old stairwell while the manual shutter remained open.

Mera and Emilia climbed under their own movement beside the rig.

The exterior court had no torches lit. The sky above the Roost roofs was the colour of cold ash. Arion had thought they were still inside the night.

The sledge reached level stone.

Anet knelt at once, fingers near but not on the binding. “Breath?”

“At the recorded limit.”

“Hand?”

“Two contracted. Carried movement unchanged.”

“Foundation?”

“Ended. I used it without clearance. The first probe entered the existing injury. Then discrete tests. Then two bounded acts: distributed load and slab return. The voice returned. I reported it.”

Her eyes held his for the length of one breath. “You will give me the sequence again after I examine you.”

“Yes.”

Behind her, Akamoto shut down the lever. The shutter stopped open at the return position. No second use remained in the order.

Mera stood with the three folds beneath her coat. Emilia stood beside her with chalk still ground into both palms. Neither spoke for him.

Morning light entered the court through the foundation crack, exposing the concealed outer-access seal in cross-section: blackened brass caught between new plaster and old mortar.
