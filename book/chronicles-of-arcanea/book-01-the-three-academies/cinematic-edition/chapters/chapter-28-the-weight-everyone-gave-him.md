# The weight everyone gave him

Arion tried the smallest finger once more.

The nail pressed deeper into his palm.

“Don't,” Emilia said.

She kept two fingers beneath his ear. Her other hand flattened against his left shoulder before he could roll back toward the wall.

“I'm moving your coat,” she said. “Not the splint.”

He nodded.

She pulled a fold of cloth between his right ribs and the stone, then another beneath the padded forearm. The coat remained on him. Its hem made an uneven support, enough to leave a dark line of air under the splint.

“Breath.”

He drew one to the binding.

“Again.”

The second stopped higher. Pressure ran from his smallest finger through the forearm and divided behind the elbow. One branch ended there. The other found the same three ribs it had occupied on the west court and entered them more deeply.

“Changed,” he said. “Right ribs. Same path, farther in.”

Emilia's open hand stayed beside the splint in case the coat gave way. She did not touch the arm.

Across the vertical water, Mera set her lamp on the far ledge.

The current broke her into moving bands. First her face, then the dark line of her coat, then both hands when she raised them empty. She lowered one hand, drew the folded page only far enough for its dry edge to cross the lamplight, and put it away again. Then she planted her boots farther apart.

Her boots held, and the page edge was dry. He left the rest alone.

Arion lifted his left hand from the floor.

Emilia pressed his shoulder down. “After we keep you off the wall.”

The deeper lead-state lines remained hidden beneath her yellow cuff and the broken cord. Her wrist was still caught under the coils where she had covered the glass. She hooked the loose length with two chalk-whitened fingers and drew one turn over the cuff, then a second. When the brass shutters were buried beneath plain fibre, she eased her sleeve free.

A soft click passed under the coils.

Neither of them looked beneath.

Emilia kept enough cord over the glass to hide the display and drew the remaining length toward the nearest visible floor joint. She had not laid it across yet.

The joint moved by less than a grain.

Pressure tightened behind Arion's forearm at the same moment. He could not tell whether the stone had pulled the branch, the branch had answered the stone, or his body had made a third movement between them.

“Changed,” he said.

“Contact?”

He checked his ribs, elbow, splint, heels. The coat held the right side above stone. His left palm rested on the floor.

“Left hand only.”

Emilia placed the remaining cord across the joint and marked the stone on both sides. Then she sat back on her heels.

“I can keep you breathing while we talk,” she said. “I won't set your arm beside these seams until you tell me what entered it.”

“What do you need?”

She put the chalk to the floor and made one short mark.

“Tell me every time it touched stone.”

Cold mineral lay beneath Arion's left palm.

Warmth entered alongside it.

It did not come from the lamp. The lamp heated his knuckles and left the center of his palm cool. This warmth travelled through the floor with the remembered sensation of summer sun remaining on a wall after the street had entered shade.

It reached the branch inside his right arm without easing it. For one breath, the divided pressure felt capable of becoming a single line.

*They ask for your choice after the weight has reached bone.*

Arion closed his left hand against the stone.

*Give me the choosing. I will set one path and keep it until the movement ends.*

With the warmth beside the branch, he could imagine the next joint moving without anyone turning to him for an answer.

“And if it returns?” Arion asked.

Emilia's chalk stopped against the floor.

*It will return by the path I chose.*

The warmth lay beside the branch. It took none of the pain. It only made one direction imaginable.

“Who are you answering?” Emilia asked.

Arion looked through the water. Mera had left the dry page inside her coat. She stood behind her lamp with both hands at her sides.

He had wanted Malachar's voice because it named the weight before asking him to show the fingers that could not open. Giving Emilia the name would turn the private line into evidence. It might place him behind glass before he ever returned to the surface.

It would also let her refuse with the fact he had kept from her.

“The first time,” he said, looking at Emilia's chalk mark, “was the hammer handle.”

She did not ask whose hammer.

“Where did you touch it?”

“Outside of my right forearm along the repair seam. My left wrist stayed straight. On the exhale, I drew the pale shim back one hair.”

“What happened?”

“The movement entered the forearm and stopped behind the wrist. Ring finger and small finger pulled in.”

“What did you know before you did it?”

“The voice said nerve, joint, and bone would carry it until another path accepted the movement.”

Emilia turned the chalk once between her fingers. “The voice you answered now.”

“Yes.”

“What did it call itself?”

“Malachar Lumenbright.”

Emilia's grip tightened around the chalk without breaking it. “Its claim.”

“Yes.”

“What did you conceal?”

“I told Akamoto the pain came from the old hand burn and that the state was stable.”

Emilia drew a second mark.

“Next.”

“West hatch. The Foundation brace had ended. Hands clear, ordinary stone again.”

“Then?”

“I set my left heel at the threshold, turned the right side into the moving seam, laid the forearm along it, and breathed out. The opposed movement entered through the arm. It branched at the elbow and across three ribs.”

His breath caught when he named it. The cord over the floor joint did not change.

“What happened outside you?”

“The lower stone moved one handspan into the free pocket. Five people crossed.”

“You saw it move after.”

“Yes. I don't know that I caused it.”

“What did you conceal?”

“I said the method was not entered. I let Anet record structural exposure instead.”

Emilia made the third mark.

Arion watched the water rather than her face.

“Here,” he said. “Right forearm against the wall seam. Exhale. I had accepted no right-arm load and no method outside the entered record. When you asked, I told you I had used no method below.”

“You answered a different question.”

“Yes.”

“Then you used it.”

“Yes.”

“After Mera was clear.”

“To keep the return slab.”

“Without asking her.”

“Yes.”

Emilia looked through the water at Mera's planted stance. “Afterward.”

“The slab held for one interval. The channels struck. The bar lifted. Before the floor threw us, something returned through the seam. I felt it enter the same path and go farther.”

“Something.”

“Pressure. Movement. I don't know its source.”

“What did you conceal?”

“All of it until now.”

The chalk stayed on the third mark.

Emilia's other hand stayed level beside his splint. Chalk dust remained caught along her knuckles.

“The warmth is here now,” Arion said. “The voice offered one path if I give it the choosing.”

“It.”

“The voice identifying itself as Malachar.”

Emilia nodded once. “That is what I can repeat.”

Her wording put distance between the name and the stone. His palm felt none.

The single path still waited beside the branch.

He imagined the next joint moving without every face turning toward him. If the path failed, the choice would belong elsewhere. His body wanted that almost as much as it wanted the pressure gone.

Arion put his left palm flat on the cold floor.

“No,” he said. “I'm not giving you the stop.”

The warmth did not argue. It remained one degree above the stone beneath his hand, close enough that he knew the path had not closed.

He looked at Emilia.

“Will you watch the joint while I move my arm?”

She considered him with the chalk still at the third mark.

“I will watch the joint and your breath. I am not clearing the method. I am not agreeing not to report it.”

“I know.”

“You knew the last terms too.”

He let the answer reach the ribs before he spoke. “Yes.”

Emilia set the chalk down. “Show me the position you want before you move.”

With his left hand, Arion indicated the loose coat below the splint and the gap he needed beneath the right ribs. He would roll farther onto the left hip, fold the coat hem twice, and rest the padding on cloth without letting arm or ribs meet stone.

“My hand stays here,” Emilia said, holding her palm a finger below the splint. “If the cloth gives, I take the weight. Not before.”

“Accepted.”

“That is not an admission term.”

“No. It is me answering you.”

He drew his left knee up. The movement pulled at the right ribs. He stopped before the splint changed height.

“Breath changed,” he said.

“Stop or continue?”

“Stop.”

They waited. Water struck water between them and Mera. The broken cord kept its shallow curve across the chalked joint.

When the breath settled, Arion cleared his own stop.

“Continue.”

He rolled onto the left hip. Emilia folded the coat hem without touching the forearm and slid the cloth beneath the padding. Her open hand followed under the splint, close enough to catch it, far enough that no weight entered her palm.

Arion let one full exhale leave him.

The cord kept its slack.

A brass shutter clicked beneath the coils covering the interface. Neither coil moved. The floor joint at Emilia's chalk marks remained the width it had been.

Arion breathed again. The pressure in his forearm did not ease. It did not advance. Emilia's open hand waited beneath the splint and received no weight.

“Holding for now,” she said.

The warmth under Arion's left palm had thinned to ordinary stone.

Emilia picked up the chalk but did not add a fourth mark.

“If we reach the court,” she said, “I decide what I report after I can ask Mera what she saw. You don't get that answer now.”

“All right.”

“It isn't.”

“No.”

Across the water, Mera lifted her lamp once.

Arion raised his left hand.

She waited until he held it steady. Then she turned the light toward the far wall.

His hand remained open after the lamp moved on.
