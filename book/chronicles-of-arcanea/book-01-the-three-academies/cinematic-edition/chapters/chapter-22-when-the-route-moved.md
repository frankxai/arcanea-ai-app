# When the route moved

The service court had two ways down before the floor moved.

The east stair descended from the upper court to a narrow inquiry walk. Behind the opposite retaining wall, the west maintenance return climbed through stone to a low hatch. The north drain entered between them through a filled seam beneath the central floor. High above all three, the wind terrace looked down through rain.

Lio sat there with his splinted leg on a bench, the route board across his lap.

Five people worked below.

Nemi checked custody wires at the foot of the east stair. Tavin tended the passive structural rig, a frame of plumb lines and mechanical pens. Iven compared exterior labels on six blue-glass vessels. Physician Anet stood beside Surveyor Pell at the north drain, one hand under a sight mirror while he read the old seam through it.

The passive rig worked without a field, its mechanical pens recording every shift in rainwater, boots, and settling stone. Arion waited above with Akamoto, Mera, and Emilia beside the maintenance rack. His right hand stayed inside the tightened sleeve; Mera's sealed two-vessel frame hung at her hip.

Rain struck the upper flagstones hard enough to silver them.

The north gate released.

Water hit the old seam from below.

The brace beneath the east landing sheared.

One of the rig's plumb weights struck stone with a sound like a small bell. Then all six struck at once.

The east stair dropped two steps and turned against the wall. The central floor broke along its filled joint. Water punched grey dust upward, spread across the upper court, and fell back through a black opening wider than Arion's shoulders.

“Lower count,” Akamoto said.

Arion reached the edge before the second word. The east landing had folded over the inquiry walk, leaving a triangular slit beneath it. Through mud and white stone dust he saw Nemi's arm, Tavin's rig frame, Iven bent over the vessels, Anet on one knee, and Pell against the north wall.

Five.

The floor settled again. The slit narrowed. Sight disappeared.

A voice struck through the brass service tube mounted in the west wall.

“Nemi. Five below. No one under the stair. Pell has a shoulder injury. Water at the first walk mark.”

“Air?” Akamoto asked.

“West vent drawing.”

“Keep count and state through the tube.”

“Accepted.”

Arion tested the broken east edge with the heel of his left hand. The landing's upper slab had lost both inner supports. Its remaining weight travelled into one wet corner above Nemi's voice.

He took his hand away.

“East is gone,” he said.

Pell answered from below. His breath hitched between words. “Rig shows the central load moving west. We still have the map.”

The white paper roll fed beneath a narrow inspection slit in the upper floor. Three lines crossed it. One marked the fallen stair. One climbed toward the retaining wall. The third descended through the central opening and continued beyond the paper's calibrated depth.

It was now the only instrument still drawing what the mud concealed.

Akamoto pointed to the maintenance rack. “Two oak shores. External brace at the east lip. No one crosses the chalk.”

Arion pulled the first shore free with his left hand. His right ring finger caught inside the cuff. He turned the contracted fingers toward his body before Anet could look up through the slit.

“I need a Foundation brace,” Akamoto said.

Arion moved closer.

Akamoto named the terms while they fitted the shore beneath the east lip. “External structure only. Visible contact. No load into your body. Stop on hand numbness or lost route state.”

“Accepted.”

Arion set his left palm on the dry upper end of the oak. He did not ask the floor to rise. He followed the shore's grain down to its screw foot and gave the stone beneath it enough Foundation to keep the foot from skidding.

The brace took weight.

The east landing stopped descending. It did not reopen.

From the terrace, a speaking tube rang against its bracket.

“West flag reversed,” Lio called.

The route board beside Akamoto showed the west maintenance return under a black closure mark. Divided-access work had been suspended after Lio's trial. The lower fields had closed after Arion's refusal. No rescue plan used that return.

“West is sealed,” Arion said.

“Sealed to entry,” Lio replied. “Not missing.”

Rain gusted across the terrace. Through the open side of the court, Arion could see the old route flags above Lio. The blue west cloth pulled toward the floor instead of away from it. The white east flag hung soaked against its pole. The split black stop flag twisted once, then held its inner half toward the west wall.

“Water moved the pressure return,” Lio said. “The west line is drawing below the sealed hatch.”

“Board says no lower connection.”

“Board said west relief when I called it. The route moved before the message did.”

Akamoto went to the lower speaking tube. “Nemi. Test west wall. No opening.”

Metal knocked below. Once near the stair. Twice farther away. Then three close taps from behind the retaining wall.

“Air and water behind west return,” Nemi said. “Stone closed at the inner hatch. No visible break.”

Lio answered before Arion could. “That is the route.”

Akamoto looked up to the terrace. Lio remained seated. His splinted leg did not touch the floor.

“West return becomes primary,” Akamoto said. “Lio holds route call.”

The black closure mark stayed on the board. Akamoto drew a second line around it and wrote *moving—unverified* rather than erasing the first.

Emilia had already opened the local mechanical panel beside the maintenance rack. Its lock recognized Akamoto's Roost authority, not her absent Forge credential. She fitted three physical shutters across the route strip: green for *open*, amber for *unknown*, black for *closed*. All three west shutters began amber.

“No silent state,” she said.

“Emergency route-state operator,” Akamoto said. “Local panel only. You stop movement on unknown.”

“Accepted.”

Mera stood beside the upper speaking tube with her own frame untouched at her hip.

“Nemi,” she said. “Six inquiry vessels. Name exterior custody classes only.”

Nemi relayed the labels one by one. Four were institutional derivatives. One carried a current Roost witness condition under Nemi's custody. One carried an Athenaeum living-source restriction under Iven's name.

“Mine travels with me,” Nemi said.

Iven's voice followed through the tube. “The living-source vessel remains in my custody. I will carry it.”

Mera asked each of them to repeat the visible seal color and current-condition mark. Their descriptions matched the exterior registry copied on the upper slate.

“The other four are institutional derivatives,” she said to Akamoto. “The duplicate case contains no current living-source condition. I recommend abandonment under seal.”

Akamoto entered the four labels and case number on the rescue slate. “Abandoned under local emergency authority. Named custodians retain their own carry decisions.”

Below, Iven marked the four derivative positions with chalk. Nemi clipped one chosen vessel inside her coat. Iven did the same with the other.

Anet's voice came through the tube. “Pell's right shoulder is stabilized. Crawl order by mobility and task: Iven, Tavin, me, Nemi, Pell.”

“Why surveyor last?” Akamoto asked.

“Rig release requires his mark,” Pell said. Pain thinned the end of the sentence. “No one else owns that choice.”

Tavin called the three current displacement lines and their times. Emilia copied them beside her route shutters. He began transferring the remaining rig states by hand to a waxed slate narrow enough to crawl with.

Arion and Akamoto reached the west hatch.

The outer plate opened on its manual hinge. Beyond it, two stone edges had moved against each other. The lower edge carried weight from the retaining wall. The upper had taken the displaced service-court floor. Between them, a wet gap showed three fingers of darkness.

Air moved through it. Not enough space for a shoulder.

Arion fitted the second oak shore beneath the upper edge. Akamoto set a screw jack against the lower return and turned it until metal complained.

“Foundation brace reauthorized at the west shore,” Akamoto said. “External and visible. No load into your body. Stop on numbness or lost route state.”

“Accepted.”

“One quarter,” he said.

Arion held the Foundation brace at the shore foot. The jack opened the gap by a finger.

“State?” Akamoto called.

Emilia checked her panel. The mechanical pin remained inside its marked tolerance. “West unknown. No lower confirmation.”

Nemi struck once from below, then slid the flat route gauge into the inner pocket.

“Gap stable. Below shoulder width.”

Emilia left the amber shutter in place.

Akamoto turned the jack another quarter. The lower stone moved. The upper followed it.

Arion felt the return through the oak before the shore made sound. Opposed loads met behind the hatch and had no direction left that did not narrow the inner pocket.

The pressure entered the outside of his right forearm where it rested against the retaining wall.

His smallest finger drew hard into his palm.

“Numbness?” Akamoto asked.

Arion tried to open the finger. It did not move.

“Yes.”

“Foundation brace ends. Hands clear.”

Arion released the shore foot. The brief Foundation hold returned to ordinary stone. Akamoto backed the jack to its last stable tooth.

The gap remained one hand narrower than Anet's crawl requirement.

Water reached the second mark below.

“Voice slit down by half,” Nemi reported. “West vent still drawing.”

Tavin called another rig state. The white line beneath the central opening had begun to branch.

Akamoto put both hands on the jack frame. “We hold here. Anet, find a smaller requirement.”

“Bone does not become narrower because stone is inconvenient,” she said.

“Then we open another route.”

Lio struck the terrace tube. “There isn't time to lose west.”

“I know.”

Arion looked into the three-finger darkness.

The shores and backed jack held. Five people still answered through the tube, and none of them could fit through.

Warm stone had taught him what followed.

*Where can it go?*

Nerve. Joint. Bone.

Arion set his left heel against the hatch threshold. He turned his right side to the moving seam and laid the outside of his forearm along its angle. His ribs met the retaining wall through wet cloth.

Akamoto saw the position.

“No,” he said.

Arion breathed out.

The opposed movement entered him.

His forearm bent nowhere visible. Pain ran behind both long bones and found the locked joints at his wrist. The two contracted fingers pulled the first branch into his hand. A second travelled beneath his elbow. A third crossed the right side of his chest and set itself between three ribs.

The lower stone moved one handspan into the free pocket his body had made available.

The upper edge stayed on the oak shores.

A shoulder-width crawl opened.

“Lower state,” Emilia called.

Nemi drove the flat gauge through from below. “One handspan gained. Pocket stable at my side.”

Emilia raised green over the west route.

“Iven first,” Anet said.

Iven entered with the blue-glass vessel strapped beneath his chest. His hands appeared in the crawl opening, then his head and one shoulder. Arion held the movement behind his ribs. The stone tried to take back the handspan each time Iven shifted weight.

Halfway through, Emilia's green shutter dropped.

Amber rose.

“Stop.”

Iven stopped with one arm on the upper ledge and his hips below.

The mechanical route pin had moved outside its line. Arion's transferred load had changed the angle the panel could see.

“I am not moving,” Arion said.

“The panel does not agree,” Emilia replied.

Below, Nemi put the route gauge against the pocket. Arion heard metal scrape once along the lower stone and twice against the upper.

“Physical pocket unchanged,” she said. “Gauge passes at all three marks. Iven has clear shoulders.”

Emilia checked Nemi's spoken state against the local pin, marked the pin *unreliable under transferred angle*, and raised green again.

“Move,” Anet said.

Iven came through. Akamoto caught the back of his harness without pulling the vessel against stone. Iven rolled onto the upper court and placed one wet palm on the ledge.

“One,” Nemi called.

Tavin read the last three rig marks aloud. He copied them to the waxed slate, signed the end of the sequence, and disconnected the paper feed from the upper inspection slit. The white line stopped advancing on Emilia's side. It continued below.

He entered the crawl with the slate between his teeth.

Arion breathed shallowly above the load, keeping each breath out of his right ribs. The movement stayed locked in his side.

Tavin cleared the opening and slapped a wet hand beside Iven's.

“Two.”

Anet made Pell repeat his finger sensation, breath depth, and the position of his bound shoulder. Then she entered third, feet first so she could watch him until the stone took her sight.

At the outer hatch she turned onto her side and looked directly at Arion's arm.

He turned his hand into the seam.

Anet left a third wet print on the ledge. “Three. Nemi.”

Nemi gave the lower state before moving. “Pocket passes. Water below knee. Vent drawing. Pell upright at rig.”

She clipped the second living-claim vessel higher beneath her coat and entered the crawl. The point of her shoulder caught once on the upper stone. Arion took the answering movement into his wrist. A white burst crossed his sight.

Nemi did not ask him to hold harder. She flattened the vessel against her sternum, turned her body by half a hand, and freed herself.

Her palm struck the ledge beside the others.

“Four. Pell remains.”

Below, the rig frame knocked against the inquiry walk.

“Last state copied,” Tavin called from his slate. “No further useful feed.”

Pell entered his release mark.

Metal pins withdrew beneath the floor. The passive rig tipped toward the central opening. Its plumb lines swung once, writing a final white curve across the roll.

“Rig released,” Pell said.

Something heavy fell below the level of the speaking tube. Glass followed it. Four abandoned vessels struck at different depths. The duplicate case opened on the way down, and dry paper made one brief wingbeat in the dark.

“Pell moving,” Nemi called.

The surveyor's left hand appeared. His bound right arm stayed against his chest. Akamoto took his harness. Tavin took the back of his belt. They moved him through without rotating the injured shoulder.

Pell's boot cleared the hatch.

“Five,” Nemi said.

The lower walk kicked under Arion's heel. The free pocket began to close.

He tried to return the movement through his forearm before the stone reclaimed it. The lower edge came back first. The final branch met a closed joint and stayed between his ribs.

Emilia dropped the black shutter.

“Closed.”

Arion pulled his forearm away.

The handspan vanished. Stone met stone behind him and crushed the flat route gauge Nemi had left inside. The oak shores held the upper edge. The jack stayed on its backed tooth.

Arion's right arm did not release.

Pressure ran from his smallest finger through the forearm, divided at the elbow, and continued along three ribs. Beneath the sealed hatch, something answered the same branching shape. It might have been settling stone. It might have been the buried response that had followed refusal through the lower chamber.

Whatever it was, it found the path inside him without opening the hatch.

Anet cut the tightened cuff rather than pull it over his hand. “Sit.”

Arion sat against the retaining wall. She bound his forearm to a straight splint and wrapped his ribs only far enough to keep the next breath from shifting the strain.

“Source?” she asked.

“Structural exposure at the west seam.”

“Method?”

He looked at the five people on the upper court.

“Not entered.”

Akamoto heard him. “Disputed.”

Tavin found the rain gate's holding pin in the upper wash. Rust filled the break except for one bright crescent. Nemi supplied the six-year repair date from the exterior drain label. Akamoto entered a provisional finding: cistern pressure had parted the corroded pin, water had entered the old seam, and the decayed brace had sheared under ordinary load. Inspection remained pending. No field or model action appeared in the sequence.

The council recorder printed a temporary assessment from the surviving state copies.

*All persons recovered. Two current living-consent vessels preserved under named custody. Passive rig and institutional derivatives lost. Outcome humane and strategically damaging.*

Pell read it with his good hand pressed to the shoulder binding. “Keep the last two words.”

Akamoto placed live identities, route vulnerabilities, and unverified cause under temporary incident-preservation and security hold. He set the review for the next session and did not call it a final seal.

Rainwater gathered along the ledge beneath the west hatch.

Iven's handprint dried first. Tavin's beside it. Anet's broad palm. Nemi's smaller print with chalk at the heel. Pell's left hand, dragged at one edge where they had lifted him.

Five.

Arion tried to put his right hand beside them.

The two contracted fingers would not open.

Below the ledge, the rig's last white line slipped out of sight.
