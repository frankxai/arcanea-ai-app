# Descent under witness

The brass shutter reached its stop before the stone behind it finished moving.

“Shut,” Mera said.

“Shut,” Akamoto read back.

Beyond the hand-wide gap, grit continued to drag across stone. The sound passed through the oak frame and entered the court beneath Arion's left boot. It was not a force he had to take or return. It was a sound. He kept it that way.

The lever rested under Akamoto's glove. The Chapter 24 supplement and the sealed report lay together on the witness bench, joined by black thread and their one unchanged expiry time. The exterior shutter protected the gap. It had not touched the buried wall and could not make that wall stop.

Anet rolled a stair-sledge into the chalk square.

It had been built for mine rescues and looked it. Two ash runners curved under a seat no wider than Arion's hips. The back ended below his shoulder blades. Four dull rings accepted lowering lines from a frame independently anchored across the court, far from Emilia's manual door. Someone had replaced one leather strap with sailcloth and written the date on it in kitchen ink.

Anet set a measuring reed across the runners. “If the opening returns to its last width, this clears with two fingers on either side.”

“If,” Akamoto said.

Neris had one hand over the council slate. No reply had entered it.

Akamoto looked at Mera, then Emilia. “Two people can preserve the interior state. Arion remains here.”

“We need all three positions,” Arion said.

Mera turned from the shutter vane. “You can name your position.”

He had used *we* before asking either of them. Mera caught it.

“My position,” he said, “is below.”

“Your position is also splinted,” Akamoto replied.

“That does not give you mine.”

Akamoto's gloved thumb moved once against the lever grip. He did not answer.

Anet placed the sledge beside Arion and loosened his sling without removing the splint. Her fingers travelled under the edge of the rib binding, checking what each breath displaced. On the third breath she stopped him before he could shorten it.

“Again.”

He breathed until the binding reached the place that hurt.

“There.”

“That was not a full breath.”

“It was the available one.”

“Then report the available one. Do not improve it for me.”

She touched the exposed tips of his right fingers in order. Thumb. First. Middle. Ring. Small.

“Same,” he said at the last two. They remained drawn toward his palm as if the tendons had been shortened while he slept.

“Same feeling, or the same inability to move?”

“Both.”

Anet wrote nothing. The medical sheet had not left her coat.

The council slate clicked on the witness bench.

Neris read the answer silently first. Her mouth tightened at one corner.

“They will consider one evidence-preservation admission,” she said. “They require a single stop authority for the descent rig.”

“No,” Mera said.

Neris looked toward the shutter. Stone still rasped beyond the brass leaf, quieter now.

“Their concern is mechanical. Three people call different states while one body is suspended. By the time the operator sorts the voices, the sledge has already moved.”

Akamoto released the winch brake by the width of a tooth and set it again. The locking bar dropped into place with a flat iron knock.

“Any named stop holds the bar,” he said. “No reverse. No restart.”

“How does lowering resume?” Neris asked.

“The person who called the stop clears it,” Anet said. “I check breath, hand, and load again. Arion may then request lowering while the admission remains active.”

Neris turned the slate so its blank face showed to all of them. “If I stop the admission, it ends. No one here clears it.”

The winch bar stayed down.

Mera stepped away from the frame at her hip. “That preserves my stop.”

Emilia examined the lowering lines where they crossed the independent support. She pulled each rope through one hand, finding old splices by touch. “And mine.”

Neris entered the amended condition.

While the slate carried it away, Mera opened neither of her red-threaded vessels. She lifted the entire frame from her belt and set it in the first compartment of an exterior custody cradle. The rain vial rested beside the self-sealed cup, each in its own ring. In the second compartment she placed a closed document sleeve.

Nemi crossed from the restricted shelves carrying a plain key board.

Mera turned one key in the vessel compartment and one in the document compartment. The locks stopped halfway.

Nemi inserted the paired keys. Together they completed each turn.

“Try yours without mine,” Mera said.

Nemi did. The first key moved no farther than the width of its cut. She tested the second compartment, then offered her board for Mera to test in return. Mera's keys also stopped alone.

“I'll hold the second keys,” Nemi said. “I can't open either compartment alone, and I won't use or pass them on.”

Mera removed one key board and kept it inside her coat. The red threads remained visible behind the cradle glass. The document sleeve showed only its closed edge.

Emilia placed her read-only receipt and the two halves of her teaching strip in the safety tray. She left a thumb's width between the cut ends. She pressed her maker mark across the tray closure.

She took three hand lamps from the local rack, a stick of plain chalk, and a weighted bell-board with a coil of cord. No current entered any of them.

Arion's tool loop hung empty against his left thigh.

He felt the absence of the hammer each time the loop touched his trousers. The repaired tool remained in the sealed chamber where he had refused further measurement. He had wanted it with him because its weight made his hand know where it was. He had also wanted it because a hammer turned fear into a surface and a next action.

There was no next action he could strike into shape here.

The council slate clicked a second time.

Three names appeared on separate lines: Arion, Mera, Emilia. Beneath them, the order authorized one ingress if an inward withdrawal followed the repeated manual offer before the witnessed window ended. It authorized one later return only if they could present themselves again at the same threshold. It granted no way to summon the opening and no authority to retrieve them through closed stone. The visible strip denied transfer, placement, custody, source access, and any automatic consent effect.

The final line read: *Any named stop holds.*

Neris pressed her witness mark beneath it.

Akamoto read all three names. “Recorded.”

He did not say he approved.

Anet lowered Arion onto the sledge. The runners took his weight before the straps did. She passed the lower band over his left thigh and across the seat, leaving his right side clear. A second strap crossed below the ribs. The upper band held his left shoulder without pressing the splinted forearm.

One loose end fell behind his hip.

Arion reached across with his left hand. His balance followed it. The near runner lifted from the floor.

Emilia caught the wooden side, not his body. The runner settled.

Akamoto stood close enough to take the loose strap. His hand remained open beside it.

Arion could have recovered the end by shifting farther. He could already feel where the motion would enter his right ribs.

“Please,” he said.

Akamoto drew the strap forward and held it where Arion's left hand could reach. He did not fasten it until Arion nodded.

The buckle closed.

Anet fitted a padded loop that kept the right splint away from both rope and stone. “No climbing. No right-arm load. No Foundation brace. No method outside the entered record. You report each change in breath or right-hand sensation. You stop if the grade changes.”

The method waited behind his ribs as memory rather than motion: keep the wrist straight, lay the forearm along the seam, let the last movement enter him.

“Accepted,” Arion said.

Her eyes remained on him for one breath longer. Then she tested the lower strap.

Arion looked at Mera. “If the opening returns, do you choose to enter?”

Her empty hip showed where the red-threaded frame had hung.

“Yes. If pressure crosses my scar or anything asks me to open or name a source, I call stop.”

He nodded. He did not add the rest for her.

He turned to Emilia. “Do you choose to enter?”

She tucked the chalk beside the unpowered bell-board. “Yes. If the route changes and I cannot account for the change, I stop. I will not call the landing safe.”

“I am not asking you to.”

“You were going to.”

He almost said he had only been going to ask whether it would hold. Her expression suggested she had already heard that defense in three workshops and broken equipment after two of them.

“Then I am asking now,” he said. “Will you tell me what you can see?”

“Yes.”

Neris moved the active order beside Mera's state board. “Admission begins only if the interface clears.”

Akamoto took the shutter lever.

“Shutter shut.”

“Shut,” Mera said.

He pulled. The state vane turned from its unbroken face to the diagonal groove.

“Moving.”

“Moving.”

The brass leaf slid into the oak frame. Its round aperture appeared.

“Clear.”

“Clear.”

Behind the hand-wide gap, the buried face had returned almost to its old seam. Only a line of disturbed grit showed where it had opened. The three Academy marks were hidden on the inner edge.

Emilia stood behind the chip shield. Her left hand took the tongue lever. The repaired yellow cuff covered the burn on her other wrist.

“Offering contact.”

The flexible brass crossed the empty gap and came to rest against stone. It bent under its own weight.

“Tongue resting,” Emilia said. “Unfastened.”

Mera entered the state.

Nothing followed.

Rain tapped the top of the exterior screen. One lowering line turned slowly beneath the rig beam until its rope fibers stopped seeking a twist. Anet kept two fingers against the pulse in Arion's left wrist. The sledge straps warmed where his body met them.

Arion watched the tongue. His forearm stayed in the padded loop. The empty tool loop tapped his thigh once when he shifted. Then he went still.

A dry grain fell through the gap.

The brass tongue straightened.

“Inner stone moving,” Mera said.

Emilia lifted the lever at once. “Withdrawing.”

The tongue cleared the opening and lay flat inside its guide.

Mera checked the stop. “Withdrawn.”

“Withdrawn.”

The vertical seam widened. Old iron and wet mineral entered the court. The buried wall moved inward by a finger, then another, exposing the worn edge of the joint authority plate. It stopped short of its first opening. At the narrowest point, the space left less than two fingers beside each sledge runner.

Neris looked from the fixed exterior marks to the active order.

“Admission active.”

Emilia took one lamp and the coiled route cord. She crossed the shutter plane without touching the manual frame. At the top tread she tested the visible stone with the heel of her boot, then put her full weight down.

“It held me,” she said. “That's all I know.”

She descended. The yellow cuff passed the joint plate and disappeared first, then the lamp. Its light travelled down the stair in short broken bands.

At the first level, she placed the weighted bell-board on dry stone entirely inside the threshold. She paid out one arm's length of cord and made a chalk mark beside the weight. No line remained between her and the court.

Mera removed three blank sheets from a waxed cover. She left the cover above and carried the paper inside her coat.

She stepped through second.

At the joint plate she paused, not to read it, but to turn her scarred palm away from the mineral crust. Then she followed Emilia's lamp to the landing.

Akamoto took the winch handle. Anet set the locking bar.

“Breath?” she asked.

“Available.”

“Right hand?”

“Thumb, first, middle. Ring and small unchanged.”

The bar lifted.

The sledge crossed the exterior frame. Its runners met the first stair together. Akamoto lowered one tooth at a time, keeping both ropes level. The back of the sledge stayed below Arion's shoulders. Stone passed close to his left sleeve. On the right, the padded splint loop held his forearm clear by less than the width of his thumb.

He could see Mera and Emilia below.

They had left space at the foot of the stair. Neither raised her hands toward him.

The sledge tipped onto the third tread. The lower strap shifted against his left thigh. His ribs caught before the next breath reached them.

“Stop.”

The winch bar struck.

Everything held. No helpful hand arrived from below. Akamoto did not draw him back. The ropes stopped with their fibers taut and quiet.

“Your stop,” Anet said from above.

Arion let the unfinished breath out. He waited through the sharp place and tried again, smaller. The right hand remained what it had been.

“Breath changed with the pitch,” he said. “Settled now. Hand unchanged.”

Anet asked for two more breaths. He gave them without making either larger for her.

She sighted the shifted strap against the left-thigh mark. Akamoto held the handle while she checked both runners and the paired line marks. They remained level.

“Medical check holds,” she said. “Your stop remains.”

Arion looked down.

“Mera?”

“My stop is clear.”

“Emilia?”

She watched the runners, not his face. “Route state unchanged. Clear.”

Above him, Neris kept the marked order raised.

“I clear it,” he said. “Lower, please.”

The bar lifted.

When the runners reached the landing, Emilia braced the sledge against sideways movement with her boot. Mera stood within reach and kept her hands at her sides.

Arion freed the upper strap with his left hand. The lower buckle took longer. No one completed it for him. He set his left foot on the level stone and brought his weight forward through the thigh as Anet had shown him.

The right ribs objected. They did not shift.

He stood.

“Still yes?” he asked Mera.

“Still mine,” she said.

He looked at Emilia.

She nudged the released sledge away from his boot. “Send that back before the gap narrows too far.”

Arion unhooked the lower rings. Emilia took the left one. Together they kept the sledge level until Akamoto's lines lifted its runners onto the stair. Neither line touched the buried wall or the manual frame. The sledge rose through the opening and vanished into the court.

The last loop cleared.

Mera looked up at the fixed mark outside. “Inner wall moving outward.”

Grey stone entered the opening.

Above it, Akamoto leaned into view. His gloved hand rested on the court side of the frame. Anet's white sleeve crossed behind him. Neither reached through.

Arion put his left hand against his own thigh, over the empty tool loop.

The joint authority plate narrowed to two marks, then one. Exterior light became a white line across Akamoto's glove. The line thinned and went out.

Stone met stone.

A second sound followed beyond it: the dull travel of the exterior shutter, then its stop.

Emilia lit the remaining lamps.

The first landing opened into a level vestibule longer than the infirmary court and not much wider. Its ceiling had been cut in three sections. Fine parallel tool marks crossed one. The next carried broad chisel hollows filled with pale mineral. The last had been resurfaced so often that no first workmanship remained. Water stood in shallow basins along the left wall.

They were not matched vessels. The nearest had a chipped lip at knee height. The middle basin sagged almost to the floor. The farthest had been cut around a crack instead of through it, leaving one side deeper than the other.

Emilia set the lamps apart. Their flames appeared in the nearest water. So did the chipped mineral lip and a pale seam in the ceiling.

Arion lowered his left hand over the basin.

The water returned the lamp beneath his wrist. It returned the seam above his knuckles. Where his hand should have been, the reflected stone remained unbroken.

He moved one finger.

Firelight trembled. The hand did not appear.

Mera had drawn one blank page halfway from her coat. Its lower corner hung toward the water.

She folded the dry corner back inside.
