# Three interviews

The other two doors closed in Mera's water before she heard them close in stone.

Their reflections crossed the cup as dark bars. One took Arion out of sight. The other cut Emilia away. By the time the water stilled, Mera sat alone with Neris in the third room and three green lines burned along the corridor panel outside.

Each line carried the same words.

*Compatible terms.*

Not *accepted*.

The room had been arranged to make the distinction difficult to remember. A round table left no head position. Two cups stood at equal distance from a shallow drain. The privacy shutter had closed without a lock sounding, and a white felt strip covered the door seam so even footsteps in the corridor arrived without direction.

Neris placed a blue-glass key on the table.

“This opens the deep restoration catalogue,” she said. “If the terms are accepted.”

The key was thin enough to disappear behind one finger. Inside its blue length, silver flecks formed the source-reader mark Mera had seen only through the catalogue room glass.

Neris laid the written offer beside it.

Six hours of inquiry custody. One named source reader. Automatic return of the original vial at the custody limit. Every derivative sealed under the same disputed-source restrictions. Mera's dissent attached to any interpretation before circulation. Her participant-witness recognition restored on receipt, with a seat as source reader for the active inquiry.

The Athenaeum had repaired every obvious defect in its first demand.

Mera read the page again.

At her belt, the rain-memory vial remained inside its external cradle. The red disputed-custody thread crossed blue glass between the amber and black seals. Neris's suspension slip was still clipped behind it. Nothing on the table touched the cradle.

“Who receives a derivative?” Mera asked.

“The named reader. The inquiry panel. You.”

“Arion?”

“If the opened interval is attributed to him and he receives source standing.”

“So not at custody.”

“Not automatically.”

“Who can extend the six hours?”

“No one. A second request would require a second agreement.”

“Can an unsafe opening delay return?”

“The vial returns sealed. A hazardous derivative may remain isolated.”

Mera looked at the key. The catalogue could tell her what occupied the four shared-source positions before three institutions altered their derivatives. It might turn the suspected Hollow Root resemblance into an authenticated source. It could give her objections a place in the room before someone else supplied the interpretation.

Her thumb stayed on the offer longer than she intended. She wanted the key.

She moved the page aside without turning it over.

“Before I answer,” she said, “define the corridor status.”

Neris glanced toward the narrow window beside the shutter. From Mera's chair, the window showed only the panel and three strips of green.

“No participant has entered a package incompatibility.”

“That is not what the panel says.”

“The service label is *compatible terms*.”

“Does green mean Arion accepted his offer?”

“No.”

“Does it mean Emilia accepted hers?”

“No.”

“Does it mean they have read them?”

“Receipt is confirmed.”

“Does it distinguish receipt from silence?”

“The private room does. The corridor does not.”

Mera put one finger on the edge of her offer. “If I refuse this, what changes?”

“Your offer closes. Your terms no longer contribute to any recommendation.”

“My line turns amber?”

“Only a package incompatibility turns the shared lines amber.”

“Then my refusal can leave a green line beneath the words *compatible terms*.”

“Your personal record will show refusal.”

“Inside this room.”

“Protected from the other participants, as theirs are protected from you.”

Neris said it without evasion. Mera could have written the argument herself. Arion's need for treatment did not belong in Emilia's negotiation. Slate Row's broken homes were not a tool the Forge should be allowed to weigh against a laboratory appointment. Mera's hunger for the deep catalogue should not raise or lower the price offered to either of them.

Mera understood the privacy rule. She still hated the green beneath Arion's closed door.

“What does a renewed green state produce?” Mera asked.

“If the open offers remain mutually operable, the panel may issue a coordinated-protection recommendation.”

“To whom?”

“The joint safety chair.”

“Can the chair transfer Arion into Luminary residency?”

“No. Arion may refuse the offer. Involuntary protective residency requires a sitting safety quorum under immediate-risk authority.”

“Can the recommendation schedule that review?”

“It can schedule a transfer review. It cannot determine one.”

“Will it say he agreed?”

“No.”

“Will it say three private terms were compatible when one person may have refused, one may be silent, and one may still be reading?”

Neris rested both hands on the table. “It will say no package incompatibility was entered.”

Mera tasted salt along the back of her tongue. The label remained green in the window.

“I request direct clarification with the other participants.”

“Denied during private deliberation.”

“By whom?”

“By the standing protection rule. The purpose is to prevent deal-shopping and reciprocal pressure.”

“I request that my objection to the shared label be shown beside it.”

“You may enter a package incompatibility.”

A small amber control sat at the lower edge of the table. Mera had seen it when she entered. The instructions called it an authorized response and nothing more.

“When does it alter the corridor state?” she asked.

“If received during a renewal intake, immediately. Otherwise it enters ordinary review.”

“When is the next intake?”

“The joint service cadence is not included in local instructions.”

“Why?”

“I did not design the service.”

“That was not my question.”

“Strategic timing was treated as a risk to independent deliberation.”

“And unmarked timing?”

“Was treated as an implementation detail.”

Neris did not ask Mera to accept that answer. Her dislike of it showed only in the extra space she left before the last two words.

The panel's side cover stood open for inspection. Three pale conductors entered a box marked *joint continuity*. Beneath them, the service strip named mirror state, retention, and source recovery—the same path Emilia had exposed in the lower assembly chamber, the same separation the council admitted had operated at intake.

Mera looked back at the offer.

Six hours. Automatic return. Derivatives bounded. Dissent preserved. A real key to a room she had spent years being told she was not ready to enter.

She thought of the three altered catalogues aligned by source position: wax over glass, blank paper stitched into a cut, copper planished flat. She thought of Neris carrying Mera's limited finding to the attributed tray even while suspending the standing behind it.

The return clause was good. Mera still could not put the vial on the table.

“I refuse source deposit,” Mera said.

Neris turned the offer page over. “Refusal received.”

A small blue time mark appeared on the room slate.

Outside, Mera's green line thinned. It did not change colour. For one breath it almost disappeared. Then it returned at full brightness beneath *compatible terms*.

Her offer was closed. The package remained open around Arion and Emilia.

Mera set her refused sheet facedown at the threshold.

“Again,” she said. “Direct clarification.”

“The request is entered.”

“When is it heard?”

“At ordinary review unless another status opens the rooms.”

“Before a coordinated-protection recommendation?”

“I cannot promise order.”

The amber control waited beneath Mera's hand. She could press it now. If the service happened to be inside its brief intake beat, the three lines would change at once. If not, her objection would wait in a review queue while the panel continued to announce compatibility.

Mera took her hand off the amber control. Arion could refuse for himself, and no recommendation could move him without review. The room gave her every lawful reason to wait.

Mera watched the water skin in her cup settle around three green reflections.

She still wanted Neris to know she had found the flaw first.

The violet scar across her palm warmed.

At intake, pressure had crossed that hand after local release. Some of the trace followed Arion's contact sequence. Some belonged to the structure, the severed conductors, the district water, or to Mera's own body receiving too much at once. She had marked the interval *unaccessed* because its source could not be separated without opening it.

Arion had seen the scar on the meal-house roof.

*Keep it that way.*

*Unless you give me a different answer, I will.*

He had not.

The rain-memory vial stayed sealed against her belt. She lifted the drinking cup instead, already knowing which refusal she meant to touch.

She touched one drop to the violet line in her palm.

Cold crossed the scar first. Then the surface of the cup tightened against her fingers as if the water had become a skin stretched over a deeper vessel.

Mera did not ask whose memory waited there.

She followed duration only.

A local refusal closed like two fingers against a thread. The thread travelled outward. White retention warmed beyond the wall. Nothing spoke. No image formed. A narrow pressure held between receipt and return, long enough for one state to remain where another had already ended.

Then came the renewal.

Only the service interval remained: a refusal correlated with Arion's, carried through the exposed path beside the panel.

Mera stopped.

The drop drew back from her scar with a fine violet edge. When it touched the cup, one ring crossed the surface and remained at the glass instead of returning.

She covered the cup with the room's dry lid. From her source kit she took an unused black thread, passed it through the lid catch, and pressed her personal mark into the soft closure bead. She wrote on a blank strip:

*Derived during private deliberation. Source class disputed. One intake interval accessed contrary to the recorded non-access condition. Physical custody: Mera Tidecrest.*

No Athenaeum seal appeared beside hers.

Neris read it across the table. She did not touch the cup.

“Do you want that entered?” she asked.

“After the corridor opens.”

“If it opens.”

Mera placed her refusal timestamp beside the pulse she had felt. The service did not breathe, but the retained state rose and returned on a fixed cadence. Her refusal had given her the phase. The trace gave her the brief intake inside it.

The next one approached.

She put her thumb on the amber control.

“Package incompatibility,” she said.

She counted the interval from her refusal mark and pressed where the trace had placed the intake beat.

The three green lines narrowed. For an instant the corridor held them as hair-thin marks.

Amber travelled across all three lines.

The privacy shutters released together.

Stone withdrew into stone with a sound like three deep locks opening underwater. Air crossed the corridor from room to room. Mera saw Emilia first, standing in the opposite doorway with an unsigned offer in her left hand and the yellow repair patch at her cuff. Arion came into view a moment later. His right sleeve was fastened tighter than the left. A folded page rested beneath his other hand.

Neris stepped into the corridor. “Package incompatibility entered during renewal intake. Private offers remain private unless their recipients disclose them. State your personal outcomes separately.”

Arion looked at the green words fading above the amber lines.

“I was offered treatment and repair for Slate Row,” he said. “The price was protective residency and supervised practice.”

The Luminary interviewer behind him began to speak.

Arion turned over the page. “I refuse the offer. Repair the street because it needs repair. Do not use it to buy custody of me.”

He walked to the corridor bench and left the sheet there.

The personal line beside his room changed to *refused*. The shared lines remained amber.

Emilia held her page flat against her chest. “Mine concerns laboratory authority. I have not accepted it. I have not refused it. I am keeping the paper.”

“Your deliberation remains open,” Neris said.

Emilia looked at Mera. “Did you know that before you opened my door?”

“No.”

“Did you know what I would choose?”

“No.”

“Then do not let the record say you stopped me from signing.”

“I won't.”

Mera pointed to the sheet facedown at her threshold. “I refused the source-custody offer. My refusal did not enter a package incompatibility.”

Arion's gaze stopped on the blue refusal mark, then the exposed white retention conductor.

“You knew the return,” he said.

Mera picked up the sealed cup.

“I opened one intake interval—the delay after your refusal. The cup holds the copy. You did not consent.”

He looked at the black thread through her personal seal.

“Not next time,” he said. “I choose another witness.”

“Entered,” Mera said.

Neris returned to the table and wrote two findings on separate pages. The first named an interface ambiguity: truthful private states had been presented as shared compatibility, with renewal timing absent from local instructions. The second named unauthorized trace access by an unrecognized participant witness.

“I witnessed Mera Tidecrest self-seal one derived cup and attribute one accessed interval,” Neris read. “I do not authenticate the source class, accept the cup into Athenaeum evidence, or restore witness recognition.”

Mera signed her own statement. The cup remained in her hand.

The corridor speaker clicked once.

“Private offers remain valid under their existing terms,” the council recorder said. “The *compatible terms* presentation is suspended pending interface and procedure review. No coordinated-protection recommendation will issue from this session.”

Emilia crossed the corridor without lowering her offer. At the bench she paused beside Arion's returned page.

“You need someone to look at your arm,” she said.

Arion kept his right side away from both of them. “The report says old strain.”

“I did not ask what the report says.”

He flexed his left hand, the one they could see. “Not here.”

She tucked her own offer beneath her left arm, yellow patch against white paper, and turned toward the stair.

Mera remained at her threshold with the sealed cup in one hand. Her refused offer lay facedown by her boot. Arion's page rested alone on the corridor bench. Emilia still carried hers.

Arion followed Emilia, his tightened right sleeve turned away from Mera. She could see the cuff, the added notch, and none of the hand it concealed.
