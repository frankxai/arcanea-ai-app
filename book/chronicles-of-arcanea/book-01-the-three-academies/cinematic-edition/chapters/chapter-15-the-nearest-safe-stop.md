# The nearest safe stop

Lio signed the revised route plan with his rescue gloves still on.

The pencil tore through the paper at the last letter of his name. He turned the sheet over, signed the back, and handed both layers to Akamoto.

“Lower course removed,” Akamoto said. “Upper course only. West and east approaches remain available until the candidate closes one. Central release unchanged. Second team stages at the north stair. Do you accept the revision?”

“I accept that someone else discovered a buried problem during the week my license was meant to become difficult in ordinary ways.”

“That is not an answer.”

“I accept the revision.”

Akamoto looked to every person on the route board in turn.

Cass and two volunteer carriers accepted the return platform. Arion accepted west support under his daily terms: Foundation attention, no remote reinforcement, no resonant-tool contact, no load transfer into his body. Physician Anet had cleared him for climbing if his rib binding remained dry and he reported any hand numbness.

Mera accepted independent observation in her personal capacity. Her slate already carried separate columns for instruction, candidate action, team action, and interruption. The red-threaded cradle holding her vial rested outside the harness strap.

Emilia accepted maker observation only. The yellow patch on her cuff had acquired a dark tea spot. Her exit witness remained mounted below under numbered seal, glowing green inside a clear shield no one was permitted to touch.

“I can provide three cloth states at the route junction,” she said. “Continuous, interrupted, unverified. No instrument required.”

“Who updates them?” Akamoto asked.

“The nearest observer.”

“Using what source?”

“Visible route condition.”

“Then the observer becomes the instrument and still cannot see both approaches.”

“That is an argument for a shared witness.”

“The witness is sealed.”

“That is an argument for two witnesses.”

Akamoto pointed to the existing sight-flag line above the course. A blue flag served west, white served east, and a split black flag stopped both. The upper platform could see the signal post. The staged second team could see its duplicate at the north stair. A runner waited at the turn where the two posts could not see each other.

“Today uses the licensed procedure,” he said.

Emilia looked at Mera's slate. “Record my objection to the single relay.”

Mera did.

The upper course crossed the Roost's roofline between a grain store and an empty sail loft. It was not built to resemble a ruin. Every brace, ladder, suspended door, and narrowing passage had a maintenance mark. White chalk identified stable joints. Red marked planned movement. A person who trained here was expected to learn the difference before dust made both colors the same.

Morning wind moved uphill from the city. It entered the west approach and left through the sail loft with a low rope hum.

Arion placed his boot against the first timber joint.

The route carried no active field. Weight travelled through pegged oak frames into two stone party walls. The central casualty bay rested on a crossed brace designed to settle by one handspan when Nemi released its guide rope. West relief supports waited on a maintenance rack outside the course. East relief used a shorter approach but passed over the return platform's load path.

“Which route?” Lio asked him.

“West is slower. East shares the return brace for six steps.”

Cass tightened her harness at the platform. “East grades faster.”

“West,” Lio said.

“You have finally become educable,” Akamoto said.

“Record that as assessor interference.”

Mera's pencil moved.

Lio checked the weighted casualty strapped beyond the central bay. The canvas figure carried sand in its chest and wet rope in its limbs, heavy enough to punish a bad lift without pretending to be a person. His examination required him to reach it, assign an extraction route, survive the planned settlement, and coordinate the return platform without losing a volunteer or a piece of required equipment.

He held up his rescue board. “First team: candidate and west support. Return team holds until my green call. Second team enters only on named route. If sight flags fail, repeat route and condition back through the relay. No shortened messages.”

The runner at the turn repeated, “No shortened messages.”

“Nearest safe stop?” Akamoto asked.

Lio pointed to a white joint before the casualty bay. Arion chose the same one from the opposite side. Cass marked the return platform's stone threshold.

Nemi struck the start bell.

Lio entered first.

He moved differently inside a route. At breakfast he wasted motion to make a point; under harness he spent none. He tested each rung with the heel, kept his rescue board inside his shoulder line, and called back every change in height. Arion followed two steps behind, reading what Lio could not see beneath the planks.

“West ladder loaded,” Lio said.

“Received,” Cass called.

“West ladder loaded,” the first signal student repeated.

Blue cloth rose above the post, crossed the blind turn through the runner, then appeared at the north stair.

At the first narrowing, Lio's board caught a rope tail. He stopped before it pulled, backed out, and tied the board flat against his spine.

“Time loss,” Cass called.

“Equipment retained,” Lio answered.

At the suspended door, Arion found an old crush mark along the lower hinge. He called it. Lio chose the upper grip. They passed without transferring load into the wall.

At the white joint before the central bay, Arion knelt and put two fingers against the oak.

The planned release sat ahead of them: guide rope taut, crossed brace carrying the casualty platform, return load behind and east. A forged hook held the upper guide line against a timber cheek. Forge scale darkened the inner curve, but its surface held no open seam.

Arion pressed the joint below it. No altered pressure reached the timber or hook.

“Surface check?” Lio asked.

“No movement outside the marked release.”

“Return team ready?”

Cass raised three gloved hands behind her.

“Ready,” she called.

Lio clipped himself to the casualty line. “Release one.”

Nemi eased the guide rope.

The central brace settled by one handspan. Red marks met red marks. The casualty platform dipped west, stopped, and transferred its expected share into the return frame.

Lio smiled at no one. “Controlled settlement. Return team advance to threshold.”

Cass and the carriers moved as one. Their platform took the load with a wooden knock.

The upper guide hook split through a dark fracture inside the grain, invisible until the curved half pulled away.

Its straight half stayed against the timber cheek. The curved half snapped free.

The guide rope dropped.

The central brace turned farther than its mark.

Lio pushed the casualty clear. His right boot remained beneath the brace.

The sound he made was one Arion had heard from buildings but never from him.

“Incident,” Akamoto called. “All teams hold.”

The return platform fell half a finger and stopped crooked. Cass caught the rail. The two carriers locked their harness tails to the overhead line. Their combined weight pressed eastward through the same brace now lying across Lio's boot.

Arion opened Foundation attention through the white joint.

The failed hook had changed the route. West still carried cleanly into the party wall behind him. East ended at the turned central brace. The return platform held three people above that interruption. The brace pinned Lio at its toe and supported their inner corner at its shoulder.

If Arion moved forward, his weight would enter the central span.

If he lifted the brace through Foundation, he could take its committed load into the white joint, through his hands, ribs, hips, and the party wall. Perhaps long enough. Perhaps not.

His palms warmed against the oak.

“Arion,” Lio said.

He was on one elbow beyond the chalk. The casualty dummy lay clear. Both harness clips remained locked. His face had lost color around the mouth.

“How many on return?” Lio asked.

“Three.”

“What moves if this lifts?”

“Their inner platform drops unless it is shored from west relief.”

“Can Cass withdraw?”

Arion followed the load behind him. “Not before the outer rail is tied back. Movement would turn the brace.”

“Nearest stable joint?”

Arion looked at the white chalk beneath his knee.

“Where you are?” Lio asked.

“Yes.”

Lio put his forehead against his sleeve. “Then do your irritating lesson.”

He could harden the west joint without telling anyone. He could move one hand beyond the chalk and call the transfer local. He could lift only the brace's toe, leaving the platform shoulder untouched. Every version ended with his body holding a route no one else could see.

He released Foundation until the oak became only oak beneath his fingers.

“Stopped at west white joint,” he called. “East route interrupted at central brace. Three on return platform. One real casualty pinned. Request second team through west relief with two external shores.”

Cass repeated it from the platform.

The blue west flag rose.

Wind struck the loose guide rope. Its broken hook swung into the signal line and tore the blue cloth from the post.

The bare flag arm snapped back against the mast.

“Sight west lost,” Cass called.

The runner at the blind turn leaned around the stone corner but could not see the course through dust from the settled frame.

“Verbal relay,” Akamoto said.

Cass called, “Second team. West relief. East interrupted. Bring two external shores.”

The first signal student repeated every word to the turn.

The runner turned toward the north stair and gave the full command. The guide rope struck the roof as the route words crossed the corner. Lio's clipped casualty line rang against the rail. One of the return carriers shouted that her outer board was slipping.

The second-team leader read back from behind the grain-store wall, “Second team. East relief. West interrupted. Bring two external shores.” Wind and the loose guide line masked both route names before they reached the runner. He heard *relief*, *interrupted*, and *two shores* in their correct positions. He raised a closed fist to signal confirmation.

At the north stair, the second team began moving behind the wall.

“Route words unconfirmed,” Emilia called from the observer boundary.

The runner looked from his closed fist to the blind corner.

Their east flag rose white above the blind roof.

“Wrong route,” Cass shouted.

Akamoto reached for the black stop line.

Arion felt the second team's first boot enter the east plank.

The load travelled through the interrupted approach and struck the central brace from behind. The brace rolled the width of a grain.

Lio's breath stopped.

Then returned as a sound without a word.

Arion drove both hands against the white joint. Foundation answered before permission, a grey fan beginning beneath his palms. He stopped it at the chalk before the timber or earth hardened. The pressure already across Lio's boot remained where it was.

“Second team stop,” Akamoto called. “Withdraw east one at a time. Maintain north stair line.”

The white flag vanished.

Mera called the time to her slate.

Emilia had both hands closed around the observer rail. “East is not continuous,” she said. “They cannot see that from staging.”

“Now they know,” Cass said.

“After entering it.”

Arion counted Lio's breaths against the guide rope's swing. Four quick. One missing. Three. A long pull through his teeth.

“Sensation?” Arion asked.

“Toes present,” Lio said. “Opinion worsening.”

“Do not move them.”

“They were not taking requests.”

The second team cleared east. Akamoto sent them around the north stair to the west maintenance rack, this time with Nemi carrying the route board beside them. Each person repeated *west relief, east interrupted* before moving out of sight.

No one shortened the message.

The first shore arrived under Lio's third set of counted breaths. It was an oak post with a screw foot and a forked head. Nemi slid it from the relief slot toward Arion.

He did not cross the chalk.

“Half pace left,” he said. “Fork beneath return shoulder, not brace toe.”

Nemi adjusted. The screw foot bit into the party-wall sill.

“Take one turn.”

The post rose. Return weight moved out of the central brace by a fraction.

Cass called the platform change. “Inner rail level one.”

The second shore entered behind the first. Lio gave its position from below where Arion could not see.

“Head is against split grain,” Lio said. “Move north the width of my board.”

Nemi moved it.

“There.”

Both shores took load. Cass and the carriers withdrew to the stone threshold in harness order. When the third pair of boots left the platform, the central brace released a shallow knock against Lio's boot and settled free of the return load.

Arion checked the path twice.

“External support carries platform. Brace toe carries its own weight only.”

Akamoto knelt at the opposite maintenance opening. “Extraction authority to Anet. Arion, do you accept local lift under physician direction?”

“Yes.”

Anet entered from west relief with a low jack, two wedges, and shears. She cut Lio's laces rather than pulling the boot.

“I need that boot for the license record,” Lio said.

“You need the foot for walking,” Anet replied.

“Both can be true.”

She cut the boot down the outer seam. Lio's ankle had swollen against the leather, and the outer bone would not lie level when Anet supported his heel.

Arion fitted the jack beneath the brace toe. He raised it on Anet's count, no farther. Nemi placed the wedges. Anet drew Lio's foot back inside the ruined leather without twisting it.

Only when his heel crossed the white joint did Arion release the jack.

The infirmary occupied one level below the course. Anet suspended Lio's lower leg in a cloth cradle and wrapped it from toes to knee while the swelling continued to rise. She found sensation in each toe, a pulse at the ankle, and pain along the outer bone that stopped Lio's jokes for several breaths.

“Compression injury with a possible fracture,” she said. “No weight until I have a plate image. License trial suspended, not failed.”

“Time penalty?” Lio asked.

“Significant.”

Mera placed the incident slate where everyone could read it.

*Stop called at west white joint.*
*West relief requested; east interrupted.*
*West sight flag lost after guide-hook failure.*
*Two-way verbal readback obscured at final turn; relay signaled confirmation without distinguishable route terms.*
*Second team entered east.*
*Secondary compression observed and attributed to east approach load.*
*External shores installed from west relief.*

Emilia stood at the foot of Lio's cot. “Mounted across east after the hook failure, the witness should have shown the interruption at that mount without clearing west or deciding who should move. A paired witness or verified relay would still be necessary.”

“Should?” Cass asked.

“It remains uncertified. A failed spring could also return amber.”

“Then it might have delayed us anyway.”

“Yes.”

Akamoto took the assessor's slate from his belt.

“I barred an unvalidated interface and retained flags with a single verbal relay,” he said. “When the west flag failed, confirmation passed without distinguishable route terms. The wrong-route entry and second compression followed. That procedure was mine.”

He wrote beneath the incident times.

*Divided-access trials suspended. Reopening requires two-way route confirmation visible to all active teams. Any aid reports route state only and carries no decision authority. Failure or ambiguity stops entry.*

He signed before handing the slate to Lio.

Lio read it with his injured leg hanging between them.

“My trial?” he asked.

“Remains interrupted by a course incident.”

“My route choice was west.”

“Recorded.”

“My candidate response after the hook failed?”

“You moved the casualty clear, retained both clips, reported support facts, and gave a correct stop condition while pinned.”

“Record that too.”

Akamoto did.

Arion stood beside the cut boot. Dust had settled inside it around the shape Lio's foot had left.

“Was the stop correct?” he asked.

Lio closed his eyes through a spasm, then flattened Akamoto's slate against his ribs.

“Your stop kept Cass's platform level,” he said. “The east team put the brace down again. If you need one sentence, choose one after my foot stops trying to bite me.”

Arion picked up the boot by its severed laces and carried it back to the upper course for the incident layout.

No one had crossed out the chalk beneath the west joint. His knee mark remained on the safe side. Beyond it, the broken guide hook lay beside Akamoto's amendment, and Lio's empty boot could no longer stand upright without a hand inside it.
