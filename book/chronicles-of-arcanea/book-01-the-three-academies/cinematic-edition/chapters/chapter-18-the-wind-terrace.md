# The wind terrace

Emilia had hidden her old brass teaching strip beneath the bread, and the bread had dented it.

The damage was slight. One corner of the tin plate had pressed a shallow curve into the strip's outer cover. It would not affect the shutters. The strip had belonged to her since apprenticeship and had never received a live state. That did not excuse carrying it like a spare plate.

She set the food basket on the wind-terrace table with the damaged side down.

“You brought a tool under lunch,” Lio said.

He lay on a long bench with his lower leg elevated in a sling made from sailcloth. Fresh binding covered the splint from toes to knee. Someone had cut the right trouser leg away and wrapped the remaining cloth around his thigh in a knot too large to ignore. His rescue board rested against the bench within reach of his left hand. A blue-bordered course-service excerpt had been fastened inside it under Akamoto's local-custodian seal.

“It is an inert teaching aid.”

“Did you bring food?”

“The tool was beneath it.”

“That answers the order, not the quantity.”

Mera came through the terrace door carrying three cups between the fingers of one hand. The external cradle at her belt knocked lightly against the doorframe. Red disputed-custody thread crossed the sealed vial. A new white slip had been clipped to the frame without touching either seal.

Lio read the slip before he looked at the cups. “You have been formally made less correct.”

“Only less recognizable.”

“Convenient distinction.”

“I have found it expensive.”

She set the cups beside Emilia's basket. Two held pepper broth. The third held something pale with a skin thick enough to resist the wind.

Emilia removed the bread, wrapped cheese, and a tin of stewed beans. The yellow patch over her healing cuff caught on the basket handle. Only after the teaching strip lay visible between them did she look at Lio's binding.

“How does it feel?” she asked.

“You examined the splint before my face.”

“The splint has changed.”

“So has my face.”

It had. The color had returned around his mouth, but not evenly. Lack of sleep pulled one eyelid lower than the other. A muscle moved beside his jaw whenever the terrace wind lifted the loose end of his bandage.

“You are in pain,” Emilia said.

“Anet has recorded it with better instruments.”

“May we stay?” Mera asked.

Lio looked at the basket, the three cups, and the brass strip. “The order says I am not to participate in the active safety inquiry while receiving treatment. It also says the terrace is open to visitors and I retain amendment rights over my candidate record.”

He tapped the board beside him.

“If this is a visit, sit down. If it is an inquiry, leave the beans.”

Emilia sat.

The terrace occupied a level between the infirmary and the upper practice roofs. Its western side opened toward the city behind a waist-high wall. The eastern side faced the Roost's interior courts. Shutters had been closed over the view of the lower fields. Above the table, a weather vane made from two thin metal leaves had lost its north leaf and struck its spindle whenever the wind changed.

It struck twice while Mera divided the bread.

Emilia placed her bounded assembly-chamber state copy beside the teaching strip. The copy had been issued after the withdrawal because her name appeared as local operator. It carried times, conductor colors, and state changes. The lines for destination and source identity were blank. A dark bar covered the joint inquiry field.

Lio looked at the blank areas. “That appears designed to make a person curious.”

“It is designed to give a local operator the state she was responsible for without transferring sealed inquiry material.”

“Did it work?”

“I know less than the service and more than the room.”

Mera opened her incident slate to the rescue-course sequence. The suspension slip on her vial frame clicked against the table edge.

“This is my personal incident copy,” she said. “I can compare its times. I cannot give the result Athenaeum-recognized witness weight.”

“I am not asking either of you to find anything for the inquiry,” Lio said. “My trial record says a hook failed, a route message failed, and my boot required structural disassembly. It does not yet say why I called west or what I saw after the brace came down.”

“Akamoto recorded your west route,” Emilia said.

“On the assessor's incident slate. Future license assessors receive my candidate file. At present, that file receives the words *trial suspended after injury*.”

He pulled the board onto his chest.

“They will see the injury. They will see the suspension. They will not see the correct route call unless I amend it.”

Mera put her pencil beside his hand rather than offering it to him.

“Begin with what you observed,” she said.

Emilia reached for the brass strip.

“The upper guide hook fractured—”

“No,” Lio said.

She stopped.

He pointed at the three cups. “Which one is me?”

Mera moved the pepper broth nearest his bench. She gave the second pepper broth to the return team and the pale one to the staged second team.

“Why do they get the bad cup?” Lio asked.

“It was nearer Emilia.”

Emilia smelled it. “Barley milk.”

“Still defensible,” Lio said.

He placed his own cup west of a crack in the table. The return cup went east. The second team waited above them near the broken vane.

“My route choice was west before the hook failed,” he said. “Arion confirmed that east shared the return brace. Akamoto accepted west. The candidate file begins there.”

Emilia moved the brass strip back into its cover.

Mera wrote *accepted west route precedes incident*.

“I began with material failure,” Emilia said.

“You began where your part became interesting.”

“That is not the same as being wrong.”

“It moved the first correct decision outside the model.”

Mera turned her pencil over and inserted a new first line above Emilia's.

“You agree with him,” Emilia said.

“I enjoy an accurately placed beginning.”

The guide hook had struck the sight line after it broke. They needed an object long enough to connect the first signal post to the blind turn. Mera offered a clean strip of bandage from her sleeve pocket. Lio rejected the first arrangement because Emilia ran it directly from candidate to second team.

“There was a runner,” he said. “If you remove the person who could have asked for the route words again, you make the failure automatic.”

Emilia moved the broken weather vane from its socket and laid it at the table's blind corner. Rust marked the stone where the missing north leaf had scraped for years. The vane served as runner, guide rope, and signal arm in turn. It proved nothing except that the terrace keeper tolerated bad maintenance.

They reconstructed the call.

Mera drew the full words on separate scraps: *west relief; east interrupted; two external shores*. She folded the route terms beneath the bandage where the table crack made the blind turn.

On the second side only *relief; interrupted; two shores* remained visible.

Emilia put two fingers on the vane. “The runner receives the correct positions and cannot distinguish the route names.”

“He raises confirmation,” Lio said.

“The last verified route remains west.”

“Not at the north stair. Their last received route was east.”

Emilia moved the pale cup toward the east side of the crack.

“Sight shows east,” Mera said. “Akamoto pulls stop. Entry has already begun.”

“That is where the brace came down a second time,” Lio said.

His voice flattened on the final words. One hand tightened around the edge of the candidate board. The movement shook the elevated sling, and the muscle beside his jaw began again.

Mera waited without writing.

Emilia wanted to correct the sling's angle. She did not touch it.

Lio breathed until his fingers released the board.

“Continue,” he said.

Emilia opened the teaching strip.

It contained five brass shutters in a narrow row. Each had a manual tab beneath it: source state, confirmed route, receiving team, release, repair. Apprentices used the strip before they were permitted near a live continuity service. No shutter moved unless a hand moved its tab.

She set source to west. Confirmed route opened west. Receiving team opened. The release and repair shutters remained closed.

Then she lifted her finger from source state.

Nothing else changed.

“The confirmed route holds,” Lio said.

“Loss of input does not equal release,” Emilia said. “The rule prevents a service from dropping an active route because one observer or one connection fails.”

“Useful during an actual rescue,” Mera said.

“Until the held state is wrong.”

Emilia moved the receiving-team shutter to east, matching the mistaken readback. The strip held west at the first route and east at the receiver. Neither side released because no release instruction had arrived. Both states appeared internally valid.

Lio ate a piece of bread without lifting his head. “That is our mistake made from better metal.”

Emilia turned to her assembly-chamber state copy.

The first page began with the assembly-chamber refusal. Local Foundation, local release, and local indicator remained cold. Remote continuity and source recovery remained active. The copy did not show what those services retained, where they led, or which source they served.

She kept her finger outside the dark inquiry bar.

“The chamber gives us a later behavioral analogy,” she said. “Remote service continued after refusal while every local channel in the room stayed cold.”

Mera read the state labels twice. “Shared behavior does not establish destination, source, or Hollow Root identity.”

“Agreed.”

“The Athenaeum currently disagrees about the value of my clarity.”

Lio raised the bread. “The bread recognizes you.”

Mera took it from him and tore off the crisp edge.

Lio unfastened the blue-bordered excerpt from his candidate board.

The header named Akamoto as local course custodian. Permitted fields followed: incident time, local course service class, retention or release state, custodian seal. Destination and source identity were excluded. No subject or inquiry field appeared.

*Retention state entered.*

Eight lines later, after the west shores took the return load:

*Release received. Retention state repaired.*

Mera aligned the times with her slate.

The first fell between the wrong-route entry and the second compression. The second followed Cass's report that the return rail had levelled.

“There was a service transition during the trial,” Emilia said.

Lio stopped chewing.

“No destination,” Mera said.

“No named local source,” Emilia answered. “But the times agree with an entry and release not listed on the course plan.”

“I heard it,” Lio said.

The wind pressed his loose bandage end against the sling. He freed it with one hand and tucked it beneath the binding.

“When east came on, something latched below the brace. Not the hook. Lower and farther north. I thought it was the course counter.”

“There is no course counter at the central brace,” Emilia said.

“I know that now.”

He pulled the candidate board closer and turned it over. On the blank back he drew the crossed brace, the white stop joint, and the position of his trapped foot. His lines were less steady than the ones on the signed face.

“Here,” he said, marking a point below the return shoulder. “A copper tag under the lower rail. Two cuts open toward one return stroke. I saw it because the brace turned. After the east team withdrew, the latch remained under tension. It released when the first shore took the rail.”

“Second shore,” Mera said.

Lio looked at her.

She checked the incident times. “The first shore levelled the inner rail by one. Full return load moved after the second shore.”

“The sound changed after the first.”

“The logged release follows the second.”

He stared at his drawing. “Then I heard load change, not release.”

Mera crossed out nothing. She wrote the correction beneath his first account and turned the slate so he could see it.

Lio read both lines. “Keep both.”

“Yes.”

Emilia lifted the teaching strip. On its underside, beside her former training sponsor's instruction mark, the retention-return class used two open cuts toward a single closing stroke. Lio's proportions were uneven. The direction was the same.

She placed it beside his sketch without covering either.

“This tag class belongs to retention-return service,” she said. “The notation format was used in my former sponsor's training system.”

“Beneath the rescue course?” Lio asked.

“Your observation places the tag beneath the course. Akamoto's excerpt corroborates operation at the incident times. Neither record tells us where the line goes.”

Mera set down her pencil. “Strongest claim?”

Emilia moved the pale cup back to the north stair and straightened the bandage between all three positions.

“An unlisted retention-return line operated during the rescue incident. Its protocol form matches a former-sponsor Forge training system. Destination, owner, purpose, and connection to the sealed lower works are not established.”

“Did it break the hook?” Lio asked.

Emilia looked at the candidate board. “Not supported.”

Lio nodded once. “Then write what I saw.”

Mera began a fresh sheet.

“Personal witness note?” she asked.

“Candidate amendment,” Lio said.

“If the line is judged part of the inquiry, Akamoto's restriction—”

“My amendment does not need your line.” He tapped each fact with one finger. “I selected west. I saw the tag after the brace turned. I heard the latch at wrong-route entry. I first mistook load change for release. Those belong to my trial.”

Mera looked at the suspension slip clipped to her vial frame.

“Future assessors should receive your correction as well as the original account,” she said.

She pushed the blank sheet toward him.

Lio wrote slowly. His first line sloped down because the board shifted against his chest. Emilia placed one hand beneath its far edge. She did not touch the writing surface.

He entered the accepted west route, the tag's location and visible form, the first latch sound, and his corrected distinction between the first-shore load change and the second-shore release time. Hollow Root and sponsor attribution remained outside his amendment. He signed as candidate.

Emilia used a separate sheet.

She cited Akamoto's course-service excerpt for the two timestamped transitions, her inert strip for the former-sponsor notation, and the assembly-chamber copy only as a later example of the hold-state rule. Her conclusion stopped at protocol match and unlisted operation. She signed as maker-observer.

Mera wrote a third note. The white suspension slip struck the table twice while she worked. She attributed the incident times to her personal copy and listed the claims the three records could not make. At the bottom she wrote *participant witness, Athenaeum recognition suspended* and signed anyway.

They laid the three sheets beside one another without fastening them together.

“The candidate amendment goes to the candidate registry,” Mera said. “Your maker attribution may enter the safety tray. Mine can accompany it as a personal limit note; Athenaeum recognition remains suspended.”

Lio read his page once more. “Good.”

“It may affect your route access,” Emilia said.

“My route access currently ends at the terrace stair.”

“After treatment.”

He looked past her toward the practice roofs. “After treatment, an assessor may decide that seeing an unlisted service tag makes me useful to the inquiry and inconvenient to a course.”

“Do you still want it entered?” Mera asked.

“I still want the part before the injury to exist.”

He handed her the signed amendment.

The work was complete. Emilia closed the teaching strip and returned it to the basket with its dent facing upward. Mera slid the three cups away from the papers without disturbing their route. The broken vane went back into its socket.

Emilia stood.

Lio pulled the rescue manual from beneath his pillow. Several page corners had been folded into narrow route arrows.

“The next license trial uses river access,” he said. “If the examination office follows its own delay schedule, I will have twelve days between walking clearance and assessment.”

“That is insufficient,” Emilia said.

“I know.”

“You cannot rebuild load tolerance in twelve days.”

“Anet used fourteen.”

Mera sat again. “You have begun negotiating with a schedule no one has offered.”

“It is better than negotiating with soup.”

The terrace door remained open behind Emilia. Nothing on the table required her. The former-sponsor attribution was signed. The strip was packed. Lio's board no longer tilted because Mera had wedged the folded bean cloth beneath one corner.

Emilia sat again too.

They argued over the river examination until the wind cooled the beans and Lio admitted that one folded page described a route abolished three years earlier. Mera divided the remaining cheese into three unequal pieces and denied doing so. Above them, the broken vane ticked against its spindle. The cooling cups held their places on Lio's signed board.
