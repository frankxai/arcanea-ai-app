# An absence with edges

The Athenaeum's price for certainty was a glass cradle exactly the size of Mera's vial.

It waited on the source-access desk between Neris and the black pool door. Clear clips stood open at four points. A white custody thread lay beside them, long enough to cross the blue glass twice.

Mera kept her own external cradle attached to her belt. Its red thread passed over the sealed vial without touching it. The amber and black seals faced outward. Behind it, in a separate document sleeve, she carried the assembly-chamber refusal copy. The folded meal story rested against the opposite side of the sleeve.

No paper touched another.

Neris placed the access terms on the dry stone.

“Full source comparison,” she said. “The Athenaeum receives temporary custody of the rain-memory. One named source reader opens only the engineered interval. The joint safety inquiry owns the authenticated interpretation. You receive a sealed derivative after review.”

“Maximum custody?” Mera asked.

“None fixed. Reading completion, or an inquiry finding that opening is unsafe. The panel may extend with the source reader's concurrence.”

“And if I dispute your interpretation?”

“You may enter a dissent. You may not publish independently while the inquiry remains active.”

The terms were not fraudulent. A named reader was safer than an unknown hand. Inquiry custody could keep the missing voice from becoming public spectacle. Authentication required something stronger than Mera recognizing a shape and wanting the recognition to matter.

The empty cradle still had four open clips.

“No,” Mera said.

Neris folded the access terms once. “Then the hammer mark, the rain absence, and the catalogue gap remain unlinked.”

“Unauthenticated.”

“For a safety inquiry, the distinction has consequences.”

“For the source, so does custody without an end.”

Neris looked at the red thread on Mera's cradle. “Your recognized witness standing is provisional. Withholding a relevant source during an active inquiry may suspend it.”

“My personal witness signatures remain in the record.”

“Yes. They may carry no Athenaeum weight.”

Mera set the sealed participant copy on the desk. Its outer page contained the Roost refusal and response times and a drawn boundary of the mark observed inside Arion's hammer seam. Arion's refusal appeared above it. Mera's source label appeared below.

Beside the drawing line, Arion had authorized the boundary for attributed public comparison. All other tool and chamber data remained excluded.

“This document is authorized for me to carry,” she said. “The geometry is part of the incident record. I request the public derivative catalogues for the Hollow Root restoration interval.”

“On what index?”

Mera knew four numbers hidden inside the folded meal story. She did not look toward it.

“Public service class, living structural field, restoration record with missing handoff.”

Neris's gaze moved from Mera's face to the closed document sleeve. Neither of them spoke.

“That search returns thirty-one catalogues.”

“Then I will begin with thirty-one.”

The public reference hall contained no water deeper than a drinking cup. Blue glass bands occupied padded racks along the inner wall. Paper abstracts from the Luminary Archive lay flat inside pale boxes. Forge indexes hung as copper foils from wooden spines so their impressed cells could be read without polishing.

Every derivative carried a public source number. None carried private memory.

Mera began with the oldest living-field catalogue and worked forward.

By the sixth record, her burned palm had begun leaving warmth on the measuring rule. By the eleventh, the tea at her elbow had cooled enough to show a skin. The vial did not move inside its cradle. The meal story remained closed.

At the fourteenth catalogue, she found the first interval.

An Athenaeum band of blue glass recorded a shared-source restoration in silver cells. Four positions near the final handoff had been cut shallow and filled with neutral wax. The filler was too even for damage. Its edges formed a blunt top, an irregular right side, and a narrow return below.

Mera placed the authorized drawing of the hammer mark beside it.

Mera began the notation for a demonstrated match, then stopped after its first stroke. She crossed it out and wrote the observation mark instead.

She entered the catalogue number on a blank public-note sheet and wrote: *Observed geometric resemblance. No source identity established.*

The Luminary derivative appeared five boxes later.

Its paper abstract had no wax. A narrow section had been removed with a blade, then replaced by a blank strip stitched at top and bottom. The surrounding fibers remained pale. The blank was older than the latest binding and younger than the paper it interrupted.

Mera counted the source positions before and after it.

Four were missing.

The Forge foil used neither cutting nor replacement. Four impressed cells had been heated and planished until their marks flattened into the copper. The planished area followed a stepped edge because the Forge index grouped positions by tool action rather than by source sequence.

Mera aligned all three beneath a transparent public measuring grid.

Blue wax, white paper, and copper flattened to dull orange sat beneath the grid. Their upper boundaries, right edges, and narrow lower returns appeared to meet.

“They do not,” Iven said.

He stood at the end of the table holding two salt cakes inside their paper wrapper. His hair was still damp from morning practice. A loop of public-copy cord crossed his shoulder.

Mera kept one finger on the measuring grid. “The boundaries share four source positions.”

“Your Forge zero is wrong.”

“The lower edge carries the first cell.”

“The lower edge carries the first cell after rebinding. The original zero is the triangular repair notch.”

He pointed without touching the foil. A small notch sat three rows above the wooden spine, easy to mistake for a hanging mark.

Mera moved the grid.

The perfect match broke.

The Forge omission shifted half a paper interval right and one blue-glass position down.

For several seconds she wanted Iven to be less correct.

He put one salt cake beside her cold tea, still inside the wrapper. “I can leave if that would improve the conversion.”

“It would improve the room.”

“The conversion?”

“Would remain wrong.”

Iven pulled a chair to the opposite side of the table. He did not ask what the records concerned. Public source labels told him enough; the external red thread told him where not to ask.

They converted each catalogue back to the shared-source sequence.

Athenaeum bands counted individual custody positions. Luminary abstracts grouped two positions beneath one maintenance heading. Forge foils assigned a cell to each material action, then marked unchanged source positions with a shallow dot. The three records could not be laid edge to edge and expected to tell the truth.

Mera ate half the salt cake while recalculating the Luminary grouping. It tasted of salt, fennel, and the paper wrapper she had failed to remove far enough. Iven finished his before contributing anything useful.

“The paper cut begins between grouped positions,” he said. “You have assigned the whole heading to the absence.”

“The stitch crosses both.”

“The stitch repairs paper. It does not restore source order.”

Mera changed the note.

The hammer-mark drawing no longer matched any derivative exactly.

The blue-glass wax removed source positions forty-one through forty-four. The paper strip removed the maintenance heading containing those same positions and preserved the adjacent text. The Forge foil planished the three material actions attached to forty-one through forty-four while leaving its unchanged-position dots visible before and after.

Each institution had altered its own material at positions forty-one through forty-four.

“Accidental damage?” Mera asked.

Iven examined the glass first. “Wax was placed.”

The paper. “Blank was stitched.”

The foil. “Copper was reheated.”

“Independent local decisions?”

“Possible.”

“All selecting the same four source positions?”

“Possible in the way three people may independently choose the same bad marginal argument.”

“You chose mine twice last meal.”

“Because it was available.”

Mera turned to the transfer fields.

Each derivative ended the restoration interval without a named handoff. The glass carried neutral wax where a source keeper should have entered. The paper line jumped from active repair to later inspection. The copper foil resumed with a new material state and no operator mark.

The custody layers established the order of work. Wax lay over the tail of the Athenaeum intake scratch. Two cut loops of original Luminary binding thread remained beneath the blank strip. Reheating had softened one edge of the Forge receipt punch. Each derivative had entered separate institutional custody before its four positions were altered.

Beside each alteration sat a local execution mark. Their institutional prefixes differed. Their joint-order suffix was the same.

Mera's attributed claim stopped there: three institutions had altered their separate derivatives after receipt, at the same shared-source positions, and had omitted the same custody handoff. The record did not identify the removed content, actor, motive, source identity, or any connection to the buried response at the Roost.

Mera wrote the claim in one paragraph.

She credited Iven's repair-notch correction beneath it.

“Do you accept contributing interpretation?” she asked.

“No.”

“You changed the result.”

“I corrected a zero. The conclusion is yours.”

“Your name remains on the correction.”

“My name may remain attached to my act.”

Mera finished the salt cake. “Unreasonably consistent.”

“I have been practicing.”

Neris arrived before the public note dried.

She read the conversion table, repeated Iven's zero-point correction, and inspected each physical alteration through the gallery lenses. She spent longest with the Forge foil.

“This establishes common source coordinates,” she said.

“And separate deliberate removal in each derivative.”

“Yes.”

“Cross-institutional coordination.”

“At the level of the removal instruction.”

Mera added that limit to the claim.

“It authenticates neither the hammer mark against the catalogue source nor the Roost response as Hollow Root,” Neris said.

“Both exclusions are in the claim.”

“It will be cited as if it does.”

“Then the citation will be wrong.”

Neris set the source-access terms beside the public note. “Assign the comparison to the inquiry. Deposit the vial. I will attach this structural finding and request immediate authentication.”

For one breath, Mera saw the result with institutional weight. The hammer mark, the rain absence, the three catalogues, and the buried path could enter one named finding before the council moved Arion again. Her witness standing would survive. The Athenaeum would have to listen when she objected to the next measurement.

The empty glass cradle waited at the source desk beyond the hall.

“No,” she said.

Neris carried the public note to the attributed tray herself. She stamped it *structural coordination established; source authentication absent*, pressed a citation seal beside Mera's name, and made the authorized duplicate. The duplicate entered the active safety shelf. The first remained public under Mera's name and Iven's credited correction.

Then Neris opened a narrow suspension slip.

“Athenaeum recognition of participant-witness standing is suspended pending source cooperation or inquiry review,” she read. “Prior signatures remain in their original records. Personal witness statements may still be entered and will be marked unrecognized.”

She signed the decision with her own name.

The registry clerk clipped the suspension slip to Mera's external transport frame. He did not touch the vial, its seals, or the red disputed-custody thread.

Across the table, the three altered catalogues remained aligned by shared-source position rather than by their edges. Blue wax, stitched white paper, and planished copper each held the same four places empty.

Beside them, the red thread still crossed Mera's sealed vial. Neris's signature showed through the suspension slip clipped to the frame.
