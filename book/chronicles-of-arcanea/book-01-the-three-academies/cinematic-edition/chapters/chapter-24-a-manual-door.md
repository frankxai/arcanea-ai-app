# A manual door

The weighted block reached the second chalk mark before the collar caught it.

Two polished pins crossed the test gap with one hard report. The block struck them, lifted against its guide, and stopped a handspan short of the line that separated the west court from the infirmary walk.

Emilia wrote *pass* before the recoil finished travelling through the frame.

Tavin kept his hand on the certified lever. He had replaced his wet morning work coat with one that smelled of hot pressing cloth, but the yellow patch at his shoulder remained. A clear guard covered his credential ring where it entered the collar's control plate.

“Outward load contained,” he said.

Neris repeated the result into the council slate word for word. The block had carried enough weight to take a knee sideways, and the collar had stopped it before the public line.

Akamoto walked the chalk edge once. He tested neither the pins nor the block. His attention stayed on the empty space between them.

Arion stood at the infirmary-side observation mark with his right forearm splinted to his chest. The binding around his ribs showed only when he inhaled. Two fingers remained curled above the end of the splint.

Mera waited beside him with a plain record board. Her two red-threaded vessels hung at her hip, closed and covered.

Tavin released the test load. The pins withdrew into the collar without scraping.

It was beautiful equipment. The outer frame met the Roost's scarred stone without a visible shim. The response fork sat protected behind clear mineral glass. Its load scale could distinguish the fall of a tool from the rush of a body and the rush of a body from the first movement of a wall. If the unknown below broke outward, the pins would arrive before anyone at the court could choose badly.

Every tolerance held.

She set down her pencil.

“One occupied-gap cycle.”

Neris lowered the council slate. “It is not in the order.”

“The order proposes contact.”

“The order proposes containment.”

“Across the same gap.”

Tavin looked at Emilia's hands. “What are you putting through it?”

She drew the inert teaching strip from the rigid sleeve inside her coat. It was narrower than two fingers and had no active material left in it. A fine instruction mark near one end belonged to Renn. The other end carried the three shallow positions Emilia had cut during the incident revision: retained, withdrawn, refused.

The strip was the only object she had carried out of the Forge that afternoon besides the read-only receipt.

Tavin touched the clear guard over his credential ring.

“It will cut.”

“I know.”

“That is not a test material.”

“It is flexible, it crosses the full gap, and it can occupy both sides without striking the pins first.”

Neris looked from the strip to the stopped block. “The result will not establish injury.”

“No.”

“Or life.”

“No.”

“Or that the response below resembles either.”

“No.” Emilia placed the strip through the open collar. “It will show where the mechanism closes.”

Tavin waited until she had stepped behind the chalk. He checked her hands, Akamoto's position, and the clear court beyond the safety line. Then he reset the load vane.

“Test space clear.”

Mera looked across every visible body before she answered. “Clear.”

Tavin cycled the collar.

The pins met through the strip.

The sound was smaller than the block's impact. One dry cut. Renn's instruction mark stayed on the far side. Emilia's three positions remained on the near.

The collar held both halves perfectly.

Neris entered the second pass.

No one spoke until Tavin isolated the control plate, removed his ring, and opened the guard by hand. He withdrew the pins. Emilia collected the first half from the court side and the second from the test frame. She held them separately.

She did not fit the cut edges together.

“It stopped the block,” Neris said.

“Yes.”

“That block would have crossed the public line.”

“Yes.”

“The same speed that cut your strip is the speed that protected this court.”

Emilia put both pieces on the outer workbench, leaving a thumb's width between them. “I wrote the pass.”

Neris's gaze moved to the lower wall hidden behind the collar's closed shutter. “Then you understand why the Academies require it.”

“I understand what it protects.”

Akamoto stepped into the test space after Tavin called the certified apparatus isolated. “And what it closes across.”

“Possibly nothing,” Neris said. “The response is unauthenticated.”

“Then there is nothing to trap,” Akamoto replied. “Remove the automatic closure.”

Neris turned the council slate so he could see its active seal. “The order governs certification and lower-approach admission. Remove the pins and the Academy test ends.”

“This is my exterior wall.”

“Your exterior trial. Not the lower approach.”

“Record the distinction.”

She did.

Neris set a termination mark beneath the official test. “The collar passed two cycles. Certified operation ends here.”

Tavin signed under operator. He isolated the control yoke, capped its supply, and withdrew his credential ring. A brass release tag went through the exterior frame under Akamoto's mark. The certified control head stayed in Tavin's custody; the bare support passed into local alteration scope.

Tavin unlocked the pin housing. The left spring released harder than its indicator predicted. Akamoto caught it against his gloved palm before it could strike the frame, then lowered the coiled metal to the bench.

Tavin caged the second spring before he lifted it free. Emilia steadied the released frame with her left hand. The yellow repair at her other cuff pulled around the healing Prism burn when she turned her wrist.

Mera reached for the response fork's custody tag.

“Not failed,” Emilia said.

Mera wrote *removed, not failed* and tied the tag through the fork before Tavin lifted it from the frame.

The threshold scale remained.

Emilia put one finger against its edge. Without the pins, it could become a passive record. No closure. No decision. A clean trace of whatever moved against the tongue.

She looked at Arion.

“Nothing attached to me,” he said. “Nothing copied from me.”

“The scale would be attached to the frame.”

“And set because I am standing here.”

The answer irritated the part of her that knew the difference between direct and proxy measurement. It irritated her more because the rest of her knew exactly how the resulting line would be used.

“Remove it,” she said.

Tavin lifted the scale free.

Tavin detached the remote return. The load vane, comparison teeth, and protected signal wafer followed without ceremony. By the time the control line went grey on Neris's slate, the polished parts filled one side of the bench. Nothing in the pile was broken.

The remaining frame looked unfinished.

An oak support stood on exterior anchors that Pell had verified before the inquiry walk failed. A broad brass shutter crossed the hand-wide contact gap. Its hinge turned a flat state vane: unbroken face when the leaf was shut, diagonal groove while it moved, round aperture when it cleared the opening. A separate lever lowered an unmarked tongue of flexible brass toward the inner stone. No scale touched it. No spring returned it. No line left the court.

Emilia tested the shutter by hand, then stopped.

“Separate operators.”

Akamoto took the shutter lever. His glove left a dark crescent on the grip.

Emilia moved to the tongue station behind its chip shield. The lever fitted badly beneath her right hand. She wrapped the yellow cuff once around her wrist and found a position that did not pull the burn.

Mera watched the state vane. “I can time the leaf. I won't read the wall.”

“No Water observation,” Emilia said.

“No Water observation.”

Akamoto opened a local sheet beside Neris's record. He entered *exterior structural trial; no entry authorized*. Beneath it he named his own shutter station, Emilia's tongue station, Mera's state record, Arion's stop authority, and Neris's institutional observation. Tavin appeared only under the completed certified cycles.

Mera ruled five separate boxes down the sheet.

Arion came no closer than the infirmary-side chalk.

Emilia signed for the leaf, hinge, and tongue. Nothing below the exterior anchors entered her maker statement. She imagined Renn circling the stripped frame and stopping at the empty pin housing. The burn pulled beneath her cuff.

Akamoto cleared the court behind them. A pair of exterior shields covered the recoil paths. No one stood within the arc of the shutter or the brass tongue. He checked the chalk himself, then took his lever.

“Local trial,” he said.

Neris raised her slate. “Observed. Not Academy authorized.”

Mera set the time point. “Shutter shut.”

“Shut,” Akamoto read back.

He drew the lever toward him.

The broad leaf moved sideways into the frame. Its vane turned. The diagonal groove appeared.

“Moving,” Mera called.

“Moving.”

The leaf reached its stop. The round aperture settled into view.

“Clear.”

“Clear.”

Behind the frame, the buried face remained closed. Rain grit had dried in the old seam, showing no break wide enough to take a blade. Six years of exterior seals continued across it without a cut.

Emilia laid her left hand on the tongue lever. The repaired cuff on her other wrist rested against the shield.

“Offering contact.”

No one repeated the phrase. It was not a state.

She lowered the lever until the brass tongue crossed the empty gap. Its end touched the buried surface and bent under its own slight weight. There was no calibrated pressure to meet and no mark to align.

“Tongue resting,” she said. “Unfastened.”

Mera entered the words.

Nothing moved.

The collar parts on the bench gave back small cooling sounds. Somewhere above the screen, a basin knocked once against an infirmary sill. Arion shifted his left foot inside the observation square. His splinted arm did not leave his chest.

Emilia could improve the contact with half a turn.

The tongue had settled closer to its lower edge than she intended. A half turn would distribute the load. Another would remove the visible tremor from the strip. She knew three ways to make the offer look more deliberate and two ways to make a resulting movement easier to repeat.

The severed teaching strip lay in two pieces beside the silent threshold scale.

Emilia left the lever alone.

A grain of dried mortar fell from the sealed face.

Then another.

The brass tongue straightened.

Not because Emilia raised it. The surface beneath its end had moved away.

“Inner stone moving,” Mera said.

The state vane stayed clear; its hinge had not moved.

A vertical line opened where no joint had been visible. The inner stone withdrew by the width of a nail, paused, and moved inward again. Dust came through the gap first. It carried the smell of wet mineral, old iron, and the dry underside of roots.

Emilia's hand found the tongue lever.

“Withdrawing.”

She lifted until the flexible brass cleared the opening and lay flat inside its guide.

Mera checked the end against the outer stop. “Tongue withdrawn.”

“Withdrawn,” Emilia confirmed.

Akamoto held the shutter lever clear.

The stone continued inward.

Only stone moved, by ordinary increments—a finger, a palm, the breadth of Emilia's yellow cuff. Its mass made less noise than Tavin's pin housing.

Neris looked first at the frame, then at Tavin's isolated control plate, then at Arion behind the chalk.

His left hand hung open at his side. The two exposed fingers of his right remained contracted above the splint. He had touched nothing.

The buried door stopped at a shoulder's width.

Darkness continued below the first tread, pale mineral flakes trembling at its edge.

Emilia saw the plate on the inner edge.

It was not decorative. Three authority impressions had been driven through one strip of blackened brass, each at a depth meant to survive resurfacing. Mineral accretion crossed plate and stone together. Emilia had filed components under one of those marks and submitted joint work beneath all three.

“Visible plate,” she said.

Mera changed pages. She did not open the black-thread folio on the restricted shelf above. The new sheet carried its reference number and the original opening time.

“Name only what you see,” she said.

Emilia leaned far enough to read the impressions without crossing the shutter plane.

“Luminary Academy authority. Abyssal Athenaeum authority. Forge authority. One joint plate. Purpose and date not established.”

Mera wrote the sequence. Neris stepped to the witness angle, compared the marks against her council slate, and added her own observation without touching the frame.

“The external-origin statement requires contradiction,” she said.

The safety order remained active on her slate.

Akamoto took black thread from the local record case. He passed it through his existing hold mark and the new supplement, then wrote the same expiry time that governed the sealed report. No new clock began. Mera added her personal local-witness mark beside his. Her Athenaeum field remained blank.

The open stone had no latch.

The visible hinge line ran free, and Emilia's frame stood clear of the inner leaf. That morning the lower route had closed as soon as the final body cleared.

She compared the inner edge with the fixed marks on the exterior frame. A moment ago it had stood clear of the first scratch. Now grey stone covered half of it.

“Inner wall moving outward,” Mera said. She entered the visible change without a duration.

Tavin looked toward Neris's active order. “The containment team is still at the east screen.”

“They do not have lower admission,” Akamoto said.

“Neither do we,” Neris replied.

Akamoto took one step toward the threshold and stopped before the shutter plane.

“My authority ends here.”

Arion looked past him into the unlit stair. “Then the next terms start here.”

No one crossed.

The returning edge covered the rest of the first scratch.

“Tongue remains withdrawn,” Mera said.

“Withdrawn,” Emilia confirmed.

Akamoto drew the shutter lever toward him.

“Moving,” Mera called.

“Moving.”

Across the narrowing opening, the stone door still stood open inward. Three worn Academy marks faced the court from its inner edge. Nothing stood on the first stair.
