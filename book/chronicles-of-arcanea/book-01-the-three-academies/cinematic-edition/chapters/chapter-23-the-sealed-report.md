# The sealed report

Rain found the word *five* before Mera decided whether it belonged to her.

One drop fell through the wind-terrace eaves and struck the public abstract. Ink spread into the paper fibers around the line she had written.

*Five recovered through the west return. Two protected vessels preserved under named custody.*

Her personal mark already sat beneath it.

Mera moved the page away from the edge of the table. She set a dry weight on each corner and watched the dark bloom stop short of her signature.

The record room opened onto the infirmary court on one side and the damaged service court on the other. Below the outer rail, the west hatch had disappeared behind a temporary stone screen. The east stair remained folded into the lower walk. Rainwater passed over the broken central floor and vanished where the passive rig had fallen.

Inside, the record had four physical forms.

A thin white notice waited beside Mera's wet draft. A brass-edged rule strip lay across Lio's old route board. The rescue account filled a heavy folio pierced for black seal thread. Anet kept the translucent medical sheet face down beneath her palm.

Mera's red-threaded vial and derived cup stayed in their frame at her hip, off the table.

She turned to the restricted folio.

Five names. Five exit positions. Two vessels. Four abandoned derivatives. One lost duplicate case. One lost rig. The preliminary mechanical sequence occupied three lines: cistern pressure had parted the corroded gate pin, water had entered the old seam, and the decayed brace had sheared under ordinary load. Inspection remained pending. No field or model act appeared in that chain. The response line carried a single word: *unverified*.

Arion's line read only:

*Structural exposure at west seam. Method—*

The next field was empty.

So was the private witness line.

Mera looked back at her marked abstract. She had written the route, the count, and the protected records before asking which of those facts the people inside them wanted made public. If the Academy accepted the finding, her name would stand beside the first bounded account of the buried response. The same name currently stood beside an accessed trace and a suspended Athenaeum recognition.

She took the page weight off her draft.

Arion appeared at the infirmary threshold with his right forearm splinted against his chest. The rib binding made his shirt pull unevenly when he breathed. Nemi stood behind him with a witness slate but did not enter the room.

Mera kept her hands away from both the slate and his bandages.

“What may I write?”

“Structural exposure. Method not entered.”

“Private witness?”

“Nemi.”

Mera wrote the first sentence into the restricted folio and entered Nemi's name as requested. She crossed *method* with a narrow withheld mark.

From the threshold, Nemi read the line back. She pressed her mark to her own slate.

“I accept that sentence only.”

Mera copied the acceptance mark beside her name.

Arion went back through the infirmary door. Nemi followed him.

Mera returned to the wet abstract. She drew one line through *through the west return* and another through *two protected vessels*. The paper kept both phrases legible beneath the cancellation. Her personal mark remained.

She placed that page in her own document sleeve instead of the public tray.

The record clerk left to find Akamoto. A kitchen runner shouldered through the door in his place with a dented tea pot, two cups, and a cloth-wrapped stack of barley bread. He poured before leaving. Steam had darkened the cloth where it touched the crust.

Lio reached from his chair. The old route board slid across his lap, and the padded stool beneath his splint shifted half a hand.

Mera put down her cup. “Do you want the stool moved?”

“The board.” He caught its lower corner before it fell. “My leg is where Anet left it.”

She drew the board closer to his body. Lio tested the reach, then tore the top piece of bread with both hands and held half toward her.

Mera took it. The crust broke over her document sleeve. She brushed the crumbs away before they could work into the fold that held her cancelled abstract.

The tea smelled scorched.

She drank. Heat found the pale center of her palm scar and stayed there.

Lio watched her take a second mouthful. “Is that the heater you fixed?”

“The heater is working.”

“So the leaf is doing this on purpose.”

Beside the bread, the runner had left a small jar of pear syrup. Lio pushed it across the table.

“Emilia uses too much,” Mera said.

“Emilia isn't here.”

The infirmary door swung inward on its quiet hinge and shut behind the corridor where Arion and Nemi had gone.

Mera left the syrup untouched. She ate the bread while it was warm.

Akamoto returned with the record clerk and stood beside Lio's crumb-marked route board. The blue west flag notation had bled at one corner; a black strip still covered the maintenance return where divided-access work had stopped.

“The permanent procedure begins with the prior incident,” he said to the record clerk.

He did not look at Lio.

“I barred the aid. I left one voice to carry two route names. The wrong-route entry gave Lio the second compression. That procedure was mine.”

The clerk wrote each sentence without combining them.

Akamoto pulled the old black strip from the board. Dry adhesive resisted at the center. He worked it free with the edge of his thumbnail, leaving a dark rectangle and one torn corner behind.

He set the brass-edged replacement beside Lio's hand.

The strip required visible route state, a readback that kept route separate from condition, and a stop whenever state failed, blurred, or changed under the weather. A mounted aid could report only itself.

At the end, Akamoto had written: *A sealed board state does not establish that a physical route has ceased to exist.*

Lio aligned the strip over the old black mark. It sat half a finger too high. He adjusted the left edge, then the right.

“Write *moving*, not *open*,” he said. “I called a route, not a guarantee.”

The clerk changed the word.

Lio pressed the strip into place with both thumbs. Akamoto signed beneath it under Roost authority. Lio added only his earlier call and the corrected language to his candidate record.

The clerk carried the completed strip to the public rules frame. It entered beneath the route board amendments before the rescue report was sealed.

Mera opened a fresh page.

She omitted names, custody labels, Arion's condition, and every measurement from the damaged court. Then she stripped every location word from the page. She wrote only the provisional ordinary failure chain and that a structural response had been reported after closure but remained unauthenticated.

The causal abstract took six lines.

“This does not expose a person or route,” she said.

Akamoto placed the thin white notice beside it.

*Mechanical service-court failure. Five people recovered. No deaths. Access closed pending safety review.*

He placed the public closure board on the other side. It listed the north court, east service approach, lower fields, and the time the restriction began.

Then he pointed through the open doorway to the infirmary movement notice posted where anyone entering could read it.

*Arion: no climbing, load work, or unsupervised movement.*

Lio's splinted leg remained visible beneath the table.

“What does his splint add?” Akamoto asked.

Mera aligned the notice with the closure board.

The five-person recovery had occurred after the north and east approaches closed. Lio's public injury placed the earlier route failure at the divided-access course below the terrace. Arion's splint and movement restrictions placed another injured body beside the infirmary court. The only maintenance path joining those visible facts without using the closed east stair rose behind the west retaining wall.

Mera drew the inferred route on the reverse of her six-line abstract.

It passed close enough to the sealed hatch that a containment team with the public board could find the damaged perimeter before the Roost finished screening it. The two protected vessels in her first draft narrowed the likely inquiry roles. Arion's visible splint made the infirmary threshold worth watching.

A redaction could remove every proper noun and still leave the path.

Mera folded the six-line abstract once and put it beside her first draft in the personal sleeve.

“The ordinary chain should become public when the perimeter is secure,” she said.

“Enter that as dissent,” Akamoto replied.

The heavy folio remained open.

Anet aligned the translucent medical sheet over the folio and locked its edge beneath her seal. Arion's permitted sentence stayed dark. The other medical fields became unreadable blocks. The five names remained visible only inside the folio.

Akamoto read the five black-tab headings into the record—identity, route, body, recurrence, lost sources—then wrote his name, authority, and reason on the seal line.

Then he passed black thread through the folio's first eyelet and through his authority mark.

Mera held the loose end away from her own papers.

“Forty-eight hours,” she said. “Named reviewer. No silent renewal.”

Beside the opening time, he wrote *forty-eight hours*. He named the outside reader, left the acceptance line empty, and entered the remaining conditions in three boxes: secured perimeter, consulted custodians, Anet's medical redactions. Any extension would require a new named order. He added *no automatic or silent renewal* and signed.

The black thread already crossed his authority line when Mera added her personal witness mark beside it.

*Witnessed in personal capacity under Roost local authority. Athenaeum recognition remains suspended.*

The paper dented beneath her mark.

Mera took a separate sheet.

She wrote that the provisional ordinary failure chain should enter the public record immediately after perimeter security; that the forty-eight-hour limit must expire rather than pass into inquiry custody; and that every extension, if one occurred, required its own named witness and dissent field.

She attached the dissent to the folio with a dry brass clip, outside the black thread.

The clerk carried only the thin white notice to the public frame. The black folio went into shadow on the restricted shelf beneath Akamoto's name and Mera's mark. At her hip, the red threads held their own two circles around sealed glass.
