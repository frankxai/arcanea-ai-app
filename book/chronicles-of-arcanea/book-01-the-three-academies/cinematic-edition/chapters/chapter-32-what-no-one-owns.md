# What no one owns

The broken weather vane struck its spindle twice while Arion tried to flatten the repair-house sketch with one hand. The right remained splinted against his ribs, and the page kept curling over the room he meant to build.

Lio set his cup on the loose corner.

“The north leaf needs one strip of brass,” Arion said.

“The vane can wait.”

“It has waited since before your accident.”

“Then it has more practice than you do. Sit back.”

Arion was already sitting. Lio meant the rest of it.

He let his shoulders touch the chair. The binding stopped his inhale at the same place it had under the foundations. Anet had checked it three times after the sledge brought him up and once more before allowing the supported move to the terrace. She had cleared that move, not the stairs, the road, or any Gate work.

Across from him, Lio's splinted leg occupied the second chair. A route board rested on his lap. Its upper edge carried a new application in his sloping hand.

“What is that?” Arion asked.

“A seated rescue demonstration.”

“Will they accept it?”

“They can reject it themselves.” Lio broke a barley biscuit over the table. Crumbs dropped into the old crack. “You are not writing it for me.”

Arion looked at the uneven line of text, found three ways to shorten it, and folded the repair-house sketch over instead.

The vane struck once.

He left it broken.

Mera arrived with a tray of tea and the expression of someone who had already tested it on herself and considered that sufficient warning. Her plain lamp hung from one hand. The pencil and three separate folds remained inside her coat.

“Who made this?” Lio asked after the first swallow.

“I did.”

“Why?”

“The water was hot.”

Arion drank. The brew began bitter, acquired salt, and ended with the faint taste of burned peel.

“Terrible,” he said.

Mera set the pot beside him. “There is more.”

Emilia came through the terrace door carrying the fixed-flame lamp. The yellow cuff at her right wrist had been brushed clean, though chalk remained in the seams. She stopped at the heater. One fingertip hovered beside the visible repair where two pieces of brass met without matching. Warm air continued to rise through the grate.

She took her hand away.

“Tea?” Mera asked.

“Is it the same one?”

“There has only been time for one.”

Emilia sat.

For several minutes, they discussed Lio's seated demonstration. He refused six suggestions and accepted one from Mera after arguing that he had thought of it earlier. Arion kept the sketch folded. The cup heated his left palm. Beyond the western wall, morning moved across roofs that required nothing from him.

The terrace door opened again.

Examiner Neris Vale entered with the council slate beneath one arm. Akamoto followed carrying the black-threaded folio from the restricted shelf. Anet came last with two medical sheets and stayed standing.

No one asked Lio to leave. He moved the route board onto his splinted leg and cleared the middle of the table with his forearm.

Akamoto placed the folio beside the tea. The black thread still crossed his authority mark. A time strip showed twenty-seven hours before the named hold ended.

“No extension has been entered,” he said. “My sealing decision and the Roost's lower-access practice enter external review at midday. I cannot remain the only local keeper of this record.”

Mera drew the basin page and the state page from one side of her coat. The third fold stayed beneath the other.

“These are personal accounts,” she said. “I will read the attributed lines. The originals remain with me.”

Neris opened the slate. “Your standing remains suspended.”

“Write that before my name.”

Neris did.

Mera read only what the pages could carry: a source interval remained sealed below; water had not entered the housing; three pages had left with her; no source content, sample, slip, or plate had. She gave the plate's three authority marks and the visible condition of the fractured outer-access seal. Each time she reached a claim the pages could not establish, she stopped.

Emilia set the bent wick key beside her cup. “The lower interface retained a physical lead-state layer. I destroyed that layer. The carrier, pane, cord, and remaining mechanism stayed below.”

“Your prism instrument?” Neris asked.

“Still disassembled under its existing evidence seal. Separate system.”

“Reason for destroying the lead layer?”

“It could continue to privilege a retained route state after present operator conditions changed. I removed that function.”

Neris entered the act without calling it approved.

Arion gave the Foundation sequence from the failed probe through the slab return. He named every stop he had called, the limit he had exceeded, and the voice offer he had reported. Anet corrected the order of two body checks. He accepted the correction.

When he finished, Neris turned the slate so all of them could read the council finding.

*Documented prior institutional contact and coordination.*

Beneath it, a longer field remained disputed: authorship, performance, present control, cause, purpose, intent, source identity, protection validity, and the identity of the buried works.

“The marks establish contact,” Neris said. “They do not establish which institution acted, what it authorized, or why. The damage establishes present risk. It does not establish sole cause.”

Another page waited behind the finding.

At the top, three words had been cut deeper than the rest.

*Joint custodial supervision.*

Below them, the proposed motion named the Luminary civic ward and Rector Vey's chain. If entered, Arion and the incident record would move into that custody immediately. His body would remain at the Roost only until Anet cleared travel. A civic-ward team waited outside the terrace with empty record cases and a medical transport frame.

Arion remembered the ward mattress pressing back through its left frame and breakfast cooling behind a door he had not been allowed to open. Anet had named every hand before it touched him. The motion named an office instead.

“One chain answers for safety,” Neris said. “No Academy may act alone inside it.”

“One Academy holds the chain,” Mera replied.

“Under joint review.”

“With its name above all of ours.”

Akamoto kept both hands away from the folio. “The Roost cannot answer by keeping the only copy. My decision is in the review.”

Arion read the motion twice. The paper listed him beside the folio, the sealed instrument, Mera's disputed records, and the damaged site. Every item had a different owner, custodian, or no established owner at all. The grammar gave them one destination.

He held out his left hand for Mera's pencil.

She passed it to him.

He drew one line through *custodial*. The point caught in the heavy paper and tore the first fibre.

Above the word, he wrote *witnessed training*.

Neris read the change. “Training does not answer another emergency breach.”

“Then enter the breach and the date you review it.”

“A review date does not contain a recurrence.”

“Neither does a ward.”

Anet put one medical sheet on the table without letting it touch the motion. “No travel. Reviewed at each morning examination. Independent physician appeal available. No climb, load work, unsupervised movement, or Gate practice under present findings.”

“How long?” Neris asked.

“Until the examination changes. My clock is not yours.”

The crossed-out word sat alone inside the unchanged motion.

“Separate records,” Mera said.

He looked at her.

“You wrote witnessed training as if the same witness record can hold all three accounts.” She drew the two personal pages back beneath her coat. The addressed fold did not move. “This calls me an Academy witness. I am not one.”

“You witnessed the descent,” Neris said.

“Participant account. Personal capacity. Standing suspended before the name.”

Neris opened a blank annex.

Emilia turned it sideways. “Separate records are insufficient.”

Mera's scar tightened pale across her palm. “For whom?”

“For anyone inside the next procedure. If Arion writes his Gate state, you write what you saw, and I write the tool state, we can still lose the point where someone stopped.”

“Then you want a consolidated record after changing the title.”

“I want act, time, place, named stop, body state, physical consequence. Nothing from your source. Nothing from his thoughts. Nothing from my sealed instrument.”

“And when your instrument records a state after he stops?”

“It does not retain. It has a local release I control, and I state that before use.”

Arion heard the old room in the word *records*. The circle under his boots. The mirror continuing after his five minutes. The easiest response was to refuse the entire page before anyone could fit him inside it.

The weather vane struck its spindle.

He kept both hands on the table where he could see them.

“What will you participate in?” he asked Mera.

She took back her pencil and ruled a narrow column on the blank page. “Named physical states. My words attributed to me. My originals stay with me. Audience and expiry written before observation. I can stop my observation. If the procedure depends on it, the procedure pauses.”

“Access?”

“None to my scar, vessels, memory, source content, or private pages.” Her hand rested over the third fold beneath her coat. “I keep the addressed page until you choose: delivered, read, or destroyed.”

“Closed today,” Arion said.

The page stayed where it was.

He turned to Emilia.

She placed the bent key on a second blank sheet. “I name the method, the tool's present state, and where I stop it. It keeps no route after release and places no one. If that changes, I withdraw it; anything using it pauses.”

“Sponsor?” Neris asked.

Emilia found the field and left it empty. “There is no instrument to certify below. My other instrument stays sealed. I sign the destruction decision under my own name.”

“The Forge may decline the page without a sponsor.”

“Then the blank will be accurate.”

“It will also leave your access closed.”

Emilia set the key across the empty line. “That is already accurate.”

Neris opened a third annex for Arion.

“Declared purpose and method before a session,” he said. “Current body limit. Named witnesses. No unsupervised Gate work. No carried method.”

The words sat close to his right forearm.

“Who clears your stop?” Neris asked.

“I do. Anet clears medical practice separately. If I withdraw, anything requiring my Gate pauses. It does not become a transfer order.”

The council slate marked the phrase for review.

Warmth formed beneath the splint.

It lay beside the carried movement through his forearm and ribs, in the same place as before. No words came with it. It had no direction he could test without touching it.

Arion removed his left hand from the table.

“Warm line,” he said. “No voice. Same place.”

Neris started a new entry on the narrow shared sheet. “Present body change?”

He waited through one breath. “No movement beyond the recorded line. Two fingers unchanged.”

Anet watched the binding settle. “Entered as reported experience and present body state.”

The warmth remained.

“It is still there,” he said.

Neris added the time. The negotiation continued.

They reduced the shared sheet to six fields: act, time, place, named stop, body state, visible consequence. Arion, the Roost file, and Neris would each keep one of three numbered copies. An amendment would preserve the first line, name its writer, and leave room for dissent.

Neris wrote the sixth evening bell in the review field and the seventh following morning bell in the expiry field. The Roost term ended there unless they signed again. Appeal remained open. Akamoto provided the training place; Neris served as external witness while the Roost remained under review. Anet's separate sheet still barred Gate work.

The old transfer motion remained visible beneath the order, marked *stayed before entry*. Expiry, appeal, refusal, or withdrawal could not activate it. Any later transfer required a new named order, new evidence, and a new appeal line.

“The original folio?” Mera asked.

Akamoto touched the time strip, not the thread. “That clock continues. The original expiry remains; no hour moves into this order. At expiry the folio follows the named return terms unless a new named order is entered with its own named witness and dissent field.”

Neris pressed her examiner mark to the cover order and activated the council seal beside it. Akamoto signed beneath *local residence and training provider*. On a separate page, he accepted external review of the Roost and the sealing decision.

The civic-ward team outside received no record case. Their transport frame stayed folded.

Mera read her annex first. She changed *witness account* to *participant account* and added *Athenaeum standing suspended* above her name. Her original stayed in her custody. She marked the operational lines the Roost could copy and allowed Neris one attributed read-only copy for the council docket. Then she signed.

Emilia entered the destruction of the lead-state layer as deliberate maker judgment. She named the spent interface separately from the sealed prism instrument. The sponsor field remained blank. She gave the Roost only method and local-stop lines, Neris an attributed read-only copy, and kept the original. Then she signed.

Arion read his page until the letters began to double from hunger. Seven days at the Roost. No home. No Gate work unless Anet changed her finding. Named limits when she did. A record of stops and consequences that would follow him farther than the repair-house sketch had.

He could appeal before the sixth evening bell. He could refuse now. The stayed motion would remain inactive either way, though the council could bring a new one on new evidence.

He signed with his left hand.

The line sloped upward at the end.

Neris made one read-only copy of each annex for the council docket. Akamoto took only the training lines their authors had marked. Mera's personal pages never left her coat. Anet folded her medical sheet under its own seal.

Lio returned his route board to his lap. “Now may I reject your vane repair?”

Arion looked up at the missing north leaf.

“You already did.”

“I wanted an independent record.”

Mera weighted her page with the pencil. Emilia laid the bent wick key across hers. Arion moved the cup of terrible tea onto his.

Barley crumbs remained in the unrepaired table crack beneath all three signatures.
