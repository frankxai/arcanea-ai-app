# The borrowed method

The cord had gained less than a finger of distance.

Emilia knelt at the nearer chalk mark and put one nail beneath the lifted fibre. The larger loop had shortened by less than a finger but still lay near the gallery mouth where she had left it. Farther back, the weighted board had rung once and gone quiet. No one had touched either end.

Between the two white marks, the floor joint was wider.

She rubbed the cord against the pad of her thumb. Chalk came away in two places: the first broad and settled, the second new enough to catch in the twist of the fibre. The line had not pulled the floor. The floor had made more route beneath the line.

“It lengthened,” she said.

Mera remained beside Arion. “The cord?”

“The joint.”

Arion looked down the level gallery. “That is a worse answer.”

“Breath first.”

He breathed without improving it for her. The inhale stopped against the rib binding; the exhale came out shorter but even.

“Right hand?” Emilia asked.

“Two fingers still tight. Small one colder.”

“Any grade change?”

“No.”

Only then did Mera help him stand. She took his left elbow, not his weight, and let him find the wall with his left hand. His splinted arm stayed across his ribs. Emilia gathered the free loop herself.

She left one lamp at the basin vestibule inside the bell-board-side cord, its flame low and visible from the gallery. If the floor moved again, the light would mark which side still belonged to their route. Mera carried the second. Emilia kept the third near her knee.

The widened joint travelled six paces, turned into the right wall, and disappeared beneath a skin of pale mineral. Emilia brushed the surface with the back of her chalk. A flake no thicker than onion paper came away.

Mineral glass waited beneath it.

She cleared only what had already loosened. A shallow recess emerged: two rows of narrow brass shutters under glass, each shutter small enough to cover with a thumbnail. Fine rods dropped from the lower row into the joint at her boots. The rods above them ran along the wall and vanished into the next floor seam.

Below both rows, three separate channels crossed the recess. Each aligned with one of the worn receipt-form impressions beside Mera's basins and continued toward the dry return groove. Alignment was all Emilia could name. No date, instruction, or maker's plate survived beneath the mineral.

One upper shutter rested between two notches.

Emilia leaned close. A shim beneath its left guide had been cut from kitchen brass, the edge still bearing the shallow crescent of a bowl maker's snips. It was not square. It was exactly thick enough to keep the shutter from choosing either notch when its paired rods disagreed.

The work was better for admitting the disagreement.

Her thumbnail found the slot in a retaining screw before she knew she had moved. The nail entered, slipped, and left a pale half-moon against the brass. The fine driver that fitted it was sealed with her certified tools above.

Emilia took her hand away.

“You want to open it,” Arion said.

“I want the correct driver.”

“A more disciplined form of wanting.”

“Sit.”

His mouth moved as if he had a reply. He saved the breath and lowered himself against the dry sill instead. Mera kept a hand near his left shoulder until his weight settled.

The gallery made a sound like a spoon placed on stone.

One shutter in the upper row moved.

The between-notch shutter trembled but did not choose. A moment later, the joint beneath Emilia's boot lengthened by the width of a thread. The matching shutter in the lower row slid into its next position.

Upper first. Lower after.

Emilia traced the rods again without touching the glass. The upper links passed beyond the bay. The lower ones ended beneath it.

“Top row feels the next joint,” she said. “Bottom row records this one.”

Mera crouched far enough away that her breath did not mist the glass. “Feels how far ahead?”

“One joint. Here.” Emilia pointed to the two linkages, not the shutters. “Observed once.”

“Then the top is not a promise.”

“No.”

Arion shifted his left heel. His right shoulder caught before the rest of him moved.

Under the glass, a line no wider than a hair advanced along the right edge of the upper row. It followed the wall seam nearest his injured side and stopped when he stopped.

One necessary movement, one corresponding advance. Emilia caught herself waiting for Arion to move again.

She laid her yellow cuff across the relevant shutters. Chalk dust on the repair clouded the glass.

Arion saw where she had covered it.

“Did another method enter your body?” she asked.

His eyes remained on the cloth rather than her face. “I have used no method below.”

“That wasn't what I asked.”

Mera stood. Arion said nothing more.

Emilia left the cuff over the line until his breathing returned to its previous depth. Then she lifted it without looking at what had changed beneath.

The bay ended at a dry sill. Beyond it, a single slab of grey stone crossed a narrow channel to a ledge on the far side. The channel water lay level with the slab's lower edge. No reflection formed on it; Emilia's lamp entered the mineral-grey surface and stopped.

Behind the slab, a narrow slot cut the floor. Another occupied the ceiling directly above it. On the far wall, a flat bar crossed the continuation of the dry return groove. Its left corner had joined its housing under a thick growth of mineral.

From the bay, the bar's working edge could not be seen.

Emilia measured the view with her empty hands and disliked every result.

“The far side can see behind the bar,” she said. “This side cannot.”

Mera placed her folded page deeper inside her coat. “What do you need named?”

“Whether the groove ends at the bar or passes beneath it. Whether the far edge is retained by mineral or by a separate catch.”

“No pressure reading.”

“No.”

“No water contact. I cross with the lamp. I stop for body sensation or if either of you calls.”

Emilia looked at the upper shutters. Current and lead still agreed at the central slab.

“I would rather move the view,” she said.

“You cannot.” Mera tightened the plain strap around the lamp handle. “I can decide whether to take it.”

Arion lifted his left hand from the sill. “Mera.”

She waited.

“The stone is dry,” he said. “That is all I know.”

“Useful.”

Mera put one boot onto the slab and kept the other on the bay sill until the lamp flame steadied. The stone did not tilt. Water touched neither sole. She moved her rear foot forward, paused at the center, then crossed the remaining distance without reaching for either wall.

At the far ledge she turned and showed them her open hand.

“Clear,” she said.

Only after both boots stood on dry stone did she crouch beside the retained-return bar. She held the lamp low, away from the water.

“The groove passes beneath the housing,” she said. “The bar sits across it. Mineral joins the left corner. The right has a narrow gap.”

One of the upper shutters moved.

The matching lower shutter beneath it stayed in place.

The lead row took the central-slab-return position while the current row still showed it held.

Emilia raised her hand. “Stop.”

Mera did not rise. She removed her free hand from the stone and held it where they could see.

“Stopped.”

Arion leaned forward at the sill.

“Stay seated,” Emilia said.

“If the slab closes—”

“Lead, not closure.”

“It's closing.”

Across the channel, Mera said, “I'm clear. Leave it.”

Arion looked at the open water between them. His ring finger tightened above the splint.

Emilia kept the lamp on both rows. The lower shutter still held; above it, the between-notch shutter trembled.

“We wait for a second cycle,” she said.

Arion pressed the padded outside of his splinted forearm into the right-wall seam.

Emilia saw the binding compress.

“Arion.”

He breathed out.

A dry knock travelled from his forearm through the wall, followed by the deeper answer of weight finding somewhere else to stand.

The current shutter beneath the central slab stayed open. For one interval, the lead shutter moved back to meet it.

It held after the knock. The shutters did not tell Emilia what had carried the load.

“Take your arm away,” Emilia said.

“The route is held.” His voice had thinned around his ribs.

“Mera was already clear.”

Across the channel, Mera rose without stepping toward the slab. “You did not ask me whether to keep the return.”

The first of the three lower channels struck its housing.

Then the second.

The third carried the sound along the dry groove to the bar at Mera's feet.

“Bar moving,” she said.

The mineral join at its left corner whitened. The bar lifted by the width of a nail.

Both aligned slots opened.

Water drove upward from the floor and down from the ceiling. The two pressures met between the far ledge and the bay in a vertical sheet, mineral-grey. Behind it, Mera's face broke into moving bands of colour.

Emilia reached for Arion's left shoulder.

The counter-motion exhausted.

The central slab withdrew into its side housing. Empty channel replaced the route beneath it. At the same instant, the bay joint shifted sideways under Emilia's knees.

The cord trapped at her first chalk mark.

Its fibres straightened one by one. White appeared inside the twist. The lamp she had left at the basin vestibule remained lit on the bell-board side until the turning wall hid it.

Arion's right arm jerked against the seam.

Not away. In.

The padding cinched around his splint. His contracted fingers closed until the nails met his palm. A hard movement crossed beneath his rib binding and lifted his right shoulder.

“It returned,” he said.

The floor moved again.

Emilia caught the back of his coat with her left hand. The bay threw them into the recess before she could bring her feet beneath her. Her lamp struck the mineral glass and rolled, flame pressed flat but alive.

Plain cord parted at the chalk mark.

The bell-board-side segment snapped away toward the basin vestibule. The free length fell into the bay across Emilia's boots.

Then the joint stopped.

Water continued between them and Mera.

Emilia found the lamp by heat along its guard and set it upright. The lower shutters had lost their clean sequence. Three stood between states. Two had dropped past their notches. Under the glass, the upper row continued to move from feelers one joint ahead.

A narrow line ran left, divided, and stopped beside a folded length of coat. Emilia lifted the lamp. She checked the stone before following the light to Arion on his left side against the lower wall. She put two fingers to the skin beneath his ear before she touched his shoulder.

Pulse. Fast.

“Arion.”

His eyes opened.

“Breath.”

He drew one that stopped high, then another that reached the binding.

“Right hand?”

“Still there.”

“Sensation.”

“Too much.”

She kept two fingers at his pulse until it slowed by one beat. “Don't move.”

Behind her, the lead row shifted again. More lines waited beyond the two she had used, thin routes crossing near the water boundary and the far ledge. Emilia took the broken free cord, laid it across the mineral glass, and tucked her yellow cuff beneath it until the deeper shutters disappeared.

She left only the two closed branches visible.

Through the upright water, Mera set her lamp on the far ledge where they could see it.

Arion tried to open his ring finger, then the small one.

Neither moved.
