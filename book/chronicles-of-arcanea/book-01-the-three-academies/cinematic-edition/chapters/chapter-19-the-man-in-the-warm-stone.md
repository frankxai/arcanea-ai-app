# The man in the warm stone

Nemi counted the pin drift twice.

It lay on the hand-tool tray beside a narrow clamp and two ash shims, one pale and one dark. She turned each piece so its outline matched the drawing on her slate. Then she lifted the tray to the evidence hatch and waited.

On the other side of the inspection glass, Arion kept his hands visible.

Akamoto threaded the local-preservation amendment beneath the old custody number. The page was shorter than the list of things it did not permit. Twelve minutes. One loose brass pin. One dry shim. Existing seam only. The hammer stayed on its plinth.

Emilia signed where the amendment appointed her monitoring-only structural operator under Roost authority. The yellow patch on her cuff caught against the slate. Nemi signed as independent witness and added a small square beside both their names: either could stop the work.

Akamoto signed last.

“Named tool contact only,” he said. “No field. If you feel a state change, put everything down and step west.”

“Accepted,” Arion said.

Nemi cut the outer chamber wire and entered its number. The inner wires remained across the bridge shield, floor joint, lower stair, and hammer plinth. She opened only the hand hatch and the west section of the evidence screen.

Emilia touched nothing inside the panel. She read the exposed conductors from beyond its boundary.

“Blue, amber, green cold. Violet, white, black at ordinary service warmth. No change.”

Akamoto started the sand timer.

Arion stepped through the west opening.

The lower assembly chamber smelled of iron, old dust, and the oil Nemi had placed in a shallow cup for the pin. The benches removed for the council session had not returned. Chalk still divided local operation from evidence and no entry. Behind a clear shield in the floor, the opened hairline joint ran toward the stair exactly as it had when they left it.

He did not look down again.

His father's hammer waited at the far wall.

The ash handle had been repaired twice before it came to the Roost. One brass pin belonged to Arion's grandfather. The other was newer, fitted by Arion after the handle split through old winter grain. The two repairs met badly beneath the head. They had always met badly. A perfect seam would have required cutting away the darkened wood that still carried the shape of his father's grip.

Through the glass that morning, Arion had seen the lower pin working outward.

Now the plinth shield stood open. The wordless light inside the seam had gone dull but not dark. Grey dust filled the narrow missing interval between its upper and lower strokes.

Nemi passed the tray through the hatch.

“Three tools,” she said.

“Pin drift. Clamp. Two shims counted as one set.”

“The slate agrees.”

Arion laid the pale shim beside the handle and tested the dark one with his thumbnail. The dark ash was old enough to match the repair, but its grain ran too straight. It would hold until the first hard strike and then open along its whole length. The pale shim came from younger wood. Its grain curved toward one edge and might compress without splitting.

He chose pale.

The clamp's leather pads fit above and below the loose pin. Arion tightened until the head stopped moving, no farther. He set the drift against the pin and tapped it with the heel of his palm.

Brass moved the width of a fingernail.

The seam opened at the lower repair.

He stopped.

“State?” Akamoto asked.

Arion held his fingers above the ash without touching. Surface cool. Slight warmth near the upper pin, where the handle had carried it since the chamber event. No pressure from the plinth or floor.

“Tool warmth only. No movement outside the handle.”

Emilia read the conductors. “Local cold. Remote unchanged.”

Arion loosened the clamp by half a turn. The seam relaxed but did not close. He shaped the pale shim against the plinth's fixed cutting edge, taking off translucent curls no longer than his thumbnail. Each curl stayed on the evidence cloth where Nemi could count it later.

The first fit was too thick at the return. He shaved the inner face.

The second fit entered without lifting the upper repair, but its edge stood proud of the handle.

“Six minutes,” Akamoto said.

Arion withdrew it and cut the shoulder back. He had made this repair on doors, chair legs, cart axles, and one cracked window latch his mother refused to replace because his father had complained about it for eleven years. He had left the first cut thick on purpose. Now the grain showed him the excess.

The third fit seated beneath the loose pin.

Arion tightened the clamp. The lower seam closed against the shim. The upper seam remained open by less than a hair.

He pushed the pin home with one clean strike.

The mark inside the handle did not brighten.

Nothing answered under the floor.

He tested the pin with his thumbnail. Level. The pale shim held its shoulder without crushing. The open hair at the upper seam could be bound beneath the plinth shield and reviewed after the wood settled.

He could reseal it here.

“Stable stop,” Arion said.

Nemi leaned toward the glass without crossing her line. “Pin level. Shim seated. No loose material visible.”

Emilia said, “States unchanged.”

Akamoto put two fingers against the timer's frame. “We can reseal there.”

Arion looked at the remaining seam.

A new line would enter the tool record: *Preservation incomplete; stable under shield.* No one would blame him. Akamoto had made that explicit before opening the chamber. The hammer would remain quiet until the next review.

Arion reached for the oil cloth to wipe his fingerprints from the pin.

The pin drift rolled against the tray.

Its narrow steel shaft struck the rim once. The ring should have lasted for four breaths in the dry room. It died before the first.

Arion caught the tool.

The stone beneath the plinth was cooling with the evening. A band of warmth moved through it from the wall toward his hand.

He took his fingers off the hammer.

The pale ash shim relaxed farther into the seam without pressure.

“Stop?” Emilia asked through the glass.

She was watching the conductors, not him. Blue, amber, and green lay dull across the floor. The three remote lines held their ordinary service warmth.

“No state change,” Arion said.

The warmth reached the pin.

*The shim is blocking the return.*

The words did not enter through his ears. They arrived with the remembered sensation of sun on a wall after the street had gone into shade.

Arion set the pin drift down.

“Which return?” he asked under his breath.

Nemi looked up from her tool slate.

He touched the shim's proud edge. “Checking the return.”

She entered the act.

*The handle moved apart along winter grain,* the speaker said. *You have repaired across that path twice. The new ash closes the last direction it still knows how to travel.*

Arion could see it once named. The older repair held the head against rotation. The lower pin held lateral spread. His new shim filled the only compressible interval. Tightening the seam now would trap the old movement inside the handle.

He should report the perception. Akamoto would stop the window. Emilia would record no local cause. The council would receive another account of Arion knowing something a tool had not been asked to tell him.

“Four minutes,” Akamoto said.

“Who are you?” Arion whispered.

*Someone who was taught the same first lesson.*

“By Akamoto?”

Warmth passed through the plinth with something like amusement, quiet and brief.

*By stone. Your teacher puts it into better words.*

Arion kept his hands flat on the evidence cloth. “He says to find the last free path.”

*He is right.*

“He says not to put a body where a structure should move.”

*He is right again.*

The answer irritated him more than disagreement would have.

“Then the safe stop is the shim.”

*Yes.*

The warmth went still. Beyond the glass, Akamoto waited with one hand beside the timer. Arion could leave the seam imperfect.

He looked at the narrow line above the pale ash. It cut through the dark place where his father's thumb had rested when he checked a wall for hollow sound. Grey dust occupied the mark inside. Behind glass, the hammer could remain like this for another week while the council argued over its silence.

“If the shim blocks the return,” Arion said, “where does the movement go?”

*Where can it go?*

He followed the load as if the handle were a small wall. Brass stopped rotation. Old repair stopped spread. New ash stopped compression. The plinth stopped the head. Beyond the tool lay his hands, the bench, the stone, and three people whose job was to refuse the wrong movement.

His right forearm was nearest. The others stood behind glass, where he wanted them.

“Into me,” Arion said.

The warmth did not praise him.

*Keep the left wrist straight. Lay the outside of the right forearm along the seam's angle. On the exhale, draw the shim back one hair.*

Arion did not move.

“Cost?”

*Nerve. Joint. Bone. They will carry the last movement until a freer path accepts it.*

“How long?”

*I cannot know which path you will choose.*

Arion flexed his right hand once. The speaker had named the damage without softening it.

“Two minutes,” Akamoto said.

Arion placed his left hand on the pale shim.

“Proceeding?” Nemi asked.

The lawful repair was complete enough. The stop remained beside him, dry, stable, and witnessed.

“One fit check,” he said.

Akamoto's hand moved from the timer to the edge of the stop slate. “No field.”

“Accepted.”

Arion kept his healing left wrist straight. He laid the outside of his right forearm against the handle, following the angle from the lower pin toward the open upper seam.

It could still be ordinary contact. A craftsperson braced work against the forearm when fingers would shift it.

He breathed in.

The room's iron smell thinned beneath remembered sunlight.

On the exhale, he drew the shim back the width of a paper fiber.

The handle's last movement entered him.

His forearm did not feel struck. It felt required to bend in two directions at once. Pressure travelled from the contact point through the long bones and caught behind his wrist. His ring finger pulled toward his palm. The smallest followed. He kept his elbow against the plinth because lifting it would turn the trapped path through his shoulder.

The ash handle released.

Its upper seam closed. The pale shim slid into the space the old movement left. Brass settled with a click too soft for the glass to return.

The plinth stayed sharp; everything beyond it blurred.

No grey line entered the floor. The blue, amber, and green conductors stayed cold. Violet, white, and black did not change.

Below the clear bridge shield, the hairline joint remained closed to touch and open to sight.

A pressure answered through it.

Not enough to move the shield. Not enough to warm a conductor. It traced the angle now held in Arion's forearm as exactly as a mason's string traces a wall, from the buried joint to his right foot and up to the two fingers he could no longer straighten.

The pressure could have begun anywhere below the floor. It had still found the exact angle of his bones.

“Hands clear,” Akamoto said.

Arion lifted his left hand. His right forearm stayed against the handle for one breath too long.

He made it leave.

“Pain?” Akamoto asked.

Arion folded the two drawn fingers around the oil cloth. “Old hand burn pulling under the grip.”

Nemi wrote the words.

Emilia studied the conductors before looking at his face. “Local channels remained cold. Remote service warmth unchanged. No initiated comparison or retention state.”

Her report was accurate.

“Step west,” Akamoto said.

Arion returned the pin drift, clamp, and unused dark shim to the tray. Nemi counted the ash curls on the cloth, the two hand tools, and the remaining shim. The installed pale shim matched the amendment's consumed-material line.

“Tool acts reconcile,” she said.

Akamoto examined the hammer through the side glass. The pins sat level. The seam lay closed around pale ash. No light remained inside the wordless shape.

“Stable?” he asked.

Arion could feel the handle's strain under his skin. “Stable.”

Akamoto looked at him for another breath, then lowered the plinth shield.

Nemi passed new numbered wire through its anchors. She checked the bridge shield, the floor-joint wire, the lower-stair seal, and the chamber perimeter. None had moved. Emilia closed the service panel without disconnecting its six warm or cold lines.

The preservation amendment, tool acts, conductor states, reported old-injury pain, and resealing entered Nemi's report. She signed. Emilia signed only the state column. Akamoto signed the custody return.

Arion fastened his right sleeve one notch tighter.

“You have not told me your name,” he said without moving his mouth.

The last warmth withdrew from the plinth and gathered for an instant inside the wall.

*Malachar Lumenbright.*

Arion's fingers closed harder around the cuff.

The name belonged to the sealed histories. First Eldrian Luminor. Lumina's champion before the fall. A man the oldest warnings placed in Shadowfen, nowhere near this chamber.

“You are sealed.”

*Yes.*

“Then how—”

*They asked whether the event changed your refusal.*

Arion looked through the glass at Akamoto. The headmaster was reading Nemi's report before adding the final seal.

“It did not.”

*Akamoto held the room. What happens when the room is theirs?*

The wall cooled.

Behind the plinth glass, the repaired hammer showed only brass, old ash, new ash, and the seams of work done at different times.

Arion kept his right hand inside the tightened sleeve. His ring finger stopped short of straight when he tried to open it. The smallest finger stopped beside it.
