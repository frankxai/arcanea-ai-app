# Correct result, wrong act

The evidence cradle recognized Emilia's maker mark and refused her hand.

Green light traced the small angular signature she had cut into the relay housing two years ago. The lock remained red. Behind clear ceramic, her instrument lay in three pieces: the prism cuff, the local relay, and the brass release she had added after the first live-body failure.

The release had been catalogued as *nonstandard operator modification*.

Emilia touched her thumb to the cradle again. Green signature. Red lock.

“Repeating an unauthorized request does not make it authorized,” Master Renn said.

“The cradle may have a latency fault.”

“Your first attempt had no latency.”

“Then I have ruled that out.”

Renn stood at the other side of the bench with his right hand wrapped to the wrist. Her own right arm wore a looser dressing from palm to elbow, leaving space for the Prism burn to change color. It had been violet before dawn. Now thin bands of amber moved beneath the gauze whenever the calibration floor opened another field.

Neither of them had slept enough to improve the conversation.

Around them, the Forge continued its morning work. Apprentices called measurements across scarred benches. A pair of senior makers argued over a hinge tolerance while eating boiled eggs from the same tray. Every shared tool carried a maker mark, a repair mark, and a narrow blank reserved for whoever would alter it next. No one hid a seam unless the seam itself caused danger.

“Twelve wide after cooling,” an apprentice called from the next bench. “Bad filing or bad alloy?”

Emilia looked at the blue cast along the hinge edge. “Bad preheat.”

“Eleven says filing.”

“Eleven made the refectory doors sing all winter.”

Someone farther down the floor objected with great feeling. The apprentice reheated the hinge, set it back in the gauge, and raised both hands when the needle returned to center. Emilia tapped the bench once in acknowledgment before turning back to the locked cradle.

“The instrument is mine,” she said.

“The cuff and manual release are yours. The relay was built under a shared-work grant. All three are incident evidence.”

“I need the state log.”

“You need breakfast.”

“Can breakfast identify which system continued after local release?”

“Today it may have better judgment.”

Renn lifted a covered bowl from beneath the bench and set it beside her. Lentil broth, dark bread, a wedge of soft white cheese. The cover had kept the broth warm. Emilia moved it two handspans away from the calibration block so steam would not alter the surface reading.

Renn noticed.

“You are still a maker,” he said.

Emilia loosened the first fastening at her burned wrist. “Sponsor a no-contact read.”

“The incident office denied your request.”

“They denied *my* request. Your credentials include supervised reconstruction.”

“Attaching my name to it.”

“It was attached when you caught my wrist in a live conductor.”

Renn's wrapped hand flexed once.

“You told me not to cut blind,” she continued. “You were correct.”

“This is an unusual way to ask for help.”

“I tried the usual way first.”

He looked past her toward the calibration floor. At the nearest bench, a first-year apprentice had set a gauge incorrectly. The senior maker beside him did not fix it. She waited until he found the misalignment, then made him state which prior measurements could no longer be trusted. The boy's ears reddened. No one lowered their voice to protect him from the lesson, and no one laughed.

Renn placed his credential ring against the evidence cradle.

“No physical access,” he said. “State and incident logs only. If the reconstruction begins an active field, I stop it.”

“Agreed.”

The cradle changed from red to amber. Two flat panes rose from the bench, each clear until Emilia rotated the relay toward them. Color assembled inside the glass.

The left pane displayed the final local state from the joint hall.

*Foundation ring: released.*
*Water comparison: closed.*
*Prism relay: released.*
*Local operator control: available.*

All four indicators were green.

The right pane showed continuity status.

*Joint mirror: receiving.*
*Pattern integrity: confirmed.*
*Remote retention: authorized.*
*Local operator control: unavailable by design.*

Those indicators were green too.

Emilia leaned closer until the gauze at her wrist brushed the cradle. It cooled the moving amber lines beneath her skin.

“There,” she said.

Renn did not follow her finger. “Which line?”

“Local release reports success while remote acquisition remains active.”

“They are separate states.”

“Displayed on separate panes to separate operators.”

“The mirror is a continuity service. The local instrument is not certified to control it.”

“The local instrument is what the person inside the ring can see.”

“That is a visibility defect.”

“It is an exit defect.”

“Only if remote observation affects the field.”

Emilia expanded the timing log. The hall sequence unfolded in bands of color: Arion entering the ring, Foundation response, local Prism baseline, hidden mirror acquisition. After Mera broke the Water comparison, Emilia's brass release opened. The local band fell to neutral green. The remote band sharpened from violet to white. Less than a breath later, Foundation pressure rose through the east wall.

She isolated the interval.

“The mirror did not merely observe,” she said. “Its retention state held the comparison channel open.”

“The structural review has not established direction of cause.”

“It does not need direction. The two states interacted after local release.”

“That proves a coupling defect.”

“A person used the only exit shown to him. The system continued.”

Renn pulled the authorization pane over the timing log. A long agreement appeared, each clause carrying the signer and time. Emilia's name waited at the bottom in her own precise script.

He selected a paragraph she remembered reading twice before she signed.

*Cross-institutional continuity services may retain state for safety, calibration, incident reconstruction, and classification. Local operators receive status appropriate to their certified control surface.*

“The mirror did not exceed authorization,” Renn said.

“Its destination was not named.”

“Authorized joint service.”

“Its retention interval was not named.”

“Determined by incident state.”

“Its effect on local release was not tested.”

“No coupling was known.”

“Because the certification tested the release without a body in the field.”

Renn's gaze moved to the brass tab inside the cradle. “You added the release after the live-body test.”

“I added a local release.”

“And you signed the continuity clause afterward.”

“Yes.”

Renn lowered himself onto the bench opposite her, keeping his burned hand against his chest until he was seated.

“Why did you sign?” he asked.

“Without certification, the instrument returned to component review. The manual release would have been removed as redundant. The next operator would have had less control.”

“And with certification?”

“I kept the tab.”

“You knew you did not control the agreement.”

“I believed I controlled the part that could hurt someone.”

Renn lifted the bowl cover, found the broth untouched, and put the cover back.

“The incident office has proposed proximate cause,” he said.

A new document opened at the edge of the pane.

*Unclassified source behavior exceeded certified domain isolation, causing unforeseen coupling between authorized systems.*

The sentence was technically honest. Arion's response had exceeded every profile the instrument held. The coupling had been unforeseen. Both systems had been authorized.

Emilia read it again.

“If I accept this?” she asked.

“The instrument enters redesign rather than misconduct review. Your access remains supervised, not revoked. You lead the coupling correction under my sponsorship.”

“Arion becomes proximate cause.”

“His behavior is already the source event.”

“His request to stop is absent.”

“The cause statement concerns field mechanics.”

“His inability to stop the remote system is field mechanics.”

“Then add it to the corrective-action section.”

“After the sentence that says he caused the incident.”

Renn rubbed the edge of his bandage against the bench. “If the Forge accepts institutional cause before direction is established, the council will call this a protection of our operator. They will be right to ask whether we are fitting evidence around someone we trained.”

“If we accept source cause, they will fit custody around someone we measured.”

“Also true.”

The panes kept their incompatible green lights.

An apprentice brought the midday trays to the calibration floor. Bowls slid into the round marks burned into each bench by years of meals beside unfinished work. Someone passed Renn a cup without asking which hand he could use. Someone else set a dish of pickled roots within Emilia's reach and took one from it before moving on.

Emilia took one root and pushed the dish to Renn's unburned side. He ate from it without interrupting the review.

If she signed the proposed cause, she would keep the bench. She would show the coupling, force remote status into the local display, and make the brass release command both systems. She could build the fix because she knew exactly where the design had failed.

She could also spend the rest of the inquiry explaining that Arion's unclassified body had exposed the defect, until exposure and cause became the same word in every summary.

Emilia drew the incident document onto the left pane.

“What are you doing?” Renn asked.

“Separating findings.”

She created three fields.

In the first she wrote: *The cross-domain pattern was observed by both local and remote instruments. Available records do not establish that the pattern was false.*

In the second: *The local relay performed within certified state. The remote service performed within the continuity authorization signed by operator Emilia.*

Her burned arm trembled before the third. She braced her elbow on the scarred bench and continued with her left hand.

*The tested person requested stop. The control surface available to him and the local operator could not stop remote retention or its physical coupling. Acquisition continued after the available release became ineffective. Operator Emilia signed the authorization and continued the procedure without confirming a usable whole-system exit.*

Renn read the three fields.

“You are assigning yourself operator responsibility.”

“Yes.”

“The mirror design was outside your access.”

“Signing did not bring it inside my control.”

“Then why accept responsibility for it?”

“For signing as if local control were enough.”

Renn looked toward the evidence cradle. Green light still followed Emilia's maker mark around the locked instrument.

“The result may be correct,” he said.

“I know.”

“That will make the council's risk petition stronger.”

“Correct result,” she said. “Wrong act.”

“If you sign,” he said, “your project access closes until review. I cannot sponsor around a declared operator breach.”

“I know.”

“Read the consequences.”

He made her read them aloud. Loss of calibration-floor credentials. Loss of unsupervised instrument access. Suspension of certification authority. Preservation of the physical evidence. Mandatory disclosure of current copies and recipients.

At the last item, Emilia stopped.

“Current copies,” she said.

Renn touched the disclosure field.

The right pane changed.

The original mirror record had been copied six times. Three remained under incident seal. One had gone to structural review. Two were attached to a joint council project named *adaptive custody model*.

Emilia opened the first custody copy.

Her instrument's color geometry filled the pane with its source labels removed. The model retained the repaired seam and the four domain reports while omitting Arion's stop request, her local release, and the hidden mirror state.

“Revoke access,” she said.

“We cannot. The evidence policy permits inquiry duplication.”

“Then amend the copy.”

“Council evidence is sealed against source edits.”

“They removed source context.”

“They derived a model. The derivation is a separate record.”

Emilia reached toward the pane. The evidence cradle detected the movement and darkened her maker mark from green to amber.

Renn placed his uninjured hand between hers and the glass without touching her.

“If you enter the model now, they will classify it as interference.”

“It is incomplete.”

“Then put that in the record they cannot attribute to anyone else.”

She returned to the physical incident file. Beneath her three findings, she added the custody-copy identifiers and listed every field removed from the derived model. She named the missing stop request, local release, remote retention, and coupling interval. Renn signed as witness to the copy state.

The council panes did not change.

Emilia signed the operator-responsibility field.

Her credentials closed before the final stroke dried.

The calibration tools nearest her went neutral. The shared reference panes cleared. Even the steam-correction field beneath her soup disappeared, leaving a ring of condensation on the bench where the system had been keeping the wood dry.

Renn removed his credential ring from the cradle. “I will appeal the immediate closure.”

“On what ground?”

“You need access to complete the incident record.”

“I just completed it.”

“Then I will think of a better ground.”

The evidence cradle released a narrow strip of paper. Arion's conditional transfer request had arrived while they worked. It named Mera Tidecrest and Emilia as participant witnesses, attached the contested status of each, and stated that either could refuse without affecting his medical care.

Emilia read her own first name followed by a deliberate blank.

“He does not know your family name,” Renn said.

“I do not use one.”

“He appears to have solved that efficiently.”

The acceptance field offered no Academy credential. Personal capacity only.

Emilia looked once more at the bench. Her soup had gone cold. A first-year apprentice had left a tolerance gauge half a degree out of alignment two stations away. Emilia could see the error from where she sat and no longer had authority to correct it.

She selected *accept*.

Inside the locked cradle, her maker mark stayed on the instrument. Across the right pane, six anonymous copies of its result waited in systems she could no longer touch.
