# The memory that could convict them

The folded page caught inside Mera's coat when she knelt at the far wall.

She freed it with her unscarred hand. The drawing from the basins remained dry inside the fold. Behind it, the two blank sheets had clung together along one bent corner. Mera separated them with a thumbnail and ran the paper edges through lamplight.

No darkening. No swollen fibre.

She set the folded page nearest her knee, the blanks above it, and the lamp beside the bronze return bar. All three sheets rested beyond the spray that rose where the vertical water struck the channel.

Through that water, Arion's raised left hand broke into pale bands. Emilia's lamp remained lower beside him. Mera could not see the right arm or the cord. Turning her lamp toward them would leave the dry groove dark.

The dry groove passed beneath the bronze bar and entered a mineral-glass housing in the wall.

Four slips of black stone hung behind the glass on a fixed spindle. Each was thin enough to turn if the spindle moved, but mineral had closed the housing on every side. Breaking it would give Mera a removable record and destroy the condition in which she had found it.

She left the glass whole.

The upper edge of each slip carried a complete date. Mera did not recognize the calendar, only the consistency of its cut figures and separators. She copied them exactly, preserving the spaces she did not understand.

The rest was less obliging.

The first slip held a clear left authority impression and half of a middle one worn smooth by repeated contact. The second carried the middle form deeply enough to show its asymmetric lower cut. On the third, the right form survived beneath a skin of pale mineral while its state position did not. The fourth carried shallow fragments of all three and a recipient box with no mark inside it.

Mera changed the lamp angle.

No single slip gave her the three forms together. Across the four, the families repeated in the same positions she had seen on the Chapter 24 joint plate. The resemblance was visual; it authenticated neither maker nor authorization.

She drew each impression where it remained legible and marked the damaged edges instead of completing them.

The state positions ran down the opposite side. A narrow receipt cut appeared on the first slip. Beneath it, a crossbar occupied the held position. The second carried an outward notch beside the same crossbar. On the third, mineral covered the lower half of a diagonal state.

Mera read it as returned and wrote the word.

The vertical water changed sound.

Its strike against the channel softened. The band of Emilia's lamp widened through the grey sheet, then narrowed before Mera could decide whether the change belonged to water, light, or her angle at the wall.

She moved her own lamp closer to the third slip.

Raking light found the end of a second notch beneath the mineral. The diagonal did not terminate at return. It continued into the renewal position.

Mera crossed out *returned* with one line.

*Retention renewed.*

The fourth slip repeated the renewal edge under a later complete date. Its final recipient field remained blank. The blank could mark omission or loss as readily as denial. Mera had no record that chose among them.

She wrote the sequence beneath the copied forms.

Four dates. Receipt. Held. Outward release. Retention renewed. A later renewal. No final recipient entered.

Beside it she placed a reference to her public structural-coordination finding from the Athenaeum catalogues. She did not draw an equals sign between them.

The visible record would support more than the basins had. It would force the next reviewer to place the recurring forms beside the joint plate and account for the dated handling sequence.

Mera began the conclusion.

*The three Academies retained a source after outward release—*

Behind the black slips, deeper in the housing, blue-grey water occupied a separate cell.

A black-thread protection-state form crossed the inaccessible identity position. Its expiry and renewal cuts had not reached their visible stop. They appeared unelapsed. The glass carried no issuer Mera could authenticate and no field that established whether the form remained valid, what legal force it had, or whether the hidden identity belonged to someone living.

Mera lifted her scarred palm toward the inner cell.

At a hand's distance, pressure sharpened along the violet line.

She stopped.

Pressure held along the scar and went no farther. The sealed interval could hold the missing order—or only an ordinary moment someone had expected to keep, with nothing relevant to the slips at all.

Mera turned her palm into her coat.

With the other hand, she drew one line through the conclusion she had begun.

Below it she wrote:

*Four dated handling records under three recurring authority-impression forms. Visual match to the joint plate. Subject and purpose unknown.*

Then:

*Protection-state form visible. Expiry/renewal field appears unelapsed. Identity inaccessible. Issuer, validity, legal force, and living status unauthenticated.*

She did not lower her palm again.

The first blank page now carried the dates, forms, state sequence, reference, corrected reading, and limits. Mera signed with her personal name. The seal space stayed bare.

She folded the page once and placed it beside the earlier basin record.

Before her fingers left the fold, the water-strike softened again—much sooner than the first return of broad light. The band widened and closed while the paper was still under her hand.

The remaining sheet had one corner bent inward. Mera pressed it flat against the dry stone with the side of her hand.

She wrote Arion's name.

*You asked me to keep the interval unaccessed unless you gave me a different answer.*

*In the private room, I took one drop from the drinking cup; the rain-memory vial stayed sealed. I touched the drop to my scar anyway. I read one interval's duration. You had not consented. I used it to time the amber control. The shutters released and the corridor opened. I told you afterward. You chose another witness next time.*

Mera rested the pencil above the next line.

She saw again the tightened right sleeve Arion had turned away from her in the opened corridor. The pencil point dented the page.

*I thought the corridor—*

She crossed it out.

The water-strike softened again. This time her own lamp stayed fixed and Arion's pale shape broadened beyond the curtain. The thinning lasted long enough for one new line, not long enough to compare honestly with the first.

*I opened what you had asked me to leave closed.*

At the bottom she wrote:

*This page is yours if you want it. You may leave it unread or destroy it; I will make no copy unless you ask.*

Mera signed her name.

She folded the sheet with *Arion* outward and returned it to her coat apart from the other two pages.

The returned water thickened. Its impact sharpened against the channel.

Mera watched the bar rather than the sheet in her coat. During each softening, the bronze had also shifted by less than the thickness of its mineral edge. Neither change told her which came first. The spaces between them had not matched.

On the lower margin of her state page she wrote:

*Unequal—call by sight.*

Water struck stone. Behind it, Emilia's lamp narrowed to a yellow break. Arion's open hand was no longer visible.

The strike softened.

Mera put down the pencil and slid the lamp one handspan with her unscarred hand.

An altered band of light crossed the water.
