# Her own system

The third drawer remembered Emilia. Its lock did not.

Red light followed her maker mark around the brass pull. Through the narrow glass face she could see the fine driver lying at the same angle she had left it, handle toward the back, point protected in a curl of felt.

She pressed her thumb to the lock a second time.

The drawer stayed red.

“No latency fault,” Renn said behind her.

“I had to rule it out.”

“You ruled it out the last time a locked object refused you.”

“That was a cradle. This is a drawer. A responsible maker repeats the test when the housing changes.”

Emilia turned from the bench before he could see how much she wanted to laugh. The old incident laboratory opened onto the Forge floor through a wall of heat-glass. Beyond it, someone quenched a long hinge and filled half the room with blue steam. Two apprentices disputed the cooling width over a plate of sliced pears. Every working surface carried old cuts, bright new repairs, and the dark circles left by meals set beside unfinished work.

She had missed the smell of hot metal badly enough to resent it.

The unsigned offer from the Roost waited beneath her left hand. She had carried it folded once, then flattened it under a book overnight because the crease crossed the authority mark.

Renn placed a second page beside it.

Three narrow brass tabs ran down its right edge.

“The council condensed the terms,” he said.

“Into metal?”

“You object less effectively to metal.”

“Who told them?”

“Your entire training record.”

He tapped the first tab. “The repair is yours. Every change in this session carries your mark and your responsibility.”

The second. “The pattern happened. You are not being asked to call the intake result false.”

The third. “The pattern may decide custody. If you sign all three, the temporary authority can continue under review.”

The tabs shared one hinge.

Emilia took a pair of sheet shears from the unrestricted tray. She cut two fine separations through the hinge until each tab could move without dragging the next.

Renn watched the cuts. “Do you intend to return those in their issued condition?”

“No.”

“Good. I was worried the Roost had made you timid.”

She signed the first tab.

*Corrective-operator responsibility accepted for this session.*

The council seal warmed. Renn set his credential ring into the bench socket and entered the offer number. He did not grant the authority. The council mark did that. His ring authenticated the room, the bench, and the person administering both.

The third drawer changed from red to amber.

Renn opened it.

The fine driver had not moved.

Emilia picked it up. The octagonal handle found the old callus beneath her forefinger. Its weight was almost nothing. She turned it once against her thumb and set it parallel to the bench edge.

Across the incident laboratory, seven model rails woke in sequence.

Layered prism panes held no figure, only declared instrument states, their times, and the protection conditions derived from them. The historical intake pattern occupied four bands whose source labels had been removed when the custody copy was made. A white path carried the last retained state forward into three possible recommendations: supervised practice, protective residency, or immediate review.

All three showed green.

The original instrument remained in a sealed cradle at the back wall. Emilia's maker mark still showed on its relay housing. The lock did not respond when she entered the room, and the temporary authority terms did not include it.

She opened the model's local-state controls.

“Participant state,” she said.

“Not represented separately,” Renn said.

“It represents source available.”

“That is instrument state.”

“The recommendation uses it as participant state.”

“It uses the last technically valid pattern.”

“After the person leaves.”

“After the local source becomes unavailable.”

Emilia pulled the local feed out of the model.

The blue input faded. White retention held. The three recommendations remained green.

She touched the brass release drawn at the edge of the historical instrument state. Local status changed to *released*. Remote pattern remained *available*. The model continued to recommend supervised practice and protective residency as if the person at its center were still waiting to be placed.

“That is the defect,” she said.

“That is continuity.”

“It is a past observation wearing a present tense.”

Renn took the fine driver from the edge of the bench. He selected an empty scaffold profile from the test shelf and fitted it to the model without touching Emilia's live session.

One worker. Two suspended platforms. A load reader on the upper brace and a local wrist indicator for the climber. The system held the worker's last safe position in pale green.

“The upper signal breaks,” Renn said. “Do you release the retained state?”

“If the person has left.”

“You do not know whether she left. The signal broke.”

He cut the simulated local feed.

Emilia's first withdrawal rule moved through the model. Participant state became *withdrawn*. The green route from the lower platform disappeared.

So did the rescue recommendation.

The simulation left the worker on a hanging scaffold with no local signal and no route assigned from her last safe position.

Renn returned the driver to the bench.

“The last state may be the only place a rescue crew knows to begin.”

Emilia reset the scaffold too hard. The lower platform rang against its stop.

She restored the scaffold profile and entered *signal unknown*. The last safe position returned as incident evidence. A yellow rescue route opened from the lower platform. Placement and practice recommendations remained unavailable.

“Accidental loss holds the last incident state,” she said. “It raises unknown and rescue. It cannot start a fresh custody recommendation.”

“And deliberate withdrawal?”

“Requires an authenticated participant act or a current witnessed refusal.”

“The difference lives in attribution.”

“The difference is a person.”

Renn tapped the scaffold profile with one nail. “Mechanism.”

Emilia corrected herself. “The model can distinguish the records.”

“Better.”

She built the two transitions side by side. Signal loss preserved the last observed state, marked its age, opened rescue use, and barred new placement use. Attributed withdrawal preserved the historical record and ended every current recommendation until a new witnessed participation record entered.

The model resisted the final branch.

Its state list contained *available*, *unstable*, *lost*, *contained*, and *unknown*. Withdrawal had been treated as commentary on a source, not a condition the system could obey.

Emilia opened the physical control tray. Blank brass tiles sat beside blue and amber state shutters. She chose the smallest square and cut a shallow edge into each side so a gloved or injured hand could find it without sight. The yellow repair patch on her cuff brushed the tray as she filed the corners smooth.

“You are building a stop plate,” Renn said.

“No. A stop plate commands a field. This changes what the model may say after a person leaves it.”

“Then why brass?”

“Because a paragraph cannot be found by touch.”

She seated the square beneath the state panes. One word appeared across it in dark enamel.

*Withdraw.*

The control had no authority to press itself.

Emilia opened the model's authorized council references. Incident times. Instrument-state attestations. Structural review notices. The Chapter 16 refusal appeared as a sealed reference line with three signatory marks, the council entry, and Arion's own words visible at its center.

*No further measurement.*

Her hand stopped above the reference.

In the Roost corridor, Mera had held up a covered cup and said, *You did not consent.* Emilia opened a blank note field before touching the reference.

Emilia added a note to the revision.

*Participant not consulted. Existing witnessed refusal referenced; no new choice attributed.*

She linked the refusal record to the withdrawal transition.

The historical pattern stayed in place. Its times, four observed bands, instrument self-state, and incident limitations remained readable. The white retention path no longer supplied any current placement or practice recommendation. Above the new brass square, the participant state changed to *withdrawn*.

The immediate-review path remained available only under separately named immediate-harm authority.

Emilia did not touch it.

She ran the scaffold countertest again. Broken signal: last safe state held, age visible, rescue route open, placement barred. Witnessed withdrawal: history preserved, rescue evidence retained, future recommendation closed.

Renn ran the test a third time with the local indicator restored after an accidental break. The model required a new witnessed participation record before reopening placement use.

“That will slow emergency reassignment,” he said.

“It should.”

“Delay can injure someone.”

“So can letting an old green state answer for them.”

Renn left both test results standing on the panes.

Emilia placed her inert teaching strip beside the white retention path. The strip carried no live input and no stored source. Its small shutters moved only when she moved them. On the underside, Renn's instruction mark sat beside two open cuts feeding one closing stroke.

She set her attributed maker-observer sheet beside it. The sheet cited Lio's separate candidate observation for the tag form and location without reproducing it.

“This notation was under the Roost rescue course,” she said.

Renn turned the teaching strip over and set his thumb on his own instruction mark.

“Retention-return,” he said.

“Your training system.”

“My notation. Half the Forge learned it from me.”

“Did you install the line?”

“No.”

“Order it?”

“No.”

“Know it was there?”

“No.”

She waited.

“I taught the rule,” he said. “If a local instrument fails during a dangerous transition, preserve the last trusted state until return or rescue. I still teach it.”

“Without a participant exit.”

“For scaffolds. Transit braces. Cooling rigs. Things that do not withdraw consent halfway through holding someone above the floor.”

“The custody model uses the same rule on a person.”

“Then name that use. Do not call the rule corrupt because someone applied it where it did not belong.”

Emilia entered the attribution.

*Protocol notation and hold-last-state rule identified as taught in former sponsor Renn's Forge training system. Installation, operator, destination, purpose, and connection to the Roost lower works are not established.*

Renn read the line and placed his witness mark beside it.

“You omitted motive,” he said.

“I don't have yours.”

“I meant theirs.”

“I don't have that either.”

Renn nudged the fine driver back within her reach. Emilia took it. For one breath they had the old rhythm again.

Emilia fit the fine driver into the brass square's center screw. A quarter turn connected the physical control to the revision. Its green edge lit beneath her fingers.

The mechanism was not elegant. The square interrupted the model's clean lower rail. Its filed sides were visible through every prism pane.

She loved it.

“Authenticate,” Renn said.

Emilia pressed her maker mark to the revision plate.

The model tested authorship, session authority, historical-record preservation, and control behavior. Four green lines returned. A fifth line required the page with three brass tabs.

She signed the second.

*Observed pattern technically present in retained instrument record.*

The third tab waited.

Renn turned it toward her. “The pattern happened.”

“I signed that.”

“It may happen again.”

“Yes.”

“The council has to place risk somewhere.”

“Then they can ask him now.”

“And if he refuses every condition?”

“A refusal can trigger review. It cannot become agreement because the old pattern is convenient.”

Renn touched the edge of the third tab. “Keep the authority. Build the next version. Put the exit on every surface. If you leave, someone else will decide what this means.”

It was the argument Emilia had already made to herself.

With the credential she could force every future revision to show signal age. She could separate rescue from placement, incident evidence from current participation, and a person's refusal from a broken wire. She could make the interface so exact that no one would be able to misuse it by accident.

Someone could read every warning and press on.

Her thumb rested beside the third tab. The model answered her touch with a low clean hum. Every rail was aligned. Every state change belonged to her session. The fine driver lay warm beside her hand.

She wanted to stay.

Emilia wrote beneath the third tab instead of signing it.

*Technical observation does not establish consent-validity or present suitability for custody decisions. Attestation refused.*

The temporary credential began its closing count.

Renn took a narrow appeal sheet from his coat. Emilia recognized its blue edge before he unfolded it. He had filed it the morning her credentials closed, arguing that incident responsibility required continued access to complete the record.

“That appeal is still active,” she said.

“For another minute.”

“You could amend it.”

“If it remains, the council can call this former-sponsor interference under the pending credential-closure appeal. Your revision becomes my correction under credentials you did not hold.”

“And if you cancel it?”

“The council authorized you. You made the change. You refused their condition. The record stays yours.”

Renn signed the cancellation under his own mark.

The blue edge went grey.

He removed his credential ring from the bench.

Tool warmth left the incident laboratory one instrument at a time. The state shears cooled first. The reference panes cleared. The third drawer returned to red around Emilia's maker mark, with the fine driver locked inside exactly where she had put it.

The model sealed last.

It issued a narrow read-only receipt naming Emilia's revision, Renn's witness mark, the Chapter 16 refusal reference, the scaffold countertest, and the two signed positions. The third remained visibly refused.

Emilia folded neither the receipt nor her inert teaching strip. She held one in each hand.

Behind the locked prism panes, the historical pattern lay above the rough brass square. Green light ringed one word on the square; Emilia's maker mark cut through its visible seam.
