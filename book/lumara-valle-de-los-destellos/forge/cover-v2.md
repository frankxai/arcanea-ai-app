# Cover v2 — The Meadow (iconic single image)

**Aspect ratio:** 3:4 portrait
**Output:** `lumara-valle-de-las-destellos-cover-v2.png`
**Book beat:** Chapter 1 climax, Lila's first true greeting — the firefly,
the first lit florchispa, the first nombre verdadero.

## What this image must do

This is the iconic single image of the book. If the aunt sees only this
one picture, she should already feel the whole book's heart. The promise:
**paying close attention to small living things is a kind of love, and
the world answers.**

## Composition

A wide nocturnal meadow seen at almost child's-eye-level — horizon high in
the frame, the meadow filling the lower two-thirds, the dusty plum sky
filling the upper third.

Lila kneels at the left-third rule-of-thirds intersection, three-quarter
view, her body forming a soft S-curve from bare feet folded under her to
the slight forward tilt of her shoulders. Her cream linen nightgown
catches a cool moon-wash from upper-left — the only warm-cool contrast
in the frame. Conejito sits on the grass beside her left knee, set down
and slightly forgotten.

Her right arm extends slowly across the centre of the frame toward a
single tall florchispa cluster at the right-third intersection. Her index
finger is extended, near but not touching the top flower of the cluster.

On that top flower rests a **small luciérnaga** — a real firefly, brown
body, lace-clear wings folded back, narrow antennae. Its abdomen glows a
single small soft gold pulse. **This pulse is the hottest point in the
entire image.**

The viewer's eye is pulled along the axis between Lila's not-quite-touching
fingertip and the firefly. That axis is the whole picture.

## Negative space

- **Lower third** (meadow grass): almost no marks — just paper texture
  with occasional pencil-line indications of individual grass blades.
- **Upper third** (Lumara sky): the paper itself, with about a dozen tiny
  pencil-point stars (single pencil dots, not painted dots).
- Upper third is where the **title type** will sit — keep it breathing.

## Colour choreography

- **Cream wash** on Lila and on a single thin moon-strip across the
  middle distance behind her.
- **Gold-silver metallic ink** for the firefly's abdomen-pulse and the
  centre of the flower it rests on.
- A constellation of fainter florchispa-gold dots scattered through the
  meadow at low intensity — quiet stars-at-ground — kept faint enough that
  the firefly remains the single bright anchor.
- Grey-violet paper for everything else.

## Light

Single source: a waxing moon at upper-left, roughly 35° above the horizon,
cool. It puts one soft cream strip across the middle distance and catches
the cream of Lila's nightgown. Meadow-level florchispa glows are the only
other light, kept very dim — the firefly is the brightest.

## Lila's face

Three-quarter view, light brown hair falling forward slightly over her
cheek. Her expression is **complete calm** — mouth closed, eyes on the
firefly, the seriousness of a child who has just understood that she is
allowed to be here. Shoulders relaxed but slightly forward. Hair has the
slight tousle of bedtime. Cheeks not flushed; eyes not big. A real child
in a real meadow at night.

## Make-or-break detail

The firefly must be **small enough that the viewer has to look twice to
find it.** Then everything in the image organises around it. The single
hottest point in the frame is no bigger than a pencil eraser. That
smallness is what makes the magic feel real.

If the firefly is large or glowing brightly, the image fails. Quiet wins.
