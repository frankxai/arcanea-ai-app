# Spread Ch 3 — The Hummingbird Who Sings Colours

**Aspect ratio:** 4:3 landscape
**Output:** `lumara-valle-de-las-destellos-spread-ch03.png`
**Book beat:** Lila and the colibrí trade notes — and the notes are
visible in the air as small brushstrokes of colour.

## What this image must do

Show **the synaesthesia of true attention.** When two beings really
listen to each other, the world produces visible evidence. The image is
about the brief miracle of being painted at by another being.

## Composition

Tight intimate framing on the right side of Lila's face in three-quarter
profile. Her face fills the left half of the frame from chin to forehead;
the right half is the air between her and a small hovering hummingbird.

The **colibrí** hovers just past her cheek at the rule-of-thirds right
intersection, wings blurred soft (visible only as a translucent oval of
faster strokes), body suggested in iridescent green-violet-gold pencil
strokes (very small, very restrained — these are the only saturated
marks in the entire image, and they are tiny).

From the colibrí's small open beak, **a single short rose-pink
brushstroke** extends into the air toward Lila — that is the current
note. Lingering in the air behind it, a faint pale-yellow stroke and a
fading deep-blue stroke — earlier notes, both reduced to pencil-ghost
versions, almost gone.

Conejito tucked under Lila's left arm, just visible at the lower-left
edge of the frame.

## Negative space

The lower-right quarter of the frame is paper with just the suggestion
of a tall florchispa stem rising from out-of-frame — the meadow context
without literal meadow.

The upper-left quarter behind Lila's head is breathing dark paper with
a single pencil-point star.

## Colour choreography

- **Cream linen** on Lila's cheek and the edge of her nightgown sleeve.
- **Gold-silver** in the colibrí's iridescent throat-feathers (tiny
  metallic-ink touches).
- **The colour-strokes** — one rose-pink, one fading yellow, one fading
  blue — are the ONLY saturated marks in the whole image. Each is a
  single small brushstroke, no bigger than a child's thumbnail. They
  must be obviously paint, obviously placed.
- Grey-violet paper for everything else.

## Light

Florchispa-gold ambient from below frame (out of frame meadow). The
colibrí itself glows faintly, just enough to be the warm anchor of the
right side. Lila's face is in soft cream half-light.

## Lila's face

In three-quarter profile, very close. Her eye is on the colibrí — calm,
attentive, lips slightly parted (she is just about to sing her own note
back). The viewer can see the soft pencil-grain of her cheek and the
careful line of her hair tucked behind her ear.

## Make-or-break detail

The colour-strokes in the air must read as **paint, not light.** They
are obviously gouache marks left in the air by the hummingbird's beak.
The viewer should be able to see the brushstroke texture in each one.
The earlier notes (yellow, blue) should be markedly fainter than the
current rose-pink one, with the yellow almost gone — time has visibly
passed in this image. If the colour-strokes look like glowing beams or
energy effects, the image fails.
