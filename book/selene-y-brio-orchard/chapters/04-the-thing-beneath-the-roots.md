The flowers were taller than the abandoned laboratory.

Selene stopped beneath one, looking up through a red petal veined with the shadow of its own stem. Rain from the previous night gathered at the lowest point, swelled, and dropped into a basin large enough to water three horses. Beyond it, terraces descended into a crater of warm green stone.

“Someone planted these?” she asked.

Damián brushed dirt from a broken marker.

“Someone wanted to keep track.”

Most of the words had weathered away. The distinction between an introduced specimen and a native one remained legible. Selene liked whoever had thought that mattered, even here, even with a flower the size of a roof leaning over their work.

They entered along a low causeway. Living jade ran through the roots in seams that glowed only where the bark had split. There was no light in the dead stone. The contrast made the forest seem to breathe in separate places.

Below them, men were loading ore into a cart.

One waved them off the causeway.

“Upper path's gone. Come through the cut.”

His sleeve carried a stitched brown circle. Selene had seen the same mark on the passage notice at Serevra's camp.

Damián saw it too.

“Who opened the cut?”

“Whoever pays you didn't.”

The man swung his hammer against a wet root. The seam beneath it dimmed.

The terrace moved.

Not much: a short, deep settling, enough to send water across the path in a thin sheet. The horses lifted their heads together. Farther down the crater, a shape Selene had mistaken for a grove raised one enormous leg.

It was a hollow trunk standing on four root limbs. Broad stone leaves leaned from an uneven crown. There were no eyes, no mouth, no face hidden in the bark. Green mineral water moved inside it, visible through long splits in its sides.

The raised foot descended onto the terrace below.

The sound passed through Selene's boots.

The cart driver dropped the reins.

“Monarch's moving.”

The man with the hammer struck again.

This time the jade seam beneath him dimmed.

“Get off that part,” Selene said.

He looked at her, then down.

The ground folded around his boot.

Damián caught his collar and hauled him onto a slab of dead rock as the cut opened. A wheel vanished into the gap. Ore spilled after it, clicking against roots that had been carrying the weight of the whole terrace.

Something gold moved beneath the falling stones.

At first Selene thought it was a chain. Then she saw the legs, dozens of them, the gold-coloured chitin plates, and the black underside pressed against a living seam. Two antennae touched the jade. The head turned toward the hammer man's dangling foot.

“Move!”

He crawled back. The creature rushed the length of the seam and struck the rock where he had been. Its plates made a dry metallic clatter. Between two of them a segment brightened.

Damián dragged the man behind the slab.

A last spill of ore struck its back. Two small conduits beneath the plates cracked. A jet hissed from one across the slab's edge; drops landed on the cart shaft and smoked. The bright segment dulled as it emptied. Farther down the body, another stayed charged.

Selene backed the horses toward the laboratory wall. Tizón crowded her. She turned his head away from Brío's flank and found room for both. Tomás cut the packhorse loose from the cart reins in which it had become tangled.

Above the broken terrace, the Monarch lifted another root leg. Three remained planted. A vein near the lifted foot shone brighter.

Blunt mineral leaves thrust upward through the path.

Selene jerked Brío aside. The pack on his off side struck the wall; its wooden frame split, scraping the skin behind his shoulder. He surged forward through a low branch. Leaves and pale green dust burst across his mane.

She lost the lead.

For a second all she could see was his dark body running toward the broken causeway.

“Brío!”

He stopped short of the gap, wheeled, and stood blowing hard among the laboratory columns.

Selene reached him with her heart beating in her teeth. The cut was shallow but dirty. She put her hand beneath his jaw, waited until he allowed her near the shoulder, and used their clean drinking water to wash it.

Behind her the gold creature struck again.

“Khaross,” said the cart driver. His voice shook. “We get the small ones in the drains. Never this.”

On the laboratory wall hung a cracked ceramic diagram: water channels, inspection points, a short drain ending on bare rock. Tomás held it against the actual terraces.

“This is still open,” he said. “Warm water. It could pull it away from the main root.”

The hammer man stared at the glowing plates.

“Break its back. Take the gold.”

“That's an animal,” Damián said.

“So are we.”

The man seized a pick and ran for the higher path.

Selene expected Damián to stop him. He took one step, looked at the horses and the people trapped below the sinking terrace, and stayed.

Tomás found two insulated wedges in the old tool cabinet. The handles were rotten. He replaced them with straps cut from the ruined pack. Selene read the channel marks aloud while he fitted the wedges into slots on either side of the drain's dead-stone outlet. Between them lay an old inspection pocket, roofed with thick slabs. There was no open side for the long body to climb over.

Damián knelt at the old release. Its rivet had seized.

He warmed it with Fire, carefully at first. The terrace settled again. He increased the heat. A strip of old binding flared against his knuckle.

He swore, pulled his hand clear, and tried the lever with the other.

It moved.

Warm water filled the drain.

Khaross's antennae turned. Its head end backed along the seam almost immediately; the long body could not reverse so quickly. It folded against itself in the narrow channel, legs fighting for purchase, then straightened and followed the warmth into the drain.

They waited until the last plate cleared the living root.

“Now,” Tomás said.

Selene drove one wedge down with a stone. The cart driver drove the other. Water struck the barriers and spilled across the bare outlet.

Khaross hit the first wedge, then the second. A segment brightened. Everyone dropped behind the laboratory threshold.

The second cracked conduit emptied against dead rock. Its charged segment faded.

They left it contained there, moving furiously between the wedges. No one reached for its plates.

The extraction valve was still open. The driver, who knew where it was because he had helped open it, crawled along the dry side and closed it. Damián and Tomás cleared the fallen branch choking the second water channel. They worked between the Monarch's steps, stopping each time a root foot lifted, staying away from the seams when they dimmed.

At last the hollow trunk settled. Mineral water rose inside it. A stone leaf detached from the crown and fell into a lower nursery bed with a sound like a door shutting far away.

Nobody cheered.

Their flour was in the sinkhole. One water bag had been pierced. Damián's right hand was blistering. Brío flinched when Selene brought the saddle near his shoulder.

They would walk.

The cart driver gave them a small sack of oats and his name: Estel. He would go back to the inhabited station and report the trapped Khaross before the old wedges failed. Tomás wrote the channel position for him and made him repeat it.

“Why are you doing Ors's work?” Damián asked.

Estel looked at the crushed cart.

“Because the last man who asked that already had enough to eat.”

On the ridge, Selene washed the last visible grains from Brío's shoulder. Under the wet black mane, his skin felt unusually warm. She could not tell whether it was exertion, the scrape, or something else.

She left the saddle off. They took the dry path, with its poorer ore and solid ground, and went slowly into the rain.
