The witness room had three doors and no water.

Selene noticed the water first by its absence. Her shoulders lowered before the rest of her trusted the room.

Nara put a lamp on the table. Leda set down the records. Damián stood near the door with his wrist wrapped again, this time too stiff to close his hand. Nobody asked him to hold anything heavy.

Vesrane brought the mask on its black thread.

Up close, Selene could see repairs at its temple: tiny drilled holes, a fine join, the care of someone who had kept a useful thing working long after it should have been replaced.

“If the central thread is severed,” Vesrane said, “every district loses its borrowed recognitions. People who have been safe for years may be named at the next door they try.”

“We heard you,” Berta said. “Show me mine.”

Nara laid the green folder before her.

Inside was Berta's route appointment, the original page taken from her book, and an account of the name she had borrowed. The owner had died long ago. A niece still lived in the district where the name could open doors.

“Does she know?” Berta asked.

Vesrane looked at the account.

“Her household offered the name for temporary refuge.”

“Then temporary is what I'm asking for.”

Nara found the household's conditions in another drawer. Return required three witnessed copies, one for the traveler, one held in the lending district, and one kept apart from both in case either archive was lost. The rule was written plainly. Later instructions required Vesrane's approval before the copies could be made.

“When was that added?” Selene asked.

Vesrane did not need to check.

“After the first false claim.”

“How many true ones have you refused since?”

The question remained on the table.

Nara summoned two registrars from the neighbouring districts. They arrived unwillingly, then less unwillingly when they saw Simón's hand and Berta's arm. One brought her own district seal. The other had to go back for his because he had assumed they were being asked to witness a punishment.

No light changed when they began.

Berta stated her name. Leda identified the woman she had worked beside. Nara compared the original record with the appointment book. They wrote only what was needed to establish Berta's claim; the lending household's address stayed in a sealed attachment she did not have to carry through an open road.

The woman with the district seal represented the lending household's district; she kept its copy. The second registrar took another for an archive belonging to neither party. Berta held the third.

Then Vesrane showed Nara which small loop connected Berta's token to the central line. They untied that loop alone, maintaining the neighbour loops under tension with two blunt pins. The porcelain went cool. Across the room, the ladder door no longer answered to the borrowed name Berta had worn.

The dry exit remained open.

Berta stood up.

Nothing stopped her.

She sat down again, breathing hard, and pressed the folded page flat with both palms.

Simón went next. He wanted his original name, a witnessed record of the work he had been made to do, and a message to his husband sent through a station he chose himself.

The work account included charges for food, damaged paper, and hours lost after the injury to his hand.

“Cross that out,” he said.

Vesrane reached for a pen.

“Not so it disappears,” Selene said. “So someone can read what you charged him.”

Vesrane drew one line through the amount. Nara copied the original figure into the witness record beside the cancellation.

The other twelve people made twelve decisions. Nine wanted their names returned now. Two wanted to keep borrowed identities while they arranged safe lodging elsewhere. One wanted to leave unregistered, by the slow footpath, and decide later where any record of him should be held.

They did not make him explain in front of the room.

As the copies accumulated, the arrangement grew harder to turn back. The two visiting registrars refused to leave them in Vesrane's sole custody. Nara took responsibility for the dry exit. Leda insisted that no person remain assigned to labour as the condition of a return.

“There will still be false claims,” Vesrane said.

“Then more than one of you hears them,” Berta answered.

It took another hour to write the temporary terms. Return requests would be heard by registrars from three districts, with the traveler present and able to bring a witness. The borrowed names of people choosing concealment would remain protected. Work debts imposed during confinement were suspended and preserved for the outside hearing. The guard who had struck Berta would stay under watch, disarmed. Vesrane's keys and central thread would remain in the room with two registrars until the hearing determined who should hold them.

She could refuse. She looked at the witnesses, the copies already held outside her district, and the people whose work she could no longer pretend had been freely offered.

She placed the keys on the table.

Selene had expected satisfaction to feel warmer.

Instead she was tired, and Berta's arm was still swollen, and a woman at the end of the table had been waiting so long for a name that she could not say it without stopping halfway through.

From the outer gallery came three soft knocks.

Tomás had reached the blocked arch.

“Ready?” he called.

Nara answered with two knocks. The final stones could come out.

Leda divided the bundles between people who had agreed to carry them. Selene took the packet of work accounts, including Damián's signed admission. Inés would decide which orchard records left with her people. No map of the hidden gardens would be copied into the public testimony.

“Your message too,” Leda told Selene.

Selene understood.

She sat down once more and wrote what she had sent through the relay, who had warned her, and who had refused permission. She signed it beneath her own name.

Outside the room, stone scraped against stone.

Then a man's voice said, very close to the old exit, “That's enough.”

The scraping stopped.
