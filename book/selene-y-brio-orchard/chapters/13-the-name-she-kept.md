Carden had put a crossbow against Tomás's chest.

Through the gap in the masonry, Selene could see the weapon, Tomás's pale face, and one course of stone still held beneath the iron brace. Beyond them stood two carts. The nearer one carried empty record chests. The farther held lamp oil.

“Pass the accounts through,” Ors said. “Then the workers.”

Behind Selene, someone began to cry without making a sound.

Leda put a hand against the wall and leaned close to the gap.

“There are copies.”

“There are always copies. The question is who can afford to have read them.”

He pressed the crossbow closer. Tomás kept his hands where Ors could see them.

Damián stood very still beside Selene. She could feel the effort of it in the small space between their shoulders.

Ors's men had found the outside work by following the removed stones. One held the new opening while the other went up to the orchard. The old exit opened into a roofed loading yard, screened from the terraces by hanging weather cloths. Selene could see a strip of daylight under the nearest cloth, and a foot moving behind it.

Someone else was there.

She lifted the lens beneath the fold of her collar, looking only at the dim living shape she had already caught through the torn weave.

One breath. The shape separated from the vines beyond the cloth: a crouched person, close to the wheel of the oil cart.

Second breath. The person shifted. Selene saw the blue sleeve she had helped Inés roll around the seed bundles.

The stone went cold.

She did not know what Inés intended. She knew where she was.

Selene lowered her hand and moved half a step so the gap hid her fingers from Ors. She pointed low, toward the cart, then looked at Leda.

Leda saw the sleeve through the torn cloth for herself.

“You need us to agree,” Leda told Ors.

“I need you to work.”

“Then you've brought too much oil.”

His eyes flicked toward the cart.

Inés pulled its wheel pin.

The wheel tilted outward. The loaded bed dropped, throwing two oil jars across the paving. They broke at Ors's feet.

Tomás struck the crossbow sideways with both hands.

The bolt went into the upper stone of the arch. Damián lunged through the gap and drove his shoulder into Ors before the man could draw back. They hit the yard floor together.

The guard at the opening swung his club.

Selene ducked behind the wall. The blow caught the iron brace. A crack opened around its old fastening. The last course of masonry shifted toward the people waiting beneath it.

“Back!” she shouted.

The guard reached through for the packet under her arm.

She let him take it.

He stumbled when the weight came free. Berta pushed a record chest into his knees. He fell across the low opening, and Nara and two workers pulled the chest after him until it blocked his way back.

The brace was slipping.

Tomás could not lift the stones from the outside; Damián and Ors were still fighting beside the broken jars. Leda found a length of timber and drove it beneath the course. Simón and another worker took the far end.

Selene could see the small fastening splitting around its pin. If it gave, the brace would turn before the timber had the load.

She put her fingers against the two sides of the crack.

Foundation came reluctantly. She was cold, frightened, and already tired. She found the metal's weight the way Iolen had taught her, brought the broken edges into relation, and held them there.

One heartbeat.

Leda shifted the timber.

Two.

Simón's injured hand slipped. The other worker took more of the weight.

Three.

The tremor began in Selene's thumb.

“Now,” she said.

They lifted. Tomás dragged the lowest stone out of the opening.

Four.

Selene let go and fell back against the wall. The fastening split. The brace turned, striking the timber instead of the people beneath it. Leda and the workers held until Tomás pulled them clear.

The stone came down with a sound that filled the hall.

Dust hid the opening. Selene could not make her right hand stop shaking.

Then daylight appeared at floor level.

“One at a time,” Tomás said from the other side. “We've got room.”

The dropped brace had left a low passage beneath the surviving arch. People crawled through on their hands and knees, passing records before them. Berta went back for the woman who had begun crying. Simón waited until the last worker from his platform had crossed.

Outside, Damián had lost his grip on Ors. His injured hand hung uselessly against his coat. Ors rolled clear and reached for a fallen lamp.

Inés struck his wrist with the wheel pin.

The lamp fell unbroken.

Marín came down from the terrace and kicked it away. Dora followed with two road officers, breathless from the northern path. One officer caught Ors's arm while the other moved between the oil and the people emerging from the arch.

“Carden Ors,” the first said. “Put your hands on the cart.”

Ors looked at Damián.

“Your seal is on the road.”

Damián got to his feet slowly.

“My statement is in two people's hands.”

The guard with Selene's packet tried to run. Leda pointed him out. A worker caught his ankle; the second officer took the packet from him before he could reach the jars, then bound him beside Ors. Above the yard, the remaining hired men saw residents gathering behind the officers. Their employer was disarmed and the narrow stair gave them no way to surround the group. They put down their clubs when ordered, one after another.

By the time Selene crawled into the yard, the crossbow was on the ground and Ors's hands were bound. There was blood on Damián's lower lip. Inés stood beside the broken cart, still holding the pin.

Selene sat on the paving because her legs had stopped offering alternatives.

Damián crouched a little distance away.

“Can I?” he asked, holding out his left hand.

She took it.

The officers closed the loading yard and took statements before allowing Ors's men to leave. Dora had reached them with the false road closure and Leda's written account. They had come to investigate, not because anyone had promised a battle would end just as they arrived. Inés's signal from the terrace had brought them down the final steps.

Their presence changed who could use the roads that evening. It did not decide the hearings.

Selene gave her statement beneath the ruined arch. She described the relay, the objection she had overridden, the people her call had exposed. Damián described the inspection he had signed without doing. The officer wrote both down in the same plain hand.

Above them, the orchard began to empty.

Berta chose the inhabited station and a message to her sister: alive, leaving, address to follow when she had chosen one. Simón asked for his husband to meet him there. Dora volunteered to accompany them. Marín would stay long enough to move the stores, then take paid work rebuilding the public road under a different contractor. Teo wanted to remain with the gardeners until he could walk without the crutch.

Leda wrote her mother three sentences. She read them once, sealed them, and gave them to the officer herself.

She did not include the route.

At the paddock, Brío came to the gate when Selene called. Salvat had loosened the shade cloth to let the evening air through. The horse's coat was still mostly dark; the little pale tuft beneath the mane looked too small to belong to everything people might one day say about it.

Selene rested her forehead against the uninjured side of his neck.

Beyond the wall, the orchard's mixed trees lowered the last of the daylight over people packing their homes. Some would come back. Inés had not decided whether she would be one of them.

Selene stayed until Brío moved away to eat.
