Ors began with the stores.

He did not shout. He put two men beside the feed room, two at the old trunk, and one at the stair to the Palace. Then he opened the folding desk and laid out claims against the orchard's residents.

The neatness of the papers made the men with clubs seem like an administrative detail.

Inés went to the feed room.

“The animals eat first.”

The guard put a hand against the door.

She looked at the hand until he called for Ors.

“Essential provisions will continue,” Ors said, loudly enough for everyone to hear.

The guard moved. Inés filled the buckets without thanking him.

Across the terrace, Marín reached for a cart's harness. A guard twisted his wrist behind his back and pushed him against the stone wall. Selene heard the impact. Damián moved, but Leda caught his sleeve.

“Look at the other two.”

The guards by the workroom had lifted their clubs. Dora stood in the doorway behind them, holding Teo's crutch.

Damián stopped. His face had gone pale beneath the weathering.

“Let him go,” he said.

Ors looked up from the desk.

“Will you guarantee his account?”

“Show it to me.”

It was more than Marín had earned in two seasons. Half was equipment depreciation. A third was interest. At the bottom was a certification that the route crew had accepted the eastern approach as inspected and safe.

Damián's seal stood beneath it.

He read the page while the guard held Marín against the wall.

“I certified the approach,” he said at last. “Not this debt.”

“The debt follows the work.”

“Then release him against my claim until it's heard.”

Ors considered the watching residents.

“Generous.”

“Recorded,” Damián said. “Put it in writing.”

Ors did. The guard let Marín go. Selene saw him hide the shaking hand beneath his opposite arm.

For the moment, the work party stayed where it was. Ors wanted their labour, their stores, and an agreement that would keep the orchard paying after his men left. A dead resident would help none of those purposes. An injured one could make the rest hurry.

By afternoon he had postponed the transfer until the following dawn, offering it as proof of his reasonableness. Guards watched the wide paths. Ors had too few men to hold every turn of the steep northern trail as well as the stores and loading ramp. He chose the places where carts could move and put one patrol on the upper steps.

Dora left by that trail with food, a short written warning, and no names except her own. Inés took her through the seed beds to join it above the patrol, a path too steep to carry the injured quickly.

Selene watched until the leaves hid her.

“She won't use the relay,” Inés said.

“I know.”

“Do you?”

Selene turned to face her.

“You said no. I gave them the route anyway.”

Inés kept rolling the seed cloth.

“Berta needed help.”

“Yes.”

“That doesn't settle it.”

“No.”

Inés handed her another empty roll.

“Blue cabinet. Only the loose packets. We leave the rooted plants.”

Selene went to work.

At dusk Damián gathered the people whose names had appeared beneath his seal. He brought no chair for himself.

He had inspected the western approach and the separate relay boards at the station. The eastern survey was delayed. He had signed the certificate for both approaches to keep the crew's contract from being cancelled, intending to correct it after the second inspection. That signature let Ors assign six couriers to the uninspected cut and charge them for abandoning it. The later joining of the relay channels was Ors's change; certifying a road he had not walked was Damián's.

“Then the rains came,” he said. “Then I was sent elsewhere. Then Leda refused the next certificate, and I told her we'd resolve it when the route reopened.”

Leda looked at him.

“You told me I was making the crew look unreliable.”

He shut his eyes briefly.

“Yes.”

“Say that part too.”

He did.

Selene sat at the end of the table, hearing the rain shelter's unfinished answer arrive too late. She could understand every step that had seemed small to him. She could also see the paper Ors had unfolded, and Marín's wrist against the wall.

“I'll withdraw the certificate before the watch,” Damián said. “I'll testify to what I didn't inspect. I can't lead the return on the strength of it.”

He wrote the admission twice. Leda kept one copy; Nara would place the other among the witnesses' records. Then he set his route seal on the table.

Nobody reached for it.

“Keep your tool,” Leda said. “We need you to know when you're allowed to use it.”

They planned without giving anyone the whole room.

Inés would move people able to manage the northern trail toward a sheltered rest camp. Dora would guide Ada's party back by that route if she reached them. Nara would find the individual record cords for Berta and Simón, and ask which other confined workers wanted their records returned. Tomás would measure the blocked arch from both sides.

Damián would help open the old exit. Selene would enter the registry again. Leda, who knew the loading schedules and the people they described, would decide when the work parties moved.

“You still need Vesrane,” Nara said.

“To agree?” Selene asked.

“To keep the other names protected. If you cut the central thread, every borrowed identity goes with it.”

“Then we don't cut it.”

Nara looked at the table: bruised wrists, a crutch, soil beneath Inés's nails, Damián's hand wrapped in cloth.

“That is the slower way.”

“Show us,” Leda said.

After the meeting, Damián caught up with Selene beside the paddock.

“I should have told you in the rain.”

She rested her hand on the gate.

“You should have told Leda before that.”

“I know.”

Brío came across the grass. He put his nose through the bars and searched the place where Selene sometimes kept a carrot. She scratched his forehead.

“I like being with you,” she told Damián. The admission surprised them both. “That made it easier to stop asking.”

“I don't want you to stop.”

“You did that night.”

He looked toward the occupied terraces.

“Yes.”

She waited. He offered no improved version.

At last she opened the gate and went in to check the horses. He stayed outside until she asked for the basin.
