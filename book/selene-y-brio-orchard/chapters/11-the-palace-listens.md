The door with the cup would not open for Selene.

She had hoped the token might admit her from the other side of the gallery. It did not. The painted ladder on its edge remained a ladder, however urgently she held it against the lintel.

Beyond the door she could hear carts moving.

“We go around,” Nara said.

“There's water all the way to the next landing.”

“Look below the reflection of that lamp.”

Selene crouched. In the black water, the reflected side gallery showed a narrow return ledge hidden from her standing view by the door's projecting frame. She found its real edge with her boot, then the wall with her hand.

They went sideways, slowly, the records they had brought wrapped above the waterline. Leda led. Nara followed. Damián came last, holding the rope with his left hand. His right stayed close to his chest.

The loading hall was awake.

Fourteen workers stood beside low carts. Berta held one handle. Simón had a folded cloth beneath his injured hand. Two guards watched them from the dry platform.

Vesrane stood beside a cabinet of original records.

“You were told to wait,” she said.

“So were the carts,” Nara answered.

For the first time since Selene had met her, Vesrane looked uncertain which person was speaking to whom.

Leda went directly to Berta.

“Can you walk?”

“Yes.”

“Do you still want to leave?”

Berta put down the handle.

The nearest guard stepped between them. Damián moved to stand beside Leda. No weapon, no raised hand. The guard looked at the bandage and chose that side.

He drove his staff into Damián's wrist.

Damián's breath left him in a hard sound. With his left hand he seized the staff near its middle and pushed it away. The guard stumbled against the record cabinet.

A drawer flew open.

The green folder fell. Its old cord, already broken once, came loose.

Ripples crossed the loading hall.

“Dry stone!” Selene shouted.

The first reflected blow struck up from the water beneath the guard. He fell against the platform, dragging Berta with him. Damián's answering shove came back from the other side, moving the fallen staff along the same line he had driven it before.

The staff hit a cart wheel. The cart tipped.

Selene could see three places to run and no safe way to reach all of them. She caught Berta's open hand. Her boot slipped on the edge. Nara seized the back of her belt and held while Berta found a foothold against the stone.

They pulled her up together.

Simón had got two workers onto the opposite landing. Leda dragged the cloth bundle of records clear of the flood. The second guard lifted his staff.

“Do that and we'll all have it twice,” Damián said.

The guard hesitated.

“Put it down. Come onto the stone.”

He did.

In the water, the blows repeated once more along their old paths. Selene saw the ripple first, then the raised shape, then the staff's pale line crossing the place she had nearly stepped. Her gift showed living people against the dim gallery. It showed nothing that made the repeated blows safe.

Nara leaned out with a long record hook. She caught the green folder by its cover and drew it onto the platform. Vesrane fastened its cord with a short length taken from her sleeve.

The water settled.

For several breaths nobody moved.

Then Berta began to shake.

Selene sat beside her without letting go of the hand. Across the hall, Damián pressed his injured wrist against his coat. He was looking at the fallen guard, who lay half in the water, breathing.

“Help him,” Vesrane said.

“We will,” Leda answered. “He stays where we can see him afterward.”

They got him onto dry stone and took both staffs away.

Nara opened the second cabinet with an ordinary key. Inside were small bundles of cord, a register of witnesses, and the district markers that matched the borrowed tokens.

“You know how to return a record without taking down the others,” she said to Vesrane.

“I know what happens outside when it is returned.”

“Then tell the people who have to live there.”

Vesrane looked at the fourteen workers, some still barefoot in the cold, and at the guard whose blow the water had repeated without improving it.

Selene waited for a speech. None came.

“The witness room,” Vesrane said. “Bring the individual records. Keep the central thread fastened.”

“And the work accounts?” Simón asked.

She turned toward him.

“We will examine—”

“I asked to leave twelve days ago.”

The hall stayed quiet.

“Bring those too,” Vesrane said.

The dry route was narrow. They went one at a time, carrying the people who could not manage it, passing each bundle from hand to hand. At the stair's turn, Selene looked back.

The water held the empty carts and the high pale walls. There was no sign in it of what anyone had meant to do.
