# The Light She Could Not See · continuation bible

Status: **independent story continuity**, 25 September 2026. The original twelve chapter illustrated novella is preserved separately in `THE_LIGHT_SHE_COULD_NOT_SEE.md`; chapters 13–16 in `THE_OPEN_ROAD.md` are the opening of a proposed second arc. This Selene is twenty-nine, unlike the nineteen-year-old Selene in the locked _Las Tierras de Luz_ novel. Shared names and locations do not make these events canonical to that novel.

## Series decision

Selene Velara remains the protagonist. Her perceptiveness does not make her responsible for everybody's feelings or authorize her to read them without permission. Brío remains a horse with judgment, fatigue, training, and preferences. The Academy is a community of practice, with real costs and working instructors; advancement through the Gates is earned. The new maker, Cael Rovan, is a fictional counterpart with his own craft, shame, work, and relationships. He can offer company, never a guarantee of a shared destiny.

**Promise:** a woman learns that she can be deeply known without surrendering her freedom; a maker learns that a brilliant bridge has failed when the people it serves cannot refuse it. Each person grows through a separate apprenticeship. Their attraction develops as a consequence of what they do together and what each does when the other is absent.

**Series question:** when you can see a possible path, who gets to decide whether anyone walks it?

**Spiritual grammar:** faith is practiced through honest attention, difficult work, restitution, rest, and willingness to act without certainty. Synchronicity provides an invitation to investigate, not proof of a prophecy or a person's consent. There are no magical shortcuts to mutual love.

## Production rules

- **Magic:** keep Gates, ranks, Wisdoms, Anima, and Luminors aligned with `.arcanea/lore/CANON_LOCKED.md`. No new divine identity, Gate, or cosmic law is introduced. New devices, town names, river guard, and characters remain proposed local fiction.
- **Encounter design:** the human opponent has a material objective; ecology has cause and consequence; the machinery has prior maintenance history. A boss can be stopped, repaired, or contained. The jaguar is wildlife, not a pet or a morality detector.
- **Love story:** no character diagnoses the other from a colored aura; attraction is not framed as supernatural certainty. A refusal, delay, or independent journey stands without an emotional penalty. Pleasure and wit appear among crises: meals, river crossings, faulty tools, riding lessons, and imperfect messages home.
- **Art continuity:** the 24 illustrations extracted from the original novella consistently show Selene with dark wavy hair, ivory blouse, red sash and small hoop earrings, and Brío as a dark bay with a white forehead star. External Llanos experiments often portray a different woman or horse. They are reference studies, not chapter art. The new river crossing key art matches the original silhouettes and is a proposed Book II cover.
- **Page experience:** read the whole twelve chapter original before the proposed continuation; keep keyboard chapter anchors, meaningful alt text, mobile typography, and reduced motion support. Use original illustrations at chapter positions; no artificial quote from a real person or fictionalized personal history in site copy.
- **Next writing task:** revise units 1–4 together for causality and river topology, then draft the House Terra hearing with the people harmed by the earlier accident. Commission further images only after the scene continuity check (costume, horse star, topology, who physically acts).

## Privacy and editorial boundary

The central relationship draws on universal experiences of recognition, distance, faith, work, and release. Selene and Cael are not depictions of private individuals. Do not use any private person's name, biography, verbatim conversations, or identifiable messages in public copy, prompts, metadata, or marketing. A reader should be able to love Selene's independent life without knowing the author's life.
