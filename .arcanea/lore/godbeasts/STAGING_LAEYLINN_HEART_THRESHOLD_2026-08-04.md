## Canon-bound proposal

Laeylinn should not arrive as a reward for suffering or an examiner who demands a prettier confession. The Worldtree Deer is more difficult than that. Her tenderness is strong enough to leave a person free.

When she is near, the first evidence is not light. It is **room**: a thicket gives the frightened person one clear place to stand; a path that looked narrow gains a shoulder-width of earth; a held breath finds its own end. This is not a new power system. It is the story-language of a being whose existing gift is to heal cracks and join living things without corruption.

She does not pull pain out of a seeker, translate it, or make it sacred. She makes it possible for the seeker to stop carrying it alone. If they refuse the offered connection, she does not punish them. The clearing remains. The choice remains.

### Encounter limits

1. **Consent precedes contact.** Laeylinn may wait, withdraw, or lower her head; she does not enter a nervous system, form a bond, or grant a Resonance Bloom by spectacle or command.
2. **The Heart Gate is not absolution.** A truth spoken beneath her bough-antlers may begin repair; it does not erase harm, grief, accountability, or the work owed to another person.
3. **Nero remains welcome mystery.** The dark between Laeylinn's branches is deep, fertile, and unthreatening. If the dark hungers, flattens choice, or asks for surrender, it is Shadow—Malachar's corruption—not Nero.
4. **Her scale serves intimacy.** Even when her antlers read like a forest against the sky, the decisive action is small: a hand unclenched, a name said plainly, a person allowed to stay.

## Scene seed: *The Place She Did Not Take*

The first root broke the soil beside the traveller's boot without touching it.

They had been walking since the sky was still grey, keeping the narrow path because narrowness was easier to explain than fear. On the left, the ground fell away into a hollow full of fern-shadow. On the right, thorn branches leaned across one another with the patient purpose of old hands. The traveller had been telling themself that the path was too small for two people, though there was no second person with them.

The root lifted a little more earth, made a low shelf of it, and stopped.

Nothing asked them to step there.

Beyond the thorns, something breathed. Not loudly. The sound was green wood in rain, a sound that had been continuing before anyone arrived and would continue after. When the traveller looked, they saw an antler between the leaves, then another, each one wider than a doorway and threaded with pale crystal that held the last light without trying to own it.

Laeylinn did not come closer.

The traveller thought of every person who had ever said *tell me*, and of how each invitation had carried a hook somewhere inside it. They put one hand over the old place beneath their ribs where the answer had learned to hide.

The deer lowered her head. A leaf fell from the nearest branch and landed on the new shelf of earth. It was not an offering. It did not need to be.

For a long while the traveller said nothing. The dark under the trees made room for that silence. It was a good dark: rich as turned soil, full of things not yet formed. No voice rose from it. No bargain waited there.

At last the traveller stepped off the path.

The ground held.

— I have been calling control love — they said.

The words were smaller than the thorns. They changed nothing visible. No wound closed. No antler blazed. Somewhere farther down the slope, water kept finding stone.

Laeylinn looked at the traveller with black, ordinary animal eyes. Then she turned her head toward the path behind them.

It was wider now, but only by the width of one person walking beside another.
