## Locked anchors held unchanged

- **Yumiko** is the Godbeast of the Sight Gate, bonded to **Lyria**; its gift refracts perception rather than light and reveals hidden intentions and things-as-they-are.
- The **Sight Gate** is the sixth Gate at **639 Hz**, governing intuition, vision, and perception.
- The corrupted Mirror Depths show Sight's failure mode: not lack of vision, but paralysis from seeing too much.
- **Lyria** teaches that sight without courage is another form of blindness.
- **Nero** remains fertile mystery and potential. Shadow is Malachar's corruption of Void, not an alternative name for mystery.

## STAGING encounter grammar: sight that leaves agency intact

Yumiko does not arrive to solve a person. It creates a condition in which a person can no longer pretend that their choice has no cost.

1. **The first image is useful, not flattering.** A seeker sees the story that protects them: the excuse, the heroic self-portrait, the enemy made simple. Yumiko does not mock it. The image is often partly true, which is why it holds.
2. **The second image is relational.** The Prism turns from the seeker toward the person who will bear the consequence. The revelation is not a verdict; it restores a missing witness.
3. **The third image does not arrive.** Yumiko withholds the future. It will reveal intention and present consequence, but it will not make responsibility painless by certifying an outcome.
4. **The threshold ends with an answerable question.** The seeker leaves with a choice they can speak aloud, not a prophecy they can hide behind.

This grammar keeps Yumiko frightening without making it punitive. Its unusual trait is restraint: a being able to expose every surface refuses to turn private truth into spectacle.

## Scene seed: *The Reflection That Waited*

The bowl of water between them was shallow enough to hold in two hands.

"It is only water," the apprentice said.

The guide did not look at the bowl. "Then tell it the lie you brought here."

The apprentice gave a tired laugh. "I came because I want to help."

The surface lifted without wind. In it, the apprentice saw a door barred from the inside, then their own hand tightening the latch. Behind the door, a voice called their name once and stopped.

"That is not what happened," they said.

Yumiko stood at the far edge of the chamber where no doorway had been. Nothing about the Godbeast's outline stayed long enough to become a fact. Only the water was certain: it held the apprentice's face with an attention no mirror had ever managed.

The guide said, "What did you protect?"

"Everyone." The word came quickly.

The water did not change. It waited.

At last the apprentice pressed both palms to the stone. "Myself. I protected myself from hearing them ask."

Yumiko lowered its head. The bowl showed no future, no absolution, no reward for saying the right thing. It showed a wet road at dawn and the door waiting to be opened.

"Will they forgive me?" the apprentice asked.

The guide's voice softened. "That is not the question Sight can answer."

The water became water again.

## Text-only visual direction — unauthorized for generation

**Purpose:** A future human-approved visual brief may depict Yumiko only as an encounter with perception, not as a fixed new creature design.

- **Composition:** An intimate, low-angle threshold scene. One human-scale figure faces a small basin or rain-polished stone; Yumiko is a peripheral sacred-beast presence, never a collectible mascot or combat mount.
- **Material and light:** Cool cosmic blue reflected through still water, restrained white-gold at the edge of an unseen horizon, living crystal and wet stone. The world should feel museum-grade and inhabited, never glossy plastic.
- **Perception effect:** No literal hologram feed, eye-beams, or infinite face collage. Let a reflection hold a subtly different emotional posture than the person above it.
- **Guardrails:** Luxury cosmic myth-tech; noble, animal-first presence; no neon, generic fantasy robes, anime-bright saturation, superhero spandex, or text baked into the image.
