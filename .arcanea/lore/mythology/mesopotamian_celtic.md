## 1. Overview & Meta-Context
*   **Primary Sources**: 
    *   *Mesopotamian*: The *Epic of Gilgamesh* (c. 2100 BCE), *Enuma Elish* (Babylonian creation myth), and cuneiform clay tablets.
    *   *Celtic*: The *Mabinogion* (Welsh), *Lebor Gabála Érenn* ("The Book of the Taking of Ireland"), and ancient Ogham inscriptions.
*   **Scale**: The fertile Euphrates/Tigris silt basins and towering ziggurats; the mist-shrouded green hills, standing stones, and deep oceans of the Celtic fringe.
*   **North Star**: Civilization decrees and shifting seasonal tides. Mesopotamian lore focuses on carving order from watery chaos via divine laws (*Me*); Celtic lore focuses on the paper-thin boundary between the material world and the magical Otherworld.

## 2. Cosmological Architecture
Reality is anchored by stone monuments and shifting boundaries:
*   **Mesopotamian Domain**:
    *   *Apsu & Tiamat*: The sweet freshwater ocean (Apsu) and bitter saltwater ocean (Tiamat) that mingled to form the cosmos.
    *   *Mount of Heaven*: The cosmic ziggurat connecting Earth to heaven.
    *   *Kur / Irkalla*: The subterranean land of the dead, a dry, dusty underworld where all souls eat dust and drink clay, ruled by Ereshkigal.
*   **Celtic Domain**:
    *   *The Otherworld*: A parallel dimension of magic, music, and eternal youth, existing alongside the physical world.
    *   *Tír na nÓg (Land of the Young)*: The heavenly island paradise in the western sea.
    *   *Annwn*: The Welsh Otherworld, a realm of abundance, magic, and transition.
    *   *Sídhe Mounds*: Ancient earth mounds (burial chambers) that serve as physical portal thresholds to the Otherworld.

## 3. Magic & Power Mechanics
*   **The Me (Mesopotamian Decrees)**: The *Me* are the absolute, physical laws of civilization and cosmic order created by the gods. They exist as physical tablets or objects (e.g., the Table of Destinies). Wielding a *Me* grants a mage absolute control over that aspect of reality (e.g., the *Me of Blacksmithing*, the *Me of Truth*, the *Me of Magic*).
*   **Geas (Celtic Taboo)**: A sacred, individualized vow or taboo placed upon a hero or mage. Observing a Geas grants immense power, luck, and magic resistance; breaking a Geas leads to immediate misfortune and certain death (e.g., Prince Cú Chulainn's Geas to never eat dog meat).
*   **Ogham Script**: The ancient Celtic alphabet of notches and lines carved into wood or stone, associated with trees. Druids carve Ogham characters to summon natural forces, create defensive wards, or communicate silently.
*   **Awen (Inspiration)**: The fluid, divine spark of creative inspiration, poetry, and magic (Voice/Flow Gate).

## 4. Extensive Glossary of Gods, Texts, and Concepts
*   **Mesopotamian Pantheon (Anunnaki)**:
    *   *Anu*: The supreme sky god, lord of heaven.
    *   *Enlil*: God of wind, atmosphere, and kingly decrees (Wind Gate).
    *   *Enki (Ea)*: God of freshwater, crafts, wisdom, and magic (Tide Gate).
    *   *Inanna (Ishtar)*: Goddess of love, war, and fertility, who descended to the underworld and returned.
    *   *Marduk*: The patron god of Babylon, who slew Tiamat and forged the heavens from her body.
    *   *Gilgamesh*: The demigod king of Uruk who sought immortality after the death of his friend Enkidu.
*   **Celtic Pantheon (Tuatha Dé Danann)**:
    *   *Danu*: The primeval mother goddess, from whom the Tuatha Dé Danann (People of the Goddess Danu) descend.
    *   *The Dagda (The Good God)*: The chief deity, who wields a massive club (which can kill and resurrect) and a cauldron of infinite abundance (Earth Gate).
    *   *Lugh Samildánach*: The sun god of many skills, master of arts and wielder of a fiery spear (Forge/Starweave Gate).
    *   *The Morrígan*: The triple goddess of war, fate, and death, who appears as a crow over battlefields.
    *   *Nuada Airgetlám*: The first king of the Tuatha, who wore a hand of silver after losing his own.

## 5. Extensive Glossary of Creatures & Monsters
*   **Lamassu (Mesopotamian)**: A protective deity depicted with the body of a bull or lion, the wings of an eagle, and the head of a human. They guard city gates and temple thresholds (Earth/Wind).
*   **Sirrush (Mesopotamian)**: The dragon of Babylon, depicted with scaling body, hind legs of an eagle, forelegs of a lion, and a horned snake head.
*   **Humbaba (Mesopotamian)**: The giant guardian of the Cedar Forest, whose roar is like a flood and breath is fire.
*   **Banshee (Celtic)**: A female wailing spirit of the Otherworld. Her mournful cry at night foretells the immediate death of someone in a family.
*   **Cú Sídhe (Celtic)**: A massive, dark green wolf that roams the highlands, capable of carrying souls to the Otherworld.
*   **Kelpie (Celtic)**: A shapeshifting water horse that tempts travelers to ride it, only to drag them into the depths of a loch to devour them.
