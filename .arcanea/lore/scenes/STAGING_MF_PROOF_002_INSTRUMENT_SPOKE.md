# Proof Scene — The Instrument That Spoke Too Soon

> **STAGING.** Camera: Emilia. Pressure: Train / Witness / **Refuse failed then repaired**.

---

House Synthesis smells like warm metal, citrus solvent, and the sweet dust of ground crystal. The practice bay is not beautiful the way Lumina chapels are beautiful. It is beautiful the way a well-made hinge is beautiful: because it admits what it is for.

Emilia’s field kit lies open on the bench like a confession. Lenses nested in repaired brass. A strip of Laeylinn-green jade no larger than a thumb, wired with hair-fine Shael thread. She has polished the thumb-jade three times this morning. Polish is easier than prayer.

“You don’t have to,” Arion says.

He stands where she asked him to stand — inside a chalk circle that is not a cage and somehow still feels like one. Mera sits on the window ledge with her boots off, as if bare feet can keep a room honest. The proctor, Master Kelth, pretends to review a slate while watching them with the bright hunger of someone who has waited years for a pattern like this.

“I know,” Emilia says. She smiles with half her mouth. “That’s why I keep offering. If I needed you to, it would already be a bad tool.”

Master Kelth clears his throat. “For the record: voluntary Confluence mapping under Synthesis safety protocol. Output restricted to instructional staff.”

Emilia’s fingers pause on a latch. “Restricted how?”

“Displayed to this bay. Logged to House archive. Redacted for public curriculum upon review.”

“Logged,” Mera repeats, soft. The word has weather in it.

Arion looks at Emilia, not the proctor. “If it shows something ugly, do I get to decide who keeps the picture?”

Emilia wants to say *yes* the way inventors say yes — as a feature. She wants the instrument to be a door with a handle on both sides. She has etched a tiny exit rune into the gauntlet’s inner plate for exactly that fantasy.

“Yes,” she says. “You can shut it down. Mid-read. That’s the point.”

She fits the gauntlet. It is heavier than it looks. Good tools should have weight; weight reminds you they can drop.

“Breathe,” she tells him. “Don’t perform.”

Arion breathes like someone repairing a beam: measure, settle, bear.

The jade warms. Lenses iris. For a moment the air tastes like rain on stone — Foundation, clean and old. Then another taste threads through it, thin as river-metal, and another like struck glass. The gauntlet’s smallest lens flares not white but a braided seam of colors that do not agree to be one flag.

Master Kelth stands up too fast. His slate hits the floor and does not break. “There. There — multi-domain signature. Do you see the lattice?”

“I see it,” Emilia says, and her voice is proud before she can stop it. Pride is a leak.

On the bay’s wall plate, the pattern blooms larger than her private lens. House archive light winks: **LOGGING**.

Arion flinches — not from power. From being seen.

“Shut it down,” he says.

Emilia hits the exit plate.

The private lenses die.

The wall plate does not.

“Kelth—” She’s already moving. “Kill the bay mirror.”

He does not move. His thumb is on his slate. A soft confirm-chime — too small for the damage it does.

“Safety mirror is House channel,” he says, still staring at the braid like it is a rare moth. “I mirrored a still to instructional review. Three frames. Not the stream.”

Emilia rips the Shael lead with her bare fingers. It bites. Blood beads. The wall plate stutters, holds a ghost of the braid, then collapses into gray — too late. The stills are already gone from the bay and into a queue she does not control.

Solvent drips. Once. Twice.

Arion’s hands are fists. Not threatening. Containing.

“You said I could shut it down.”

“You could shut *mine*.” Emilia’s voice cracks on the ownership. “I built a door in my glove. I forgot the room had its own mouth.”

Mera is off the ledge without sound. “Pull the stills.”

“They’re stamped instructional,” Kelth says. Color high. “I can request purge. I cannot promise the White Ledger isn’t listening to instructional. You know that. You built half their favorite toys.”

Emilia’s laugh is ugly and short. She sets the ruined lead on brass; blood makes a coin of itself.

“Arion.” She makes herself look at him without designing the sentence. “I will file the purge. I will name myself in the fault log. I will rebuild so a room cannot speak when a person says stop. If you never stand in my circle again, I still rebuild it.”

He does not loosen his fists yet. “Who has the three frames tonight?”

Kelth swallows. “Review queue. Possibly two eyes. Possibly a listening script.”

“Then we are already late,” Mera says. Not poetry. Inventory.

Arion’s fists open one finger at a time. “I don’t need you perfect. I need you to stop being surprised when the hinge becomes a latch.”

Emilia nods once. No clever line left.

On the bench the thumb-jade cools. Somewhere above the bay, a queue light that none of them can see finishes writing Arion’s braid into a file with a number instead of a name.
