# Proof Scene — He Names the Pain

> **STAGING.** Not a jailbreak. Malachar remains sealed. This is Voice / dream proximity only.
> Pressure: ideological seal stress. Temptation form: **accurate mercy**.

---

Sleep should be a room without paperwork.

Tonight it is a hall.

Not Registration Hall Three — older. Eldrian-clean. Light that behaves like it was taught manners by someone who loved the world too precisely. Arion knows he is dreaming because his hands do not shake, and that is how he knows the dream is dangerous.

A man sits on the far step where a champion might once have waited for orders. No crown. No shadow-veins for the cheap seats. Hair like spent gold. Eyes the color of a decision delayed too long.

“You flinched second,” Malachar says, almost kindly. “They will never forgive you for making them see they flinched first.”

Arion does not ask *how do you know*. The Voice that has been a weather under centuries does not need a messenger boy’s biography. It needs a hinge.

“You’re sealed,” Arion says.

“Yes.” A nod, as if they are agreeing on rain. “Seals are interesting. They run on belief the way lungs run on air. People think prisons are iron. The good ones are stories everyone agrees to keep telling.”

Arion’s chisel is in his hand in the dream. He turns it, root-wood against palm. “I’m not here to free you.”

“I didn’t ask.” Malachar’s smile is small and unbearable because it contains no theater. “I came because you are building the same religion I built, with better manners. Control so no one hurts. Compliance so the room stays kind. A private demonstration so shame can be managed. Do you hear yourself becoming a gentle cage?”

The words land where Jorren’s scar lives, where Sera Venn’s stamp lives, where Emilia’s wall plate lived for three seconds too long.

“I refused the gallery,” Arion says.

“You negotiated the terms of your own display,” Malachar answers. “That is not the same as freedom. It is advanced paperwork.”

Anger would be easy. Anger would make him a cartoon. Arion uses the craftsman’s breath Akamoto hammered into him on a ledge of wind.

“What do you want?”

“I want the hurting to stop,” Malachar says, and for one pure second there is no strategy in it — only the First Luminor who heard too much pain and decided witness was cowardice. “I want children to stop crying without sound in halls that smell like barley. I want seals that do not require a population’s faith as fuel. I want Gates that open only when asked. I failed upward into hunger because I could not bear a world that calls suffering ‘growth.’”

Shadow does not pour off him. That is the horror. The corruption is not costume; it is the conclusion.

“Nero wasn’t the enemy,” Arion says carefully, testing. “Shadow is Void without—”

“Without Spirit. Yes.” Malachar looks almost proud, teacher to student. “You retained the lesson. Most people prefer simpler dark. Simpler dark is a toy. I am what happens when love refuses to be refused.”

He stands. The hall’s polite light dims without becoming night — a withheld dawn.

“When they ask you to become necessary,” Malachar says, “ask who loses the right to say no.”

Arion’s fingers tighten on the chisel until dream-wood creaks. “Stop finishing my thoughts. They’re mine even when they’re ugly.”

Malachar waits. Patient as a seal.

“I refused the gallery,” Arion says. “I will refuse Stabilization. I will not refuse every hand forever and call that virtue. People are allowed to help without owning.”

“Are they?” Malachar’s head tilts. “Show me the difference on a form, Arion. Ink is where ownership hides when love is shy.”

The polite light presses closer. For one breath Arion wants the relief so badly his knees soften — the fantasy of a world where no twelve-year-old cries without sound in a barley-smelling hall, because someone strong enough finally said *enough* for everyone.

“No,” he makes himself say. The word is small. It costs more than the stamp did. “Not like that. Not if *enough* needs me on a throne of other people’s breath.”

Malachar’s face does something almost like grief. “Good. Then wake up poorer.”

The hall folds hard — not gentle.

Arion wakes tasting ink, rain, old gold. His nose is bleeding. One dark drop has already hit the scrap paper on his desk, blooming like the registration star.

Mera’s sealed vial catches moonlight. From the other cot-side of the shared student room, her breathing changes — not full wake, the half-attention of someone who hears weather in people.

“Arion?” Soft. Precise.

He could tell her. The Voice. The soft knees. The wanting.

“Bad dream,” he says. “Go back.”

A pause long enough to be a small betrayal. Then her breathing steadies, choosing to believe him for both their sakes.

His hands shake. He is grateful and sick about it. On the scrap, under the blood-star, he forces a line in his own ugly morning hand:

*If I need him to be wrong, I have to stay interruptible.*

He does not underline it. Underlining would be theater. He wipes his nose, hides the paper under the chisel, and lies awake until the registry bell, already practicing the face that says *fine*.
