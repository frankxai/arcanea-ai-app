## The Core Tension You Keep Hitting

You want **maximum platform value** with **zero legal surface area**. These two goals are in direct opposition once you host user content, process payments, or intermediate IP. Every feature you described — chat UI, social feed, monetization layer, asset hosting — adds legal exposure proportional to the control you exert over the user's creative output.

The answer isn't "build less." It's **choose the right liability boundary**.

## Legal Architecture (The Part That Actually Matters)

Three structures that give you maximum protection:

**1. IP Holding Entity** — A Dutch BV or US LLC that owns the Arcanea trademark and canonical lore. This entity licenses the IP. It doesn't operate anything. It doesn't host anything. It doesn't process payments for users.

**2. Operating Entity** — Separate entity (or even just your existing FrankX structure) that runs arcanea.ai, sells CLI subscriptions, manages the community. Standard SaaS terms. You're selling access to tools, not hosting user content.

**3. Creator License Agreement** — The critical document. Key clauses: creators own everything they create, Arcanea grants a non-exclusive license to reference canonical lore, creators indemnify you against all third-party IP claims arising from their use, you retain right to revoke license for brand-damaging use, no liability for AI-generated output quality or originality. This is modeled on how Wizards of the Coast runs the Open Gaming License (before they tried to revoke it — learn from that mistake, make yours irrevocable).

The TOS for arcanea.ai chat: you're providing AI-assisted tools. Users are responsible for their prompts and outputs. Same model as Midjourney, Runway, every AI tool. You're not more liable than they are. The key is: **don't curate, don't moderate content quality, don't make editorial decisions about what gets published.** The moment you start picking winners in a feed algorithm, you become a publisher with publisher liability.

## The MCP Architecture for Multi-Tenancy

This is the technical insight that makes the whole thing work:

```
Central (you provide):
  arcanea-lore-mcp/        → canonical Guardians, realms, frequencies, rules
  arcanea-magic-mcp/       → magic systems, abilities, constraints
  arcanea-bestiary-mcp/    → creatures, entities, cosmic forces
  arcanea-validator-mcp/   → checks user creations against canon consistency

Per-creator (they provision):
  {world}-lore-mcp/        → their custom lore, characters, storylines
  {world}-assets-mcp/      → references to their generated images, music, text
  {world}-state-mcp/       → world state, timelines, event logs
```

Central MCP servers are read-only for creators. Their local databases (SQLite via the filesystem MCP, or hosted Supabase for pro users) store their world state. The `arcanea-validator-mcp` is the brilliant piece — it checks that user creations reference canonical lore correctly, suggests connections to existing Guardian frequencies, flags contradictions. This is how you maintain brand coherence without editorial control.

From Claude Code, a creator runs `/arcanea init my-realm` and gets the full MCP stack configured. From Arcanea Chat, they get the same lore access through the hosted interface. Same canonical data, two access patterns, one ecosystem.

## The OpenRouter-Style BYOK App Question

You've been asking this since at least August 2025. The idea keeps recurring because it's structurally correct — Arcanea Chat *should* be a BYOK model router with Arcanean intelligence layered on top. But here's why it hasn't shipped and what the actual decision is:

**What you already have:** An OpenRouter wrapper package, a Next.js app with chat routes, a design system, canonical lore as MCP-ready data, and a LiteLLM config spec from the February proxy conversation.

**What you don't have:** A working deployed chat app. The deployment incidents from Feb 25-26 are still open. The monorepo has build issues that have persisted across multiple conversations going back months.

**The fork in the road is this:**

**Option A: Arcanea Chat as a thin BYOK wrapper (SillyTavern model).** User brings OpenRouter key. You provide the Arcanean system prompts, Guardian personas, Luminor coaching, world-building context via MCP. You show them token usage, model selection, cost tracking. The app is basically a themed OpenRouter frontend with your intelligence layer. Revenue: near-zero from the app itself — it's a funnel into CLI Pro, knowledge packs, and creator licenses. Legal exposure: minimal. You're just a frontend. Users own their keys, their data, their outputs. You never process payments for third-party content.

**Option B: Arcanea as a hosted AI platform (ChatGPT model).** You provide the models, absorb the cost, charge subscriptions. Token tracking dashboard, usage tiers, the works. Revenue: subscription-based but you eat model costs. Legal exposure: higher — you're now an AI service provider with all the compliance that entails (EU AI Act, GDPR data processing, content liability). Margin: thin unless you're doing 10K+ users, because model costs eat your subscription revenue.

**Option C: Hybrid — free BYOK + premium hosted tier.** BYOK is the default. For users who don't want to manage API keys, you offer a "Managed" tier where you proxy through your LiteLLM instance with a 30-40% markup. This is the OpenRouter model itself.

**My recommendation: Option A with a sprinkle of C.**

Ship the BYOK chat app first. It's 80% built — you have the wrapper, you have the routes, you have the design system. The 51 open issues and deployment failures are your actual blockers, not architecture decisions. Fix the build. Deploy to Vercel. Let people bring their OpenRouter keys and experience Arcanean intelligence on top of any model.

Then add the managed tier later when you have enough users that the demand justifies running your own LiteLLM proxy.
