**Frank**:

Why sign out of accounts before factory reset not all done automatically

**Claude**:

Because a factory reset wipes *data*, not *ownership*. Two different layers.

**Factory Reset Protection (FRP)** is an anti-theft lock. When a Google or Samsung account is active on the device and you wipe it, the phone records "this device belongs to that account." On next boot it demands those exact credentials before setup proceeds. The design intent: a thief who steals your phone and wipes it still can't use it. Working as intended.

The problem is it doesn't distinguish thief from legitimate return. Wipe the S21 with your accounts still signed in, and the next person — MnD, or whoever Oracle reissues it to — boots into a lock screen demanding *your* password. They can't get past it. That traces straight back to you on a form with your name and the serial on it: you returned a brick.

**Signing out first** removes the account-to-device binding *before* the wipe, so FRP has nothing to enforce. Then the reset produces a genuinely clean device.

Why it isn't automatic: the reset is triggered from *inside* the OS, by someone already authenticated — the system can't tell if that's you decommissioning it or a thief covering tracks. So it deliberately preserves the lock through the wipe. Making it automatic would defeat the entire anti-theft purpose.

Order: sign out of Google, sign out of Samsung account, remove SIM, *then* factory reset. Reverse it and you're entering credentials on a wiped phone you no longer have to un-brick it.
