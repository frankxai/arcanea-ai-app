## The retainer model — explained from first principles

You asked what a retainer actually is. Here's the full picture, without consulting jargon.

**The basic structure.** A retainer is a fixed monthly fee in exchange for a fixed bundle of outputs and access. The client pays the same amount every month regardless of how many hours you spend. You commit to deliver the bundle every month regardless of how busy you are with other work. Predictable for them, sovereign for you.

**Why this matters more than hourly.** Hourly says: my time is the product. Every efficiency gain you create through agents, automation, or experience accrues to the client (they pay for fewer hours). With a retainer, every efficiency gain accrues to *you* — you deliver the same output in less time, and the saved time becomes profit margin or capacity for other work. Given that you run ACOS and a 31-tool MCP server, your hourly-equivalent productivity is 5–10x a normal consultant's. Hourly billing would be self-sabotage.

**Why this matters for vacations and lifestyle.** With hourly billing, time off = lost income. With a retainer, you deliver the monthly bundle on whatever schedule works. If you want three weeks in Marbella in July, you front-load the workshop and posts in early July or stack them in late July. Client only sees the deliverables hit; they don't see your calendar. Your contract should also include a clause: "Up to 4 weeks per year of agreed pause, with deliverables rescheduled around the pause." That's standard, not greedy.

**The output bundle is the magic.** The bundle has to be (a) specific enough that Susanne can defend the budget to her director, (b) loose enough that you have flexibility on *how* you deliver, and (c) compounding — every output should also build your audience, brand, and IP, not just serve Arrow. Workshops do this. Co-branded LinkedIn posts do this. Architecture demos with Deploy-via-ArrowSphere CTAs do this. *Internal Slack support hours* do not — never put "always-available coaching" in a retainer; that's how retainers turn into hourly-equivalent slavery.

**Pricing the retainer.** The number isn't picked from the air; it's reverse-engineered. Start with: how much do you want this engagement to contribute to your annual income? Say €100K/year as the Arrow contribution. Subtract the build-out fee (€40K) and event uplift (~€30K from 12 events). That leaves ~€30K from the retainer over months 4–12 = ~€3,300/month. Too low — undersells the position. Push the retainer up to €6–8K/month and the build-out becomes a smaller fraction of total. Or anchor higher.

For a Wolfgang-handover scenario, the retainer should probably be **€8–12K/month**, because you're not just delivering outputs — you're inheriting a relationship book and providing strategic continuity. That's worth more than ordinary advisory. Wolfgang himself likely earns somewhere in the €15–25K/month range from Arrow, given his seniority and EMEA scope. Expect to start at 60–70% of his number while he's still active, then grow into and past it as he hands over.

**What goes wrong with retainers.** Three failure modes to watch for:

The first is *scope creep.* Susanne calls about a "small thing" three times a week. Solution: contractual clarity on what's in the bundle and a documented add-on rate for anything outside it.

The second is *deliverable drift.* You miss a workshop one month because you were busy. Then a post. Then they question what they're paying for. Solution: deliverables tracked publicly, ideally on a shared Notion or in your monthly check-in note — proof of work without proof of hours.

The third is *brand contamination.* You become "Arrow's guy" and your other partnerships dry up. Solution: explicit non-exclusivity, your own URL not theirs, your own audience not theirs, every co-branded asset says *FrankX × Arrow*, not *Arrow*.
