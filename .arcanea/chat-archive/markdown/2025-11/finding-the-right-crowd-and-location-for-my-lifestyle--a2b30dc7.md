## 2. BALI: The Comprehensive Breakdown

### **Uluwatu (My Top Recommendation for You)**

This is where surf culture meets luxury, with a more mature/serious crowd than Canggu's party scene.

Uluwatu features world-class surf breaks such as Padang Padang and Suluban Beach, attracting surfers from around the globe. The relaxed atmosphere, coupled with a mix of luxurious resorts and laid-back beach clubs, makes Uluwatu a favourite for travellers seeking both adventure and relaxation.

**Top Luxury Options:**

Bulgari Resort Bali comprises 59 exclusive villas and mansions with bespoke butler service, private pools. Utilising local materials including volcanic stone, the interiors are suitably glamorous boasting exotic art, Balinese antiques and moody dark wood. The Bvlgari Resort Bali truly exists in an echelon of its own.

Six Senses Uluwatu was created from local wood, with heat-absorbing lava roofs. It has 103 rooms made up of suites and villas, two pools, a spa. With an emphasis on local links and eco-friendly living, ingredients are organic, mostly grown on-site.

Anantara Uluwatu is a luxury resort located in the epicentre of Bali's best surf breaks. Directly in front of the resort is Impossibles. A 10-minute drive south and you'll find yourself at the world famous Uluwatu break and 10 minutes North you're at Bingin.

Uluwatu Surf Villas clifftop resort is a sacred space to rebalance and get away. With unique dwellings, wholesome cuisine, soulful movement through yoga, and world class surfing. Premium cliff-front villas with access to world-class waves.

**The Surf:**
World famous, ultra-consistent 'Ulu's' is the focal point of Balinese surfing thanks to its ability to handle any sized swell and spread the biggest of crowds across a wide playing field of reef. It is a sectioning wave, offering many barrels along the way.

### **Canggu/Pererenan (More Social, Younger, Party)**

TimeOut Magazine named Canggu's Pererernan as the Coolest Neighbourhood in the World for 2025, attracting a whole new wave of tourists, travellers, digital nomads, and expats to the area.

The Regent Bali Canggu is one of the most anticipated new 5-star hotel openings in Bali for 2025, marking the legendary Regent brand's return to Indonesia. Situated on the sun-kissed shores of Canggu Beach, this ultra-luxury resort immediately set a new standard for opulence on the island.

Oshom Bali is one of the most intriguing new resorts in Bali. Tucked in amongst the up-and-coming Nuanu Creative City, this destination is for people who think they've seen it all on the Indonesian isle. This is a Bali resort intended to appeal to creative or wellness-minded souls, and digital nomads seeking seclusion and space.

**Cost:** €100-500/night depending on level, €2,000-4,000/month lifestyle

**The Crowd:** Bali has long been celebrated as a paradise for travelers, but beyond its picturesque landscapes lies an entrepreneurial playground with limitless potential. The island is no longer just a getaway for surfers and yogis—it has transformed into a hub for ambitious individuals looking to build high-margin businesses.

**Downside:** 6-7 hour time difference from EU, internet can be inconsistent in spots, some areas overdeveloped

## 5. INDIAN OCEAN ISLANDS: Maldives, Seychelles, Mauritius

### **Maldives**
Every digital nomad can find their ideal "work-from-paradise" base in the Maldives. For those seeking opulent and fully-serviced experiences, numerous luxury resorts boast state-of-the-art MICE facilities. Imagine swapping offices for luxurious overwater bungalows with dedicated workspaces or beachfront pavilions transformed into inspiring meeting rooms.

Several premium resorts have invested in fiber optic internet connections. Resorts like Soneva Fushi, Anantara Dhigu, and Kandima Maldives offer packages specifically tailored for remote workers with co-working spaces, private workstations with ocean views, and even concierge tech support.

**Reality Check:** "Coliving in the Maldives" sounds like an impossible concept. But nowadays even one of the most touristic destinations can be visited avoiding overpriced and non-sustainable resorts.

**The Truth:**
- GORGEOUS for short luxury escapes (1-2 weeks)
- NOT a nomad community destination - you'll be alone
- Very expensive (€500-2,000/night for quality)
- Limited entrepreneur networking
- Best for: recharge/reward trips, NOT base-building

### **Mauritius**
The digital nomad visa (Premium Visa) is specifically designed for remote workers who want to live and work in Mauritius for an extended period. Allows you to stay for up to one year, with option to renew. Application process is straightforward and you can apply online for free.

Mauritius offers a moderate cost of living: Rent (1-bed apartment): $400–$900/month, Meals: $3–$8 for local food. There's a growing remote worker and expat community. Grand Baie offers beachfront cafés with ocean views, social scene with mix of expats and locals.

Port Louis and Grand Baie offer coworking spaces, cafés with stable Wi‑Fi, and expat communities. Inland towns provide peaceful environments for focus.

**Best For:** Quieter, more affordable Indian Ocean option with actual nomad infrastructure  
**Cost:** €1,500-2,500/month lifestyle  
**Downside:** Limited nightlife outside of tourist areas, island life can feel quiet after a while if you're used to big cities.

### **Seychelles**
Seychelles offers the Workation Retreat Program for digital nomads. Under the program, applicants get the right to stay in the country for 1 year. Seychelles has the highest GDP per capita in Africa but also a high cost of living.

**Reality:** Beautiful but expensive, minimal nomad community, more honeymoon vibe than builder vibe.

## 6. CURAÇAO (Direct from Amsterdam!)

This one's interesting for you - Dutch territory, direct flights from Amsterdam.

Digital nomad life in Curaçao is a blast. Everywhere you go there is music, fun things to do, and friendly people to meet. Curaçao digital nomads have it made. Many places offer free Wi-Fi, there is a close-knit digital nomad community in Willemstad, and there are plenty of co-working spaces.

The best place to enjoy the nightlife in Willemstad is by heading to the Pietermaai district. The party is usually out on the streets. Best beach parties are on Jan Thiel beach and Mambo beach. Check out Madero Beach Club on Mambo for the best parties.

Curaçao launched the digital nomad visa program (@home in Curaçao visa) for remote workers. They offer longer stays from 6 months to 1 year. The island has a rich multicultural community, which makes fitting in easy.

Another advantage of living in Curaçao with the digital nomad visa is that there is no income tax. Digital nomads don't have to pay taxes on their income while living on the island.

**Pros:**
- Direct from Amsterdam (9h flight)
- No income tax
- Beach parties, young crowd
- Snorkeling is excellent (crystal clear Caribbean)
- Dutch/English speaking
- €1,500-2,500/month lifestyle

**Cons:**
There's quite a bit of crime on the island, so you need to take precautions and avoid certain places. We played it extra safe by only booking accommodations inside resorts or gated communities.

**Best For:** Caribbean test run, direct accessibility from NL, party beach vibe
