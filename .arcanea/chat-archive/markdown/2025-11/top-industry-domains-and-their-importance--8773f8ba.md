For Oracle specifically, the key industry domains break down into clear strategic priorities:

**Oracle's Core Strength Industries:**

**Financial Services** - Absolutely massive for Oracle. Banks, insurance, capital markets rely heavily on Oracle Database, especially for transaction processing, risk management, and regulatory compliance. The autonomous capabilities and security features are critical here.

**Healthcare & Life Sciences** - Growing rapidly, especially with AI/data requirements. Patient records, clinical trials, drug discovery, medical imaging - all data-intensive workloads perfect for Oracle's stack.

**Retail & Consumer Goods** - Supply chain, inventory management, customer data platforms. Oracle Fusion and the database layer power major retailers globally.

**Manufacturing & Supply Chain** - Oracle's ERP dominance here is huge. Production planning, logistics, IoT sensor data from factories - all Oracle strongholds.

**Public Sector & Government** - Massive installed base. Governments need reliable, secure, compliant systems. Oracle's database and cloud infrastructure fit perfectly.

**Telecommunications** - Network management, billing systems, massive data volumes. Telcos are heavy Oracle users.

**Utilities & Energy** - Grid management, asset tracking, regulatory reporting. Traditional Oracle territory.

**For your AI CoE work specifically:**

Given Oracle's strategic push, you're likely seeing the most traction in industries where:
- **Data sovereignty matters** (financial, healthcare, government) - this is Oracle's competitive edge against hyperscalers
- **Mission-critical workloads** can't tolerate downtime
- **Regulatory compliance** is complex (EU AI Act positioning)
- **Hybrid/multi-cloud** strategies are needed

The creative/tech convergence industries (media, gaming, creator economy) are probably *not* Oracle's sweet spot currently - those tend to go AWS/GCP. But that could be an opportunity for differentiation in your positioning.
