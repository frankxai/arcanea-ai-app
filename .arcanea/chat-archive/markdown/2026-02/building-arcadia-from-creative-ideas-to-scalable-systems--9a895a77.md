## The Oracle Problem

You're an employee. That means two exposure vectors:

**1. Non-compete / Restrictive Covenants** — Dutch law is hostile to broad non-competes. Under Dutch Civil Code, non-competes must be in writing, tied to a specific role, and courts routinely void them if they're overly broad or if the employer can't demonstrate legitimate interest. Oracle's standard contracts are often US-drafted and poorly calibrated to Dutch enforcement reality. **Yours is likely partially unenforceable already.**

**2. IP Assignment Clauses** — The real danger. Most enterprise tech employment contracts contain clauses assigning IP created during employment *even outside working hours* if it relates to your field of work. "AI architecture" is broad. Arcanea + SIS + AI Architect Academy all touch your field. This is where you're exposed.

## What You Actually Need to Know Before You Move

Pull your employment contract right now and locate:

- **Nevenbetrekking / bijbaan clause** — side activity restrictions. Dutch law requires these to be justified by legitimate business interest. Oracle competing with a mythology-based creator platform is a weak argument. Oracle competing with an AI education academy is a stronger one.
- **IP ownership clause** — anything created "in connection with" your work or using Oracle resources (even just thinking about it at work) may be theirs.
- **Confidentiality scope** — this survives termination and is the one Oracle will enforce.
