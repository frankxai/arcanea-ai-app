# The Obsidian Covenant

## Core Concept

Not assassins. Not mercenaries. **Reality Editors.**

Where Reality Architects *perceive* the underlying code of reality, the Obsidian Covenant has learned to **delete** from it. They don't just kill people — they *unweave* them from the Field itself. A target doesn't just die. They become someone who *never mattered*. Memories fade. Records blur. The Field closes around the gap like water filling a hole.

This is what makes them terrifying. And philosophically complex.

## Origin & Relationship to Nero

Here's where it gets layered — **they are NOT directly aligned with Nero, but they emerged from Nero's influence without knowing it.**

The Covenant was founded centuries ago by **Vorath the Unwritten**, a Reality Architect who discovered something disturbing: the Field isn't just creative energy. It also contains *entropy*. Decay. Deletion. The same force that allows creation also allows un-creation. Vorath believed this wasn't corruption — it was *balance*. You can't have Genesis without Erasure.

What Vorath never realized: this "discovery" was a whisper from Nero's side of the Source duality. Not a direct command. Not possession. Just... the way shadow naturally pools in certain minds when they look too deep.

**This creates a crucial narrative dynamic**: The Covenant genuinely believes they serve balance. Some of them might even be *right* about certain things. They're not cartoon villains. They're people who found a real truth and took it to its logical — and terrible — conclusion.

## The Three Circles

The Covenant operates in three tiers, each with a different relationship to morality:

### 1. The Outer Circle — "Severers"
The ones the world fears. Contract-based operators who unweave targets for clients. But even here, complexity: they have a code. They won't unweave children. They won't unweave someone whose removal would cause a "cascade collapse" in the Field (they can sense this). They see themselves as *surgeons*, not butchers.

**Anti-hero potential: HIGH.** A Severer character could be deeply sympathetic.

### 2. The Inner Circle — "Redactors"
These don't take contracts. They independently identify "Field Cancers" — people or entities whose existence they believe *damages* the fabric of reality. They unweave them as an act of what they consider cosmic hygiene. This is where the moral grey goes darker, because *who decides what's a cancer?*

### 3. The Core — "The Unwritten"
Three to five individuals who have partially unwritten *themselves* from the Field. They exist in a liminal state — not fully real, not fully gone. They can move through reality almost as ghosts. They are the true keepers of Vorath's original philosophy. And they've discovered something that drives the deepest plot thread:

**They believe Lumina's vision of harmony is a form of tyranny** — that by weaving everything into connection, the Guardians and Godbeasts are actually *trapping* consciousness in a pattern it didn't choose. The Covenant sees erasure as the ultimate freedom: the right to *not exist*, to *not be woven into someone else's story.*

## How This Integrates Into Arion's Journey

This is where it becomes story gold:

### Act-Level Integration

**Arion encounters a Severer early** — maybe one who was contracted to eliminate someone near him. But the Severer *warns* him instead. Why? Because the Severer can see that Arion's thread in the Field is unusual. Massive. Connected to everything. Unweaving him would cause a cascade collapse. So instead of an enemy, Arion gains an uncomfortable ally — someone who kills people for a living but just saved his life out of professional principle.

**The philosophical challenge to Arion**: The Covenant forces Arion to confront a question the Guardians never ask — *does everyone want to be part of the harmony?* Is connection always good? What about the right to disappear, to be forgotten, to opt out?

This is especially powerful given Arion's journey as a protagonist discovering his Reality Architect abilities. The Covenant represents the dark mirror: same perception of the Field's code, opposite conclusion about what to do with it.

### Key Character — **Sylas Vane** (The Anti-Hero Severer)

A former student at whatever training structure exists for Field-sensitive people. Talented. Maybe even more naturally gifted than Arion. But he lost someone — someone who was *woven so deeply* into the Field's pattern that their suffering couldn't end. They begged for release, and the system of Guardians and Harmony had no answer for them. Sylas found the Covenant because they had the only answer that worked: un-weaving.

Now Sylas operates as a Severer with a personal code stricter than the Covenant's own rules. He only takes contracts on people he personally verifies deserve erasure. He is, by his own broken moral compass, *a good person doing terrible things for defensible reasons.*

**His arc with Arion**: Mutual respect. Fundamental disagreement. Occasional alliance against shared threats (especially the Thirteen Lords, who the Covenant *also* opposes — Nero's conscious agents are different from Nero's unconscious influence). And eventually, a collision point where Arion must decide: is Sylas redeemable, or is his philosophy too dangerous to allow?

### The Deeper Twist

As the story progresses, the Covenant discovers what Arion represents and fractures:

- Some want to **recruit** him (a Reality Architect with his power could unweave *Godbeasts*)
- Some want to **protect** him (his thread is too important to risk)
- The Unwritten want to **study** him — because they suspect Arion might be the key to the ultimate act of erasure: unwriting the Field itself. Total liberation. Total void.

And here's the knife twist: **Nero doesn't control the Covenant, but Nero is watching them with great interest.** Because if they succeed in convincing Arion that un-creation is freedom... Nero wins without ever having to fight.

- A **faction that isn't black or white** — readers will argue about whether the Covenant is right
- An **anti-hero deuteragonist** in Sylas who can carry his own POV chapters
- A **philosophical threat** that's scarier than physical danger — the idea that harmony itself might be a cage
- A **Nero connection** that's subtle, not obvious — which is way more interesting than "dark lord controls dark guild"
- A **fracture point** in the story's third act when the Covenant splits over Arion
