## What Actually Works — The Audience Borrow, Legally

You don't need their *footage* to borrow their *audience*. You need their **search terms, hashtags, and aesthetic adjacency.** Here's how the smart operators actually do it:

### 1. Hashtag piggybacking (free, immediate)

Your original content uses #naruto #onepiece #jujutsukaisen #anime #animeedit #animerecommendation as *discovery tags*. TikTok's algorithm serves your original content to people *searching those terms*. You're not claiming to be Naruto — you're claiming to be *for Naruto fans*.

This is how @anime.vibes, @shonen.lore, @otakurecs grew. Original commentary or original animation, anime-adjacent hashtags.

### 2. The "If you love X, watch this" formula

Hook: "If you love Gojo's cursed energy, wait till you see Kage's frequency technique."
Body: your original character, your own animation, Arcanean aesthetic.
Caption: #jjk #gojo #animeedit

This is transformative, it's honest, and it converts JJK fans into Kage fans. **You're borrowing the attention, not the asset.**

### 3. Reaction / analysis format (fair use, safe ground)

You film yourself (face to camera, your brand) talking over *brief* clips (3-5 seconds max, commentary required, clearly transformative). This is how Mother's Basement, Gigguk, Totally Not Mark operate. Mark got DMCA'd by Toei and won — because his use was genuinely transformative analysis.

**But this requires you on camera, doing actual criticism.** Not just a re-edit with commentary slapped on. The commentary has to be the point, not a fig leaf.

### 4. Reference/study animation (the sharpest play)

You post "Studied the lighting in Demon Slayer's Mugen Train fight — here's how I recreated it for Kage." Side-by-side or process video showing your *technique learning*, not footage theft. Educational framing. 

This is extremely effective because:
- Anime nerds love craft breakdowns
- You're openly acknowledging the reference (respectful, not theft)
- The viral payoff is *your original shot* that matches their beloved aesthetic
- Builds you as a studio, not a reposter

## The Legal Reality (Because It Shapes Everything)

Fan art generation is **structurally different** from reposting footage, and you need to understand the distinction cleanly:

- **User-generated fan art of copyrighted characters** = legally grey but broadly tolerated. Pixiv, DeviantArt, Etsy, Redbubble all host billions of pieces of fan art. Rights holders generally tolerate non-commercial fan art because it drives engagement with the IP.
- **Platform-generated fan art** = platform assumes some liability. Midjourney and Sora have both dealt with this. The legal pattern emerging: platforms are OK as long as they're *general-purpose* and don't specifically market "generate Naruto images here."
- **Commercial exploitation** (selling Naruto t-shirts, mugs, etc.) = hard no. This is where rights holders sue.

**The workable posture:**
- Platform is general-purpose anime-style creation tool
- Users can generate whatever they want with their own prompts — that's on them
- You don't host "Naruto mode" buttons, but you don't block prompts either
- Your monetization is on **platform access** (subscription, credits, tools), not on **selling derivative merch of copyrighted IP**
- Merch/TikTok Shop revenue comes from **original content** only (yours or user-OCs)

This is the Midjourney / Runway / Sora model. It's the only model that scales without legal collapse.

## The Editing Question — Tools Only or Teach Them Human Editing Too?

You asked about CapCut, Remotion, video editing. Here's my take:

**For members:** teach CapCut + Descript. That's 95% of what anime creators need. It's mobile, it's free, templates work, it's fast. Anyone can do it. Most will never need Premiere. When they do, that's a niche workshop.

**Remotion:** powerful but niche. Programmatic video generation for things like auto-generated character cards, batch intros, dynamic templates. **Useful when you have 1000+ legends in the community and want to auto-generate their personalized Legend Reveal videos.** Not month one. Month 12.
