## On IJburg and man-made islands

Your brother's perspective has roots in both practical observation and a kind of theological naturalism — the idea that human engineering against nature's patterns is hubris that will eventually be corrected.

**The practical kernel of truth:**

Man-made land *does* require perpetual maintenance. The Netherlands itself is a testament to this — much of it exists below sea level through constant human effort (polders, dikes, pumping stations). IJburg sits on artificial islands in the IJmeer. Dubai's Palm Islands are already experiencing settling and erosion issues.

So yes — without ongoing human stewardship, these places would eventually return to water. That's just hydrology and geology.

**The deeper question: Is this "unnatural" or wrong?**

This is where it gets philosophically interesting. Consider:

- All human habitation modifies nature (cities, agriculture, even ancient settlements)
- The Netherlands has maintained reclaimed land for *centuries* — it's not inherently unstable, just requiring stewardship
- By this logic, would any coastal city be "what God wants"? Venice? New Orleans? Amsterdam itself?

Your brother seems to equate permanence with divine approval. But *nothing* human-built is permanent on geological timescales. Even mountains erode.

**A reframe:** Perhaps the question isn't whether nature "takes back" — it always does eventually — but whether living somewhere brings you alive, connects you to purpose, and lets you create what you're meant to create during *your* lifetime.

You love the water. You thrive there. That's not nothing.
