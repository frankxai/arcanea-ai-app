### From Tools to Orchestration

After a short 15-minute intro from my colleague, I took over and ran the full AI workshop.

I focused on one core message: the most valuable skill in this new era is becoming an **orchestrator**.

Not just:
- Prompting a chatbot  
- Clicking “generate” on a single tool  

But:
- Combining multiple models and tools  
- Designing your own workflows  
- Building your own agents and capabilities  

Many of the students had used AI in a very surface-level way (mostly ChatGPT-style use), but:
- Almost none had built their own AI agents  
- Most had never seen Suno or other AI music tools  
- Very few had thought about AI as a *stack* they can architect  

For them, this wasn’t just a workshop; it was like stepping a few years into the future.

### Live Demo: Claude Code, ACOS and Open Source

We then moved from concepts to code.

I showed them:
- Claude Code in action as a coding partner and automation engine  
- My own open-sourced project (ACOS), explaining its basic topology  
- How it’s structured, how they can clone it, and how to start experimenting themselves  

The best part: the Q&A didn’t stop. Students kept asking follow-up questions about:
- How the orchestration works under the hood  
- How they could adapt this for their own ideas  
- What a “real-world” AI workflow looks like beyond a single prompt  

That level of curiosity is exactly what we need in the next generation of builders.

### Creating With AI: Music, Agents and Ideas

To make it tangible, we also explored the creative side:
- How to generate custom music with tools like Suno  
- How to turn AI from “a magic box” into a pipeline they can control  
- How these skills apply not just to engineering, but to startups, content, and creative work  

By the end, several students came up to me:
- Four of them scanned my QR code to connect and explore more  
- One group pitched me a startup idea on the spot, and we immediately saw how AI agents and orchestration could accelerate it  

For me, that was the highlight: seeing the switch flip from “this is cool” to “I can actually build something with this.”

### Why This Matters

We often talk about “AI skills” in very generic terms. What I see in workshops like this is a clearer pattern:

The real edge will belong to people who:
- Understand multiple AI tools and models  
- Can connect them into systems  
- Are willing to experiment, share, and build in public (open source, small projects, experiments)  

That’s what we tried to demonstrate in this session: not just how AI works, but how to work *with* it as a collaborator and infrastructure.

If you have a severance package (transitievergoeding or settlement):
