Proposed scene: The blank side

Vale had brought the orchard a small, portable version of itself.

He unpacked six clay tiles on the bench by the east gate, opening them from their cloth so carefully that Neri put down her empty basket to watch. The gate had opened at noon. A few minutes later, sunlight still occupied the threshold while the bench lay in shade.

"Don't tell me what they mean yet," he said. "Tell me if the lines are right."

Neri took the nearest tile to the tree beside the bench. A forked stroke on the bark separated into two fine ends. On the clay, they joined. She held the tile against the trunk, keeping it below the glyph rather than covering it.

"This one has closed."

"In the firing?"

"I don't know when. It isn't the same now."

Vale leaned close enough to see both surfaces at once. His sleeve brushed her hand. "Can it still be read?"

"By someone who knows it. Perhaps."

"That sounds like a yes."

"It was a perhaps."

He drew back and gave her room to lower the tile. The other five looked tidier. That did not make them right.

Neri wanted him to leave the samples and go. She had remembered the melody all morning, through dressing and finding her baskets, and somewhere in the ordinary movement she expected the last verse to return. It had not. She had forgotten it yesterday; now another day had begun using the same blank.

Above them, the fruit she had sung to glowed faintly. Its stems had loosened. Nothing had fallen.

"I have to finish here," she said.

"Of course." Vale gathered the cloth around the other tiles. "I'll tell them you checked the copies."

"Who?"

He took a folded list from his pack. Six names, a copper marked beside each, and beneath them his own cramped words: harvest signs.

"They can collect after their meal. I left the time open, in case you were busy."

She knew the hand of one buyer from a letter she had answered that spring. The woman had put her own name on the list, carefully, as if making a purchase were something that required good writing.

"You took their money already."

"For six tiles. I have six tiles."

Neri set the faulty one face down on the bench.

"You said these would tell them what they could harvest?"

Vale glanced at the list. "That is what the marks on the trees do."

"You cannot read those marks. You copy their shapes."

"I thought you would tell me which was which."

"I know this tree's. I have not checked your other five."

He unwrapped them again. He did not spread them into neat rows this time.

Neri could give him names for the two shapes she recognized. He could carry that much away, and she could finish trying the song before anyone else arrived. She would have endorsed six tiles by checking two, unless he made the distinction as carefully as she had.

Or she could refuse to name them for sale, return with him to those six people, and explain why there was nothing in her baskets either.

Vale touched the blank back of the first tile. "Could they just be copies?"

"If you say so."

"I didn't say so when they paid."

"Then ask them again."

He took six coppers from his purse. They clicked against the tiles. One coin remained in the purse, and he shut it away before the sound had finished.

"Will you come?"

Neri looked at the fruit. She could sing the remembered part once more, while he wrapped his pack. Instead she brought the empty basket to the bench and set it down beside his list.

"I don't have the last verse," she said. "I haven't had it since yesterday."

He looked at her without looking up into the tree.

"All right."

"I can't promise them fruit today."

"Then we should get there before they eat."

A woman came through the gate and stopped at the bench. She pointed to her name on Vale's list.

"Are mine ready?"

Neri kept her hands on the basket. "The copies are. I haven't checked what they say. We shouldn't have sold them as harvest instructions."

The woman looked past her at the glowing fruit. "Can I pick today?"

"I can't finish the song."

"Give me my copper, then."

Vale slid one coin across the bench. The woman picked it up, waited to see if either of them would say more, and left. Neri waited until her steps were out of hearing before she looked at Vale.

She turned over the other five tiles. Their plain backs made six small spaces in which nothing had yet been claimed.

"I will check them against their trees," she said. "After we give the money back."

Vale returned the other five coppers to his purse and lifted the cloth corners. Neri took the list and her empty basket, and stepped into the sunlight at the gate.
