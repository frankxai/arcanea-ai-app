Proposed scene: The six tiles

Neri heard the potter before she saw him. His pack made a dry clicking against the east gate, which had opened a few minutes earlier at noon. She stopped humming. The fruit above her stayed bright, each stem stretched thin, each fruit still attached.

"I brought the samples," Vale said.

He laid six clay tiles on the bench beneath the first tree. Each carried a copied orchard glyph. The tiles were small enough to cover with a hand, and he arranged them two by two, like settings for a meal.

Neri had hoped to finish the song before he arrived. With the fruit down, she could have answered his questions among full baskets. Now there were only baskets.

"Those weren't meant for sale yet."

"I took an order. You said you would look at them."

"I said bring them."

Vale unrolled a piece of paper and weighted it with his smallest tile. Six names stood in a column. Neri knew two of them. He had taken one copper from each buyer, and promised to deliver before their midday meal.

He touched the first glyph. "This is the ripe one?"

"You cannot read it."

"That is why I am asking you."

The reply was mild. She wanted it to have been rude. Then she could have refused him for his manner and gone back to the tree.

Neri picked up the tile. One copied stroke joined two others where the bark kept them apart. She knew the glyph on this tree well enough to see that much. She did not know what the joined mark would mean elsewhere.

"This one isn't the same."

Vale reached for it. She held it a moment longer, then let him turn it in the light. He put a fingernail into the shallow groove.

"The clay moved."

"Will the buyers know that?"

"Not unless I tell them."

He set it face down. The first exposed back was pale and plain among the five dark signs.

Neri could send him away. He could return the money, and she could try the melody again without anyone watching her mouth. Or she could help him mark the copies as copies, and let the people whose names were on the paper ask why she was not harvesting.

Vale waited. He had stopped touching the tiles.

"Can you give me ten minutes?" she asked.

"To check them?"

"To finish here."

He looked up at the fruit. "I can wait. The others may not."

She turned to the tree and began the melody once more. It came easily; that was the worst of it. The fruit brightened while her hands rested on an empty basket. At the place where the last verse should begin, she kept her mouth open a little too long.

Vale lowered his eyes to the paper.

"Yesterday," she said, "I lost the words."

"Will they come back?"

"I don't know."

He folded the list. "Then I will take these back."

Neri sat on the far end of the bench, leaving room between them for the tiles. "If you tell them these show which fruit they can take, you must return the money."

"That is what I told them."

He opened his purse. Six coppers made a small heap on the wood. There was one other coin; he kept it inside. Neri watched him separate the returns by name. They were not numbers to him either.

"I can write you a note," she said. "Copied shapes. No harvest instructions."

"They didn't pay for shapes."

"No."

He picked up the faulty tile. "And this?"

"Keep it with the note. They should see what an error looks like."

Vale rubbed his thumb over the joined stroke. "Will you tell them that yourself?"

The empty baskets stood in full view of the gate. Neri moved one aside and made space on the bench for whoever came first.

"Yes."

The first buyer arrived while Neri was reaching for the paper. Vale offered her copper back and explained that the tile was a copy, not a checked instruction. She took the coin. Neri remained beside him, with the empty basket between her feet, and answered when the woman asked why.

She took the paper from under the tile, turned it over and wrote the two sentences he would carry with his refunds.
