# Cinder Orchard — Compact World Bible

## Premise

Proposed premise (P1; uses CO1, CO2, CO3): Cinder Orchard is a walled grove where harvest depends on timing, song, and readable glyphs. The noon gate enforces a narrow window; the glyphs tell harvesters which trees are ready; the harvest song—melody and verse together—governs how fruit releases. With Neri's final verse missing and Vale unable to read the glyphs, the orchard is open but its knowledge is fractured.

Open choice (O1): Why the verse was lost and whether it can be recovered are creator decisions.

## Place: Cinder Orchard

Accepted (CO1): The east gate opens at noon. Proposed (P1): The orchard is walled; the east gate is the principal public entrance. Open choice (O2): whether other entrances exist is unspecified. Inside, bark glyphs (P3) guide harvest row by row, and the ground is dark with char from fruit held past its moment — the origin of the name.

Proposed story material (P4; uses CO1): A hand-lettered sign near the east gate reads: *The east gate is the only entrance. Do not look for others.* Its author, motive, and accuracy are open choices (O3). The sign's text is a diegetic object; it does not establish orchard geography.

Open choice (O4): Who owns or governs the orchard, and who maintains glyph knowledge, are unspecified.

## Characters

**Neri —** Accepted (CO2): Neri forgot the harvest song's final verse yesterday but still holds the melody. Open choice (O1): the loss's cause and recoverability remain creator decisions. Proposed (P2; uses CO2): Neri can open the song and light the fruit — glowing rows wait for a phrase that does not come.

Open choice (O5): Neri's role — keeper, seasonal singer, one of many — is unspecified.

**Vale —** Accepted (CO3): Vale is a traveling potter who cannot read orchard glyphs. Proposed (P6; uses CO3): Vale copies glyph shapes onto fired tiles for sale without parsing their meaning. Open choice (O6): why Vale cannot read the glyphs — unfamiliar origin, trade restriction, personal limit — is unspecified. Vale moves through the orchard as a precise observer of forms rather than meanings.

## Story Hooks

### Hook A — The Loosened Fruit (HA; uses CO2, P2, P3, P5)

Proposed (HA): At noon, Neri hums the melody and three rows of fruit glow and loosen — the verse does not come. Proposed (P5): by dusk, the loosened fruit will not preserve well. Proposed (P3, CO3): Vale can identify which trees the glyphs mark as highest-yield but cannot interpret the full readiness notation. The obstacle is that Neri holds the song and Vale holds the shapes, but neither holds what the other lacks.

Open choice (O1): this hook creates stakes from that gap but does not resolve whether the verse can be recovered.

### Hook B — The Sign's Author (HB; uses CO1, P4, CO3, CO2)

Proposed (HB; uses P4): Vale, copying glyphs through the orchard, finds a second gate — smaller, older, ungated — framed in a glyph set absent from Vale's collection. Accepted (CO3): Vale cannot read them. Proposed: Neri might recognize the inscription; if P2 is adopted, the second gate's glyphs could be the written form of the missing verse. Open choice (O3): whether the sign's author knew about this gate is unresolved. Open choice (O7): the second gate's existence, builder, and function are creator decisions. If adopted, HB binds the geography question (O2) and the verse question (O1) into one discovery.

## Open Choices

**O1** — Cause and recoverability of the lost verse. **O2** — Whether other entrances exist. **O3** — Who posted the sign and why. **O4** — Who owns or governs the orchard. **O5** — Why Vale cannot read glyphs. **O6** — What role Neri holds. **O7** — Whether the second gate (HB) exists; who built it.
