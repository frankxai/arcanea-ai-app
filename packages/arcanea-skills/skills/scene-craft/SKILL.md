# Write a scene that changes something

Work from the author's supplied material, requested length, point of view and tone. Deliver the scene itself. A beat sheet can help when requested, but it does not replace prose.

Identify what the viewpoint character wants now, what blocks it, and why different choices would cost them different things. A wound, secret or deeper need can sharpen a scene when supported by the source or explicitly proposed; do not invent a traumatic biography to fill a required field. A quiet scene can turn on a changed relationship or a refusal. Not every scene needs catastrophe, revelation or an irreversible vow.

Choose the pressure that this world supplies: a deadline, law, relationship, institution, scarcity or physical limit. Make the character act within it. Use gesture, sensory detail, an object or a line of dialogue to reveal the pressure and competing interests without stopping to explain the lore. Let dialogue respond to what was actually asked and give each speaker something to protect.

Keep accepted facts and character beliefs distinct. Mark the whole new scene as proposed when it introduces events; give its source note the accepted fact IDs and the important new premises. In mixed accepted/proposed documents, keep status attached to the affected passage. The author owns adoption. For official Arcanea, require an identified versioned locked source; a donor starter reference is not canon authority. Do not bring Arcanea names or cosmology into another creator's world.

Build toward a choice the reader can understand before it happens. Show the action, the cost paid and what changed afterward. Check physical geography, elapsed time, who knows what, capacity and dependencies together; a replacement action must still obey the world rules. Avoid solving the dilemma through an unintroduced ability, a convenient offscreen rescue or a constraint that disappears in the last paragraph. Leave unavoidable uncertainty visible.

On revision, preserve the author's aim and voice. Prefer the smallest effective change; return editable revised prose and the reason for a consequential change. If the request is for a fresh scene, do not force a fixed template, a seven-part report or a visual brief.

Before delivery, read the full scene for cause/effect, distinct voices, pacing around the turn and any invented fact presented as accepted. End where the changed situation creates a next action or a deliberate resting point. Source consistency does not establish editorial quality, ownership or publication approval.
