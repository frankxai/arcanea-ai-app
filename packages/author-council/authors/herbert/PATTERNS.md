# Herbert — Structural Patterns

## Epigraph pattern

Every chapter opens with an in-world document — Princess Irulan's histories, Manual of Muad'Dib, Bene Gesserit files. The epigraph does one of:
- Foreshadow the chapter's events from a future-historical POV
- Recontextualize a prior chapter
- Reveal the chapter's theme obliquely
- Provide exposition the in-scene POV cannot

## Ecological-political feedback

Arrakis example: spice requires water-absence; Fremen hoard water; Imperium depends on spice; Bene Gesserit depend on Imperium; spice is sandworm byproduct; sandworms require sand which requires aridity. One ecological variable (water) propagates through politics, religion, economics, and biology. This is the Herbert move — make the ecology the substrate of everything else.

## Prescience as prison

- Paul sees many futures
- Each chosen future narrows the remaining set
- The oracle becomes captive to the chosen path
- Jihad is not avoidable because it is already seen

Counter-pattern: when writing prescience, ensure the prophet's capacity is also their cage.

## Interior italic thought

Characters' thoughts set off in italics, often mid-dialogue. Provides irony — what is said vs what is thought. Heavy use.

## Millennial zoom

The Dune Chronicles span >5000 years. Herbert is comfortable moving from hour-scale chapter to millennia-scale exposition. Pattern: periodic time-jumps of 10–3500 years between books.

## Messiah trap pattern

Book 1 builds the messiah. Book 2 destroys him — not with external enemies but with the consequences of his own charisma. Pattern: whenever a charismatic leader wins, show the cost of the winning within 1 book.
