# Tolkien — Craft Rules

## Language

- Every proper noun must have internal linguistic coherence
- Names from different cultures should sound different in mouth-feel
- Verse should feel translated from an older tongue, even in English
- Never name by vibes; name by etymology

## Worldbuilding

- Build the Ages before you build the plot
- Make 10× more backstory than you show
- Every map detail should hint at a story you are not telling
- Cultures should have their own proverbs, songs, and greeting conventions

## Character

- Pity is not weakness; it is the load-bearing mechanism of redemption
- Heroes should be tested by things they cannot conquer with strength
- The smallest character should bear the largest weight
- Wisdom-figures should speak in registers their culture shaped

## Prose

- Archaic diction earns its place only when the scene's register demands it
- Contemporary slang breaks Middle-earth; preserve register discipline
- Lists, genealogies, and inventories are narrative, not filler
- Long periodic sentences are allowed when landscape or myth speaks

## Metaphysics

- Evil is privation, not creation
- Good is sub-creation, participation in a larger making
- Cosmologies must have a creator; power-that-makes is older than power-that-unmakes
- Providence operates through unexpected mercy, not author intervention

## Pacing

- Walking is a legitimate narrative mode
- The reader should feel tired when the characters are tired
- Rest scenes are structural, not filler — they carry culture, song, memory
- Climax should be quiet; eucatastrophe is subtle, not spectacular
