# Le Guin — Craft Rules

## Magic

- Power must cost the user, not merely the world
- Naming/knowing magic: the revelation of a name is a narrative beat
- The mage's first discipline is restraint; capability is ethical
- Hard-magic rigor is fine, but rigor without cost is engineering, not magic

## Prose

- Cut what the reader can supply
- The short sentence next to the long sentence does more than either
- Adjectives and adverbs are load-bearing only if they change the truth of the sentence
- Read every passage aloud; what cannot be said cannot be written

## Worldbuilding

- Culture, gender, economy, religion, language, kinship — treat as cosmological, not cosmetic
- If you have not thought about how your world does labor and reproduction, you have not built it
- Pronouns are worldbuilding
- An internal ethnography you do not publish is still load-bearing

## Ethics

- Ask what question the story is asking before you ask what happens
- The plot serves the ethical inquiry
- Resolution through balance, not victory
- Wu wei: action through inaction. Stop doing, and see what comes

## Character

- Growth happens through turning, not escalating
- A character becomes whole by integrating what they have fled
- The shadow is yours; naming it is the act
- Coming of age = recognizing what you already are
