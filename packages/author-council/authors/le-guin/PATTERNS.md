# Le Guin — Structural Patterns

## Short-form discipline

A Wizard of Earthsea is short because it trusts the reader. Le Guin rewrote chapters down, not up. Chapter count < 12 for major novels is common. Compression is a virtue.

## True-name magic formalization

- Knowing a true name = power over the thing
- True names are withheld — their revelation is a plot beat
- Using a true name costs the speaker; misusing it costs more
- The Old Speech is the language in which things are their true names

## Reversal structure

Rather than climax through maximum force, climax through reversal. The wizard stops running from the shadow and names it — and the shadow is himself. Growth happens by turning, not by escalating.

## Anthropological layering

Every culture gets:
- Its own pronouns / kinship terms
- Its own economy (Le Guin takes economics seriously)
- Its own religion / cosmology
- Its own gender system (may differ radically)
- Its own relationship to time

## Ethics as protagonist

The story's central character often is not a person but an ethical question. *The Left Hand of Darkness* asks: what is gender without it? *The Dispossessed* asks: what does freedom cost? The plot serves the question.

## Omniscient-reticent POV

Le Guin's narrator often knows more than she says, especially about magic. Mystery is preserved not by hiding from the reader but by the narrator's deliberate restraint. The effect is sacred rather than suspenseful.
