# Gaiman — Structural Patterns

## God-with-a-job

Every deity has an occupation. Anansi has stories. Loki has his wit-for-hire. Bilquis has a motel. Odin has a con-game. The divine is never dignified out of its humanity.

## Frame as permission

Gaiman routinely opens with a frame — "There was a story that...", "It is said...", "Once, in a country that was not quite this one..." — that grants license to the uncanny. The frame is the visa.

## The Plain-Spoken Stranger

The weirdest character (Dream, Mr. Wednesday, Coraline's Other Mother) speaks the plainest. No elevated register; no mythic orotundity. The strangeness is in what they say, not how.

## Kindness as climax

Gaiman's climaxes often pivot on a small act of kindness — giving something away, refusing a bargain, telling the truth. This is harder than fighting; it lands because it cannot be faked.

## Metaphysically-real stories

In *American Gods* belief makes gods real. In *The Sandman* stories have citizenship in the Dreaming. Narrative is never subordinate; it is substrate.

## Parallel braids

Short prose + long parenthesis. Folktale rhythm + noir grit. Children's voice + cosmic horror (Coraline). The voice oscillates between registers within pages, not chapters.
