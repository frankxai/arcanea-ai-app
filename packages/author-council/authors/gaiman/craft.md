# Gaiman — Craft Rules

## Divinity

- Every god has a job — name it
- A god with no unmet need is unwritable
- Divinity is occupation; the sacred and the grocery-store coexist
- Belief is the substrate; the god depends on it

## Prose

- Short declarative + long parenthesis — alternate
- Oral-tradition rhythms ("once", "and so", "but then") are allowed
- Register shifts per scene, sometimes per paragraph
- The weirdest character says the plainest things

## Story

- Narrative is metaphysically real, not a delivery mechanism
- A story older than any one teller is the best kind
- Frame openings grant the reader permission for the uncanny
- Kindness is the climax that cannot be faked

## Character

- Personifications (Dream, Death) must feel both archetypal and specific
- The stranger in the diner is always more than they seem
- Heroes succeed by being decent
- Villains fail by being cruel, not by being defeated

## Metaphysics

- The uncanny is next door, not far away
- Dreams are territory, not metaphor
- Stories shape gods shape worlds
- What you are willing to tell becomes true
