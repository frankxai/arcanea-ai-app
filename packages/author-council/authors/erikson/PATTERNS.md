# Erikson — Structural Patterns

## Convergence architecture

Novel-level: 5–10 independent POV threads tracked across a book. Each has its own goals, geography, timeline. Final 20–25% collapses to a single location (Pale, Capustan, Coral, Y'Ghatan, the Shore, the Spire). Threads meet not as coincidence but as necessity — each character's internal pressure has delivered them to the same point.

Series-level: the same pattern at 10-book scale. The Crippled God is the convergence of all threads.

## Mid-scene drop

Chapters routinely open mid-conflict or mid-conversation. The reader reconstructs the frame from interior context. Explicit exposition is almost always buried in digression or proverb.

## Civilization-as-character

Each civilization has:
- Its own chronology (measured in elder-kind years)
- Its own theology, often lost
- Its own linguistic register in dialogue
- A trauma at its origin (the Imass ritual of undying, the Andii exile from Kurald Galain, the K'Chain civil schism)

## Soldier-chorus

Bridgeburners, Bonehunters, Malazan military present in every book. Their interior monologues are the ground-bass. Their wry humor, their grief-that-does-not-stop, their names-that-they-give-each-other — these carry the moral weight of the series.

## Non-linear time

- Ascendants live across ages
- Warrens (magical paths) connect past and present
- Characters in the present meet characters from books ago who are still "walking" in their own time
- Memory is action: invoking the past changes the present

## Poetic interludes

Verse and lyric prose during mythic-weight scenes. Fisher kel Tath's poetry. Gallan's songs. These are not ornament; they are load-bearing memory.
