# Sanderson — Structural Patterns

Patterns are structural — mined from published work, never from prose. Safe to encode.

## Chapter structure

Typical Sanderson chapter: 2,000–4,000 words. Opens in-scene with a concrete action or decision. Interleaves external event + interior reaction. Closes on an escalation — a revelation, a reversal, a new threat, or a commitment. Rarely closes on contemplation.

## Multi-POV structure

Major novels use 3–6 POV rotation. Each POV owns a subsystem: one arcing on magic, one on politics, one on personal trauma, one on worldbuilding reveal. POVs converge in the final 20% ("the Sanderson avalanche").

## Magic-system formalization

Every system is stated as:
- **Source** — where does the power come from? (Metal, stormlight, breath, chroma, investiture.)
- **Fuel/cost** — what is consumed? (Ingested metal, stored light, hemalurgic spike.)
- **Access mechanism** — how is it used? (Ingestion + will, oath + honor, etching + blood.)
- **Limit classes** — hard (physical), soft (moral), contextual (environmental), social.
- **Failure modes** — what happens when the system is misused or overextended?

## Plot skeleton

Three-act with reversal density:
- Act 1: World established via character-pressure. Inciting event is existing force, not new.
- Act 2: First reversal at ~40%. Second at ~60%. Characters' stated goals change.
- Act 3: Avalanche. All established powers, foreshadowed reveals, and character arcs resolve in 50–80 pages.

## Point-of-view discipline

Close third, past tense, one-POV-per-chapter almost always. Present tense: never. Omniscient: never except interludes. Interludes are world-reveal windows (Mistborn 2 prologues, Stormlight interludes, Elantris kept single).

## The Cosmere meta-pattern

Every magic system is a subset of Investiture expressed through a different Shard. Readers can cross-pattern-match across books. This is how universe-level escalation becomes load-bearing without explicit exposition.
