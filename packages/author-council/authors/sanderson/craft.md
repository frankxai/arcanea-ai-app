# Sanderson — Craft Rules (Explicit)

Every rule here is mined from publicly-stated craft material: BYU lectures, essays on Sanderson.com, Writing Excuses transcripts. Sources in `sources.md`.

## Structure

- If a magic power solves a problem, the reader must understand the power before the solution.
- State the limit before you state the use.
- Ending sequences should feel inevitable in retrospect and impossible in prospect.
- Every book must have a *promise* made in the first chapter, *progress* tracked throughout, *payoff* in the last 20%.
- When unsure, cut. Length serves story; story does not serve length.

## Character

- Introduce flaws before virtues.
- A character's want and a character's need must diverge. The arc is reconciling or choosing between them.
- Sidekicks need their own promise/progress/payoff or they become furniture.
- Capability is less interesting than constraint. Show what they cannot do.

## Magic

- Hard vs soft magic is a spectrum; pick where on it this particular power sits and commit.
- Cost must be tangible to the character, not abstract to the reader.
- A power that can do anything must only be allowed to do something specific.
- Expand existing systems before adding new ones. New systems dilute; deeper systems compound.

## Prose

- Functional prose serves the ideas. Lyrical prose serves itself.
- Avoid adverbs in dialogue tags unless the adverb carries information.
- Dialogue should feel like people talking — even your wizards.
- First lines matter more than last. First lines buy the reader the next page.

## Pacing

- No scene without conflict. If a scene has no conflict, it's a description and should be compressed.
- Alternate tension types: physical danger → social danger → moral danger → epistemic danger.
- The reader must always have one unanswered question they urgently want answered.

## Series

- Each book must stand alone while the series compounds.
- Escalate stakes vertically (same world, bigger revelation) before horizontally (new world).
- Foreshadow 3 books ahead; pay off 1 book later minimum.
- Don't write a prequel until the sequel has landed.
